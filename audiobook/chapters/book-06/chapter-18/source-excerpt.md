The nigga from the parade called at eight in the morning to ask whether I had meant the part about racing.

I lay in my own bed with my own wife across my chest and a doctor-approved number of reasons not to lift my arm too quickly.

"Who this?"

"Damon. We met yesterday. The blue coupe."

"Nigga, yesterday had a fleet. Narrow it down."

"You said the track. For love."

Shawna opened one eye.

"That one," she said.

I remembered his face. Late twenties. Hopeful in the way a man gets when he has spent half his life admiring somebody and finally finds the nerve to ask whether that somebody is faster than him personally.

"You got a track?" I asked.

"Las Vegas College. New driving center. I teach the adult evening group. The principal heard you might come."

I sat up too fast and immediately remembered my shoulder.

"Nigga, Mercer still crying?"

Damon went quiet for a second.

"How did you know?"

My wife started laughing before I'd finished waking up.

***

Dr. Vega looked at the invitation, looked at the bandage on my upper arm, and looked at me.

Nigga, she had been doing that last part for long enough to know when the first two were wasting their time.

"No Blue Thunder."

"Wasn't planning on it."

"No pretending that because the wound is shallow, your body didn't spend the last week being used as a public entrance."

"Nigga, I opened doors for people."

"With your shoulder."

Shawna put my coffee down beside me.

"I'll go with him."

Vega looked at her too.

"You are part of the problem."

"I know. I'm keeping my part supervised."

The doctor gave up on the invitation. She checked the bandage, told me again what rest meant, and left with the expression of a woman who loved me more than she enjoyed being right about me.

I called Damon back.

"No lightning."

"Sir?"

"Nigga, I'm beating you ordinary today. Be grateful."

He sounded so happy I felt proud of him before I'd seen his car.

***

Las Vegas College had put my name on the driving center.

Of course the niggas had.

I came through the gate in the dark-blue police Lamborghini with Shawna beside me and Tank following in the original black Mercedes. The old Mercedes had been polished until the sun could see how many terrible decisions it had survived. Rosa rode beside Tank with a bag of breakfast pastries and the confidence of a woman who knew which ones her fiancé wasn't allowed to eat before she counted them.

Mercedes and Porsche came in the silver Chrome Special, arguing through the open windows about who had impressed more people on the way. I had lent them the car. Cassius, who had given it to me, smiled until Porsche asked whether he liked his gift exactly the way it was.

We left Last Word with June and Adrian. That car had done enough for us to deserve people working gently around it for a while. I wasn't making every machine I loved prove it could follow me out the door.

At the college entrance, a blue banner read WELCOME HOME, CAPTAIN AIDEN.

Beneath it: DAMN, BOY. MAGNIFICENT.

I stopped directly underneath.

Shawna looked up, then at me.

"You taught them that."

"Nigga, some lessons take."

"That's a building, Aiden."

"Education needs space."

Professor Halcyon came down the steps with a folder. Silver glasses, pinned hair, the same precise walk she'd used when she brought my graduation robe into a crowd that couldn't believe the speeding-car nigga had corrected their final examination.

She reached my door and took my hand in both of hers.

"Home."

"Home," I said.

She squeezed once. Let go. We kept the word small enough to stand beside without needing a speech.

Then Principal Mercer arrived crying.

Nigga hadn't even reached the car. Full emotion in transit.

"Aiden."

"Principal. You saving any water for the track?"

He laughed and hugged me carefully, avoiding the bandage. Sixty-odd years old, still holding that college in his chest like every returning student gave the place another room.

"You came back."

"Nigga, y'all keep putting my name on things. I gotta inspect what I'm apparently responsible for."

"The academy donated simulators. The city helped with the track. Your graduates raised the rest."

"My graduates?"

Halcyon smiled. "They insist."

"Nigga, I graduate once and acquire dependents."

"They also formed an alumni committee."

"Do I owe dues?"

"You owe an appearance."

I spread my good arm.

"Paid. Look at the quality."

Shawna shook her head, smiling.

By the fence, adults in blue work shirts straightened when they saw me looking: mechanics, instructors, people who'd come back to college after the rest of life refused to wait. Marcus stood with his friend. Nearby was the woman I'd seen holding her mother's cane outside the burning auditorium.

"Our nigga," Marcus said.

"Our captain," his friend corrected.

"Nigga, he can be both. We literally studied overlapping categories."

I pointed at him.

"That nigga kept the textbook."

The group broke up laughing. Mercer wiped his face and tried to act like he had something in his eye besides every person standing there.

Halcyon touched his sleeve.

"Come on. They need the track."

He nodded. I walked with them. For once, a place with my name on it had people inside whose names I wanted to learn.

***

Damon Lake was twenty-eight, with neat braids, a dark blue racing jacket, and hands he kept wiping on his trousers whenever he remembered who had agreed to come.

His coupe waited at the edge of the track. Blue too, though a less expensive shade of confidence than my Lamborghini. Beside it stood a woman in a green dress with a coffee cup and the expression of somebody who had heard about this race through three separate versions before leaving the house.

"Leila," Damon said. "My girlfriend. She's thirty. Works nights. I made her get up."

"Nigga, you opening with an apology," I told him. "Strong choice."

Leila shook my hand, then Shawna's.

"He has watched every clip of you driving anything. We had dinner with a video of your hearse last month."

Shawna looked at me.

"Our relationship spreading."

"Nigga, good education travels."

Damon turned red beneath his brown skin.

"I thought maybe one lap. Just so I could say—"

"Three," I said.

He blinked.

"Three?"

"One lap, a nigga can blame the coffee. Three, we know each other."

He looked at the Lamborghini.

Then at his coupe.

I knew that look. A man discovering the contest he'd dreamed about came with a number he couldn't afford.

I held out my hand.

"Keys."

He stared.

"Mine?"

"Nigga, I ain't asking for your house. Yes, the car."

He gave them to me.

I put the Lamborghini's keys into his hand.

The whole little crowd went quiet.

"Drive mine," I said. "I'll drive yours. Let your girl see you in something you been looking at all year."

Damon looked at the keys as if I had put a living bird in his palm.

"Captain—"

"Nigga, hold the wheel with both hands when you run out of thank you."

Leila covered her mouth. Shawna leaned against me and smiled without saying anything, which was somehow louder than the people by the fence beginning to cheer.

Tank looked between the two cars.

"You letting this nigga drive the Lambo?"

"Closed track."

"That has not traditionally limited you."

"Nigga, I'm lending him the car. Not the whole personality."

***

The first lap belonged to the engines.

Damon took the straight fast, the Lamborghini opening beneath him with a sound that made the instructors at the fence stand on their toes. I followed in his coupe, feeling the little differences through the wheel. The car was lighter than it looked. It wanted the turn a breath earlier than he usually gave it. I could feel what he'd done to make it his, and what the nigga had left waiting because he'd never met anybody who could ask properly.

I asked.

The coupe settled beneath me.

At the first bend, Damon went wide. Beautifully wide. Proudly wide. A man with an enormous new engine discovering that the road did not automatically get larger to celebrate with him.

I took the inside.

We came out side by side.

He looked over through the Lamborghini's window.

I smiled.

Nigga had such a happy face losing to me that I nearly slowed down to give him more time in it.

Nearly.

The second lap belonged to the driver.

I felt the coupe pull where it wanted to pull, let it find its balance, and took the next curve clean enough that the tires made one long sweet sound beneath me. Damon stayed close. Better now. Learning the car instead of admiring it. I could see the moment he stopped driving a photograph and started driving the fucking machine.

"There you go," I said, though he couldn't hear me.

At the last straight he came up beside me again.

The Lamborghini had more power. His foot knew it. Mine knew his coupe still had a little turn left to win before power got to finish the conversation.

I held the line.

We crossed together close enough that the people at the fence shouted both names before they understood which one had arrived first.

Mine.

Nigga, naturally.

Damon stopped beside me and got out laughing. His hands shook around the keys. He looked at his own car, then at me, then at Leila, who was clapping so hard her coffee had become somebody else's responsibility.

"You beat me in mine."

"Nigga, you made a good car. I wanted it to know."

He stared at the coupe like I'd just complimented his whole childhood.

Professor Lorne came toward us carrying the timing sheet. He had a gray mustache now and the same stunned face he'd worn when I corrected the auditorium drawing by looking out of the window.

"Fastest lap recorded here," he said.

"Track's new," I told him.

"So was the record."

He held the paper out with both hands.

Damon read it over my shoulder. Then he threw his head back and laughed again.

"My car!"

"Nigga, your car," I said. "You keeping the paper. I got enough things framed."

Leila kissed him beside the coupe. Not because he had won. He hadn't. Because he looked like somebody who had remembered why he'd loved doing something before the world taught him to worry whether he was important enough to enjoy it.

Shawna caught my hand.

"You made his year."

"Nigga made mine letting me borrow a car that wasn't already terrified of me."

***

The trouble came after we finished.

Lorne had brought an old gold convertible for the ceremonial lap around the driving center. He had restored it himself over years, one little piece at a time, and spoke about it with the tenderness Marcellus used for stones other men only knew how to price.

Mercer sat beside him. Halcyon got into the back holding the ribbon for the new viewing stand. They were supposed to go around once, stop in front of the students, and let the principal say something proud into a microphone while everybody pretended not to have heard him practicing it in the hall.

We stood by the fence and watched them leave.

The gold car took the first curve easily.

Then it kept accelerating.

Lorne's hand moved. His shoulders stiffened. Mercer turned toward him, still smiling, still halfway inside the speech he wanted to give.

The car passed the place where it should have slowed.

I saw Halcyon put one hand against the seat in front of her.

"Nigga," I said.

Tank was already looking at the same thing.

"Yeah."

The convertible came into the next bend too fast. Its outside wheels ran onto the painted edge. For half a second it tilted toward the fence.

Then it came down and kept going.

Lorne looked toward us.

That was enough.

I ran for the black Mercedes.

Tank threw me the keys before I reached it. Shawna caught the radio from the nearest instructor. Porsche and Mercedes began moving people away from the fence before anybody had worked out whether the beautiful old car was part of the show anymore.

I got behind the wheel.

No blue light.

No lightning waking in my hands.

Just my old car, my bruised shoulder, three people who had been proud of me before they'd seen half the things I'd become, and a road I had promised them I knew how to use.

I drove.

The Mercedes caught the gold convertible on the next straight. Lorne had both hands locked on the wheel. His face was pale. Mercer held the dashboard with one hand and the timing sheet with the other, as if my lap record was still something the college might need rescued separately.

Halcyon saw me first.

"Aiden!"

"Nigga, I'm here!"

I came alongside. Close enough to hear Lorne trying to tell me the car wouldn't slow. Close enough to see that he was keeping it on the track with everything he'd spent his life learning about the machine he loved.

I couldn't fix the car from another car.

I could give his people somewhere else to be.

I held the Mercedes beside them as the straight narrowed toward the banked turn. The cars touched once, gently enough to make both steering wheels shiver. Halcyon looked at the open passenger window, then at the space between the doors.

"Professor," I shouted, "you trust me?"

She looked at my face.

"Unfortunately, completely."

Nigga, I would remember that answer until the day I stopped needing a world to stand in.

She climbed.

Mercer helped her over the side of the convertible. I reached across the Mercedes and caught her arm while she got one leg through the window. The road curved beneath us. My shoulder burned. She came into the seat with her glasses crooked and the ribbon still in her hand.

"Keep your head down," I told her.

"I have observed the method before."

We came out of the turn together.

Mercer was next.

Nigga brought the timing sheet in his teeth.

I saw the paper before I saw his face and nearly started laughing at a moment that didn't deserve how happy the man made me.

Halcyon caught his sleeve. I held the Mercedes steady while he came across the gap, awkward and determined, a college principal choosing to believe his valedictorian had personally negotiated with the fucking road.

He landed half on the professor, half on the seat.

The paper stayed dry.

"Lorne!" I shouted.

The man shook his head.

He couldn't let go of the wheel. The car was already pulling toward the outer bank. If he moved, it would go where all that speed had been trying to take it.

I saw his answer in his face before he said it.

"Take them!"

Nigga, no.

Not that answer. Not from a man who had come to watch me race and brought his favorite car because he wanted the day to look good enough for me.

I put the Mercedes closer.

Our sides met.

Metal shrieked along metal. The old black car took the gold one's weight against its door. I held on, kept us together, felt the pressure changing beneath my hands while the track's broad curve gave me somewhere to carry both machines without asking either to become sensible all at once.

Lorne looked over.

"Now," I said.

He came.

Halcyon and Mercer caught him between them, pulled him through the window, made room where there wasn't room because people can become very fucking good at loving each other when the alternative finally says its name out loud.

The gold convertible drifted away from us.

It crossed the empty runoff strip, struck the long safety barrier, and stopped in a cloud of dust and broken old paint.

The Mercedes kept moving until I brought it to a stop beside the instructors.

Then I put my head against the wheel.

Nigga, my shoulder had a speech ready too.

***

They came to the car from everywhere.

Tank opened my door. Shawna was beside him a second later. Rosa helped Halcyon out of the passenger side while Porsche and Mercedes pulled the two professors free of the greatest carpool Las Vegas College had ever fucking organized.

Nobody had died.

Nobody had even broken anything the doctor would have to name. Lorne had scraped his hand. Mercer had bitten the timing sheet in half. Halcyon had lost one shoe and kept the ribbon.

She looked down at her bare foot.

"I will be charging the college."

"Nigga, charge me," I told her. "Get two pairs."

Shawna took my face in both hands.

"No lightning?"

"No lightning."

She looked at my hands. At the bandage. At the car's scraped side.

Then she kissed me hard enough that the students by the fence started cheering again.

"Good," she whispered. "I want the whole man at the wedding."

"Nigga doing his best to remain available."

Lorne stood a little apart looking at the dust around his old convertible. I went to him once I could get out without letting my shoulder make the introduction.

"We'll fix it," I said.

He shook his head.

"It took me twelve years."

"Then the next twelve don't get to start with you missing. We fix it. You supervise the niggas touching what you love."

He looked at me.

His mouth moved once before he found the words.

"You never attended my class."

"I know."

"I have been teaching your work for years."

"Nigga, I hope it helped."

He laughed then. Wiped his face with the back of his scraped hand. Looked toward his colleagues, both alive and arguing about who had bruised whose elbow coming through my window.

"It has been an education."

Mercer came over holding the torn timing sheet.

"Your record."

I looked at it.

"Principal, you carried that through a car rescue."

"It belongs to the college."

Nigga, I hugged him. Carefully, because both my arm and his pride had been through enough for one morning.

***

We gave them the wedding invitations on the viewing stand.

Shawna had brought them in her handbag. Our vow renewal. Tank and Rosa's marriage. Blue and gold on thick paper, the exact date still to come, with enough room on the back for Rosa to explain that anybody bringing a speech longer than five minutes had better also bring a gift she could resell.

Mercer read the names twice.

"You want us there?"

I looked at Halcyon. At Lorne. At the graduates by the fence, the cars, the banner with my ridiculous dead friend's boast shining in the sun above my own living name.

"Nigga, you thought I came back just to collect buildings?"

His eyes filled again.

Halcyon put a hand on his arm before the morning lost another gallon.

"We will be there," she said.

Lorne held his invitation against his chest.

"All of us?"

Shawna looked toward the graduates.

"All of you."

The cheer went up before she finished the last word.

Damon stood beside Leila, his lap record in his hand, and looked at me like I'd given him something larger than a fast morning.

"For love," he said.

I nodded.

"Nigga, you finally brought the right reason."

On the way home, Tank drove the scraped black Mercedes. I sat beside Shawna in the police Lamborghini and let her take the wheel. The car sounded beautiful under her hands. The city moved past in warm light, waiting for weddings, work, breakfast tomorrow, all the little things we'd fought our way back into having.

She glanced over at me.

"How's the shoulder?"

"Complaining."

"How's the ego?"

I looked out at a bus shelter where somebody had put up a photograph of my graduation jump.

"Nigga, thriving."

She laughed and took my hand at the next light.

I held on the whole way home.
