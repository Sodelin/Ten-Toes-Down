# 14. The Last Road Out

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

Zero's private corridor ended in a garage full of black cars. Nigga, even the man's escape plan had a showroom. We came through the door as his last guards opened fire from behind a limousine. Tank pulled Rosa behind a pillar. Shawna and I dropped beside a low sports car.

Bullets struck its hood, leaving bright scars in paint somebody had probably spent a whole family's wages polishing. I fired toward the limousine. "Zero!" At the far end of the garage, the emperor turned beside a black-and-gold hypercar. He looked at my bleeding arm, my sword, my wife crouched beside me with her gun steady. The nigga looked tired of discovering we were still real.

"You could have stayed in Vegas," he called. "Nigga, you could have stayed out of my fucking life!" He got into the car. A blast door rose behind him, revealing a road that climbed through the palace gardens toward the upper launch deck. His engine roared. The car shot through the opening. The door began closing. Shawna looked at the nearest vehicle.

A small white electric cart stood beside the wall. We both looked at it. Then at each other. "No," she said. "Nigga, I'm flexible, not humiliating." The Last Word crashed through the garage's opposite entrance. June leaned out the window. "You ordered a car?" "Nigga, I ordered a miracle!" "Then quit scratching it!" She jumped out while the car still rolled slowly.

Adrian caught her hand and pulled her behind cover. I slid into the driver's seat. Shawna took the other side. Tank climbed onto the rear step, then Rosa caught his jacket. "You stay with me." He looked at her. "Brother needs—" "His wife is with him. This woman is asking you to stay." The nigga stopped. I met his eyes through the window.

"Get our people out." He nodded. Then he hit the roof with one broad hand. "Bring your ass home." "Nigga, save me a seat." I accelerated beneath the closing door. The metal scraped the roof as we passed. Shawna ducked on instinct. We burst into a garden full of white statues and orange trees, Zero's taillights already climbing the road ahead.

Behind us, the blast door slammed shut. For the first time since morning, it was just me, my wife, and the man we had crossed the world to finish. Nigga, the world got very quiet around that fact. Zero took the garden road fast. He knew every curve. His car hugged the stone wall, black paint flashing beneath the lights.

I followed close enough to see him glance into the mirror. Shawna held the radio. "Liora. Where does the upper road go?" "Launch deck. Private escape craft. There is a bridge across the canyon before the hangar." "Can you reach it?" "We are bringing the airship around. Victor is with me." "Good." She looked at me.

"No private escape craft for that nigga." "Nigga should've booked commercial." Zero fired through his window. The bullet cracked the Last Word's windshield. A white star spread across the glass. I swerved, returned a shot, and heard it strike his rear panel. He accelerated. We climbed a spiral road around a tower.

Below us, the inner courts flickered with gunfire as his remaining men fought people who had finally stopped obeying them. Above, aircraft circled the launch deck. One turned toward the airship rising beyond the wall. Victor flashed blue across the sky. Shawna watched him. "Your nigga's still up." "He's stubborn." "Wonder where he learned that." I smiled without taking my eyes off the road.

At the top of the spiral, Zero's car struck a gate and burst onto the launch deck. We followed through falling ironwork. The deck stretched over the canyon on black steel supports. A narrow bridge connected it to a hangar built into the opposite cliff.

Beyond that, the desert rolled away beneath the late sun, wide enough to hide a man if the man could get there. Zero drove toward the bridge. A private aircraft waited at the hangar, engines turning. The pilot saw us coming and began to move. Then Liora's airship rose beside the hangar like a silver moon with my brother's name on it. The pilot stopped.

Porsche's voice boomed from the loudspeaker. "Nigga, departure is canceled!" Mercedes added, "And keep your hands where the women can see them!" I laughed so hard Shawna slapped my arm. "Drive!" Zero heard them too. His car slowed. For one second I thought the nigga might finally stop running. Then he turned sharply toward a side road descending beneath the bridge.

"There another way?" I shouted into the radio. Liora answered immediately. "Old service road. It reaches the bottom of the canyon." "Of course it does." We followed. The road narrowed against the cliff. No guardrail on the outside. Rock on the inside. The canyon fell away beside Shawna's window in a long red drop. She looked down once. Then she looked at me.

"You got me?" Nigga, she did not say it like a frightened woman asking a hero to become somebody else. She said it like my wife, who had held my shaking hands after lightning, driven me out of gunfire, and watched me put every version of my life between her and a door. "Always." She nodded.

Then she leaned out and fired at Zero's rear tire. The first shot struck the road. The second struck metal. The third made his car twitch sideways. "Baby!" "I told you to keep up!" Zero fought the wheel. His car clipped the cliff, throwing sparks, then straightened on the narrow road. He had skill.

I hated that nigga, but I would not lie about what my eyes saw. The man could drive when his whole life sat behind the accelerator. So could I. We descended toward a lower bridge spanning the canyon's narrowest point. The bridge was old, decorative, built for ceremonial cars before Zero had discovered armored excess. Its stone arches shone in the dying light.

Beneath them, dark water moved through the canyon bottom. Zero reached it first. Halfway across, his damaged tire failed. The car swung sideways. He struck the bridge wall hard enough to break the stone. I braked. The Last Word slid toward him. For a moment our two cars moved together in the dust, mine still straight, his broadside across the bridge.

I turned toward the gap beside him, trying to pass without pushing his wreck into the river. Then the bridge began to break. A deep crack opened beneath Zero's car. Stone dropped into the water. The whole middle section sagged. Shawna grabbed my arm. I saw the far end rising relative to us. Saw the gap opening where the road should have been.

Saw Zero trying to reverse while his rear wheel spun uselessly in the air. I had one burst left in me. Maybe. Nigga, maybe is a terrible word when your wife is in the passenger seat. I put both hands on the wheel. Blue light ran across my fingers. Pain followed it. The Last Word's engine surged as the charge passed through the car.

The world sharpened, not slow enough to make me a god, just clear enough to show me exactly how little room remained for a man. I drove. The car jumped the breaking seam. Its rear wheels struck the far edge. Stone shattered beneath them. We landed beyond the failing middle span and skidded into the cliff wall on the other side. The engine died.

The blue light went out. My hands fell off the wheel. For a moment I could not move. Shawna turned toward me. "Aiden." I tried to answer. Breath. Pain. Nothing useful. She unfastened my belt, caught my face between both hands, and made me look at her. "Stay here with me." I blinked. Her face came back into focus. Dust on her cheek.

A small cut near her eyebrow. Eyes furious and wet and alive. "Nigga," I whispered, "I'm trying." "Try harder." I laughed once, weakly. She kissed me, then opened her door and climbed out. Across the broken bridge, Zero crawled from his car. He was alive. Of course the nigga was alive. Evil had purchased an excellent seat belt.

His car hung at the edge of the missing span. He climbed onto the remaining stone, drew his gun, and aimed across the gap. Shawna fired first. He ducked behind the broken wall. I forced my hand toward my pistol. My fingers shook too hard to close around it. Nigga, I had spent everything getting us across. The strength was gone.

The crown was somewhere behind me with an old man. The sword lay in the car. The whole world could have called me great and it would not have made my hand obey one second faster. Shawna stood beside the wrecked Last Word and kept the emperor behind cover. My wife. My beautiful, impossible wife.

She was not waiting for greatness to recover before she decided to live. I dragged myself toward the open door. Above us, the airship appeared over the canyon rim. Victor called my name. Shawna did not look up. "Aiden! Stay down!" On the far side of the gap, Zero rose with his gun in both hands. I saw his eyes settle on her.

Nigga, my hand closed around the pistol.
