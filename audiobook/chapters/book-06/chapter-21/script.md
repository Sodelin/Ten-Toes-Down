# 21. Something Old, Something Loud

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

Marcellus made our wedding rings in a room that smelled of polished wood, hot metal, and the excellent coffee he refused to let Tank touch with sugar. "Nigga," Tank said, looking into the cup, "why it taste like a serious decision?" "Because you drink syrup," Marcellus replied. Rosa laughed and took the cup from her fiancé.

"Give him something sweet before he proposes to the biscuit tray." We sat around the jeweler's worktable while morning light came through the windows. Outside, Vegas prepared for the wedding with the patience of a city that had already decided patience was somebody else's problem. Florists called. Drivers called.

A man from the sound crew called to ask whether the Sky High choir required separate support for the bass section because one member could accidentally levitate equipment when enthusiastic. Rosa said yes, then demanded to know why nobody had asked that question before ordering the stage. My wife sat beside me, turning our original wedding ring around her finger. "We keeping those," I said.

"Of course." "Nigga, I survived too much for a trade-in policy." She smiled. "The new ones go with them." Marcellus set two narrow bands on the velvet between us. Mine was white gold with a thin blue line through the center.

Shawna's matched it, finer at the edges, bright enough to catch the light when she moved without trying to compete with the ring she already wore. Inside mine, tiny letters read: I CAME BACK. Inside hers: I WAS HERE. I looked at her. She held my gaze.

Nigga, I had crossed continents looking for ways to say more, and my wife had put the whole fucking thing inside four words. "You choose that?" I asked. She nodded. "You like it?" I tried to make a joke. Nothing came. Shawna smiled, softer now. "There he is." Tank leaned forward. "What mine say?" Rosa covered her ring with one hand.

"You find out at the wedding." "Nigga, I need time to prepare emotionally." "You had months." "I used them on flowers." Marcellus laughed and brought out their rings. Tank's was broad, dark gold, built for a hand that had never encountered a delicate situation it could not accidentally turn into furniture.

Rosa's held a small blue stone surrounded by warm gold, a ring that looked good beside both a microphone and a fist. She stared at it. Tank stared at her staring. Nigga, that was his favorite part of being rich. Not the price. The second before the woman he loved found her voice again. "Baby," she said quietly.

"Yeah?" "You did good." The nigga looked down at his coffee like he needed somewhere private to put his face. I put a hand on his shoulder. "Don't fall apart before the fitting. We need your measurements stable." He laughed and shoved my hand away.

After they left to argue with the tailor, Shawna took Dante's watch off her wrist and laid it beside the rings. Marcellus picked it up carefully. "The clasp?" "A little loose," she said. "After the bridge." He examined it beneath the lamp.

I watched his hands move over the watch he had known in a different part of the story, before I stood inside it wearing a dead man's cross and wanting a living woman's whole heart. The old jealousy did not rise. That surprised me for half a breath.

Then I realized it had been quiet for a long time and I had finally stopped listening for it. Shawna looked at me. "What?" "Nothing." "Nigga, that's not your nothing face." I touched the cross beneath my shirt. "I was thinking about the first night. That watch on your wrist." She smiled faintly.

"You thought it meant I was leaving." "I thought a lot of stupid shit with excellent confidence." "Past tense?" "Nigga, let me grow for a minute." She laughed. Marcellus took the watch into the adjoining workroom, leaving us alone at the table. Shawna rested her head against my shoulder.

"I'm wearing it tomorrow." "I know." "And your rings." "I know that too." "And a dress you're going to embarrass yourself about." I turned toward her. "Nigga, why you hide the important information at the end?" She kissed me. The watch ticked softly from the other room while Marcellus worked. It was an ordinary sound now. A little machine keeping time. No accusation. No contest.

Just something a woman I loved had carried through days I could not change and into a morning where she had already chosen where to sit. I put my arm around her. "You think he would've liked me?" I asked. She considered it seriously. Then she smiled. "He would have thought you were unbearable." "Nigga, fair." "And he would have laughed." "At me?" "With you.

Eventually. After you stopped introducing yourself to every mirror." "That's an important professional relationship." She laughed against my chest. I held her there until Marcellus returned. He handed back the watch, repaired and shining. Then he opened a small box of his own. Blue Mercy lay inside.

The stone caught the light with that deep blue fire I had seen men lose their minds trying to own. It had crossed prisons, marriages, lies, dead hands, rich rooms. Now it rested in the palm of the man whose love had given it meaning before any of those niggas tried to put a price on it. "You wearing it?" I asked. He nodded.

"At the wedding." "Good." "I have kept it in the pouch long enough." He slid the ring onto his finger. It fit. The old man's hand trembled slightly, then steadied. Shawna reached across and touched it. "Beautiful." "She was," he said. We did not correct him. He took Celeste's letter from a drawer.

The paper had been folded and unfolded so many times its creases seemed part of the handwriting. He looked at her name at the bottom, smiled to himself, and put it back beside a photograph. Then he closed the drawer. "Now," he said, "show me what you intend to wear with my work." Nigga, the man had returned to his profession. I respected the shift.

The afternoon fitting took place at the mansion because the tailor had looked at the guest list and decided his shop was not structurally prepared. Tank stood on a low platform in a blue suit while three people circled him with pins. He held his arms out like a dangerous angel waiting for somebody to finish making wings. "Too tight?" the tailor asked. Tank flexed.

A seam made a small sound. The tailor closed his eyes. "Please do not answer physically." I laughed from the next platform. "Nigga, he asked." My own suit was midnight blue, dark enough to look black until the light found it. The jacket fit around the sling Vega still insisted I wear when I was not pretending the doctor could not see me.

A narrow opening inside kept the badge where I could reach it, because Knox said she refused to attend a wedding where her best officer had to undress before becoming a problem. The tailor adjusted my collar. Then he stepped back. Then farther back. "What?" I asked.

"I need perspective." "Nigga, it's a suit." "On you, Captain, it has become an event." Tank pointed at him. "That nigga understands the assignment." Rosa came in to check the colors. She saw Tank. Stopped. Then put both hands over her mouth. Tank looked down at himself, alarmed. "Baby?" She shook her head. The nigga stepped off the platform so fast the tailor nearly fainted.

"What's wrong?" "Nothing," she said through her fingers. "You look—" She could not finish. Tank went still. Then he smiled, slow and astonished. I looked away before the moment became something I had stolen by watching too closely. On the terrace, Knox and Seraphine shared a bottle of wine while discussing the logistics of putting a floating choir over a city full of fireworks enthusiasts.

"They will remain at a sensible altitude," Seraphine said. Knox looked at me through the open doors. "My understanding of sensible has suffered recently." "The captain does encourage revision." "That's one word." They laughed together. Two women who had spent years commanding rooms found it pleasant to sit in one where nobody needed rescuing for at least twenty minutes.

Magnolia and Bernard argued over table placements. Cassius and Inez arrived with both fathers, who immediately began correcting each other's memories of old races. Mercedes and Porsche brought a guest list covered in lipstick marks and changes. Liora called from the First Dance to say the airship was ready. Sienna's musicians rehearsed in the garden.

June and Adrian arrived late, tired, and smiling like two niggas hiding a mechanical surprise. Shawna remained upstairs. I tried to go find her. Rosa blocked the stairs. "No." "This my house." "And that dress is none of your business until tomorrow." "Nigga, I'm the groom." "That's why you under restriction." Tank came up beside me.

"We could go through the balcony." Rosa looked at him. He stepped backward. "I was identifying a security concern." I laughed so hard the tailor shouted from the other room that my shoulders were moving again. That night, after the guests left and the house quieted, Shawna came downstairs wearing one of my shirts. No wedding dress.

No jewels except the rings she already wore and the watch ticking on her wrist. She sat beside me on the sofa and put her feet in my lap. "You ready?" I looked at her. Nigga, I had been ready in a motel with a bullet in my side. Ready in a hospital while pretending pain did not frighten me.

Ready on a floating school, on a racecourse, on a broken bridge with my hands refusing to work. But that night I was ready in a quieter way. No gun in my hand. No door to break. Just the woman, the house, and the next morning. "Yeah," I said. She smiled.

"Me too." We stayed on the sofa until the city lights went soft beyond the windows, and for once I let tomorrow arrive without trying to beat it there.
