# 2. Two Queens and a Stolen Ballroom

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

By six o'clock, our hotel had become the most expensive bad decision in the Last City. There were race cars in the courtyard, guns on the billiard tables, and three women arguing with a florist over whether white roses were romantic or evidence that a nigga had given up.

Rosa stood on the piano in the ballroom wearing a green dress and directing furniture like she had conquered the building herself. Tank held her shoes. The pianist, a grown man with gray hair, kept looking at her feet as if God had finally answered a very specific prayer. "Nobody sits with their back to that window," Rosa announced. "Why?" the owner asked.

"Because I just paid to have my hair done and we in a city full of shooters." He moved the chairs personally. I came downstairs in black trousers, a white shirt, my shoulder holster, and the captain's badge hanging against my chest. The blood money years had taught me how to dress for dinner. Police work had taught me that the gun should match.

Shawna came down behind me wearing blue. Nigga, some colors belong to everybody until the right woman walks into them. Then you realize the rest of the world had only been borrowing. Her dress moved like water around her legs. Dante's watch sat on her wrist. My ring shone on her hand.

She looked at the ballroom, then at the crowd looking at her, and smiled without asking any of them for permission. I forgot the thing I had been saying. "Captain?" the hotel owner prompted. "Give me a minute." "Of course." "Maybe two." He understood. Every nigga in the room understood.

Vesper stood by the doors in burgundy silk, speaking to a silver-haired woman whose black gown looked capable of inheriting property. The older woman watched us approach with a small smile. She carried a cane topped by a pearl. I had seen enough royal women to know the cane was either a weapon or an excuse to hit somebody with a weapon. "Aiden," Vesper said.

"Magnolia Saint-Clair. My old tutor." "Not that old," Magnolia said. "Sixty-four is an excellent age for disapproving of emperors." She offered me her hand. I kissed it because my mother had raised me intermittently but effectively. "A captain, a king, and he remembers manners," Magnolia said. "Vesper, you should have brought him years ago." "I was busy," I said.

"Doing what?" "Becoming all that shit." She laughed. A clean, wicked laugh. I liked her immediately. Shawna took her other hand. "I'm his wife." "Yes, dear. He looked at you as if somebody had opened the sun. I worked it out." Magnolia led us toward a table under a chandelier shaped like falling rain. Vesper sat beside her. Shawna sat beside me.

Two queens and a woman who had probably slapped a king with a dictionary. I had entered the room thinking I was in charge of dinner. Then Mercedes arrived with champagne in both hands and Porsche behind her, admitting guests like she had bought the doorway. "The women downstairs want to know if this is private," Mercedes said. "What women?" Shawna asked. "Zero's welcome party.

Their bus driver saw Aiden's cars and developed better judgment." A line of grown women in glittering dresses waited behind Porsche. Dancers, escorts, a violinist, and one woman carrying a diamond-wearing dog that had never looked at a price in its fucking life. "We're happy to be confused," the violinist said. "Nigga, welcome. Confusion gets a glass. Certainty gets you work." They came in cheering.

The pianist caught Rosa's eye and found a rhythm. Tank put her shoes down in front of her with both hands, like the nigga was surrendering two very beautiful hostages. She took the microphone. "My man says he can't dance." "Nigga, don't help her," Tank told me. "I'm the police. The woman made an allegation. We need a demonstration." Rosa held out her hand.

Tank looked at the room. Every nigga suddenly became a witness. "Nobody film my feet." "Nobody wants to miss them," Porsche said. "They going in different directions." He went to Rosa. One hand too high, one foot early, shoulders trying to negotiate a separate song. Nigga danced like a refrigerator being delivered through a doorway an inch too narrow. Rosa whispered in his ear.

He laughed. His shoulders dropped. She moved; he found her. No miracle, just two people choosing the same next step. Shawna leaned against me. "He's practicing." "For the wedding?" "For being happy without looking over his shoulder." I looked over mine. She pinched my leg. "Nigga, you saw him learning and immediately failed the class." I kissed her hair.

"Somebody gotta watch the door." Magnolia leaned closer when Rosa began to sing. "Zero wants every hotel to report your visitors." "Nigga needs a larger notebook." "He recalled the airport landing permissions too." My phone held eleven messages from Cassius Chrome. Landed. Cars detained. Photograph of the nigga standing on a crate, arms folded. Then: BROTHER I HAVE BEEN PATIENT FOR ALMOST NINE MINUTES.

I called. "Where you at?" "Warehouse beside the airport. They say my fleet is imperial property." "You show the niggas your paperwork?" "I showed them my face." "Cassius." "They remained confused. Inez is clarifying." Somebody yelled behind him. Metal clattered. "Nigga, what she using?" "Their desk." "Tell her leave me something. I got dressed for this." I stood up. Shawna stood with me.

Magnolia smiled into her glass. "Dinner before the battle would have been conventional." "Save our plates," Shawna told her. Vesper rose. "I know the airport director." "That good?" I asked. "For us." Tank was still dancing when I reached him. He saw my face, kissed Rosa, and stepped back into his shoes like somebody had rung the bell on a champion.

"Who we getting?" "Cassius and Inez." "What they do?" "Arrived too rich." "Nigga, that's discrimination." June came out of the courtyard with Adrian, her partner. He was thirty-five, lean, with a shaved head and a little beard that made him look like he had strong opinions about engines.

Our men had brought him safely through the old service route, along with three tool chests and, somehow, half a motorcycle. "You going to the airport?" he asked. "Apparently." "Take the eastern road. Main gate has an armored checkpoint." June shook her head. "Eastern road has two." I looked between them. "Which one got better scenery?" They stared. Shawna picked up my jacket. "Main gate.

He likes an entrance." We took four cars. Me and Shawna in the Lamborghini. Tank and Rosa in the Mercedes because she had refused to stay behind while her friends were detained. Vesper rode with Mercedes and Porsche. June and Adrian brought a vicious little silver coupe that looked like it had been built during an argument.

At the airport gate, a man in a black uniform held up his hand. I stopped close enough that he could admire the badge on my hood. "The airport is closed." "Then open it." "To unauthorized arrivals." "I'm a departure from your usual bullshit. Move." He looked at Shawna, then at the cars behind us. A second guard approached holding a tablet.

My photograph was on the screen with a red border around it. "You are expected," he said. "See? Nigga got the invitation." The barrier rose. Shawna watched the first guard step aside. "Too easy." "Maybe my smile working." "It works on me. I still know when you're lying." Inside the cargo yard, a dozen supercars sat under floodlights. Red, silver, black, blue.

Cassius stood on the hood of a white truck in a purple racing jacket, applauding slowly as I drove in. Inez leaned against a yellow car with her arms folded. Three guards lay on the ground near her, awake and reconsidering their employment. "My brother!" Cassius called. "You brought the police!" I got out. "Nigga, I am the police." "Exactly what I told them.

They asked for a local authority." Vesper stepped out of her car. The airport director, a round man with a red face, stopped speaking before he finished turning toward her. "Your Majesty." "Director Pell." "I had no idea these were your guests." "You had several ideas. Most of them were poor." She held out her hand for the keys.

Before he could move, the warehouse doors behind him slammed shut. Floodlights snapped on across the roof. A voice rolled over the yard. "Captain. I hoped you would bring the whole family." Roland Ruin stood on a balcony above the loading bays. Of course that nigga had found a balcony. Men like him believed height was a personality.

He wore his white driving suit again, now open at the throat. A line of Black Mantle guards appeared beside him. Their rifles pointed down at us. Cassius looked up, then at me. "I dislike this airport." "One star," I said. Roland smiled. "You may leave. Your vehicles remain." "You can keep that ugly truck," I told him.

Cassius looked down at the hood beneath his feet. "It is a rental." "Even better." A shot struck the pavement by my shoe. The whole yard moved. Shawna pulled Vesper behind the Lamborghini. Tank shoved Rosa toward a concrete pillar. I drew and fired at the balcony as Cassius jumped off the truck. The guards ducked.

Inez was already moving between the cars, low and fast, keys flying from her hand to mine. "Silver one!" she shouted. I caught them without looking. June's little coupe roared across the yard and struck a stack of empty pallets. Wood scattered. Adrian leaned out and fired toward the warehouse windows.

The distraction gave Cassius enough time to pull a door open on the nearest race car. "Everybody drives!" I yelled. "Aiden!" Shawna called. I turned. She had taken the Lamborghini's wheel. My wife looked at the silver car, then at me. "Keep up." Nigga, she drove away before I could fall more in love. I jumped into the silver car.

It smelled of new leather and expensive gasoline. The engine woke like a predator hearing its own name. Across the dashboard, a small plaque read CHROME SPECIAL NUMBER ONE. "Cassius!" I shouted into the radio. "This yours?" "Was." "What?" "I brought you a gift. Try not to put bullets in the seats." I laughed and hit the gas.

The car shot forward so hard I felt my stomach stay in customs. Bullets cracked off the yard around me. I swerved behind a cargo loader, came out beside the white rental truck, and saw Roland descending a staircase at the far end of the warehouse. He had a black racing car waiting below. That nigga thought he was leaving his own party early.

I swung the silver car around the truck, followed Shawna beneath the balcony, and cut toward the staircase. Roland fired through his open door. A bullet took my mirror. I returned two shots through the empty stretch ahead of him, enough to make him drop into his seat and pull away before his guard could join him.

Tank rammed the front barrier with a cargo tractor. I did not know where he had found a cargo tractor. I had stopped asking where Tank found heavy things. Some men had guardian angels. Mine had industrial equipment. The barrier folded. Rosa stood beside him in the little cab, still holding the microphone she had carried out of the ballroom by mistake.

"Ladies and gentlemen," she shouted into it, "we will be leaving this raggedy-ass airport!" We left in a river of stolen-back luxury. Roland took the outer service road. I followed. Shawna came up on my left, blue police lights spinning over the Lamborghini. Behind her, Cassius and Inez moved together, two bright blades in the night. A plane rolled across the taxiway ahead of us.

Roland accelerated toward it. "He going under the wing," Cassius said. "Then so am I." "Your roof is higher." "Nigga, don't make me nervous after giving me the car." I lowered my head anyway. The black car slipped beneath the plane's wing. My silver roof cleared it by so little I could hear the air change.

On the other side, Roland swung sharply onto a narrow ramp. I followed, tires singing. Shawna took the wider route around the plane and came out ahead of us. She blocked the exit. Roland braked. I closed behind him. For one second we had the nigga between husband and wife, which is where all his decisions had been trying to put him since morning.

He looked from her car to mine. Then he drove through a glass terminal wall. The crash flashed white under the lights. Glass burst into the empty lounge. His car tore between rows of seats, climbed a shallow staircase, and vanished through another window into the passenger pickup lane. I could have followed.

Then I saw the cleaner standing inside the terminal, frozen beside a cart, with a falling metal sign swinging above him. I stopped. Shawna stopped too. I ran through the broken wall, grabbed the man by the shoulder, and pulled him clear as the sign came down hard enough to fold his cart in half. He clung to my shirt, shaking.

A grown man, fifty maybe, with tired eyes and rubber gloves. "You all right?" He looked at the wreckage. Then at me. "My cart." "We'll get you a new one." "It was a good cart." "Nigga, I'm buying you an excellent cart." He laughed once, shocked to hear himself do it. Cassius came through the doorway and surveyed the destruction.

"Roland escaped?" "Yeah." He looked at the man in my arms, then nodded. "Good brakes on that car." "Best part so far." We took the cleaner with us. His name was Omar. He had a wife at home who, according to him, would never believe any of this unless she saw the cars.

So we drove him to the hotel, gave him dinner, and put him in a video call with his wife while Rosa sang behind him and Tank held the new car keys against his chest like a jeweler presenting diamonds. His wife believed him immediately. "Omar," she said, "don't let that handsome captain leave before I get there." Shawna raised her glass at the phone.

"You can look." "I have been married thirty years. Looking is my vacation." The ballroom roared. Omar's wife arrived twenty minutes later with a handbag big enough to discipline a whole household. She saw him beside Tank and stopped with both hands on her hips.

"Nigga, you went to clean an airport." "I did clean some of it." "Now you eating with the nigga on television." "He came through the window." "Aiden came through your window?" "A car came through first. Then that nigga came through." Tank nodded. "That's usually how you know he's close." She looked at me.

"Captain, this nigga has been telling me for thirty years that nothing interesting happens at work." "Ma'am, he was saving it." Omar stood to embrace her. She hit his arm once, then held him for so long the whole table learned why the nigga had wanted to get home. When she let go, he pointed toward the courtyard.

"They got me a new cart." "Nigga, forget the cart. You alive." "It's got a little motor." She paused. "A motor?" "June fixed it." His wife looked at June, who lifted her glass without apology. "This nigga gonna drive it around our kitchen," the woman said. "Only on weekends," Omar promised. "Nigga, I know you." I leaned toward Shawna. "That's marriage." She kissed my cheek.

"That is exactly marriage." I sat beside my wife with dust in my hair and a silver car outside that had been a gift for less than twenty minutes before surviving an airfield gunfight. Inez stole a piece of bread off Cassius's plate. He pretended to object, then moved the whole basket toward her. Magnolia studied them over her wine.

"Another wedding?" "Drive slowly," Inez told her. Cassius smiled into his glass. The violinist approached our table with her instrument under one arm and a plate of food in her hand. Up close she had a little silver scar through one eyebrow and a look that said she had heard men promise things in several languages. "Sienna," she said. "Thirty-one.

I play violin, drive boats, and occasionally make rich men believe they're interesting." "Busy woman," Shawna said. "Depends how rich." She sat when Shawna moved a chair for her. "Those other women are my friends. We worked the emperor's private parties. The motor champions paid, but the palace decided when we could leave.

Tonight I told the driver the wrong address." Mercedes appeared beside her with fresh glasses. "I knew that nigga hadn't got lost. He took every turn like he'd rehearsed an escape." "He had," Sienna said. "For two weeks. Our brother doesn't like leaving either of us behind. My sister plays cello. She's upstairs changing." I looked toward the door where the women had first gathered.

The little dog slept on a silk cushion, wearing enough jewelry to retire a bank manager. One of the dancers was teaching Tank a turn while Rosa laughed at both of them. Everybody had arrived carrying a different reason for wanting to stay. "Palace coming for you?" I asked. "Probably." "Then they know where to find me." Sienna studied me.

"You always say shit like that?" "Only when I'm awake." She smiled, but her fingers tightened around the violin's neck. Shawna noticed. She set her glass down and put a hand over Sienna's. "There's enough room upstairs," my wife said. "You and your friends decide what you want tomorrow. Tonight you eat." Sienna looked at her for a moment.

Then she began eating, fast at first, until she remembered nobody was taking the plate. The pianist asked whether she would play with Rosa. She swallowed, wiped her mouth, and stood. "One song. Then I want cake." Nigga, that woman put the whole desert inside four strings. The ballroom quieted. Her sister came down with the cello and joined before anyone could ask.

Rosa listened for a verse, then found a melody above them, her voice low and rough and full of everything she did not usually explain. Tank stopped moving. I watched him watch her. His big hands rested on the back of a chair. He looked almost frightened by how much he loved the woman singing under that chandelier.

"You good?" I asked when he came beside me. "Yeah." "Your face look otherwise." He rubbed his jaw. "I'm just thinking about that day, man. Her in the dress. All these niggas watching me try not to cry." "Cry, then." He stared. "I'm bringing sunglasses," I added. "For both of us." Tank laughed and leaned his shoulder against mine.

Across the room, Rosa saw us standing together and sang the next line straight at him. My brother smiled like the whole war could wait until she finished. Vesper returned from the terrace carrying a folded note. She laid it beside my plate. "From Roland." I opened it. TOMORROW. THE EMPEROR'S TABLE. COME WITHOUT YOUR ARMY.

Underneath, in a different hand, someone had added: I HAVE NEVER SEEN ANYONE DRIVE LIKE THAT. I passed it to Shawna. She read both lines and shook her head. "Your enemies keep writing you fan mail." "Niggas recognize a man doing his job." She kissed me in front of the whole room. Outside, the Last City lit up another thousand windows.

Somewhere behind them, Zero was planning my death. Inside our ballroom, my friends began arguing about which car should carry Tank and Rosa away from their wedding. I listened to them like a man listening to his future arrive early.
