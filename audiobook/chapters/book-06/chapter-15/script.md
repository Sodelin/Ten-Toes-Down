# 15. The Last Nigga Who Thought He Could

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

I shot him before he could shoot my wife. No speech. No last question for the sky. Nigga, I had heard everything Emperor Zero wanted to say, and none of it mattered more than the woman standing beside my car. My first shot struck his arm. His gun jerked sideways. Shawna fired. He staggered against the broken bridge wall, one hand pressed to his chest.

For a second he stayed standing. I saw the disbelief on his face, the terrible little hope that the world might still remember what he called himself and correct the mistake. Then he lifted the gun again. I fired twice. Zero fell onto the stone. The gun slipped from his hand. He did not rise.

I watched the man die on the bridge he had broken trying to leave everybody else behind. Emperor Zero. All those towers. All those guards. All those niggas told to kneel because one man could not bear the sound of somebody walking away. Dead. My arm fell against the car door. Shawna turned. For one second neither of us moved. Then she ran to me.

She caught me before I could fall out of the seat, climbed halfway into the wrecked car, and held my face against her chest. I could hear her heart. Fast. Alive. Mine was trying to keep up like it had finally found a race worth winning. "Baby," I whispered. "I'm here." "You hit?" "No." "Tell me again." "Nigga, I'm here. I'm not hit.

Look at me." I looked. The cut near her eyebrow had stopped bleeding. Dust streaked her face. She was crying and angry about it, which made her look more like herself than any song, photograph, or expensive dress ever could. "You scared the shit out of me," she said. "I had a difficult afternoon." She laughed through the tears and hit my good shoulder.

"Don't be funny." "Nigga, we discussed my limited settings." She kissed me so hard I forgot the emperor lying across the gap. Above us, the airship lowered toward the canyon. Victor landed on the far side first. He approached Zero carefully, moved the gun away, and looked down at the body for a long moment. Then he looked across at me.

He shook his head once. Finished. I closed my eyes. The word moved through me without needing a voice. Finished. Not my life. Not the trouble I would undoubtedly manufacture in a grocery store before the year ended. This man. This road. This one long chain of niggas deciding they could take what I loved and expect me to become reasonable about it. Finished.

Liora brought the rescue platform close enough for Shawna to help me onto it. Victor crossed the gap and took my other side. Between them they got me out of the Last Word while the car ticked and smoked against the cliff. I looked back at it. The beautiful blue hood was crumpled. One wheel pointed in a direction June had never approved.

The silver MAGNIFICENT badge still shone above the grille. "Nigga," I whispered, "you did good." Shawna looked at the car too. "June's going to rebuild it." "She gonna rebuild me first with a wrench." Victor laughed and held me more firmly. "I will tell her it flew beautifully." "Nigga, do not lead with flew." We rose into the airship.

Rosa reached us at the cabin door. Tank was right behind her. My brother saw the blood on my arm, the way Victor held my weight, the tears on Shawna's face. The nigga stopped like somebody had put a hand through his chest. "Brother." I lifted my good hand. "Nigga, don't start. I'm alive." He came forward and held me anyway. Carefully at first.

Then not carefully at all. His arms closed around me and Victor together because apparently the nigga did not care which part of the rescue package he hugged as long as I remained inside it. "You stupid motherfucker," he whispered. "Accurate." "You magnificent stupid motherfucker." "More accurate." He laughed against my shoulder. Then the nigga cried. Not a lot.

Not a beautiful movie tear somebody could light from the side. Two rough breaths and his face pressed into my jacket while he held the brother he had almost lost again. I put my hand behind his head. "Nigga, we got a wedding." He nodded without letting go.

"Yeah." "Blue flowers." "Yeah." "You still need me to stop you dancing like a refrigerator." He laughed, hard and sudden. Rosa wiped her eyes. "Both of you sit down before Vega murders the survivors." The doctor pushed through the crowd with her bag.

"At last," she said, "somebody understands the stakes." She made me sit on a padded bench and cut the ruined sleeve away from my arm. The wound was ugly but shallow. The shoulder hurt worse. She checked my hands, my eyes, the old scar, the new bruises, and every expression I attempted to disguise as confidence. "No more lightning," she said.

"Today?" "Aiden." "Nigga, people keep making vague medical requests." She looked at Shawna. My wife crossed her arms. "No more lightning until she says." I nodded. That was a prescription I understood. Outside the airship windows, the fortress grew smaller beneath us. Smoke rose from the courts. Cars moved through open gates. Buses carried people toward the desert road.

The black banners still hung, but nobody below seemed interested in saluting them. Porsche stood by a camera mounted near the window. Her face was wet. "I filmed it," she said. I looked up. "What part?" "The bridge. Both of you. The whole city saw." Shawna went very still. "They saw him die?" Porsche nodded.

"And they saw you get Aiden out." Mercedes put a hand on Shawna's shoulder. "Nigga, the comments are losing their minds." I laughed weakly. "What they saying?" Shawna pointed at me without looking away from the window. "Do not feed him while injured." Porsche ignored her.

"One man says you are the greatest driver, officer, swordsman, husband, and airborne passenger in recorded history." Tank sniffed and wiped his face. "That nigga forgot singer." "Another one included singer," Mercedes said. "Educated nigga." The cabin laughed. I leaned back and let the sound hold me while Vega wrapped my arm. We landed in the midnight court's outer garden. Hundreds of people waited.

Noor stood at the front with Salma beside her. Vesper held the ledger folio. Magnolia supported Bernard's arm. June and Adrian stood near the Last Word's damaged transport cradle, already arguing about how to bring the car up from the canyon. Cassius and Inez waited together, her hand in his. Marcellus stood in the center holding the crown. Nobody cheered immediately.

They watched me come down the airship steps with my wife beside me, my brother one pace behind, my arm bandaged, my coat torn, and the whole long day still shaking in my knees. Then one of the palace workers raised a hand. "Captain." Another voice answered. "Aiden." The names moved through the crowd. Captain. Aiden. King.

Nigga, I had heard people say my name because they wanted money, protection, a photograph, a song, a fight. This sounded different. Not purer. I was not about to become some saint because a crowd had found a good rhythm. It sounded like people realizing the door behind them would remain open after I left. I liked that sound. Marcellus approached. He lifted the crown.

"You might sit for this," he said. "Nigga, let me have posture." Shawna laughed and steadied my elbow. The old man placed the crown on my head. This time the crowd did not whisper. The cheering hit the garden walls and came back twice as loud. Tank raised both fists.

Victor shouted my name like we were back at Sky High and I had just ruined somebody's prediction again. Cassius bowed with a hand over his heart. Inez whistled. Knox saluted. Noor and Salma did too, followed by guards who had spent the morning trying to decide whether I was an enemy and were now making a different choice in front of everybody.

Vesper stood beside me. "King of Kings," someone called. The crowd took it up. I looked at my wife. She smiled at me, not at the crown. That was the part that almost broke me. "You impressed?" I asked. She moved closer. "Nigga, I'm trying to remain manageable about it." I kissed her while the whole garden shouted.

When the cheering softened enough for me to speak, I raised one hand. "All right. Listen." The crowd quieted. "The emperor's dead. His roads are open. His prisoners leave. His staff gets paid. Anybody wants to keep shooting about it can find me after I take a fucking nap." Laughter rolled through the garden. "The captains handle their people. The workers decide where they work.

The cars—" Tank sat forward. "—we're discussing the cars." The crowd laughed harder. "And nobody," I said, looking at Bernard, "is charging that man to rebuild his hotel." The owner covered his face. Magnolia held him. I lowered my hand. That was enough governing for the day. Knox approached with a palace officer carrying a velvet cushion.

On it lay Zero's ceremonial key ring, heavy with gold. "The inner treasury," she said. "The surviving officials are requesting your direction." I took the keys. Nigga, some habits survive even a meaningful afternoon. Shawna saw my expression. "Aiden." "Baby, the wedding ain't paying for itself." She laughed and put her hand through my arm.

We walked into the palace together, past guards who bowed, past workers who waved, past portraits that already looked like they belonged to a dead man. Behind us, the airship's painted heart moved gently in the evening wind. TANK + ROSA. Ahead of us, the treasury doors opened.

And for the first time in a long time, the next thing waiting on the other side was a future we had already chosen.
