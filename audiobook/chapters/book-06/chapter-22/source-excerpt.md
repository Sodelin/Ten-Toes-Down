On the morning of the wedding, Tank forgot how to tie a tie.

This nigga had driven through gunfire, carried grown men out of burning rooms, stolen a boat under an emperor's palace, and stood beside me while the world repeatedly attempted to become inconvenient. Now he held a strip of blue silk like somebody had handed him a venomous snake with a dress code.

"Brother."

I looked at him.

"Nigga, you shaking."

"The fabric slippery."

"Your hands slippery?"

"Don't start."

I took the tie and fixed it around his neck.

The room was quiet except for the distant sound of musicians warming up in the garden. Sunlight came through the curtains. Our suits hung perfectly. Our shoes shone. Nobody had called to report an aircraft losing an engine.

That last fact felt like a wedding gift from the universe.

Tank looked at himself in the mirror.

Then at me standing behind him.

"We really doing this."

"Nigga, they rented chairs. We committed."

He laughed, then swallowed hard.

"I used to think getting through the night was the whole thing."

"I know."

"Then another night. Then another. You get enough of them together, you start calling it a life because nobody told you there was more."

I straightened his collar.

He watched my hands in the mirror.

"Then she looked at me like I was somebody she might want around in the morning."

Nigga, my brother's voice broke on morning.

I put both hands on his shoulders.

"She does."

He nodded.

"Yeah."

"And you ain't got to win her again every time the sun comes up. Just be there when it does."

Tank looked at me.

"That sound like something Shawna told you."

"Nigga, I cite my sources privately."

He laughed and hugged me.

Carefully. Vega had threatened everybody about the shoulder.

When he let go, I handed him his jacket.

"Come on. Your woman waiting."

The garden had become a city made of flowers.

Blue roses climbed white arches. Long tables glittered under hanging lights. Beyond them, the First Dance floated above the grounds, its painted heart renewed in gold. Sky High's choir stood on a broad platform beneath the airship, every singer firmly instructed to keep both feet where the music director could see them. Cars lined the drive in colors that made the sunset look underfunded.

The Last Word waited near the entrance.

Rebuilt.

Dark blue. Polished. Its silver letters shining.

June stood beside it with Adrian, both dressed beautifully, both looking like they had slept three hours and considered the result worth it.

I stopped.

"Nigga."

June smiled.

"Wedding gift."

"You fixed it."

"I improved it. Fixed would imply accepting the original driver behavior."

Shawna's name had been engraved on the passenger-side sill.

I ran my hand over it.

Adrian nodded.

"She brought it home as often as you did."

Nigga, I could not argue with excellence that precise.

I hugged them both.

Then Rosa's voice came from somewhere inside the house.

"If the men are crying over cars, tell them to save something for the women!"

Tank stood straighter.

"Yes, baby!"

The guests rose when we entered the garden.

Not because anybody asked them to. Because every nigga there had apparently agreed that watching us walk between those flowers deserved a standing event. Cops saluted. Drivers cheered. Professors applauded. Sex workers whistled louder than all of them and improved the occasion immediately.

Principal Mercer already had a handkerchief pressed to his face.

"He hasn't even done anything yet," Professor Halcyon whispered.

"He's arrived," Mercer said, voice breaking. "He so rarely did."

Professor Lorne nodded with grave academic pride.

"And yet, look at the outcome."

I heard every word.

"Niggas, I graduated once. Let it rest."

Mercer began crying harder.

Tank bent over laughing.

Knox stood near the front in a white uniform. Seraphine sat beside her, elegant and calm, though I saw both women watching the floating choir with the alertness of people who had learned music could become an emergency in my vicinity.

Marcellus held the rings.

Blue Mercy shone on his hand.

Magnolia and Bernard sat together under the shade of a flowering tree. Vesper sat beside them. Noor and Salma had brought their mother, who looked at the two captains, then at my suit, and declared she understood why they had both crossed a desert to attend.

"Mother," Noor murmured.

"I am old, not blind."

Shawna's friends laughed from the next row.

Nigga, everybody had brought their whole selves. I wanted it that way.

The music changed.

Tank stopped breathing.

I felt it beside me.

"Nigga," I whispered, "you need air for vows."

He did not answer.

Rosa appeared at the garden doors.

Her dress was ivory with a blue lining that flashed when she moved. No giant veil hiding her face. No attempt to make her look like a woman who had never lived before this morning. She looked like Rosa: beautiful, bold, nervous beneath the boldness, walking toward the man who had learned every version of her laugh and wanted the rest of them.

Shawna walked beside her.

My wife wore blue silk, pale at the shoulders and deepening toward the hem, with a narrow white wrap over one arm. Her wedding rings shone on one hand. Dante's watch shone on the other. She looked at me as she came down the aisle, and every room I had ever entered without her became suddenly difficult to remember.

Tank made a small sound.

I made a worse one.

The nigga turned toward me just long enough to whisper, "You too?"

"Nigga, look at them."

"I am."

"Then mind your own collapse."

We both laughed through eyes that had become inconveniently wet.

Rosa reached Tank.

He took her hands like he had been given something fragile and then remembered she would beat his ass for thinking that.

"You look—"

"I know," she whispered, smiling.

He laughed.

"Yeah."

Shawna came to my side and slipped her hand into mine.

The officiant began.

He was an older man Rosa had chosen because he could speak warmly without sounding like he was selling furniture. He looked at the crowd, the airship, the fleet of cars, the two captains in the front row, the jeweled king standing as best man, and took one deep breath before deciding to keep the introduction short.

A wise nigga.

When it was time for Tank's vows, my brother unfolded a sheet of paper.

His hands shook.

Rosa waited.

He looked at the page.

Then folded it again.

"I wrote a lot," he said.

The crowd laughed gently.

"Aiden said some of it sounded like I was buying a refrigerator."

"Nigga, the warranty language was concerning," I said.

Rosa laughed and squeezed his hands.

Tank took a breath.

"I know how to stay," he told her. "I stayed for people who didn't deserve it. Stayed in rooms where leaving would've been smarter. Stayed because I thought leaving made a man weak."

He looked into her face.

"But with you, staying don't feel like proving something. It feels like getting something. A morning. A song. You cussing at me because I put the wrong shirt in the wash. You standing in the kitchen eating cake with a fork you pretend you ain't using."

Rosa wiped her cheek with the back of one hand.

"I don't promise I'm gonna become smooth," Tank said. "These niggas know me. I can't lie in front of this many witnesses."

The garden laughed.

"I promise you won't have to wonder where I am when you call. I promise I will learn the dance. I promise I'm gonna be there when the music stops too."

His voice broke.

"And I promise every good thing I get, my first thought is gonna be, Rosa should have some of this."

Nigga, I looked away at the flowers and found no help there.

Rosa held his face between her hands.

"You big beautiful man," she whispered.

Then she began her vows.

"I had people listen to me sing and never hear a fucking word I said afterward. You heard me when I was tired. You heard me when I was angry. You heard me say I wanted the green shoes and came back with three pairs because you forgot which green."

Tank laughed.

"They all looked right."

"They did."

She smiled through the tears.

"I promise to tell you what I want. I promise not to make you guess when the truth would be kinder. I promise to keep dancing with you even when you try to count the steps out loud."

"That helps me."

"I know, baby."

The whole garden seemed to lean toward them.

"And I promise that when somebody asks who my husband is, I'm gonna point at you with my whole fucking hand. No little embarrassed finger. That man. That beautiful, loyal, loud, impossible man. Mine."

Tank closed his eyes.

One tear ran down his face.

Rosa kissed it away before the officiant could get to the next instruction.

Nobody objected.

The rings came from Marcellus's hands.

Tank read the words inside his.

STAY FOR THE LAST SONG.

He looked at Rosa.

Her ring read:

I'M STILL DANCING.

Nigga, that old jeweler had no respect for anybody trying to remain composed.

They exchanged the rings.

They said yes.

They kissed.

The garden erupted.

The choir rose a foot off the platform in excitement. Seraphine lifted one finger. Every singer came back down immediately without missing the note.

Knox watched, impressed.

"Excellent control."

"Practice," the dean said.

Tank lifted Rosa off the ground.

She laughed against his mouth and told him not to wrinkle the dress.

He put her down as carefully as he had ever handled anything in his life.

Then he turned toward me.

Nigga, my brother was married.

I hugged him hard enough for Vega to make a disapproving sound from the front row.

"We did it," he whispered.

"You did it."

"Nigga, you stood here. That counts."

I held him another second.

Then Shawna took my hand.

Our turn.

We did not need a second grand entrance. She removed the white wrap from her arm and let Rosa place it around her shoulders. The music softened. The guests sat. The same garden held a different quiet as my wife and I stood beneath the arch.

I looked at her.

The first wedding had felt like grabbing happiness before the world could close its fist.

This one felt like opening my hand and finding she had chosen to remain inside it.

The officiant asked whether we wished to renew the promises already made.

"Nigga," I said softly, looking only at her, "more than anything."

Shawna smiled.

"Yes."

I took both her hands.

"The first time I married you, I thought I had to promise the whole world because I didn't know what else a man could give a woman like you. Houses. Cars. Money. A city if I could find one with poor supervision."

The guests laughed.

"I still like giving you those things. I'm not about to embarrass myself by pretending I stopped enjoying being rich."

Shawna laughed, bright and familiar.

"But you met me in the parts I couldn't buy. The hospital. The car after the lightning. The bridge when my hands wouldn't work. You were there when being Aiden didn't feel like enough for Aiden."

Her eyes shone.

"And every time I got scared you might finally see all of me, you looked closer. Nigga, you looked closer and stayed."

I swallowed.

"So I promise this. When I say home, I mean where you can find me. When I say forever, I mean the next morning too. When I say I'm the greatest man alive—"

The crowd began laughing before I finished.

"—I mean I'm gonna spend every fucking day giving you new evidence."

Shawna laughed through tears.

"I knew you'd get that in."

"Baby, the vows got standards."

She squeezed my hands.

Then she spoke.

"You came into my life loudly."

Tank nodded from beside us.

"Very loudly."

"You were jealous, ridiculous, brave, and convinced a mansion could solve a conversation."

"Sometimes it helps," I said.

"Sometimes it does."

She smiled.

"You never became easy. I didn't marry you because I thought you would. I married you because beneath all that noise was a man who would hear me when I said my own name. A man who learned I could carry a past and still choose a future. A man who comes back."

She touched the new band in Marcellus's hand.

"I promise to keep choosing you on purpose. I promise to tell you when you're being unbearable."

"Nigga, that's been consistent."

"I promise to laugh when you're funny and when you're so full of yourself I need the relief. I promise to sing in the house even when you insist on joining the chorus."

"The public appreciates it."

"The public isn't married to you."

The garden laughed.

She stepped closer.

"And I promise you will never have to compete with a ghost for a place that already belongs to you. You are my husband. My living, impossible husband. I am here."

Nigga, I could not have answered if the whole world had offered me another crown.

I kissed her hands.

We exchanged the new rings beside the old.

I CAME BACK.

I WAS HERE.

Then the officiant said we could kiss, which was kind of him, given the evidence that we had never intended to wait for permission.

The choir sang.

The guests stood.

My wife put both hands on my face and kissed me beneath the blue roses while the city, the sky, and every nigga I loved made enough noise to let the dead know we were still doing all right down here.
