The shame had been sitting on him like sweat, but now something deeper came up. He looked past me into the house. I heard him, Tank said. I was standing right there. I shoved him once more. He let me go. The front room still looked like a place where money went to get drunk. Two bottles of champagne sat in silver buckets.

A woman I didn't know had left one red heel under the piano. Somebody had put a little pile of cocaine on a photograph of me and Shawna because there wasn't enough table in the whole fucking house. Her face was under it. I swept everything onto the floor. Get these niggas out.

There were only four men left inside, and all four were already moving. Everybody, I said. One of them had a woman's handbag slung over his shoulder. He looked down at it like he'd just discovered a child. He went back toward the hall, remembered my face, and left with it. Tank shut the ruined door as well as it could be shut.

You want me gone too? I looked at him. His jacket was torn. He'd lost a cuff link at the Saint. There was blood on his hands that wasn't mine or his, and he'd followed me out anyway. Get the money. He went upstairs. I sat on the piano bench because it was the nearest thing and because my legs had started shaking.

Shawna had been sitting there that afternoon. She'd played three notes, missed the fourth, and told me the piano was cheap. I'd offered to buy her another one. She'd asked if I was going to buy a different instrument every time she made a mistake. I'd told her yes. Nigga, I'd meant it. We would've had a fucking orchestra by Christmas.

Now her perfume was still in the room and Red had her. I put my head down. For one ugly second I saw him kissing her. I saw the dance again, her hand on his shoulder, the way she'd leaned close because we'd agreed she had to. I'd said I could take it.

I'd stood there dressed like a king and discovered I couldn't take shit. The case upstairs hit something with a heavy thump. Don't you drop her money, I shouted. I got it. You been saying that. He came down carrying the money in two bags. No silver case.

He'd saved the cash and left the case broken in the pool, just like he'd left the rest of us thinking it was all gone. He opened the bags on the dining table. Three million dollars filled the space between two candles Shawna had bought because she said the house smelled like men lying to each other. Tank stood back. All of it.

You count it? More than once. That make you feel good? He shook his head. I picked up a bundle and threw it at him. It hit his chest and broke against the floor. There's your fucking tip. He watched the bills settle. Nigga, look at me. He did. What did you think was gonna happen? Nigga, I thought you were gonna die.

The room got quiet enough for me to hear the refrigerator. Tank rubbed both hands over his head. That morning, before she drove off. You were bleeding all over the seat. She leaned through the open door and kept asking you to look at her. You kept saying the same shit about a house. Water. A room for singing.

Nigga, you weren't answering her half the time. I thought you were already gone. So you went shopping. So I went back. His voice rose. He looked ashamed of that too, but he kept going. T was there. The money was there. I looked at that nigga on the ground, and I thought about all the years I stood behind him. Every room. Every car.

Every time somebody said his name wrong, I was the nigga who got up. You loved that nigga. Yeah. I stared at him. Say it again. I loved him. You want me to lie about that too? His lip was bleeding down his chin. He didn't wipe it. You knew him one night. I knew him ten years. He was a piece of shit.

I know. He'd give you something with one hand and keep the other around your neck. I know that too. But when I got there, I still wanted the nigga to get up. I looked down at the money. And when he didn't? I took what was left.

That was the first thing he'd said that didn't make me want to put him through a window. It didn't make me forgive him either. That nigga Red got to you after. Tank nodded. Said he knew. Until he put his hand on her and you stopped breathing. I felt the room tilt. Nigga, don't. I'm telling you. Don't tell me what I looked like. You looked like you did in that car. I picked up a candle and threw it into the fireplace. The glass broke. Tank didn't move. I ain't dying in another fucking car. Then let your nigga drive.

Let me get one fucking thing right. The lights outside went blue. For one stupid second I thought they had brought Shawna home. I was on my feet before I heard the cars stop. Tank moved toward the front window, then turned back with a look I didn't like.

"Not ours." A man came through the broken door wearing a police uniform and the kind of confidence that had never learned what a door could mean to the man behind it. Captain Wade Frost. Forty-six. Silver hair. Pink face.

Two officers behind him, both younger, both looking at the money on the table as if the room had just asked them a very personal question. Frost looked at my blood, then the bags, then my badge. "Take it off." I stared at him. "Nigga, you selling shirts now?" "The badge." Tank stepped between him and the table.

"You got an appointment?" "Move." "Nigga, this a house. Ask like you walked into one." Frost put his hand on his gun. The two officers behind him looked at each other. Neither moved closer. I came around Tank. "What you want?" "Redmond filed a complaint. Theft. Assault. Armed men at his property. A woman being held against her will." I felt my face go cold.

"He got that last part right." "We're here to recover the money and suspend your authority until this is settled." "Nigga, who sent you?" "You don't question me." "That's interesting. I'm doing it beautifully." He took a folded paper from his pocket and held it toward me. I didn't take it.

"Knox sign that?" "The chief has larger concerns." "Nigga, the chief bought me a Lamborghini yesterday. She thinking about me plenty." One of the younger officers looked at the paper again. A Black woman, thirty-one, name of Bell on her chest. She'd been at the station when I brought the robbers in. She had eaten the lunch I bought.

I could see her trying to fit that morning into the room she was standing in now. Frost noticed her hesitation. "Bell. Collect the bags." She didn't move. "I said collect them." I put my phone on the table and called Knox. Frost stepped toward it. Tank caught his wrist. "Nigga, let the telephone ring." Knox answered on the second ring. "Aiden.

Tell me you have her." For half a second I couldn't speak. The fact that she knew. The fact that her first question wasn't about the money or the fire or the badge she had pinned on a criminal's chest the morning before. "Not yet. You send Frost?" Silence. Then her voice changed. "Put me on speaker." I did. "Wade," she said.

Frost stopped pulling against Tank's hand. "Chief. We're securing—" "You at his house?" "We have evidence of—" "You at his house, Wade?" He looked at the phone. "Yes." "Then you listen carefully. Captain Aiden retains his command. You have no order from me.

You bring whatever Redmond put in your hand to my office, and you bring your own badge with it." The room went quiet enough for me to hear Bell breathe out. Frost's face changed. Whatever authority he had brought through the door began leaving him through his eyes. "You choosing this thug?" I tilted my head.

"Nigga, you can say Captain while you cry." Knox didn't laugh. "I'm choosing the officer who carried people out while your friend locked the doors. Get out of his house." Frost's hand jerked toward the phone. Tank let go of his wrist just as I came forward. The man stumbled into the table, knocking a candle over.

One of the money bags slid toward the edge. He reached for the bag with one hand and his gun with the other. I hit him before either hand got what it wanted. The table rocked. Money flew across the floor. Frost went backward into the chair and took it down with him. His gun skidded under the piano. Bell already had her weapon out.

It was pointed at Frost. "Stay down, sir." He looked up at her in disbelief. "You're taking his side too?" "I'm following my chief." "Nigga," I said, "people keep making this clear for you." Frost tried to get up anyway. Tank put a hand on his shoulder and returned him to the carpet with the calm of a man correcting where a heavy box belonged.

I picked up the folded paper. Red's handwriting sat across the bottom. Three short lines. The money back. Aiden disarmed. Shawna's name used like a rope around both requests. I held it toward Bell. She read it. Her mouth tightened. "He sent you for her rescue money," she said. Frost didn't answer. She put him in cuffs.

The other officer picked up the paper, folded it carefully, and stood beside her. He looked at me before they left. "Captain, where do you need us after this?" I looked at the floor. At Shawna's million-dollar future scattered beside the leg of a chair. At Tank kneeling to gather the bills before anybody stepped on them. "With Knox.

Make sure nobody else comes here in a uniform they borrowed from Red." He nodded. "Yes, Captain." They took Frost out through the door he hadn't bothered knocking on. The police lights moved across the ceiling, then went away. Knox was still on the phone. "Aiden." "I'm here." "I have officers at the Saint. He's moved her. Your car's safe.

We can bring it to you." "Keep the roads open. Keep my people from getting stopped on the way to that unfinished casino. That's where he's taking her. Midnight." "I'll hold the outside when you get there." "Chief." "What?" I looked at the badge inside my ruined white jacket. "Nigga doesn't forget this." Her voice softened just a little. "Then bring your wife home, Captain.

Give me something worth remembering too." The call ended. Tank put the last bundle back into the bag. Every dollar stayed. He set the fallen candle upright and looked at me. "Nigga, you really are the police." "We discussed that at graduation." "I heard the words." "You seeing the picture now?" He nodded slowly.

Then, for the first time since Red opened that drawer, my brother smiled at me without looking ashamed of the fact that he wanted to. The front door moved behind us. Rosa came in wearing a man's coat over her gala dress. One earring was gone. She had Marcellus by the arm.
