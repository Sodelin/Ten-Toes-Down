# 16. The Last King of Vegas

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

Red had her standing beneath a chandelier that had never been turned on. Nigga hadn't even finished building the place where he wanted to die. Everything around them was unfinished. Marble on one wall, naked concrete on the next. A staircase big enough for a king coming down into a floor covered with building dust. The place was supposed to be Red's next casino.

He had brought my woman here to make me watch him take my whole life away before the fucking doors even opened. I stepped out of the Mercedes. Tank got out beside me. Behind us, the convoy stretched across the parking lot, headlights burning through the dust. Niggas I had known for years. Niggas who had started calling me boss eleven days ago.

Blue on their wrists, blue at their throats, guns in their hands. Nobody was smiling. Nobody had come to make peace. I looked up at Shawna. Her dress was torn at one shoulder. Somebody had hurt her mouth. She held her chin high, but I could see her looking for me in the dark. I raised my hand. She saw it.

That was all I needed. That was the whole motherfucking world right there. Every fucking dollar I'd ever touch could burn before I'd leave her up there. Red stepped into view beside her, wearing white from his shoes to the collar of his shirt. The blue stone on his right hand flashed when he put that hand on her shoulder. Forty million dollars. Marcellus's life.

Celeste's last letter. Dante's blood. All that shit sitting on a finger like it had always belonged to him. "Aiden," he called down. "You brought everybody." "I told them there was a dead nigga here." Tank looked at me, and I could feel what he wanted to say. Don't let this nigga make you move before you think.

Don't give him another piece of you because you're mad. But I was past mad. I had gone through mad on the highway. Whatever was in me now had no place left to go except straight up those stairs. "Come inside," Red said. "Just you and Tank." I started walking. Tank caught my sleeve. "Nigga." I stopped.

"We get her and we leave." "We get her," I said. He looked at me for another second. Then he let go. Inside, Red's niggas stood between roulette tables covered in plastic. I could see guns beneath jackets. I could see the ones who weren't bothering to hide them.

There must've been thirty motherfuckers waiting for us in that room, and I walked between them with my head up because Shawna was watching. Thirty niggas. I counted them because I wanted to know how many men I'd tell her I walked through when we got home. My captain's badge showed inside the ruined white jacket.

Some of those men looked at it longer than they looked at my gun. Outside, Knox and Bell held the road; Red could hear the sirens and knew there was no uniform left coming to save him. We were going home. I had already decided that shit in the car. Red came down the staircase with Shawna beside him. He held her elbow.

Every time his fingers tightened, I wanted to tear that arm out of his fucking jacket. "Nigga, let her walk," I said. "She is walking." "Nigga, to me. Let my woman walk to me." He stopped halfway down. "You haven't paid for anything yet." Tank stepped closer to me. "We got your money." Red laughed.

"My nigga, you think this is about the three million now?" "That's what you been asking for." "I asked for respect. The money was the easy part." He looked at me. "Tell me something, Aiden. How much did she tell you about that first night?" Shawna closed her eyes. I didn't look away from him.

"Enough." "Did she tell you she was supposed to get in a car with whoever T sent? Could've been you. Could've been Tank. Could've been any nigga with a pulse and a place to hide the money." "Don't," Shawna said. Red leaned close to her. "We're being honest. That's what lovers want, isn't it?" I could hear Tank breathing beside me. Red lifted Shawna's wrist.

Dante's watch hung loose against her skin. "Look at that. You got a palace, cars, an army. You got motherfuckers out here willing to die because they think you're the next big thing. And she still wears a dead man's watch." Something opened up inside me. It wasn't the wound. I had looked at that watch every morning. At the breakfast table. In bed.

When she put her hand on my face. I had pretended it didn't bother me because I thought a man with everything wasn't supposed to be jealous of somebody in the ground. But I was. I was jealous enough to hate myself for it. "Tell him," Red said to her. "Tell him whose chain made you go over there." She looked at me.

"Dante's." I felt Tank turn toward me. I kept my eyes on her. "First time I kissed you," she said, "I was looking at his cross. I wanted something of him back. I didn't know what I was doing." Red smiled. Nigga thought he had me.

He was watching my hand, waiting for the gun to sag, waiting for me to look at her the way he wanted. He'd have paid to see me hate her. "And now?" I asked. Shawna swallowed. There was blood at the corner of her mouth. "Now I'm looking at you." Red's smile went away.

"Don't give that nigga that shit." "I'm looking at you, Aiden." I nodded once. "Then keep looking, baby." Red hit her. Nigga hit her right in front of me. I moved before I thought about it. Tank grabbed my arm, but I was already pulling free. Guns came up all around the room. I heard men shouting my name.

I heard Red tell them not to fire yet. Shawna caught the banister instead of falling. She lifted her head slowly, and the look she gave him was the same look she must've given Valentino in that car. Red saw it too. He put his pistol under her jaw. Everything stopped. "One more step," he said. I stood at the foot of the stairs.

My heart was beating so hard it hurt the place where T had shot me. "You win," I said. Tank stared at me. "Aiden." "Let her come down. You want the west side, take it. You want the house, take it. My money. My name. Whatever the fuck you want." Red watched my face. I meant it.

I had been telling everybody I was a king, but I would've walked out of that room with nothing but her hand in mine. We could sleep in the fucking car. Let the nigga have the house. "You hear that?" he asked Shawna. "He'll give me everything." "Then take it and let me go." He shook his head.

"Dante offered me everything too." The words hung between us. Red looked down at the ring on his hand. "Your husband thought having a brother made him untouchable. You think having these niggas outside makes you untouchable. Everybody thinks there's one nigga who won't sell them." He looked at Tank. "How much you take out of that pool again?" Tank's face hardened.

"I gave it back." "After I told him you had it." "I gave it back." Red laughed at him. "Come stand over here, Tank. You don't gotta die for a nigga you met eleven days ago." Tank looked at me. I didn't say anything. I had already asked him for more than I had any right to ask.

I wasn't going to beg him now with Red watching. Tank put his hand on my shoulder. Right over the place where Big T had put his hand on the first night. "That's my brother," he said. Red shot him. Tank hit me as he went down. His weight knocked me sideways.

The first burst of gunfire tore through the room before his body reached the floor. I fired up the stairs. Red pulled Shawna against him and went backward through the doorway at the top. I tried to follow him with the gun, but there was too much of her in front of him.

Too much of my whole fucking life between me and the man I wanted dead. Tank grabbed my ankle. "Go," he said. Blood was coming through his shirt below the shoulder. "Nigga, go get her." The men outside hit the doors. Glass came down all over the entrance. I heard our people shouting. I saw blue rags moving between the tables.

Somebody went down across a roulette wheel. Somebody else fell through the plastic over the bar. Guns flashed in the dark like the whole casino had caught fire from the inside. I went up the stairs with Tank's voice still in my ears. The first man came around the landing in a brown jacket. He fired before he saw how close I was.

The shot broke the rail beside my hand. I hit him against the wall and kept moving while his gun struck the steps behind us. Below, Rex Crown came through the doors with our people. He didn't have a gun in his hand.

He had a man in each hand, one by the jacket, one by the belt, and the look of a fighter who had finally found a room large enough to pay all his debts at once. "Captain!" he shouted. "Stairs!" He threw one man into the other and came toward them. I reached the first landing. Red was above me with Shawna.

He looked down, saw I was still coming, and pulled her harder toward the unfinished corridor behind him. She stumbled. Caught herself. Turned her face toward me for half a second. Nigga, that half second got me up another flight. A gunman stood between two empty light fixtures at the top.

He was young enough to think standing high made him safe and old enough to regret learning otherwise. Twenty-seven, I found out afterward. Malik. Three people depending on the money Red paid him to stand in that spot and die if necessary. He had his gun aimed at my chest. I stopped one step below him.

"Move." He looked at the badge showing through my torn jacket. "Captain Aiden?" "Nigga, this is a terrible place to introduce yourself. Move." "Red said you weren't really—" A shot came from behind him. Malik ducked. The man deeper in the corridor was firing past his own employee to get at me.

I caught Malik by the collar and pulled him down the step beside me. The second shot struck the wall where his head had been. He stared at the white dust falling over his shoes. "Your boss very generous," I told him. His face changed. I fired down the corridor. The man at the far end fell back behind a pillar.

Malik turned his gun away from me and toward the pillar instead. "Go," he said. I looked at him. "Nigga, decide properly." He raised his voice. "Go get her, Captain." I went. Behind me I heard him shouting at the others to put their guns down. Some did. Some didn't.

The casino filled with choices being made at the loudest possible volume, and for once the niggas who chose me got to keep breathing long enough to appreciate the decision. At the foot of the stairs, Porsche dragged a wounded man behind the roulette table where Tank lay. Mercedes stood over both of them, firing toward the dark bar whenever another shape moved there.

"Rosa's coming!" Porsche shouted up at me. I didn't know how she knew. I didn't need to. My people had begun finding each other without waiting for me to hold all the names in my head. Little Saint came through the side entrance with three more men. He pointed up at me. "That's our nigga! Clear him a road!" I was already on it.

The corridor split around an unfinished fountain. Red's white jacket disappeared through the far doors. I followed, one hand against the wall, blood warm beneath my bandages. A man stepped out beside the fountain with a shotgun. I dropped as he fired. The edge of the fountain burst. Water left in its bottom jumped into my face.

He tried to bring the gun down toward me, but I was already against his legs. We hit the floor together. The shotgun slid away. He caught my wounded side with an elbow and the pain opened so wide I couldn't see. I heard Shawna shout my name from the room beyond us. That brought me back.

I hit him until his hands came off me. Not because I stopped hurting. Nigga, I hurt everywhere. I hurt in years I hadn't even lived yet. But she had said my name like the whole world depended on whether I answered, and I wasn't going to make her ask a dead man again. I got up. The man reached for my ankle.

I looked down at him. "Don't make this your whole life." He let go. I took the shotgun off the floor and fired toward the far wall when another man came through the doorway shooting. The man dropped behind a stack of marble tiles. I left the gun and kept my own pistol in my hand. Ahead, the desert showed through a room without glass.

Headlights burned below it. The balcony rail ran along the open edge, thin and dark against all that distance. I heard Red breathing hard. Then his voice. "You don't get tired?" I came through the door. "Nigga, you keep giving me somewhere to be." The room beyond was open to the desert. They hadn't put the glass in yet.

A balcony ran along the edge, looking down over the lights of our cars. Red had Shawna against the railing. Her little silver pistol fell out of his jacket as he hauled her against the railing. It struck the floor beside his shoe. "Nigga, that gun drops now," he told me. I stopped in the doorway.

"Drop it or she goes over." There was nothing beneath the balcony but concrete and steel. Shawna looked at me. Her eyes were wide. She was scared now. Really scared. No black dress, no diamonds, no cold story about killing Valentino could hide that from me. "Put her down," I said. "Put the gun down." I lowered it. Set it on the floor.

Red watched me do it. Then he smiled. "That's how your husband died, Shawna. That's how Dante went down. Hands open. Begging. T couldn't even look at him." "He wasn't begging for himself," she said. Red's arm tightened.

"What?" "You said he wanted somebody to take care of me." "Shut the fuck up." "He wasn't begging for himself." She drove her head backward into his face. He staggered. His gun went off into the empty sky. Shawna dropped to the floor, reached for her little silver pistol, and came up firing. The first shot hit his leg.

The second hit him in the chest. Red fell against the railing, one hand still reaching toward her. "Dante," she said. Just his name. She tried to raise it again. Her hand was shaking so badly the barrel fell below his chest. Red was still standing. He lifted his pistol. I had mine back in my hand by then. I put him down.

I fired until his gun fell out of his hand. Until his knees hit the balcony. Until the nigga who had taken years from her life was on the ground looking up at a sky he wasn't ever going to own. Then I went to her. She was on her knees, still holding the silver gun. "Baby." She didn't look up.

"Shawna." Her hand wouldn't open. I put mine around it. "It's done." She shook her head. I wanted her to say my name. Nigga, I wanted it so bad I could hardly breathe. She kept saying Dante's. I put my gun down beside hers and got both arms around her. I held her on the floor while she cried for Dante.

And I held her tighter because I was the one who was there. Downstairs, somebody shouted that Red was dead. Then somebody shouted my name. More voices picked it up. Through the hall. Across the casino. Out into the parking lot where all those headlights were still burning. Aiden. Aiden.

My nigga, I had blood in both shoes and her face against my neck, and they were calling me the king of Vegas. I believed them. Before we left, I took Blue Mercy off Red's hand. He'd stopped reaching for anything by then. Forty million dollars in my fist, and I closed it so hard the setting cut my palm.

Tank was alive when we got back downstairs. Rosa had come after us with Dr. Vega. She told me later she'd sat in the house watching the clock until she couldn't fucking stand it, then gone to get the doctor and driven out behind the convoy. They came through the doors after the shooting stopped.

Vega was bent over a wounded man near the entrance when I saw her. Tank lay beside the roulette table with Rosa holding a folded tablecloth against him. Her dress was covered in blood. She looked at me like she was ready to fight God if he tried to take another person out of that room.

"Where the fuck you been?" "With her." She looked past me. Saw Shawna. Her face broke, and she had to press her mouth shut before she could talk again. "Come here, baby." Shawna went down beside her. Tank opened his eyes when I knelt. "My nigga. You got her?" "Yeah." "Red?" "Dead." He tried to smile. It hurt him.

"You better pay my fucking hospital bill." I took his hand. "Every penny." "And the car." "What car?" "I want one." "Nigga, a hundred. Your nigga will buy you a hundred fucking cars." He shut his eyes again. "Black ones." I held on until the doctor came. I kept my hand around his.

The last nigga who called me family had put a bullet in me before the night was over. Tank had taken one instead. Every time a voice came through the door, I looked up expecting somebody to tell me what else I could pay to keep him breathing. I wasn't letting him wake up alone. Marcellus came into the casino after the shooting stopped.

He walked through the broken glass slowly. His shoes were old and polished. He looked at the walls, the marble, the great staircase Red had built with other people's lives. Then he looked at me. I put the ring in his hand. Blue Mercy flashed under the work lights. For a long time he didn't speak. His thumb moved across the setting.

Red had put her stone in his own ring. Marcellus turned it in his hand, held it beneath the light, and closed his eyes. Then he took Celeste's letter out of his coat. He laid the ring against the paper and bent his head over both of them. I let him take all the time he needed. "What are you going to do?" I asked.

He closed his hand around the ring. "Take my wife home." I nodded. Forty million dollars. I put forty million dollars in an old man's hand and watched him walk away because the woman I loved had just killed a man while saying another man's name, and for the first time that night I understood why some things couldn't be bought back.

Everything else in that casino was mine. Knox came through the entrance with Bell after the last guns were down. She looked at Shawna in my arms, the gold badge under the blood, and the men who had changed sides because a living captain had offered them more future than Red. "You brought her home," she said. "Told you my nigga doesn't forget." She saluted.

Bell followed. Then every officer in the doorway stood straight while the men inside the casino began saying my name again. Captain. King. Aiden. Shawna heard all three and held me tighter. I made sure every nigga understood that too.
