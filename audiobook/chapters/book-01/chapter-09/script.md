# 9. Our Valedictorian Has a Gun

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

Nigga, I found out I was graduating from college while I was deciding which gun went with breakfast. Tank brought a cream envelope into the kitchen. Gold crest. My name in beautiful handwriting. A nigga had spent more time on the A than I'd spent on some relationships. "School," Tank said. "Whose?" "Yours." "Nigga, I got a school?" Shawna stopped pouring coffee. Rosa turned around.

Mercedes, who'd stayed because my guest room understood her better than her apartment, lowered her toast. "Las Vegas College," Tank said. "Oh. Those niggas." "You went to college?" Shawna asked. "I went to the building." "When, nigga?" "Twenty-two. Free lunch. Excellent chicken." Tank opened the second page. "Nigga, it says valedictorian." I nodded. "They paying attention." "Give me that," Rosa said.

"Nigga, don't wrinkle my achievement." "I'm looking for the payment demand. Mercedes, check if his picture's on a milk carton too. This nigga been somewhere for two years and forgot." Mercedes leaned across the table. "Highest marks in the college's history." Tank looked up. "Marks in what?" "History, nigga. She just said." Shawna turned my face toward hers. "Baby.

What did you do to these people?" I thought about the chicken. Two years earlier, a friend had told me the college's adult scholarship day had food. Everybody in the program was at least twenty-two. I was exactly twenty-two and hungry enough to have a future if it came with a plate. The woman at registration said the lunch was for applicants.

I became one before she finished saying applicants. She gave me a booklet. Math, reading, some music that looked wrong on the page, a question about two promises that couldn't both be kept. I ate and wrote. Ate and wrote. Nigga, by the time the chicken was gone, I'd given them answers, corrected the music, and stolen two rolls.

Apparently those niggas had been discussing the lunch for two years. I explained that. Nobody spoke. Then Tank said, "You ate a degree." "Nigga, I thought while I chewed. You act like this forbidden technology." Mercedes turned the letter over. "They want him there at eleven." Shawna checked the clock. Nine twenty.

"We need clothes." "Nigga, I got plans." Porsche came through the doorway with sunglasses on and a garment bag over her arm. "Your plans just got a hat. You're going." "Red's gala—" "Is in three nights. This is in a hundred minutes. Even your nigga can count that far." Tank lifted a finger. "Actually he has the highest—" "Don't help him," all three women said.

Shawna kissed me. "I want to see my man graduate." Nigga, she could've asked me to graduate from the moon right there. I stood. "Then get dressed. My niggas going to college." Tank looked at his breakfast. "Bring it," I told him. "Apparently that's the entrance exam." We arrived in a convoy because education deserved respect. Tank drove the black Mercedes.

Porsche drove Leon's newest Bentley, which was dark green because he said white brought back too many memories. Mercedes sat beside her with a camera. Rosa wore a yellow suit bright enough to make the campus flowers look underfunded. Marcellus had his clean shirt on and a tie Rosa had chosen for him. He looked like somebody had remembered he was worth inviting places.

Shawna wore white again. This time nobody had to die for the outfit. I wore black trousers, an open-necked shirt, my gold cross, and a jacket cut to hide the bandages. My gun sat where a man could keep it without making his diploma nervous. The campus gates were crowded when we turned in.

Graduates in blue gowns stood everywhere, grown men and women carrying flowers, checking their phones, trying to get through one morning without their families embarrassing them. Then they saw me. A man near the fountain pointed. "Nigga. That's him." His friend looked up from a program. "Who?" "The Mercedes nigga." "This a college, Marcus.

There's seventeen Mercedes in the parking lot." "Nigga, the flying Mercedes." The second man looked properly then. His mouth opened. He looked at the program again and started hitting his friend in the shoulder. "Our valedictorian?" "That's what I'm saying!" "That nigga?" "That nigga!" By the time I got out of the car, half the fountain had turned toward us.

A woman came running down the steps holding a blue robe over both arms. She was about forty-six, dark skin, silver glasses, hair pinned so precisely I could tell she'd spent her life correcting men who thought imprecision was a personality. "Aiden," she said, almost breathless. "Professor Yvonne Halcyon.

We met at your admission." "You had the chicken." She smiled like I'd remembered a wedding vow. "You remember." "Nigga doesn't forget a good plate." She put the robe into my hands. "We have been looking for you." "I been around." "We've seen." Her eyes went to the Mercedes, and I realized she'd seen a lot.

Principal Percival Mercer came out behind her in a blue academic robe with so much gold on it that I wanted to ask whether he was graduating or getting crowned. Sixty years old, deep voice, handsome gray beard, a man accustomed to being the most impressive thing approaching a microphone. He saw me and began crying before we shook hands. "At last," he said.

Tank leaned toward me. "Nigga, what the fuck was in that booklet?" "Apparently me." Mercer took my hand in both of his. "Your work changed this college." "Which part?" "The final page." I tried to remember it. "The blank one?" "You used it to correct the examination." "There was a mistake." "There were six. We had found two in twenty years." I nodded.

That sounded like something I'd do while waiting for dessert. He held up a folder. Inside was my handwriting, impatient and slanted, beside a little grease mark from the lunch. They had framed the original. This was a copy. "The scholarship board accepted your independent work in place of the standard course sequence," Halcyon said. "Your essays circulated through four departments.

Your music analysis repaired the conservatory's examination. Professor Lorne has been teaching your final solution since last spring." "Nigga, I hope Lorne got a nice voice." A man behind her lifted one hand. "I do my best." He looked genuinely nervous about disappointing me. Shawna turned toward me, eyes bright.

"You didn't tell me any of this." "Baby, I didn't know the niggas kept it." The graduates nearby laughed. One of them took off his cap and shook his head, smiling helplessly. "I came every night for three years." I looked at him. "And you here, ain't you?" He straightened. "Yeah." "Then come on.

Niggas graduating." He put his cap back on and fell in beside us like I'd just personally admitted him into the morning. They took me through the science hall on the way to the auditorium because Principal Mercer wanted me to see what my name had done to a wall. There it was. AIDEN HALL. Gold letters. Taller than my head.

I stood under them while Tank took a picture and Shawna laughed into her hand. "Nigga, I got a hall." "You got several," Mercer said. "Don't tell him that," Rosa said. "He'll start naming the doors." Inside, three professors stood around a long table covered in papers. They had been arguing when we came in.

I could see it in the way they all stopped speaking at slightly different times. Professor Lorne looked down at a page and then up at me. "Perhaps you could settle something." Halcyon made a little warning noise. "He has a ceremony." "One minute." "Nigga, everybody thinks they need a minute until I get here," I said. "Show me." He turned the paper around.

A drawing of the new auditorium roof. Lines crossing. Numbers beside them. Somebody had used a red pen to circle the same span three times. "The model gives us two possible answers," he said. I looked at the drawing. Then through the window at the actual auditorium. "You got the old roof in here." He blinked. "The dimensions—" "Changed when they moved the stage.

Look at that window. It's eight panels across. Your drawing got seven." All three professors turned toward the window. I took the pencil and moved the line. "Now your load goes there. Those two numbers stop fighting." Lorne looked at what I'd written. His mouth moved without sound.

The professor beside him pulled another sheet from the pile, compared them, and sat down so suddenly his chair rolled backward. "He's right." "Nigga, say it like you enjoyed finding out." "He's right!" The room laughed. Mercer started crying again. I looked at Shawna. "Your man got a roof." She hooked her fingers through my robe.

"My man better get under it before they name another building." As we left, Lorne caught my arm gently. "What do you do for a living?" Tank answered before I could. "Nigga improves transportation." The auditorium was packed. A choir stood above the stage. Blue banners hung from the balcony.

The youngest graduate was twenty-two and the oldest was sixty-eight, and every one of those grown people had brought somebody who wanted to see their name become something they could hold. Our people took the front row. Shawna sat in the middle. Tank sat at the aisle because every chair in that room looked like it had been built to underestimate him.

Chief Gloria Knox stood beside the stage. I stopped when I saw her. "Nigga, you following me to school now?" "I was invited." "You graduating?" "I was hoping to recruit the valedictorian." Behind her, under a dark blue cloth, stood something low and wide enough to make my pulse change. A car. I could see the nose through the fabric.

I knew what kind of nose that was. Nigga, a Lamborghini could wear a bedsheet and I'd still recognize its ass in a church parking lot. Knox watched me looking. "Finish your speech first." "Chief, that's cruel." "Motivation." She smiled and took her place beside Mercer. The ceremony began. Names. Applause. People walking across the stage trying not to cry before their family did.

I stood behind the curtain listening, the robe hanging open enough to let my cross shine. Shawna kept looking back at me. Every time she did, my chest got bigger.

I had a bullet in my body, three nights until a man tried to take her from me, and a whole college waiting to find out how I sounded when somebody gave me a microphone on purpose. Then the lights flickered. I looked up. A man stood in the upper aisle wearing a gray suit. He wasn't watching the stage. He was watching the doors.

Another man stood beside the exit under the balcony. Their jackets hung wrong. Nigga, I knew that shape better than I knew my own fucking curriculum. I caught Knox's eye. Looked toward the balcony. She saw him. The man under the balcony pulled his gun as the first flame ran up the curtain behind the choir. The fire moved fast. Blue fabric turned orange.

Somebody screamed from the choir. The gunman by the exit fired into the ceiling, and the whole auditorium tried to stand up at once. I grabbed the microphone. "Stay off the stairs! Niggas on the floor, heads down!" My voice filled the room louder than his gun. Knox fired from beside the stage. The man at the exit fell back behind a row of seats.

Her officers moved toward him while Tank got the people in our row down and Shawna pulled Rosa away from the aisle. Above us, the balcony gunman raised his weapon. I fired. He fell against the rail and dropped behind it. His gun hit the empty aisle below.

The choir was coming down from its platform, adults in white robes stumbling through smoke, and I ran up to meet them before the first person fell. "This way!" A woman with a cane had stopped halfway down. I caught her under the arm. A man behind her took her other side. We carried her to the stage while the curtains burned overhead.

Mercer stood by the lectern holding my diploma against his chest. "Principal! Move!" He looked at me through the smoke. "Your degree—" "Nigga, I need somebody alive to sign it!" He moved. A beam fell across the side exit. Sparks burst upward. The doorway vanished behind fire and broken wood. The main aisle was jammed with seats somebody had overturned while trying to run.

People at the back were getting out, but those closest to the stage couldn't reach them. The fire was between us and the loading doors behind the choir. I looked at the covered car. Then at Knox. She was already pulling the cloth away. Dark blue. Gold shield on the doors. Low black wheels. A light bar shining across the windshield.

The most beautiful fucking police Lamborghini a criminal could ever deserve. She threw me the keys. "Don't scratch it." "Nigga, don't limit me." Tank reached me as I opened the door. "You driving in the school?" "I'm improving the exit." "Nigga, I went to the wrong fucking college." He pulled the overturned seats clear of my bumper. I got behind the wheel.

The engine woke up with a roar that cut through the fire and the screaming, and every head in front of me turned. Shawna stood at the edge of the stage holding Mercer's arm. She looked at the car. Looked at the fire. Looked at me. Then she smiled. Not because she wasn't scared. Baby was scared enough to shake.

But she knew which nigga she'd come to watch that morning, and she wasn't looking away while I became him. I drove. The Lamborghini crossed the stage, took the shallow steps in one hard drop, and hit the blocked aisle with the whole weight of my morning behind it. Seats flew aside. Wood snapped. Smoke closed over the windshield. I kept going.

Flame washed across the glass as a curtain fell over the hood. For a second I couldn't see anything but orange. Then it slid away, and the far doors appeared directly in front of me. I put my foot down. We broke through into daylight. Both doors came off their hinges.

The Lamborghini shot down the broad front steps and landed in the college courtyard with its siren screaming. Behind me, fresh air rushed through the opening I'd made. People came out after it. The choir first. Then graduates helping graduates. Then Tank carrying the woman with the cane while Rosa and Mercedes pushed two overturned benches out of the way.

Porsche had a fire extinguisher in each hand and was shouting at niggas like she personally owned oxygen. I got out and ran back up the steps. Knox met me at the opening with Mercer beside her. Shawna came behind them, one hand over her mouth, the other holding my graduation cap. "Baby!" I caught her.

She hit my shoulder with the cap, then put it on my head herself. "You almost forgot something." "Nigga, I was making an entrance." "You made an exit." "A great man does both." She kissed me while the whole courtyard watched. Behind us, the fire crews arrived. Knox's people brought the surviving attackers out in handcuffs.

One of them looked at the Lamborghini and shook his head. "We burned the building." "And the nigga still came through it," his partner said. "With a police car." "Nigga, I saw." I heard them. Of course I heard them. They'd come to ruin my graduation and now they were reviewing the best part of it together. I straightened my cap. "Principal.

You got a microphone out here?" They brought the lectern into the courtyard. Mercer stood beside it still crying. Halcyon put a hand on his back. Professor Lorne looked at the smoke coming out of the auditorium and then at me with the expression of a man whose roof calculation had just acquired a personal reason to live. The graduates gathered in front of us.

Some had soot on their gowns. Some were holding shoes. Everybody had somebody by the hand. I stepped up to the microphone. "Aight. Where we at?" The laughter rolled across the courtyard, wild and relieved. Tank stood behind Shawna with his arms folded and his face shining. Marcellus had one hand on Rosa's shoulder. Mercedes lifted her camera. "Niggas, I ain't gonna lie.

I wasn't here much." The laughter got louder. "You were not here at all!" somebody shouted. "And look what happened the morning I came. You welcome." They cheered. A man in the third row took off his cap and waved it over his head. I looked out at them. All those people.

All those grown men and women who'd come back to a classroom after life had given them plenty of reasons to leave it alone. They were looking at me like I could tell them something worth taking into tomorrow. Nigga, I could do that too. "You came for your name," I said. "Some of you worked all night and came anyway.

Some of you had people telling you this wasn't your kind of room. Some of you got somebody missing today who should've been here making too much fucking noise." Shawna looked down at her watch. Then back at me. "But you here. And when the doors caught fire, niggas started carrying each other. I saw that. That part belongs to you. Keep it.

Don't let anybody make you little enough to forget it." I put my hand on the cross. "Three letters on this. D. B. M." Tank's eyebrows lifted. Shawna began smiling before I said the words. "Damn, boy. Magnificent." The courtyard erupted. "That's you too," I told them. "Men, women, every grown motherfucker standing here with a cap and a dream.

You look in the mirror tomorrow, say it properly. Don't mumble greatness in your own house." Mercedes shouted, "Say it again!" "Damn!" The whole courtyard answered, "Boy!" I spread my arms. "Magnificent!" They shouted it back so loudly the pigeons left the roof of Aiden Hall together. Mercer put the diploma in my hand while he was still crying.

I had driven through a burning auditorium and finished my valedictorian speech before the principal finished his first fucking breakdown. Nigga, education was going beautifully. Knox stepped up beside me before anybody could start the next hymn. Two officers brought a blue velvet box. Another brought a dark uniform jacket with gold braid on its shoulders.

The whole courtyard went quiet in stages as people realized the police had come to offer me more than a ride.

"Aiden," Knox said, "you've been making this offer difficult to postpone." "Chief, I been busy graduating." "You've also saved my officers' lives, protected this city's people, and demonstrated a relationship with automobiles I have never seen in any man who still has all his limbs." Porsche applauded first. Knox opened the box. A gold captain's badge lay inside. Not honorary. Not a souvenir.

The chief told the entire courtyard that before any nigga could get confused and make me explain it with a siren later. "Captain Aiden," she said. "Las Vegas Police Department. Actual command. Actual badge. Actual trouble for anyone who thinks otherwise." Tank bent forward with his hands on his knees. "Nigga." Rosa caught his arm.

"You all right?" "This nigga's a cop." "I heard." "My nigga's the police." "Stand up before he arrests your posture." Knox held out the badge. "Raise your right hand." I did. The oath she gave me was short. Keep faith with my people. Stand when somebody needed standing for. Use the authority of the badge with my whole name behind it.

I spoke every word in front of the chief, the officers, the graduates, and the woman I wanted beside me for the rest of my life. Then Knox pinned the badge inside my jacket. "Captain." I looked down at the gold. Nigga, my mother would've had to sit down twice. Shawna came up the steps. Took my face in both hands.

Kissed me slowly enough that the police officers started applauding before the students did. "My man," she whispered. "Your captain." "Still my man." I could've stood there forever letting her settle the rank. Knox gave me the Lamborghini's keys again. One door had a scratch from the auditorium. The hood smelled like smoke.

The siren still worked, because God wasn't going to give a nigga that much greatness and then get petty over wiring. "It's yours," she said. "I noticed." She leaned close enough that only I could hear. "Red's people used to believe they owned every uniform that mattered in this city. Let him see yours." I looked at her. She smiled.

"I know exactly who I recruited, Captain." We left the college with my diploma on Shawna's lap and the badge inside my jacket. Tank followed in the original Mercedes. The rest of the convoy came behind him, horns sounding all the way to the gates. Every graduate turned to watch the Lamborghini pass.

At the fountain, Marcus raised his cap and shouted, "That's our nigga!" His friend shouted, "That's our captain!" I hit the siren once for both of them. Shawna put her hand on my leg as the campus disappeared behind us. "What you doing tomorrow?" I looked at her. Looked at the road. Let the car stretch its legs beneath us.

"Baby, niggas better not schedule anything they can't afford."
