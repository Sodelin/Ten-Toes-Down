By the eleventh morning, three cities had offered me keys, two had offered me houses, and one had asked whether I would consider becoming its most famous resident without technically having to live there.

"Nigga," Tank said, looking at the envelopes, "they recruiting you like weather."

We sat in the palace breakfast room with the sea beyond the windows and eight white ships still trying to make a horizon threatening. My people had spread across every comfortable chair. Mercedes read the Velvet Mile paperwork with Porsche. Rosa wrote wedding ideas on the back of a menu and crossed out every place that wasn't Vegas because Tank said if he was going to cry in front of a whole city, he wanted a city that already knew him.

Shawna sat beside me with her feet in my lap.

I opened the first envelope.

Port Carmine wanted to name its waterfront after me.

Gilded Bay wanted a photograph of me holding its rescue-service flag.

San Paloma wanted to know whether I would attend the opening of a sports arena built by a man whose nephew had been on the ferry.

"Tell them yes," I said.

"To all?" Shawna asked.

"Nigga, I got a calendar."

"You have me reminding you what day it is."

"Premium calendar."

She pinched my knee.

Then Vegas called.

One of my westside managers, a man named Boone who had never met a dollar he couldn't persuade into staying overnight. He told me the clubs were full, the new cars had arrived, and two niggas had tried telling him my absence meant their debts had become historical.

"History got interest," I said.

"That's what I told them."

"No trouble at Shawna's property. No touching the staff. And if a nigga says my badge made me forget my money, show him the part where I'm still asking about it."

Knox looked up from her coffee.

I looked back.

She took another sip.

Nigga, we understood each other. I wasn't going home to become a decorative good man. I had built an empire full of cash, cars, vice, and people who knew which door to knock on when their own night got complicated. The badge had given my jacket another beautiful place to shine. It had not turned the rest of me into a church bulletin.

By noon the visiting dignitaries arrived carrying velvet boxes.

No speeches about policy. Thank God. These people had seen enough of my week to know a nigga should not be trapped behind a podium if anything with an engine remained nearby.

The woman from Port Carmine handed me a gold key shaped like a fish.

"Our harbor," she said.

The man from Gilded Bay gave me a silver key shaped like a wing.

"Our sky."

San Paloma gave me a key that looked almost normal until I realized it had my face engraved on the teeth.

Tank leaned close.

"Nigga, you can unlock your own mouth."

I nearly ruined the photograph laughing.

Then Shawna received a key too.

Blue enamel. A little treble clef worked into the handle. The woman from Port Carmine presented it with both hands.

"For the hall where we want you to sing," she said.

My wife stared at it.

I watched her thumb move over the blue enamel. The crowd waited, cameras raised, but she took one small second for herself before smiling.

"I'd like that," she said.

Nigga, I would have bought that whole harbor for the way she looked at the key. But somebody else had heard her and wanted her there. I couldn't improve that by reaching for money. So I stood beside her, proud enough to hurt, and let the room give my wife something she had made happen herself.

The parade afterward was less restrained.

Cassius and Inez brought the Monaco prize cars out in a line of color so bright the street looked wet. Red, white, silver, gold, my dark-blue Lamborghini leading them with its repaired side reflecting the crowd. Mechanics had fixed the scrape while I slept. The chief's gold shield was clean again. Nigga looked rested even when I wasn't.

Mercedes drove a green convertible with Porsche beside her, both wearing scarves like the wind had been hired specifically to flatter them. Darius and Kong rode in a car too small for either man's pride, their knees practically in the windshield. Tank drove Rosa in the old black Mercedes because he said some cars earned the right to carry the good part too.

I rode with Shawna.

We passed the market. Passed Alma's kitchens. Passed the repaired ferry dock where the man I'd pulled from the water stood beside his husband holding a sign that said THE CAPTAIN CAME BACK.

Shawna touched my arm when she saw it.

I lifted my hand to them.

The whole street answered.

At the top of the hill, an armored car burst out of a side road and struck the lead escort.

The celebration broke apart.

Another car came behind it. Then a third. Black paint. Covered plates. Saint's men had stopped wearing visible crests after Porsche made the joke, but a nigga doesn't become mysterious by changing his laundry after everybody has seen his face.

Shots cracked across the road.

I pulled Shawna low and turned the Lamborghini between the attackers and the crowd. Cassius swung his red car across the opposite lane. Inez came up behind him, trapping the first armored car between two machines whose owners had already beaten an entire continent at driving.

The attacker's driver tried to force his way through.

Cassius smiled at him through the windshield.

Nigga, that smile meant a repair bill was about to become a biography.

I took the second car up the hill away from the spectators. It followed, gunfire chipping the wall beside us. Shawna held the radio in one hand and my spare magazine in the other, her face calm, her voice telling the captains exactly where the people needed space.

"Left at the fountain," she said.

"I see it."

"The lower road's clear."

"I see that too."

"Aiden."

I glanced at her.

"I am enjoying this less than you."

"Baby, I can enjoy enough for both of us."

She laughed despite the gunfire, and I took the turn.

The armored car came after us too fast. Its weight dragged it wide. I went around the fountain, crossed the little stone square, and came back against its flank while the driver still tried to persuade physics to consider his rank.

We struck hard enough to fold its front wheel under the body.

The car stopped in the fountain.

Water climbed over the hood.

For one beautiful second every statue in the square appeared to be spitting on the nigga simultaneously.

I stepped out with my gun up.

"Everybody out."

The driver opened his door into waist-deep water.

"Nigga," I said, "you picked a dramatic place to park."

Behind us, Tank had stopped the third car by putting the old Mercedes across its path and letting Darius and Kong emerge from the tiny convertible together.

The gunmen looked at the two enormous men.

One put his weapon on the dashboard before either fighter spoke.

"Intelligent nigga," Tank said.

The first armored car belonged to Cassius and Inez now, in the sense that neither of them intended to permit it another decision. Its driver came out holding both hands over his head and asking which champion was arresting him.

"All of them," Knox said, arriving with the island police.

No spectator had been hit.

One escort officer had a broken wrist. Two attackers were wounded. The fountain needed a new statue hand. My Lamborghini's new paint had lasted approximately forty-seven minutes.

Shawna looked at the fresh scrape.

"You couldn't keep one side pretty?"

"Baby, I'm keeping both our sides alive. The car understands the priorities."

From the stalled armored car, a phone began ringing.

I answered on video.

Saint's face filled the screen.

He saw the fountain behind me.

His eyes closed briefly.

"Nigga," I said, "you should stop buying these cars if you're going to keep giving them to my city."

He opened his eyes.

"Tomorrow. The final challenge. No more proxies. You and me."

"I been here."

"At dawn, the twenty champions ride. By noon, one reaches the Crown Gate. I will be waiting."

"Wear the necklace."

His hand moved to the Lion's Heart beneath his collar.

"Earn it."

I ended the call before he could enjoy saying anything else.

The visiting officials stood at the edge of the square, velvet key boxes still under their arms. They had watched the whole thing. Their escorts had watched it. Their photographers had photographed every expensive second.

The woman from Port Carmine looked at the car in the fountain.

Then at my wife.

Then at me.

"We should give you a bigger key," she said.

Tank bent over laughing.

Nigga, every city really did want me.
