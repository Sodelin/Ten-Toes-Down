Saint tried to leave himself an escape route by burning everybody else's.

At nine that night, the guard ship Apostle turned its guns toward the outer docks.

Keel saw it from the Aurelia and called me before the first shot.

I stood on the palace balcony with Shawna beside me, still holding the tie she'd just taken off my neck. Below us the festival lights shone along the harbor. People were eating, drinking, making one more bad decision before the final championship gave them something else to discuss.

"He's aiming at the boats," Keel said. "Passenger launches. Anything that could carry spectators away."

I looked toward the white ship turning in the bay.

"Then that nigga just volunteered to become ours."

Shawna put the tie down.

No complaint. No pretending she'd rather have married a man who would watch the docks burn from a comfortable room. She went inside, brought my jacket, and helped me settle it over the shoulder that still reminded me I was made of meat under all the beautiful ideas.

"Come back," she said.

"You know where I live."

"Aiden."

I stopped smiling for one second and looked straight at her.

"I am coming back."

She nodded.

Then she picked up her phone and called Rosa to get the harbor cleared.

I took the speedboat with Tank and Graves. Knox stayed ashore directing the island officers. Keel came out from the other side in the Aurelia's launch. Four blue-flagged ships moved behind us, their captains finally given a reason to stop waiting politely for Saint to finish being wrong.

The Apostle fired.

Water erupted beside the outer pier. People ran beneath the lights. The second shot struck an empty warehouse and punched a bright hole through its roof.

I heard Rosa over the radio, steady and furious.

"Keep moving toward the square. Nobody back for a bag. Your bag can meet Jesus by itself."

Tank laughed once, hard and proud.

"That's my fiancée."

"Nigga, I recognize the management."

We reached the Apostle as its deck crew argued with the soldiers stationed above them. That mattered. Saint had brought his army aboard ships whose crews had no desire to become the men who burned a harbor full of people they had spent the week watching us save.

A sailor dropped a ladder over the side when he saw our boat.

Another man tried to pull it back up.

The sailor hit him with a bucket. The noise reached us before the bucket came tumbling after it.

Tank caught it against his chest.

"Nigga," I said, "Mercedes started a movement."

"Tell her this one's got a dent."

A burst cracked above us. Graves pushed my head down as a chip flew off the rail. The three of us crouched in the launch while the ladder swung against the hull. This was the part no nigga ever included when he told the story afterward: waiting below the guns, trying to hear one friendly voice among men shouting orders nobody believed anymore.

The sailor leaned out.

"Stairs forward! Two soldiers at the hatch! Crew's below!"

"Can you get them back?" Graves called.

The sailor disappeared. There was shouting overhead, then the hard scrape of something being dragged across the deck.

Tank set the bucket down. His hands were steady. Mine wanted to hurry because my shoulder didn't want to climb.

"I'll go first," he said.

"You're twice my size."

"Then nigga, when they see me, they'll be real disappointed there's more."

He put a boot on the lowest rung and looked back at me.

"Use the good arm. Don't turn climbing a ladder into a fucking audition."

I nodded. Graves checked the space above us again and signaled.

Tank went up. No speech, no pose. Just my brother making a dangerous opening big enough for the rest of us to follow.

I caught the rung he had left warm.

We climbed.

On deck, the world became light, shouting, and the sharp crack of guns over open water. Graves pulled one sailor behind a hatch cover as bullets struck the rail. Tank drove into the soldiers guarding the forward stairs, huge shoulders clearing a path nobody had expected a human body to negotiate.

I went toward the bridge.

The officer in command waited beneath the windows with a pistol in his hand and a white scarf around his throat. He shouted for his crew to hold their positions.

Nobody moved toward him.

He shouted louder.

A woman at the wheel turned and looked at me.

"Captain," she said.

The officer smiled, thinking she meant him.

Then she stepped aside so I could pass.

Nigga's face changed beautifully.

"Put it down," I told him.

He raised the pistol.

I shot him before he finished deciding I was another man he could order to be afraid.

He fell against the wall and did not get up.

I stepped over the gun and looked at the woman at the wheel.

"Turn us away from the harbor."

She did.

The ship's bow began to move. On the deck below, Saint's soldiers saw the guns swing toward open water and understood command had changed without waiting for a speech.

Some dropped their weapons.

Some didn't.

The ones who didn't met Tank, Graves, and an entire crew who had become tired of letting strangers tell them what their own ship was for.

By the time Keel came aboard, the Apostle's white flag lay on the deck.

Five ships with us.

Seven with Saint.

Not for long.

Keel stood beside me on the bridge and opened the fleet channel. He didn't ask me for a speech. He simply said the captain was listening if anybody had something to say.

For a moment there was only static.

Then another voice answered.

"Saint Clement requests passage into the harbor."

Another.

"Saint Mercy joining."

Another.

"This is the Orator. We will not fire on the island."

I watched white flags come down across the bay.

One after another.

Three ships moving out of Saint's line and into ours.

Eight with us.

Four with him.

Tank came up to the bridge wiping blood from his knuckles. He looked at the changing formation and started counting again.

"Nigga, we need a bigger dock."

Graves stood beside the window, breathing hard.

"The men wanted a choice," he said.

"They made one," I answered.

The flagship's lights flashed across the water.

Saint called me.

I put him on the bridge screen so every sailor could see the face of the man who had told them to burn their own welcome.

"You have stolen my fleet."

"Nigga, your fleet drove over here."

"You bought weak men with cheap kindness."

Tank looked at the deck outside, where two of Saint's soldiers lay dead and several more sat wounded beneath a guard.

"Cheap?" he said. "Nigga, we worked."

Saint ignored him.

"The final challenge stands. Tomorrow. You will not win by collecting cowards."

I leaned close to the screen.

"Then come ashore alone and show us what courage looks like without twelve ships standing around it."

His eyes narrowed.

"I will be there."

"Wear something pretty. Whole world's watching."

I ended the call.

Keel laughed quietly beside me.

"You know exactly where to press him."

"Nigga built a whole personality out of one sore place. It's hard to miss."

We came back to the harbor before midnight.

The warehouse still smoked. Nobody had been inside. Two dockworkers had cuts from flying glass. One island officer had been shot through the leg and was already under Vega's care. Three enemy soldiers had died aboard the Apostle. The crew laid them beneath clean covers before we left. I had no soft story to tell about it. They had aimed at people who couldn't shoot back. I had brought my own answer aboard.

Shawna waited at the dock.

She counted me, Tank, Graves, Keel.

Then she came straight to me and put her arms around my neck.

I held her with both arms despite the shoulder.

Vega could bill me in the morning.

"Back," I said against her hair.

"I see you."

Behind her, Mercedes and Porsche handed blankets to the sailors coming ashore. Rosa stood beside the band, telling Silk which song the harbor needed next. Isolde and Marcellus had brought lanterns from the ballroom, and the little flames lined the repaired pier in a row of gold.

Eight blue flags moved against the dark sea.

The island began to sing.

Not my name this time. One of Shawna's songs, the harbor song, the one about getting through a night together without letting the water keep anybody it had taken a liking to.

I stood there with my wife in my arms while my ships answered with their horns.

Nigga, a man can be loud by himself.

This was what happened when the whole ocean decided to speak beside him.
