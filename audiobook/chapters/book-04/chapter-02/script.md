"Tell him it was so beautiful you died afterward." I answered. Saint appeared on the screen sitting beneath a chandelier made of swords.

White hair, black beard, white military jacket, medals down one side like somebody had spilled a jewelry box on him. Fifty-something and still trying to make a face last a whole conversation. "Captain Aiden." "General, your limousine's fucked up." "It served its purpose." "Nigga, its purpose is hanging over a hotel." He looked at me floating. He looked at my badge.

"Every king should defend his crown. I propose twelve days of competition. Racing. Combat. Music. Strategy. Athletic trials. A final contest worthy of the victor. The World Championship of Everything. On your island." "Get your own island." "I have six." "Then why you calling about mine, nigga?" For the first time, he didn't answer immediately. There it was.

Some little door behind his eyes he didn't want opened. I had met men who wanted money, men who wanted women, men who wanted other men to stop laughing whenever they stood up. Saint wanted something under the words he was saying. "If you win," he said, "my gold fleet becomes yours. Twelve ships. My championship treasury.

My concessions throughout the southern sea." "And if I lose?" "Isla Gloria." Shawna sat beside me. He could see her now. Water ran from her shoulders, and the watch on her wrist caught the sun. "The people on that island aren't a prize," she said. Saint bowed his head a fraction. "Then win for them." I felt her hand close around mine underwater.

"You sound brave on a telephone," I told him. "Will you come?" "Nigga, it's my house. Will you?" We ended the call with his smile still trying to recover. Mercedes took the phone. "That man gets dressed in front of a painting of himself." "I got a billboard," I said. "Your billboard doesn't tell you good morning." Porsche sat up.

"You notice what he didn't put on the list?" "What?" "Swimming. Nigga picked an island championship and forgot water. He wants the land." I looked at her. Porsche shrugged. "Men tell me things when they think I've fallen asleep. It's half my education." Across the pool, Marcellus had stopped turning the pages of his book. "Bring me the medal," he said.

I put the little gold crown in his palm. His thumb went straight to the seven points. For a moment he wasn't beside my pool. His face had gone somewhere older and darker, somewhere with music he had been trying not to hear. "Tomorrow," he said. "I need to make a call." He walked into the house carrying the medal.

"Everybody got a mysterious tomorrow," Tank said from behind us. "Mine involves a jewelry store." Rosa turned her head. Tank stopped with a plate of chicken balanced against his chest. He had said the thought outside his body, and now the thought was standing there with witnesses. "For a watch," he added. "Baby," Rosa said, "you already wear two." "Different time." Porsche lowered her newspaper.

"Two o'clock and married?" "Nigga, help," Tank said to me. I reached for a cherry. "That's my mysterious tomorrow. Helping. Tomorrow." He gave me a betrayed look, then tried to take a bite big enough to close the subject. Rosa caught the plate before the chicken slid off it. "What time's the appointment?" she asked.

"Didn't say appointment." "You put on cologne to eat by a pool. There's an appointment." Mercedes turned to Shawna. "He's going to propose to the jeweler for practice." "I'm researching," Tank said. "A nigga can research." Rosa's smile softened enough that he almost survived.

Then she said, "Don't let anybody talk you into something because it's the biggest thing in the window." Tank glanced at me again. This time I nodded. "Get the one you look at and think of her, nigga." He looked at Rosa. For once there wasn't a joke on his face, and she stopped teasing him because she saw it too.

"That's useful," he said quietly. Porsche raised one finger. "Also ask how it catches on clothing." Mercedes raised another. "And whether you can afford it without suddenly announcing we all owe you gas money." "Niggas, I can buy a ring." Silence. The chicken finally fell. Rosa caught that too. "A watch," Tank said to the chicken.

She kissed his cheek, handed him back the plate, and let him have that last terrible little lie. That evening the heavyweight champion came to my house to ask if I would please hit him for charity. Darius Diamond was thirty-four, six feet four, and built like a man who had swallowed another champion whole.

He had gold teeth only in the back because he said the front teeth were for business. He brought his trainer, his publicist, and a blue velvet robe with my name sewn across the shoulders. I looked at the robe. "Nigga, you prepared for my consent." "The workers' hospital needs a new wing," he said. "We do three exhibition rounds tomorrow. People pay to watch.

Nobody gets hurt." Tank coughed. "Nobody gets hurt bad," Darius corrected. "Which nigga gets hurt a little?" Tank asked. Darius looked at me. I looked at Darius. Tank looked between us. "Niggas, I'm asking a medical question." "The losing nigga," I said. "Both niggas think the other nigga losing." "Good promotion," Darius said. Tank shook his head.

"Hospital about to make its money twice." Shawna took the robe from him and held it against me. She stood back, considering. "You can't say no now," she said. "Why?" "Because I need to see this with the gloves." Darius grinned. "Your wife has excellent taste." "Nigga, look at her husband." We agreed on three rounds and no Blue Thunder.

I didn't want anybody saying electricity had done something my hands had already been capable of doing when I was broke. Darius shook on it and nearly crushed the hand I needed to prove the point. When he left, Tank followed me into the kitchen. "I need help," he said. "With a different-time watch?" "Don't start." He took a box out of his pocket.

Inside was a ring with a stone shaped like a drop of sunlight. Marcellus's work. The band had tiny roses running around it, each petal cut so fine it looked soft. I stopped joking. Tank stared at the box instead of me. "You think she wants it?" "Nigga, she wants you.

The ring just gives her something to throw at you when you piss her off." "I'm serious." "So am I. That's an expensive argument." He laughed once, then got quiet again. "I know how to stand in front of a gun," he said.

"I don't know how to stand there waiting on a woman to tell me if she wants the rest of my life." I leaned against the counter. "You remember that night I couldn't breathe and you carried me anyway?" He looked up. "You didn't ask if you were carrying me right. You just wouldn't let me go. That's you, Tank.

She already knows the answer to the hard part." He closed the box with his thumb. "On the island," he said. "When she sings." "Then win her a good night." The next afternoon fifteen thousand people paid to watch Darius Diamond teach me what a heavyweight championship felt like. It felt like getting punched by an expensive refrigerator.

First round, thirty seconds in, he touched my guard and drove me backward across the ring. The crowd made one enormous surprised noise. Darius smiled around his mouthpiece. His eyes said he liked me and was absolutely going to beat my ass for a hospital. "There you are," he said. "Nigga, I'm right here." He hit me again to confirm it.

I could hear Shawna at ringside. Not screaming. Worse. Giving instructions. "Aiden, stop admiring him and move." My wife knew me. I had been admiring the man. He was clean. Every step meant something. Every time I thought I saw an opening, the opening had a fist waiting in it. I let him push me another half step. Watched his shoulder. Watched his feet.

The bell rang before I answered. Tank climbed through the ropes with a towel. "You all right?" "Nigga got hands." "He the champion." "I noticed when he introduced my teeth to each other." Across the ring, Darius's trainer was laughing. Darius wasn't. He was watching me watch him. Second round, I stopped trying to be faster than everything at once.

I gave him the look he had been waiting for, let him believe I was going where he wanted, and stepped somewhere else. His glove brushed my cheek. Mine found the part of his ribs that hadn't expected company. He grunted. I heard Rosa say, "Oh, shit." Darius came forward harder. I met him. Three exchanges, all heat and breath and ropes under our backs.

No lightning. No car. Just another man's will trying to move mine. I loved the motherfucker for making me work. This nigga wasn't giving me a famous-man round. Nigga wasn't letting the cameras carry my hands. He wanted to know what kind of nigga stood underneath the jacket, and I wanted to show him. Two niggas asking the same question with different fists.

Some niggas call that hostility. Nigga, I call it an introduction worth remembering. At the end of the round he tapped my glove. "You learn fast." "You teach violent." Third round, we forgot the word exhibition together. He caught me once so clean the arena lights took a short vacation.

I stayed up because Shawna was wearing the robe now, and if I fell over in front of my wife wearing my name I would have to move to another planet. He came to finish the thought. I turned with him, found my feet, and sent the answer up between his gloves. Darius Diamond sat down. For a moment nobody moved.

Then the arena went fucking insane. He blinked at me from the canvas, touched his chin, and laughed with his whole chest. "Nigga." "Yeah," I said, breathing like I'd stolen every breath in the room.
