Cassius Chrome arrived on a red speedboat and parked so close to my yacht that Tank asked if the nigga intended to come upstairs through the kitchen.

Inez Inferno stood in the passenger seat holding two bottles of champagne and looking at the eleven white ships across the bay. Her engagement ring caught the sun. Cassius watched her notice it and smiled the helpless smile of a nigga who had won something he still couldn't believe had said yes.

"You collect enemies in matching sets now?"

"Nigga sent the whole catalog," I said.

Cassius climbed aboard and hugged me carefully around the shoulder. Somebody had told him about the train. Everybody had told everybody about the fucking train. By then a man could order coffee on another continent and have the waiter explain my landing before asking about milk.

"I left you alone for a week," Cassius said.

"Long week."

"You won more trophies?"

Tank pointed toward the cabin. Laurels hung from three hooks, the mirror, and a lamp whose original purpose had lost the argument.

Cassius stared.

"Nigga."

"Don't start," Mercedes said. "We already doing that."

Inez went straight to Shawna's piano and put the champagne beside it.

"I came for the music," she said.

"Everybody says that until he starts driving something," Shawna told her.

"I can appreciate two disasters."

We had a party that night because a nigga couldn't spend his whole island worrying about the man trying to steal it. The Crown of the Sea began the next morning. Saint's last captains were expected to compete in racing launches while their general watched from the flagship. Keel said some of them wanted to talk. I said they could talk with a drink in their hand like civilized criminals.

By ten the yacht and the harbor terrace had become one long expensive mistake everybody intended to remember fondly.

Silk brought the band. Rosa brought a voice that made the men leaning against the rail stand up straighter. Mercedes and Porsche brought friends who arrived beautiful, loud, and entirely uninterested in being described as atmosphere. There were singers, sex workers, drivers, thieves, fighters, a chief of police wearing gold earrings, and one professor who kept telling people he normally went to bed at nine while refusing every offered ride home.

Cocaine appeared in a room farther down the terrace where two merchants had decided they were younger than their knees. I didn't ask for anybody's life story. I had enough crimes of my own to remember without taking attendance at other niggas' parties. One of the merchants emerged convinced he could freestyle against me. Silk handed him the microphone. Nigga rhymed ocean with ocean four times, bowed, and demanded a boat. We gave him water and a chair. Even the criminals have standards when the band is that good.

Tank danced with Rosa beneath the lanterns. He kept showing people her ring as if she hadn't been holding the hand herself all evening. Darius challenged Kong Harrow to arm-wrestle and lost so spectacularly a waiter caught the table before it followed him. Then both men insisted I try. My wife looked at my shoulder. I looked at the men. They looked at her. Two heavyweight champions withdrew the challenge with excellent manners.

"Nigga," Darius whispered, "your wife got reach."

"Across continents," I said.

The first enemy captain came without his white jacket.

Keel introduced him as Alaric Dawn, forty-nine, commander of the Saint Verity. He had a scar through one eyebrow and hands that looked like they'd spent years holding the sea away from other people. He took the drink I offered and watched my party for a long time before speaking.

"Your people are not afraid of you."

"They know when to be."

He glanced at me.

I smiled. "Nigga, I'm not running a monastery. Somebody steals from my wife, we'll have a memorable conversation. But they can dance without asking whether I'm in the mood."

Dawn looked at Rosa singing with Tank's hand resting at her waist. At Mercedes telling a wealthy man exactly where he could put his offer. At Knox laughing beside Graves, the two of them close enough that nobody could pretend the flowers had been an office supply.

"Saint calls that weakness."

"Saint watches it from a boat he won't get off. Nigga can call long distance."

Dawn laughed once.

Then he told me what he wanted.

His crew had families ashore in other ports. He wanted wages paid, promises kept, and the right to stop sailing behind a man who aimed guns at the people fixing his engines. In return he would race me honestly, lose honestly if he lost, and bring his ship into the harbor under whichever flag he chose when the contest ended.

"And if you win?" I asked.

"I keep my ship."

"Nigga, I was going to let you keep steering it anyway."

He looked surprised by that.

"You don't want command?"

"I want an ocean my wife can look at without seeing eleven motherfuckers preparing a tantrum. You want to help, help."

We shook hands.

Two more captains came after him.

By midnight, Saint's fleet had three men at my table discussing what a new morning might look like. Nobody pledged their souls. Nobody made a speech about the future of nations. Niggas asked practical questions about whether a promise would still mean something after the applause, and I answered them with Keel sitting beside me, alive, fed, and still captain of the ship he'd brought home.

Shawna sang one last song before we went upstairs.

I stood behind her chair, my good hand resting lightly at the back of her neck, and let the party move around us. There were beautiful people everywhere. Men stared at her. Women looked at me. Money changed hands over games nobody would admit losing later. Somewhere Darius was teaching the nine-o'clock professor to throw a proper punch without hurting the champagne glass he refused to put down.

My wife looked up at me at the end of the song.

"Ready?"

Nigga, I was ready before she asked.

We left the party to our friends and took the night upstairs. In the morning her lipstick was on my collar, my shoes were under a chair I hadn't meant to touch, and she was asleep across my chest with one hand carefully clear of my injured shoulder. I lay there longer than a champion's schedule recommended because no crown in the world had ever asked me to stay warm like that.

Then Tank knocked.

"Nigga, boats."

I closed my eyes.

"Nigga, marriage."

"Nigga, boats waiting."

Shawna laughed without opening her eyes.

"Go win the water, baby."

The Crown of the Sea began under a clear sky.

Sixteen launches waited in the harbor, bright hulls nosing against the tide. The course circled the eastern headland, crossed the open bay, passed between two old sea towers, and returned beneath the high bridge at the harbor entrance. Cassius rode with me. Inez took another launch with Porsche because she said it would be useful for the world to remember I was not the only person who could make a vehicle ashamed of its previous speed.

Shawna watched from the yacht with Vega beside her. The doctor had examined my shoulder again. Better, she said. Better did not mean indestructible. I told her I respected the distinction. She told me to demonstrate it before she believed in miracles.

I used no Blue Thunder at the start.

The boat had plenty to say without borrowing my bloodstream. We hit the first stretch with the bow rising over the chop, spray breaking white against the windshield. Cassius laughed beside me, one hand gripping the rail, the other pointing toward Dawn's launch as it cut across our wake.

"That nigga means it!"

"I asked for honest!"

"Nigga brought enthusiastic!"

Dawn took the lead around the headland. Inez came up outside him, her red boat flying low over the water. Porsche's purple scarf streamed behind them like the sea had decided to dress for a better woman. The other launches spread across the bay, engines hammering, crews leaning into every turn with their whole bodies.

I let the water teach me its rhythm. Not every wave deserved a fight. Some lifted us where we needed to go. Some needed to pass beneath the boat without finding my temper. I found the clean line through the middle stretch and felt the hull settle into a speed that made Cassius stop laughing long enough to appreciate it properly.

"Nigga," he said, very softly.

That was the compliment I wanted from another driver. The quiet one. The one that came when a nigga had stopped performing admiration and simply had to say what his body knew.

At the old towers, a black launch came out from behind the nearer wall.

Too fast. Wrong direction.

Its driver wore Saint's colors. The man beside him lifted a gun toward Inez's boat.

Dawn saw it first.

He turned across the attacker's path, forcing the black launch wide. A shot struck his windshield. Glass flashed into the sea. His boat lurched, hit the next wave badly, and rolled onto its side.

Two men went into the water.

I turned before Cassius finished shouting.

The race broke apart around us. Inez swung back. Porsche was already standing, pointing to where Dawn's passenger had surfaced. The black launch tried to circle for another attack.

I put our boat between it and the men in the water.

Cassius drew the gun he'd worn under his jacket and fired toward the attacking launch. The gunman ducked. I drove hard across its bow, close enough for our wake to strike broadside. The black boat bucked, lost its turn, and slammed into the stone foot of the sea tower.

It stopped moving.

Its crew crawled onto the rocks with their hands up when the island patrol boat arrived.

I was already beside Dawn.

He gripped our rail with one hand, blood running from a cut above his eye. Cassius hauled him aboard while I held us steady. Inez and Porsche got his passenger out on the other side.

The general's captain lay on my deck coughing seawater into the boat he'd come to beat.

"The race," he said.

"Nigga, you nearly drowned."

"Finish it."

I looked down at him.

He was smiling through the blood.

"I'd hate to think I lost to a man who didn't finish."

Cassius met my eyes.

"We can still catch them."

I looked toward the scattered launches racing for the bridge.

Inez's boat came alongside. She gave me one sharp nod.

We went together.

No lightning. No miracle button. Just two boats cutting through the last stretch with men and women inside them who had come to understand each other at dangerous speed. Inez took the outside line. I took the inside. We passed one launch, then two, then the leader, all three boats so close that a man on shore later swore we had tied them together.

At the finish, the nose of my boat crossed first.

Inez came in beside me, laughing so hard she had to lean against Porsche to stand up.

Dawn raised one shaking hand from the deck.

"Champion," he said.

I gave him my hand and pulled him to his feet.

The harbor erupted.

By sunset three more white ships had lowered Saint's flags.

Dawn's Verity. The Saint Orison. The Saint Lucia.

Four ships with us. Eight still behind him.

Tank stood on the dock counting on his fingers with the satisfaction of a nigga who liked subtraction when it made his enemies poorer.

"Nigga, you winning a fleet in installments."

I looked at the four blue flags rising together.

"Then tell the general his next payment's due."
