The cathedral was underground because the man who built it had been banned from building anything else above ground.

That was what the driver told us.

I believed him as soon as I saw the elevator.

It held six cars, had a chandelier, and played organ music while lowering us through the bones of Midnight City. Shawna sat beside me in a white dress with a slit that could have started its own criminal organization. Tank leaned against the back of the elevator with his arms folded while Rosa checked whether the chandelier had real diamonds in it.

"Leave it," he told her.

"I'm looking."

"You looking with your bag open."

Mercedes and Porsche had come in boxing jackets over their dresses. Mercedes's said TEAM AIDEN. Porsche's said HE ALREADY WON. They had commissioned them that morning from a tailor who apparently understood the speed of history.

When the elevator stopped, the noise hit us first.

Men shouting. Money changing hands. Music loud enough to move somebody's religion around. The cathedral's old columns rose around a boxing ring built where an altar might once have stood. Above it hung six giant screens. Below them, rows of velvet seats held rich people who wanted to see poor decisions happen close enough to feel expensive.

At the far end of the ring stood the champion.

Malik "Graveyard" Gault was thirty-three and looked like a building that had learned resentment. He wore black gloves, black trunks, and a chain thick enough to tow a boat. Somebody had tattooed a crown across his chest. Underneath it were eleven little marks.

Years undefeated.

Nigga, he was about to need a new tattoo arrangement.

He watched me cross the floor.

"That the nigga?"

His voice came through the speakers because somebody had put a microphone too close to a man who should've been left to breathe quietly.

"That the college nigga?"

I stopped beneath the ring lights.

"Valedictorian. Nigga, say it with the whole education."

The crowd laughed. Gault didn't.

"And the cop nigga?"

I touched the badge on my jacket.

"Captain. You checking the nigga's credentials before you lose?"

"And this nigga sings?"

Shawna answered before I could.

"When he feels like it. Usually when the nigga hears a song he can improve."

Gault leaned on the ropes.

"What this nigga when he bleeds?"

"Still better dressed than you," Rosa called. "You asked about four niggas and they all him. Which one you ready for?"

Tank turned his head away so Gault wouldn't see him laughing. It didn't work. Nigga, Tank's shoulders were shaking like a truck at a red light.

Vesper sat in a raised box beside an empty chair draped in black cloth. Zero's chair. He wasn't there, but he had arranged for his absence to take up more room than everybody else's body.

Rusk stood beneath the box with a scroll.

"The captain enters three trials. Strength, courage, dominion."

"Y'all named three fights like they got degrees."

"The first is fought against the chosen hand of the cathedral. The second against the reigning champion. The third is determined by the queen."

"And if I win?"

Vesper leaned forward.

"A seat among the criminal kings."

"I sat in the throne yesterday."

"Yes. We noticed."

A thin older man approached carrying my gloves. He had silver hair cut close, a straight back, and a scar that crossed one cheek without disturbing the calm around his eyes. His suit was dark blue. Unlike everybody else in the room, he wasn't trying to look dangerous.

That made me watch him more carefully.

"Bastien Moreau," he said. "I will keep the rounds."

"You a referee?"

"Tonight."

"What were you yesterday?"

"Tired."

I liked the nigga immediately.

He checked my gloves while Shawna helped me take off my jacket. A few women near the ring started calling out before the shirt even cleared my shoulders.

"Wait till he breathes," Porsche told them. "You haven't seen the shoulders move yet."

Shawna looked around the crowd with a little smile, then put her palm on my chest where everybody could see whose hand belonged there.

The shouting doubled.

I bent and kissed her.

"How fast you want this done?"

"Fast enough that my dinner's still hot. Slow enough they learn your name."

"Baby, that's a narrow window."

"You're gifted."

My first opponent wore a red mask and had two attendants carrying a hammer behind him. I looked at the hammer. I looked at Bastien.

"That's allowed?"

"It is ceremonial."

"Good, because my ceremony didn't bring one."

The red mask came over the ropes, bounced on his feet, and hit his own chest three times. He was fast. I saw that before he moved toward me. Some fighters advertise power because they want you looking somewhere other than their feet. This one hardly seemed to touch the floor.

The bell rang.

He threw three punches before I had finished deciding where his face probably was under the mask. One caught my shoulder. One brushed my ear. The third met my glove hard enough to wake my arm all the way up.

The crowd roared.

"Aight," I said. "Nigga got somewhere to be."

He came again. I moved. His fist passed my face, and my short right turned the red mask far enough that its left eyehole ended up beside his ear.

The nigga threw another punch at where I used to be.

"Hold up."

He froze with both gloves raised.

"That you talking?"

"Nigga, who else you fighting?"

He tried to turn the mask with a glove and dragged it farther around. Bastien stepped between us. The whole cathedral booed.

"Let him fight!" somebody shouted.

"Nigga can't see!" I shouted back. "Put a bucket on your head and come demonstrate!"

Porsche pointed into the expensive seats.

"That one. Purple suit. He volunteered."

Purple Suit immediately sat down.

Bastien straightened the mask. My opponent blinked through the proper holes and nodded once. The old man backed away.

The bell hadn't rung. The nigga knew it. He hit me in the ribs while I was still looking at Purple Suit.

"Oh, you ungrateful motherfucker."

"We fighting," he said.

"Fair."

He came after the laugh in my mouth. Quick left, quicker right. I caught the right, stepped close, and put a clean shot into his body. His breath went out. His knees followed. I backed off and let Bastien count.

At eight he lifted a glove.

"Finished."

I helped him up.

"Name?"

"Adrian."

"Adrian, my nigga, you got good feet. Let your eyes join the team next time."

He pulled off the mask and looked at it with personal disappointment.

"My brother said that."

"Your brother here?"

A man three rows back stood up so fast he hit the seat ahead of him.

"I TOLD THAT NIGGA!"

Adrian shut his eyes. This, apparently, hurt worse than the body shot.

"Nigga, get him a microphone," Tank said.

"Do not get that nigga a microphone," Adrian said.

The audience finally cheered the right man. Adrian smiled despite himself, then held the mask toward me.

"Sign it?"

"After that sneaky rib punch?"

"You said fair."

"Nigga remembered the ruling. What's your brother called?"

"Luis."

I borrowed a pen from ringside and wrote inside the mask: LUIS WAS RIGHT. Then my name.

Adrian read it. His whole mouth fell open.

"Nigga, I meant your signature."

"That's beneath the medical advice."

He laughed. Luis came down the aisle demanding to inspect the document, and Adrian went through the ropes holding it above his head where his brother couldn't reach.

"You were fast," I called after him. "Come back with your face next time."

He turned long enough to nod. Heard that part. Kept it.

Shawna pressed cold water into my hand when I reached our corner.

"You let him hit you."

"Nigga took advantage of my community work."

She checked my ribs with two fingers.

"Do your community work after the bell."

Tank leaned over her shoulder.

"And quit signing evidence while there's two more niggas waiting to produce some."

"One."

"You keeping count?"

"I'm keeping dinner warm in my imagination."

Before Gault entered, a man in a silver jacket climbed up beside our corner carrying a little board covered in betting prices.

"Captain, the odds have changed."

"Nigga, I ain't changed. Catch the board up."

He swallowed.

"You are still the underdog."

Tank leaned over him.

"That nigga what?"

"The underdog. In the market."

"Nigga, look up. Does that look underneath anything?"

The man stared at my shoulders, then at the people behind him calling my name, then at Shawna wiping my face as if the whole room had been built to make sure she could reach me without standing on tiptoe.

"The champion has eleven years—"

"Nigga, we heard the years," I said. "We about to discuss the minutes."

Mercedes came over with Porsche and a stack of betting slips thick enough to make the man reconsider which direction he wanted to leave.

"We backed Aiden," Mercedes said.

"Obviously," Porsche added.

The silver jacket looked alarmed.

"How much?"

Porsche told him.

Nigga, his face lost money just hearing it.

"That is a significant exposure."

"Then cover your ass with a better opinion," she said.

He hurried away.

Shawna looked at the slips.

"You two bet that much?"

"On him?" Mercedes pointed at me like the answer should've been visible from space. "Baby, this nigga lands cars where helicopters get nervous. I know a sound investment when it takes its shirt off."

Porsche handed Shawna one slip.

"That's yours."

My wife looked at it, then at me.

"Now you really need to win."

"Nigga, I was planning to lose economically till you said that."

She laughed and kissed my shoulder.

Tank leaned close while the women returned to their seats.

"Listen. This nigga ain't one of those pretty fighters who gets surprised by a punch. He good."

"I know."

"I seen him move."

"So have I."

"Then don't be so busy being the greatest nigga in the building that you forget this nigga brought both hands."

I looked at my brother.

He wasn't doubting me. A nigga who loves you will sometimes be the only person in the room willing to remind you that your blood still works like everybody else's.

I touched his fist with mine.

"I got you."

"You better have you. I already got me."

"Nigga, that's a beautiful sentence from a man who lost his room key twice today."

"The hotel got too many doors."

We laughed together, and the laugh loosened something under my ribs the crowd couldn't reach.

Then Gault entered without music.

The cathedral supplied its own.

Eleven years made a heavy noise. Men stood when he passed. Women touched the edge of his robe. His attendants put the chain around a wooden stand shaped like a kneeling man.

I looked at the stand.

"Nigga, your necklace got staff?"

He hit me before the crowd finished laughing.

The punch landed on my gloves and still sent me backward. My heels touched the ropes. Something sharp went through my ribs, a little hello from all the trouble my body had survived before this city thought it was special.

Gault came after me.

He was not slow. That was the dangerous part. Big men sometimes give you enough time to admire how much they're bringing toward you. This nigga brought it right now.

I ducked one punch and took another against my side. The cathedral tilted. I heard Tank shout, but the words were lost beneath the blood rushing through my ears.

Gault leaned close.

"Stay down when I put you there."

"Nigga, you talking about next week? Do something."

He did.

He caught me hard enough that one knee touched the canvas.

The crowd exploded.

Shawna didn't move. Her eyes stayed on mine. There was no panic in them, and somehow that hurt more than panic would've. She believed I knew what I was doing. I had to become the man she was looking at before the next bell.

I stood.

Gault smiled for the first time.

"Good."

I wiped my mouth with the back of my glove.

"Nigga, you enjoy that?"

"Very much."

"Remember it pretty. Tell your niggas you had me kneeling."

"I'll tell them I had you down."

"Then explain how this nigga got back up while you were practicing the story."

I changed the fight.

Stopped giving him the straight line. Made him turn. Let his shoulders tell me what his gloves were lying about. When he swung at my head, I wasn't there. When he turned to find me, I put a fist where his breath had been.

The first good shot made his smile disappear.

The second made the crowd quiet.

The third made Tank climb halfway onto the ring apron before Rosa pulled him down by the back of his jacket.

"Nigga, let the man work!"

"That's my nigga! I'm supporting!"

"Then support him from the floor. Nigga, you shaking his whole workplace!"

Gault drove me into the corner. I could feel blue thunder waiting under my skin, eager and expensive. I left it there. Nigga, I wasn't about to need lightning to convince this man my name was Aiden.

He swung wide.

I stepped inside, hit him twice, and moved before the answer came. His glove struck the corner post. The sound made everybody flinch except him.

Then he came at me angry.

That was when he became easier.

Not easy. A storm ain't easy because you can see it. But anger told me where his next foot would land before he knew himself. I gave him room he couldn't use and took back room he thought he owned.

By the next bell, he was breathing through his mouth.

Bastien came between us.

"Corners."

Gault didn't turn. He stared at me, chest rising hard.

"You really don't have to do this," he said quietly.

"Nigga, I'm in the ring. We past invitations."

"If you win, he doubles what I owe."

For the first time, I looked beyond the fighter and saw the chain stand, the attendants, the empty black chair.

Eleven years undefeated.

Eleven years belonging to somebody.

I went to my corner.

Shawna put the towel against my face.

"What?"

"Zero owns his debt."

"Can you buy it?"

"Probably."

"Then win first. Men like that hear money better when you're standing."

She kissed the clean side of my mouth.

Nigga, how was I supposed to lose after that?

The last round started with Gault trying to take my whole head home.

I let him come. Took what I had to take. Gave more back. The ring shook beneath us. The crowd stood. Somebody threw a glass and Bastien caught it against his jacket without taking his eyes off the fight.

Gault hit the ropes. I hit him once more, clean and final, and the big man sat down like a building had decided it needed a minute.

He tried to stand at five.

Tried again at seven.

At nine, I held out my hand.

He looked at it.

The bell rang.

I kept the hand there.

He took it.

Nigga, the sound that room made could have raised the cathedral to street level.

Shawna came through the ropes and nearly took me off my feet herself. Tank shouted my name like he was mad at anybody who hadn't heard it. Mercedes kissed Porsche on the cheek, then told her not to get ideas because she'd just been overwhelmed by athletic excellence.

Vesper rose in her box.

"The second trial is complete."

I held Gault's wrist up with mine.

"The nigga's debt is complete too."

She went still.

"That is not a prize you were offered."

"Then offer it now."

A murmur passed through the kings. They didn't mind money changing hands. They minded somebody deciding which hands mattered.

Vesper looked toward the empty chair.

"The emperor will expect compensation."

"Tell him send an invoice with a picture of his face on it so I know who to disappoint."

Gault let out one shocked laugh.

I looked at him.

"Nigga, you free enough to laugh yet?"

"I think so."

"Then make it a good one. That's your laugh now."

He tried again. Louder. The nigga looked surprised by how far his own voice could travel without waiting for somebody to allow it.

"That nigga laughing," Tank shouted.

"I hear him," Rosa said.

"Nah. Nigga laughing like he got tomorrow."

Gault heard that too. He looked at my brother and nodded, once, hard.

Vesper descended from her box and approached the ring. Bastien held the ropes for her. She didn't climb in. She stood outside with one hand resting on the top rope, looking at me like the evening had stopped following the order she'd memorized.

"The final trial," she said, "is dominion. You may claim the cathedral's purse. Ten million. Or you may purchase the champion's freedom."

The room quieted again.

Gault's hand fell from mine.

"Take the purse," he said.

"Nigga, be quiet."

"I mean it."

"I know. That's why I told you to be quiet."

I looked at Vesper.

"Buy his freedom."

She studied me for a long second.

"Done."

The cheering started, but I lifted my glove.

"And I'm charging the cathedral eleven million for the fight."

Shawna bent forward laughing.

Rusk actually grabbed the rail.

"You cannot simply—"

"Nigga, I just sold out your dead church, beat your champion, saved your parade yesterday, and brought my own beautiful wife. You think all that come free?"

Vesper put her hand over her mouth.

It didn't hide the smile.

The old silver-bearded king from dinner stood in the front row.

"I'll pay it."

Everybody turned.

His wife pulled him back down by his sleeve.

"We'll pay it," she corrected.

That was how I won three trials, freed a champion, and left a million ahead of where the queen had meant to put me.

Gault met us outside the changing room afterward. Without the chain, he looked younger. Not smaller. Just less arranged for somebody else's benefit.

"If you need me," he said, "send word."

"I need you to go eat something you chose yourself."

He nodded, then looked at Shawna.

"Your husband is—"

"I know," she said.

Gault smiled.

"No. Nigga, I don't think anybody knows all of it yet. I came in here thinking I knew the man I'd have to fight."

"And?" she asked.

"The nigga I needed to meet showed up afterward. Same face. Same hands. Different damn use for them."

She looked at me, proud enough to make my battered ribs sit up straighter.

Nigga, I liked him better every minute.

Bastien waited by the elevator. While everybody else celebrated, he slipped a folded card into my hand.

No crown on it. No fancy threat.

Just an address and a time.

"There is a woman who ought to have seen that fight," he said.

"Who?"

He looked toward Vesper, standing alone beneath the cathedral's last chandelier.

"Someone she was before this city taught her what love cost."

Before I could ask another question, the elevator doors opened. Shawna took my arm. The whole room applauded as we went up.

I looked at the card again once the city lights returned.

A garden. Tomorrow afternoon.

Underneath the address, in a different hand, somebody had written:

COME WITHOUT A CROWN.

"You going?" Shawna asked.

I folded it and put it beside my badge.

"Depends if they got dinner."

"Baby."

"Nigga freed a champion. Let him investigate a menu."

"You already decided."

I smiled at her.

"Yeah. We going. But your nigga taking snacks. I can't keep changing these people's lives on ceremonial portions."
