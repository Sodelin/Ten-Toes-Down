A nigga offered me six hundred million for Isla Gloria before I finished my first breakfast as its owner.

Cream suit. Briefcase. Polished shoes he kept checking against the terrace stones. He approached while Shawna was eating strawberries and I was enjoying the view from directly across the table.

“Captain Aiden. An exceptional acquisition.”

“Nigga, it's breakfast.”

“I refer to the island.”

“I refer to the strawberries.”

He smiled carefully and opened the case. Papers. Pen. The number displayed where I could see it.

“Six hundred million.”

I looked. Shawna selected another strawberry.

“No.”

The man's fingers paused on the pen.

“You haven't considered the offer.”

“Nigga, she picked the curtains.”

Shawna laughed. He looked toward the palace windows, apparently hoping the curtains might be cheaper to negotiate with.

“Those can be included.”

“They already are,” she said. “They're in our house.”

He tried a larger number. I took a strawberry. He explained opportunities. Shawna poured more coffee. He used the phrase liquid position, and I pointed at the sea before my wife caught my wrist and lowered it.

“Behave.”

“Nigga brought that phrase to my ocean.”

She shook with laughter. The man capped his pen. Uncapped it. Nigga couldn't find a financial instrument that would make either of us stop enjoying breakfast.

“Is there a figure you'd entertain?”

“She's sitting right there.”

Shawna threw her napkin at me.

“All right, that one's terrible.”

“Nigga, I'm off duty.”

The visitor closed his briefcase. She thanked him for coming, which was considerably nicer than the farewell I had prepared. We watched him cross the terrace, still protecting the shoes.

Then she leaned over and kissed me.

“You like money.”

“Love money.”

“Love me more?”

“Nigga, money don't look at me like that.”

She gave me the last strawberry and moved her chair around beside mine. I made room. The ocean stayed exactly where she'd asked me to bring her.

We stayed three more days.

Long enough for the island to become more than the finish line. Long enough to learn which bakery opened first, which balcony got the evening breeze, which road made Shawna lean against the window and stop talking because the view had found the quiet part of her.

I drove her around the coast in the blue Ferrari. Slowly sometimes. Nigga, I know. Nobody tell the commentators. They would have spent twenty minutes investigating whether I had been replaced.

We stopped at the little beach beneath the eastern cliffs. White sand. Clear water. No palace visible from it, no flags, no trophy ceremony waiting to begin. Shawna took off her shoes and walked straight into the sea.

I followed.

The water reached our knees.

She turned toward me with her dress gathered in one hand.

“This is what I asked for.”

I looked around.

“All that racing for this little patch?”

She splashed me.

“Nigga, don't make the ocean regret choosing us.”

I caught her around the waist and lifted her out of the water. She laughed, arms around my neck, feet bright in the sun.

For a while there was nobody to impress except the woman already holding me like I had won.

I impressed her anyway.

Habit.

In town, the old race posters came down. New ones went up with all five finalists on them, though the printer made my photograph twice as large and claimed it was necessary for balance. Sabine sent me a message saying she had never seen balance work so much like vanity.

I sent her a signed poster.

She asked for three more.

Adrian paid his remaining wagers without complaint and invited me to race his new boat the following summer.

“Nigga,” I told him, “you sure you can afford friendship?”

He laughed.

“I can't afford another enemy like you.”

Rafe came to the palace with flowers for Inez and a bottle for me. He apologized for the things he'd said before he knew us. I told him if I refused every nigga who had doubted me, I'd spend my whole life eating alone.

He stayed for lunch.

So did Sabine.

So did half the harbor once somebody heard Rosa was singing in the garden.

The palace learned what a table was for.

Mercedes and Porsche spent the days finishing No Second Door. They chose Alina as their local partner, gave her a real share and asked her opinion before buying the chairs. She cried at the question, which made Mercedes furious on her behalf, then made her sit down and choose the fucking chairs before the tears dried into indecision.

Lark and Mateo opened the garage with six of the rescued mechanics. The sign read RED SAINT WORKS. Beneath it, in smaller letters, Mateo had added CARS RETURNED ALIVE WHEN POSSIBLE.

Lark pointed at me when I read it.

“That line is for you.”

“Nigga, my vehicles have rich experiences.”

“They have rich owners. There's a difference.”

Mateo laughed, and she kissed him without stopping her argument.

His plant had survived in their mainland apartment. A neighbor had watered it. He brought it to the island himself and placed it in the garage window like it had also been rescued from a difficult month.

Inez and Cassius postponed choosing a wedding date until they could get through one dinner without both fathers arguing about the cars in the procession.

Nero wanted the White Heron.

Esteban wanted the Red Saint brought into the harbor at the same moment.

Inez wanted everyone to stop making her wedding a racing exhibition.

Cassius looked at her.

“You met me at a race.”

“I also met you standing still occasionally. We can build on that.”

She turned to me.

“You'll come?”

“Nigga, try to stop me.”

She hugged me hard.

Cassius waited until she stepped away, then took my hand and pulled me into his own embrace.

“My father,” he said quietly.

“I know.”

“My life.”

“Nigga, you helped save mine too.”

He stepped back, eyes bright.

“You ever need us.”

“I know.”

This time the words meant exactly what they should.

Celadon kept command of the Sovereign Sun. I told him the flagship came with the fleet, but the wheel stayed with the man who knew its name. Renee moved her things aboard the same afternoon and began rearranging his cabin with a confidence that made him look happier than any crown could have.

He showed me the bridge before we left.

The brass fittings shone. The little white bird had been restored above the doorway. His old logbook lay open on the desk, the first new entry written in a steady hand.

HOME.

One word.

Nigga had earned the period.

Marcellus came with us to the harbor farewell wearing the Blue Mercy ring and a clean linen suit. He handed Shawna a small silver box.

Inside were earrings shaped like two little waves.

“For the room,” he said.

She looked at him.

“Earrings?”

“You are most of what makes it beautiful.”

Nigga, the old man still had dangerous hands even when he wasn't holding tools.

Shawna kissed his cheek.

I shook my head.

“You trying to take my job?”

“I retired from competing with young men. They make it exhausting.”

We laughed together.

Knox, Tank and Rosa flew back to Vegas a day ahead of us. Knox said the department needed her. Tank said the original black Mercedes had been waiting long enough and he wanted to make sure Rosa's seat was exactly how she'd left it. Rosa said both niggas were planning something and she intended to be present when it became obvious.

They took Mercedes and Porsche with them to handle the mainland arrangements. The new clubs would run through Alina and the island team. The women could own something without standing beside it every minute to prove it belonged to them.

My police Lamborghini went home on the earlier cargo flight too, cleaned and repaired again, its gold badge catching the light as it was loaded. The same car that had crossed yachts, market stairs, a floating road and the duke's palace gates would be waiting when I landed in Vegas.

Nigga, some vehicles deserve a vacation.

Mine was getting a change of scenery.

On our last afternoon, a black launch entered the harbor without music.

That was the first thing I noticed.

Everything else arriving that week had announced itself with horns, cheers, somebody waving a bottle. This boat moved quietly to the private quay. A man in a dark uniform stepped off it and walked toward the palace.

The stranger.

The nigga who had watched the duke lose.

He came onto the terrace while Shawna and I were drinking coffee with Celadon, Renee, Nero and Esteban. He stopped at a respectful distance and inclined his head.

“Captain Aiden.”

I set down my cup.

“You keep showing up.”

“I wanted to see whether the island suited you.”

“Nigga, it adjusted.”

A faint smile.

“General Solomon Saint.”

The name meant something to Celadon. I saw his shoulders tighten. Nero looked up from his glass.

Saint was fifty-one, composed, broad through the chest, dark uniform plain except for a small emblem at the throat: a crown with seven points. He carried no visible weapon. He had the stillness of a man accustomed to other people carrying enough for him.

“You were Aurelio's friend?” I asked.

“An interested guest.”

“Your interest look expensive.”

“It can be.”

He looked toward the harbor, the fleet, the road climbing past the opera house.

“I offered him a festival. Ships. Champions. Demonstrations of power. He wanted to remain the largest thing in every room.”

“Nigga, poor timing.”

Saint smiled more openly.

“Indeed.”

Shawna put her cup down.

“What do you want from my husband?”

He turned toward her with a formal bow.

“To find out whether the stories are large enough.”

“They've been trying to catch up,” she said.

I loved her so much I almost forgot to dislike him.

Saint took a black envelope from his jacket and laid it on the table. The seven-point crown shone on its seal.

“A challenge,” he said. “When you return to Las Vegas. I believe a man who wins every road on one island should have the opportunity to meet the rest of the world.”

I didn't touch the envelope yet.

“Nigga, you selling tickets or trouble?”

“Sometimes the distinction is unprofitable.”

Celadon spoke before I could answer.

“The island isn't a weapons fair.”

Saint looked at him.

“No,” he said. “Not under its present captain.”

He let the sentence sit there, pleasant enough to be heard two ways and ugly in both.

I stood.

He looked back at me.

“My island. My people. My wife. If you got a challenge, put your own name on the risk.”

For the first time something bright moved behind his still eyes.

“I intend to.”

He stepped away from the table.

“Enjoy your return, Captain.”

Then he left as quietly as he had arrived.

I watched the black launch cross the harbor.

Nero put down his glass.

“That man doesn't collect trophies. He collects the men holding them.”

“Nigga, he picked a heavy shelf.”

Shawna took the envelope and turned it over in her hands.

“You don't have to answer today.”

“I know.”

“You have a home to get back to.”

I looked at her.

Then at the island.

Two homes now, at least. More if you counted every place my people had made room at a table and expected me to show up hungry.

“We go home,” I said.

She smiled.

I called Tank that evening.

"Nigga, the Mercedes good?"

"The Mercedes good. The nigga driving it good. His woman good. Why you checking on the car first?"

"Because you answered. I already knew you alive."

Rosa took the phone. "Nigga, he washed it twice."

"That's care."

"Then he sat in it waiting for you."

Tank's voice came from farther away. "Nigga, I was checking the seat."

"This nigga loves you," Rosa told me.

I smiled at the dark sea beyond the terrace.

"Tell that nigga I know. Tell him I'm coming."

The next morning the harbor came to say goodbye.

Not every person. Enough to make the road look like it had grown an audience. Celadon rang the bell. Renee waved from the bridge. Lark and Mateo stood beneath the garage sign, both holding coffee, both finally looking like people allowed to plan a week. Mercedes and Porsche called from Vegas while Alina held the phone toward the crowd so they could shout their own goodbyes into it.

Cassius and Inez drove us to the airfield in the White Heron, Nero following in a second car with Esteban. On the way, Inez took one bend too quickly just to hear Cassius complain.

“Nigga, this is my father's car.”

“Your father taught you to be brave.”

“He also taught me to stop talking to you while driving.”

She laughed and reached for his hand at the next straight.

At the plane, I hugged them both.

“Take care of my ocean,” I told Inez.

“Take care of the road between here and us.”

“Always.”

We boarded with the island deed in my case, seven keys wrapped carefully beside it, Shawna's music in the blue bag and the black envelope waiting unopened in the inner pocket of my jacket.

Marcellus sat across the aisle, already asleep before the plane moved.

Shawna took the window seat.

I took the one beside her.

As we climbed, Isla Gloria shrank beneath us. The palace became white stone. The harbor became a curve of light. The roads became little lines I knew with my hands.

My wife rested her head on my shoulder.

“You really got me the ocean.”

“Nigga, I pay attention.”

She laughed softly.

Then she fell asleep with her hand over mine.

Hours later, the lights of Vegas appeared beneath the wing.

I looked down at the city where the whole beautiful disaster had begun. Somewhere below, my black Mercedes was waiting with Tank beside it. My police Lamborghini was clean for perhaps the last time that week. My chief had a department full of niggas who had watched me win an island and apparently still believed I needed supervision.

Shawna woke as the wheels touched down.

“Home?” she asked.

I kissed her.

“Home.”

The black envelope pressed against my chest beside Dante's cross.

General Saint could wait until I saw what Vegas had done with my name.

Nigga, I had been gone a month.

The city had better have used it.
