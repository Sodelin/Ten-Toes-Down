That told me everything.

Not at the driver who had beaten him. Not at the police coming through his gates. At the little gold object that would make it impossible for him to keep pretending his promises were only decorations.

Lord Orlan froze with the case in his hands.

I stood three steps away.

Behind me, Shawna moved toward Rosa and the others. Not running. Making room. Keeping the people she loved out of the line the duke was trying to draw through the courtyard.

I had no more to spend.

Nigga, I still had me.

“You lost,” I said.

Aurelio laughed.

It sounded painful.

“I built this.”

“Nigga, half the men you stole it from standing behind me.”

He glanced toward Nero and Celadon.

Looked away quickly.

“You think they made it beautiful? They made boats. Cars. Little races for men who wanted trophies cheap enough to carry home. I made kings come here.”

“And one came.”

His eyes hit mine.

The stranger in the dark uniform stood beside the palace door, watching us with his hands clasped behind his back. He had moved out of the gun's line without seeming to move at all. His attention bothered me more than the weapon.

But Aurelio had the weapon.

One nigga at a time.

“You walked into my harbor,” the duke said. “You wrecked my boats. You humiliated my men. You took my guests, my drivers, my staff—”

“They were mine.”

“They stayed because I came back.”

Some of them had spent years lowering their eyes when he passed.

They weren't lowering them now.

I smiled faintly.

“Nigga, you already asked that. Answer got louder.”

A hand touched his shoulder.

The stranger in the dark uniform.

“Put it down, Aurelio.”

The stranger removed his hand.

“Then finish your lesson.”

He stepped back.

Nigga, I did not like him at all.

Orlan's hands shook.

He looked at the gun.

Then at me.

Then, very carefully, he placed the case on the ground between us and stepped away from it.

The duke's face changed.

Another man had chosen.

He fired.

The shot struck the stone beside Orlan's foot. Orlan fell backward. The crowd screamed. Knox shouted. I moved.

Not thunder.

Me.

I crossed the last steps and struck Aurelio's gun arm upward as he fired again. The bullet shattered a palace window. I drove him against the stair rail, his shoulder hitting stone, and we went down together with the gun trapped between our bodies.

He was stronger than he looked.

Anger can lend a nigga strength too. It just charges interest in judgment.

He hit my ribs. The old wound flared. I answered with an elbow and felt him gasp. His gun came free for half a second. I caught his wrist and slammed it against the rail until the weapon fell between the steps.

He kicked me hard enough to break my balance.

Aurelio ran.

Up the palace stairs. Through the open doors. Past the stranger, who moved aside and let him go with an expression almost like disappointment.

I followed.

Shawna called my name behind me.

I looked back once.

She stood beside Knox, gun lowered, eyes on mine.

“Come back,” she said.

“Nigga, always.”

Then I went into the palace.

Aurelio had built the interior to make a man feel small. High ceilings. Marble floors. Portraits twice the size of the people they described. Gold everywhere, even on things nobody needed to look at. He ran through it like a man fleeing his own advertisement.

I chased him past a fountain full of blue stones and into a gallery lined with cars.

His private collection.

Ferraris. Bentleys. A gold roadster. A black racing machine on a raised platform beneath a portrait of him holding a falcon. Nigga had made the bird look impressed too.

He reached the black car and pulled open its door.

I caught the edge before he closed it.

He struck my hand with the frame.

Pain shot up my arm.

I let go.

The engine started.

Aurelio looked at me through the windshield.

For a second he smiled again.

Then he drove straight at me.

I jumped onto the raised platform beside the gold roadster. His car tore past, struck a display stand and smashed through the gallery's glass doors into the inner courtyard.

Nigga had finally joined me in respecting architecture creatively.

I went after him.

Outside, the black car spun around the courtyard fountain. A narrow ramp climbed toward the palace's upper terrace, wide enough for one vehicle. Aurelio took it, tires shrieking, heading for the private road beyond the northern wall.

He had a way out.

I had a gold roadster with its keys still hanging from a little ceremonial hook.

Some rich niggas mistake display for security right up to the end.

I started it and drove through the broken doors.

The roadster roared beneath me, engine huge, hood long and bright as sunrise. I hit the ramp behind Aurelio and climbed through a storm of dust and broken stone. The palace courtyard fell away below. Ahead, his black car reached the terrace and turned toward the outer gate.

I came alongside at the top.

There was barely room.

He looked over, and I saw the exact moment he realized I was driving one of his favorite cars.

“Nigga,” I said through the open side, “you got good taste in defeat.”

He rammed me.

The roadster struck the terrace wall. Stone broke away. Beyond it lay the lower gardens, a long drop broken by cypress trees and statues of men who had never had to fight me.

I held the wheel.

He hit me again.

The gold car's front wheel climbed the low wall. For a breath I looked down at the gardens beneath my door. Then I turned into him, hard enough to catch his front corner and force his black car sideways across the narrow terrace.

Both engines screamed.

The cars locked together.

We slid toward the fountain at the terrace's center and struck it in a crash that threw water over both windshields.

The engines died.

Nigga, we had reached the end of the road in a very expensive manner.

I got out slowly.

My legs shook. My hand burned. Blood ran from a cut at my hairline into one eye. I wiped it away and saw Aurelio climbing from the black car, a pistol in his hand.

Another one.

The nigga owned backups for everything except character.

He aimed.

I went behind the roadster as the shot tore through its open door. Gold paint flew. I drew my own gun, came up on the other side and held him in my sights.

For the first time we stood still together.

The sea spread behind him.

The island lay below.

He looked around as if hoping the palace, the cars, the whole brilliant thing he had built around himself might speak on his behalf.

Nothing did.

“You could have taken the money,” he said.

“Nigga, I got money.”

“The ships.”

“Got a captain for them.”

“The women.”

I stared at him.

“My wife chose me. That's why you don't understand any of this.”

His face twisted.

“You are nothing but noise.”

I thought of the harbor crowd. Of the band changing its song when I stepped off the ferry. Of Rosa singing after the attack. Of Shawna filling an empty opera house with a voice big enough to make it hers before I owned a single stone.

I smiled.

Aurelio fired.

I fired back.

His shot struck the fountain beside me.

Mine struck him in the chest.

He took one step backward.

Then another.

The gun fell from his hand. He sat down heavily against the broken edge of the fountain, green racing suit darkening, eyes fixed on the palace doors.

I kept my weapon trained on him until he stopped moving.

Aurelio Cruel died looking toward a house that no longer answered him.

No speech at the end.

No secret son. No last contract. No letter explaining that he had loved somebody too much to stop being cruel.
