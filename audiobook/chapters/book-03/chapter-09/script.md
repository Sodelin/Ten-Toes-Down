# 9. I Put My Badge on a Speedboat

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

Nigga, you don't own a racing yacht. Tank announced this while standing on one of my regular yachts, looking at two more of my regular yachts, eating breakfast brought by a waiter who had asked which fork he preferred for melon. Did that sentence feel poor coming out? I asked. Aiden. Nigga, do your mouth need a loan?

Rosa moved her coffee clear of the argument. Experience. Tank slapped down the announcement. The Sea Crown would award the third key: high-powered boats, a course around the offshore islands, crews chosen by the drivers. Competitors could bring a vessel or accept one supplied by the duke. Mine was called Second Chance. Yellow paint. A little ladder at the back.

In the photograph, a man stood beside it with a mop. Nigga, Tank said. That's the crew. That's a hostage, I said. Shawna leaned over the photograph. What's wrong with the boat? Where you want me to begin? Lark asked. The part that floats. That might take longer. She turned the picture around.

Nigga had sent us something so old the nearest seagull looked like its grandchild. Lark tapped the engine housing with one fingernail. I'm not putting you in that. Thank you. I got enough work without retrieving you. Tank raised his fork. I could tow it. Rosa lowered his hand. You could finish breakfast before inventing another occupation.

Cassius laughed and caught himself against his healing shoulder. The sling was gone; Inez's supervision wasn't. She reached for his coffee before he could make the wrong arm responsible for it. My father kept a boat here, he said. Before the island. Esteban looked up. The Red Saint? Yes. Nigga, I said, every vehicle in this family got a religious problem. She's fast.

Then I'm prepared to convert. Lark already had her bag. She took the yellow boat photograph with her. Why keep that? Tank asked. In case anybody complains about my estimate. The Red Saint sat in a locked shed beyond the fishing harbor. Cassius had inherited the lease and paid it for years without opening the place.

He told us that on the drive there, looking out the window as if the words were written on the sea. I thought if I opened it, I'd have to decide what to throw away. Now you can ask him, I said. He nodded once. The shed doors rolled back to reveal a red racing boat beneath a gray cover.

Its shape was low and clean, all muscle. Lark walked around it slowly. Esteban touched the side with the tenderness of a man finding an old friend's coat. Nero beat me three times in this, he said. Cassius smiled. He said four. One was wind. Nigga, wind participates in boats, Tank said. Esteban laughed. The sound came easier now. Not easy, but easier.

We uncovered the Red Saint together. Dust rose in the light. Beneath the windshield lay a pair of old gloves and a little photograph of Cassius at twenty-four, standing beside his father with both of them grinning like the camera owed them money. Cassius picked it up. Inez stood beside him. She didn't say anything. Just stayed there while he looked.

Lark found the engine sound enough for the race after work and attention. We gave her both. She brought in three mechanics from the rescued crews, paid them before they touched a tool and told them this boat was carrying a man who considered walls a rumor. Nigga, one mechanic said, looking at me, you the fountain driver? The yacht driver, another corrected.

The staircase driver, the third said. Tank folded his arms. All them niggas is the same nigga. Keep up. They stared at me with open delight. Then they made the Red Saint shine. That night we went to the Coral Crown, a casino built above the harbor on pillars shaped like women holding the roof.

Its owner, Rocco Silvar, met us at the door in a pink jacket and enough gold to make Marcellus squint. Captain Aiden, he said. A pleasure. Depends how the night goes. Rocco was forty-two, round-faced, merry and dangerous in a way that didn't require pretending he wasn't enjoying himself. He had known Mercedes for years.

She kissed his cheek, then held his chin between two fingers. You still owe Paloma. His smile faltered. I sent the payment. You sent flowers. Very expensive flowers. Nigga, flowers don't pay rent unless she sells the whole funeral. He looked at Porsche. She held out her phone. She's awake. You can apologize live. He paid before we reached the table. I loved my people.

The casino had prepared a private room for us, but I wanted the main floor. I liked the noise, the light, the sight of grown men begging colored chips to improve their lives. Rocco gave us a table near the band. Rosa sang one song because the singer recognized her and almost dropped the microphone.

Tank stood beside the stage trying to look calm while six men told him the woman he loved had the voice of an angel who knew exactly what a bad night cost. Nigga, I know, he kept saying. Then he tipped the band so much the drummer stood up.

I played cards with Cassius, Rocco and a man named Count Adrian Tide, who would captain the duke's newest boat in the Sea Crown. Adrian was thirty-five, broad, blond and handsome enough to have learned how rarely apologies were required if he smiled at the right angle. He looked at my chips. You drive well, he said. Water is different. Nigga, I been wet.

Rocco laughed into his drink. Adrian kept smiling. Have you ever captained a racing boat? No. Then tomorrow will be educational. For somebody. He put down his cards. A very good hand. I put down mine. Better. The table went quiet. Then Cassius started laughing. You did that on purpose. Won? Yeah. Rocco pushed the chips toward me with both hands.

Nigga, the house is impressed, he said. The house should add a balcony. Adrian watched me collect the pot. His smile had become something he was carrying rather than wearing. Ten million on tomorrow, he said. Twenty. He blinked. Rocco made a delighted little noise. Twenty, Adrian agreed. I looked toward Shawna. She sat with Mercedes and Porsche, laughing at something Alina had said.

The rescued woman wore a green dress Mercedes had helped her choose and looked like somebody returning to her own face. Shawna caught my eye. I held up two fingers. She looked at the chips. Then at Adrian. Then back at me. Bring me something nice, she mouthed. Nigga, the wager became sacred. We stayed until the band changed sets twice. There was champagne.

There were women in diamonds and men trying to become stories worth telling them. In one private room, rich fools had laid out enough cocaine to make their noses seem ambitious. I walked past with my wife and let them ruin their own timing. I needed my hands steady in the morning, and I already felt like the most dangerous drug in the building.

Mercedes and Porsche left the casino with a contract for the Grand Prix's official hospitality team, written on terms they had chosen, and Rocco's apology recorded for Paloma. Tank left with Rosa on his arm and a thousand people convinced he was the luckiest man alive after me. I left twenty million richer in possibility. The Sea Crown began at noon.

Our boat waited at the end of the quay, red hull shining, my gold badge fixed above the dashboard. Cassius had insisted on placing his father's little white bird beside it. He'd like that, he said. Tell him yourself when we bring it back. Lark took the engineer's place. Esteban came as navigator.

Tank squeezed into the rear seat and immediately accused the boat of being small on purpose. Shawna stayed on the viewing yacht with Rosa, Mercedes, Porsche and Inez. She had kissed me before we boarded, then put a folded handkerchief into my pocket. For when you pretend the spray isn't in your eyes. Nigga, you think of everything. That is why you keep surviving.

The starting horn sounded. Adrian's white boat leaped ahead. Three others followed in a roar of engines and spray. I brought the Red Saint out behind them, feeling the water through the hull, listening to Esteban's voice and Lark's sharp little comments from the engine station. Easy through the chop. I got it. Nigga, the sea doesn't care you won cards.

Then it's about to learn. Tank gripped the side rail as we hit the first swell. Nigga! What? The water punched me! Punch it back. He looked at the sea with genuine consideration. Esteban laughed so hard he had to repeat the next turn. We rounded the first island in fourth. Took third across the open channel.

The Red Saint handled like it had been waiting seven years to be angry at somebody. I learned its movements fast, the lift before a wave, the shiver when I asked too much, the smooth powerful answer when I stopped fighting what it already knew. Adrian stayed ahead. He was excellent. I could see why the duke had chosen him.

His boat ran clean, straight and wickedly fast. He used the islands to break the following boats' sight of him, opening space whenever the water narrowed. But he kept looking back. Nigga knew I was coming. The Red Saint lifted over another swell. Tank grabbed the rail with both hands. "Nigga, we in the air." "Briefly," I said.

"Nigga, briefly is where the fear lives." Lark laughed. "You want him to slow down?" "I want that nigga to warn the boat before he promotes it." Esteban pointed ahead. "Next marker." "You heard the nigga," I said. "We got a marker." "Nigga, I can see the marker.

I want to remain where seeing is possible." Then we landed cleanly, the hull cutting through the next wave, and Tank's complaint turned into a shout of pure delight. At the second marker we passed a blue boat whose captain had driven too hard into the crosswind. It listed sharply, one side low, two crewmen waving for help. Adrian went past without slowing.

Esteban saw it. Aiden. I see them. We turned. Tank stood up before I told him. Lark threw a line toward the damaged boat. The first crewman caught it. The second slipped, vanished into the water and came up coughing beside the hull. Tank reached over the side and hauled him aboard with one arm. Nigga, you heavy when wet.

The man lay on the deck, gasping. Thank you. Thank the captain. I was eating lunch before he made this a lifestyle. We stayed until the rescue boat arrived. By then Adrian had gained nearly a minute. Esteban looked at me. My wife would have liked you. Nigga, she had taste. Then I turned the Red Saint toward the last channel. We chased.

The coast blurred. Spray hit my face. Tank shouted every time we rose over a swell, and after a while his shouting changed from protest to celebration. Lark leaned forward, watching the boat, watching me, her mouth open in disbelief as the white shape ahead grew larger. You're catching him, she said. Yeah. You stopped. Yeah. You shouldn't be catching him.

Nigga, stop telling the boat. Esteban pointed toward the final turn, a gap between two rocky islands with the finish line beyond it. Adrian entered first. I came alongside halfway through, the red and white boats so close their spray struck together in a high shining wall. He looked over. I smiled. That same fraction of a second again.

Some niggas never learn what their pride costs them. We came out of the channel level. The harbor opened ahead, full of boats and flags and people standing wherever a human body could fit. On the viewing yacht I could see Shawna at the rail, both hands raised. I heard her even over the engine. Aiden!

I gave the Red Saint everything it had asked me for. We crossed first by a breath. The horn sounded. Tank stood up and roared. Lark grabbed my shoulder hard enough to bruise it. Esteban took off his cap and held it against his chest. For a moment he wasn't looking at the finish. He was looking at the little white bird.

Adrian came alongside after the race. He sat still in his white boat, wet and furious and trying to decide whether he had just witnessed an insult or a miracle. You've never done that before? he called. No. He looked at the Red Saint. At my badge. At Tank, who was now waving at strangers like he owned their country. Then Adrian laughed, helplessly.

You magnificent bastard. Nigga, pay the woman. He did. Twenty million went into Shawna's account before I got off the boat. She met me on the quay, took my wet face in both hands and kissed me while the third jeweled key hung between us. What'd you buy me? she asked. Options. She smiled. I want the opera house. Then we better finish the month.

Above the cheering crowd, I saw Aurelio standing on his balcony beside the stranger in the dark uniform. The stranger wasn't watching the boats. He was watching me. When our eyes met, he raised two fingers in a slow salute. I didn't salute back. Nigga, a man that still in the middle of all that noise was either very impressed or very dangerous.

I had room in my schedule for both.
