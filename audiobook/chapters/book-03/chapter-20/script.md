# 20. Breakfast Before Paradise

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

The morning of the final race, Tank burned the toast. After everything we had survived, nigga, that felt personal. He stood in the opera house kitchen with two black slices pinched between his fingers. Rosa stared at them. He turned one over, as though the other side might be an alibi. You can lift a car, she said. Car don't burn this fast.

You left it. I was checking the doors. Nigga, the toast wasn't attacking the doors. I came in just as he put the slices on a plate. Duke send that? He threw a towel. I caught it and put it over the plate. Have some respect. Those niggas were bread once. Rosa laughed so hard she leaned on the counter.

Tank lifted the towel, inspected his work and sighed. It ain't that bad. Then bite it, she said. He considered that. An enormous man considering two square inches of breakfast. I had seen him make quicker decisions while people shot at him. Nigga, he said at last, I got to save my teeth for the victory dinner.

He scraped the slices into the bin and reached for more bread. Rosa put her hand over his. Stay here this time. I am. Both feet. In the kitchen. Nigga, I know where a kitchen is. Apparently only after it starts smoking. She kissed his wrist and let go.

He stood before the toaster with his arms folded, guarding breakfast so intently that nobody passing behind him could keep a straight face. How's security? I asked. Nigga, secure. When the new slices popped, he caught one in the air, dropped it immediately because it was hot, then caught it again on the plate. Rosa applauded once. There you go.

We were still laughing when we sat down. Through the open doorway I could see the bullet marks beside the entrance. It was good to hear the kitchen louder than the memory of them. The injured had been taken to the clinic. The wounded officer was stable.

The violinist had insisted he could still play and then been told by Valentina that optimism was not a substitute for an uninjured arm. Nobody from our company had died in the attack. Two of the duke's men had. Their bodies were taken away before breakfast, and the square had been washed clean without pretending the night had never happened.

Knox sat at the kitchen table with Odette, coffee between them. They had the calm, tired faces of women who had spent the night keeping other people from becoming tomorrow's bad news. Captain, Knox said when she saw me. Chief. Race. Win. Come home. Nigga, concise leadership. I'm practicing for when you start interrupting. Shawna came in carrying a plate of eggs. Clementine's eggs.

The marshal's wife had insisted on cooking them for us before the final race. Apparently groceries could become ceremonial if a nigga drove them fast enough. We ate at long tables pushed together beneath the kitchen windows. Drivers, musicians, mechanics, police, sex workers, boat captains, jewelers.

Marcellus had come from the mainland with a case of tools and the real Blue Mercy ring on his hand. He had spent the early morning repairing the clasp on my key case because, he said, one should not win an island and lose its entrance into a drain. I sat beside him. You been quiet. I've been watching you damage things.

Nigga, that's almost a career now. For both of us, apparently. He lifted the case. Six jeweled keys lay inside, each different, each bright. The gold wheel, the little steering crown, the sea-blue stone. He had cleaned them all. Original work, he said. The old race commissioned a different maker for each. Your last one is the largest. How you know?

I know the man who made it. Of course you do. He was very vain. Nigga, was it you? Marcellus smiled into his coffee. Tank nearly choked. You made the big key? Forty years in a trade leads to the occasional relevant object. I looked at him. Nigga, you could have mentioned it. You were busy driving through the doors the smaller ones opened. Fair.

Tank leaned over Marcellus's shoulder. "Nigga made the biggest key before Aiden even got here." "Years before," Marcellus said. "So that nigga was preparing." "I was working." "Preparing while working," Tank insisted. I shook my head. "Nigga, let the man have a past without making it my appointment." Shawna looked at me in surprise. "That was almost modest." "Nigga, almost.

The key still ends up in my hand." Marcellus laughed softly and opened the case wider. "You young niggas exhaust a very old object." He showed me a little photograph from the case. A much younger Marcellus stood beside an older jeweler, both holding the unfinished ceremonial key. The date was more than twenty years earlier. No secret prophecy. No destiny waiting in a workshop.

Just an old man whose life had been larger than the corner of it I'd met. I wanted the teeth shaped like waves, he said. My teacher told me a key must first open something. And? It does both. I shook his hand. Then I'll bring it back for inspection. He nodded. Please bring the hand holding it too.

Across the table, Mercedes and Porsche argued about the names of the clubs they planned to open on Isla Gloria. Blue Hour stays, Mercedes said. For the yacht, Porsche replied. The island room needs its own name. Nigga, it's the same ocean. Different door. Alina leaned between them. What about No Second Door? They both looked at her. She shrugged.

Nobody gets sent around the back. Mercedes smiled slowly. That I like. Porsche wrote it down. I pointed at them with my fork. You got the building by the eastern quay. You haven't won it yet, Porsche said. Nigga, put it on hold. She grinned. Already did. Lark and Mateo planned a garage. Not a palace workshop where drivers swept in, shouted and vanished.

A place with windows, decent food and enough room for mechanics to sit down without somebody accusing them of laziness. Mateo wanted a little garden behind it. Lark told him if he managed to keep the plant at home alive for a month, she would consider expanding his responsibilities. He kissed her cheek. Nigga, I watered it before they took me.

Then it survived better than your communication. He laughed. She did too. Nero and Esteban wanted the White Heron on the final road, but Cassius refused to race it that day. She's done enough, he said. I'll drive my own. Nero nodded. No argument. The old car would wait at the finish with them inside it.

Inez sat beside Cassius, watching him across the rim of her cup. What? he asked. You started a sentence last night. Tank looked up immediately. Nigga. Cassius put his cup down. I remember. You planning to finish it? After the race. She considered him. Then she smiled. Drive well. Nigga, that man had acquired a finish line inside the finish line.

After breakfast I went to the courtyard. The police Lamborghini waited in the sun. Dark blue, gold badge, repaired body, familiar shape. Lark stood beside it with Mateo, both of them wearing clean team shirts for the first time all month. She held out the keys. She'll bring you through. I know. Don't ask her to be a boat. Nigga, that was another car.

Don't ask her to be a plane. Context matters. Mateo laughed and stepped back as Shawna came through the doors. She wore white. Not a wedding dress. White trousers, a fitted jacket, blue silk at the throat. Dante's watch on her wrist. My earrings. Her own beautiful face, looking at me like she had found the one place in the courtyard she meant to stand.

You riding? I asked. Final race is single driver. I can argue. Nigga, I know. She put her hand against my chest, over the cross. I'll be at the finish. I want you to see me before you stop. I held her close. The noise of the courtyard moved around us. Tools. Footsteps.

Tank asking somebody whether the toast had been thrown out or merely relocated. Life, nigga. All these people making plans because they expected a tomorrow big enough to hold them. I love you, I said. Shawna looked up. I know. I love you too. Even when I do too much? Especially when you come back and tell me. I kissed her.

Then a messenger arrived from the palace. No feather this time. No velvet tray. Just a young man in a gray jacket holding an envelope with both hands. He looked frightened enough that I thanked him before opening it. Inside was an offer.

The duke would surrender the prize fleet, withdraw his objections to the rescued workers leaving and pay me fifty million if I left Isla Gloria before noon. He would retain the island. The opera house was not mentioned. Nigga really thought he had found a number larger than my wife. I handed the letter to Porsche. Keep that. She read it and whistled.

You want me to answer? Yeah. What should I say? Shawna looked at the blue curtains visible through the upstairs window. Tell him I already chose the room. Porsche smiled and began writing. We left for the starting line with the whole square cheering.

The final race would begin at the eastern harbor, climb through the old vineyards, pass the naval fort, cross the island's interior and descend through the palace gates into the great courtyard. The seventh key waited there. So did the duke. He had withdrawn from the driving field.

After the floating road, perhaps the nigga had learned that a steering wheel was not improved by ownership papers. Five drivers remained: Inez, Cassius, Adrian, Sabine and me. At the grid, we stood together beside our cars. No speeches about noble competition. No promises to slow down for one another. We wanted to win. That was why we had come.

But every one of us had seen enough of the duke to know the last road would ask for more than speed. Sabine held out her hand. See you at the palace. Inez put hers over it. Cassius added his. Adrian looked at me. I'd like to beat you once. Nigga, ambition keeps a man young. He laughed and put his hand in.

I covered theirs with mine. Then we went to our cars. From the Sovereign Sun in the harbor, Celadon rang the ship's bell. Nero and Esteban stood beside the White Heron at the far end of the quay, ready to take the ordinary road to the finish. Shawna waited in the open car with Rosa, Mercedes and Porsche. Tank drove.

Chief Knox sat in the car behind them with Odette, and Bruno occupied most of its remaining hope. My wife lifted one hand. I lifted mine. The lights began counting down. Nigga, the whole month had brought me to this one beautiful stretch of road. I intended to own the end of it.
