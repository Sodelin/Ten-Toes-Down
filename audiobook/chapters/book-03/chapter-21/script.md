# 21. The Finish Line Was a Fucking Palace

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

Inez took the lead at the first turn. Cassius took it from her at the second. I took it from both of them at the third, because a nigga can appreciate romance without letting it control the standings. The Lamborghini climbed out of the harbor with the sea flashing behind us. Flags snapped above the road.

People crowded the balconies, shouting our names as we passed. Mine came loudest, but I could hear Inez's too, and Cassius's, and once Nero's from a man who saw the silver car and forgot which generation had come back to race. The engine settled beneath my hands. No passenger this time.

Shawna's seat held only the blue handkerchief she had given me before the boat race. I had folded it carefully and tucked one corner beneath the belt so it wouldn't fly away. Nigga, a man can drive alone without leaving everybody behind. The first miles ran through vineyards. Low stone walls. Green hills. Sun on the hood.

Sabine pressed hard on my left, her yellow car taking the turns with clean, patient precision. Adrian came up behind her. Inez stayed close to Cassius, then split away on the long bend and passed him with a laugh over the race channel. Try to finish that sentence before next year, she told him. Nigga, I'm busy. So am I.

I smiled and took the next turn faster. At the old fort, the road narrowed beneath an archway. I went through first. Sabine followed. Behind her, a black car came out of a side entrance wearing no number and no interest in the rules. Here we fucking go. It struck Adrian's rear corner. His car slid toward the wall.

He caught it, swore and kept moving. Two more black cars emerged behind the first, engines roaring, windows dark. The duke's final team. Unmarked cars at the fort, I said into the channel. Odette answered immediately. We see them. Knox's voice followed. Keep your road, Captain. Nigga, I planned to. The first black car pushed between Sabine and me.

Its driver wore a helmet with a gold stripe. He looked over as he came alongside, and I could see the smile behind his visor. That nigga had been hired to be a problem. I could see him rehearsing the moment in his own head: the famous captain frightened, the crowd quiet, the duke nodding like he'd bought himself the correct nigga.

"You see him?" Inez asked over the channel. "I see the nigga." "He's looking at you." "Nigga should look where he's going." Cassius laughed. "You about to change where that nigga's going, aren't you?" "Depends how hard he asks." Sabine's voice joined us. "These niggas do this every race?" "Every beautiful race," Inez said. I hoped he had been paid in advance.

He moved toward me. I let him come. Then I braked just enough to let his front cross mine and turned behind him before he could correct. He struck the wall with his rear quarter, bounced back onto the road and found Sabine already gone past him on the other side. Nigga, she said over the channel, that was rude. You talking to me? No.

I'm learning your punctuation. I laughed and accelerated. The black car recovered. Came again. Behind us, Inez and Cassius boxed the second attacker away from Adrian, keeping him from being forced toward the drop. They didn't touch the black car. They gave its driver no clean space to become violent without losing his own road. He lost it anyway.

The car struck a low wall, spun and stopped across a gravel turnout. Its driver climbed out with both hands raised when Odette's pursuit car reached him. One down. Two still coming. The interior road ran through a pine forest. Sunlight broke in strips across the windshield. My speed climbed.

The gold-striped driver stayed with me, closer now, his car powerful enough to make arrogance feel supported. He showed me a gun through the side window. Nigga, that was his introduction. I answered with my siren. The blue lights flashed through the trees. He fired. The shot struck the road ahead of my front wheel.

I moved, felt the car slide, brought it back and saw the narrow service lane opening on my right. I took it. The black car followed. The lane led through an old stone winery courtyard and rejoined the race road above the next bend. Barrels lined one wall. A tractor stood beneath a canopy.

Two workers jumped aside as I came through, my siren giving them the only explanation they needed. The black car entered too fast. Its side struck the tractor's rear wheel. The machine spun across the courtyard in a long ugly scrape and stopped against a stack of empty barrels. The driver tried to reverse. Couldn't.

His gun hand came up again as I turned through the far gate. I fired once from my window. His windshield cracked in front of him. He ducked and dropped the gun. I kept going. Nigga, stay parked. At the rejoining road, Sabine flashed past. Then Adrian.

I came in behind them, still third, with Inez and Cassius close enough to make the race feel like a promise instead of a procession. The last unmarked car waited at the gorge. Not on the road. Across it. Beyond the car, the old stone bridge had been blocked by two service trucks. Men stood beside them with weapons visible.

The race officials on the near side waved frantically, trying to stop us before the road narrowed. Sabine braked. Adrian pulled beside her. I stopped behind them. The other drivers arrived and got out. For the first time all day, the engines sounded small against the space around us. Across the gorge, the palace road climbed toward the finish.

We could see its flags above the trees. The duke had put a wall between the race and the result. A man in a green coat stepped onto the bridge with a loudspeaker. By order of His Grace, the Grand Prix is suspended. Nigga, I said, he losing so hard he canceled geography. Inez came beside me. There's an old bridge below. I looked down.

A narrow stone crossing lay farther along the gorge, reached by a steep track cut into the hillside. It had been built for carts, not modern race cars. Ivy covered one side. A fallen branch lay across its far end. Cassius followed my eyes. No. Yes, I said. Aiden. Nigga, you keep saying my name like it means stop. Sabine looked at the track.

Then at my car. You're serious. Very attractive too. We got a list going. Adrian laughed once, sharp and disbelieving. The man on the bridge lifted the loudspeaker again. All drivers will remain where they are. I held up my badge. Captain Aiden. All niggas with borrowed guns can put them down. He stared. I got back in my car. The others followed.

We took the old track together. Slow at first. The slope was rough, the edge close. I kept the Lamborghini steady while stones cracked beneath the tires and dust rose around the hood. Above us, the duke's men shouted. One fired into the air. Odette's officers reached the main bridge behind them and answered with commands that sounded much more practiced than mine.

We kept descending. At the old crossing, I stopped. The stone bridge was narrower than it had looked from above. My mirrors had inches on either side. The branch at the far end was larger too, a whole fallen limb across the exit. I got out. Tank's voice came through the channel. Nigga, why you out of the car? Moving a tree.

Of course you are. I climbed onto the bridge and crossed on foot. The gorge opened beneath me, water bright far below. The old stones felt solid under my shoes. At the far end I caught the branch in both hands and pulled. It moved a little. Sabine got out behind me. Then Adrian. Then Cassius and Inez.

We lifted it together and rolled it off the road. No speech. No photograph arranged afterward. Five drivers who wanted to beat one another cleared the same bridge because none of us had come to let Aurelio choose where we stopped. I looked at them. Niggas ready? Inez grinned. Move your beautiful car. I did. The Lamborghini crossed first.

Slowly enough that every stone had time to judge us. Then Sabine. Adrian. Inez. Cassius last. As his silver car reached the far end, the duke's men appeared on the upper path. Gunfire struck the slope above us. We drove. The track climbed toward the palace road in a series of tight turns. Dirt flew. Engines roared.

I hit the paved road first and let the Lamborghini stretch beneath me, all the careful restraint of the gorge releasing at once. Ahead stood the final hill. Beyond it, the finish. The duke had one more idea. A helicopter rose from the palace grounds and hovered above the road, low enough for its wind to tear leaves from the trees.

A man leaned out with a weapon aimed toward the cars. Nigga had upgraded the weather. I saw the first flashes and drove beneath the line of trees. Branches struck the roof. The helicopter followed, its shadow racing beside me. Behind, the others spread out, making themselves harder to trap in one straight line. I called the thunder. One short burst.

Blue light ran through the wheel, into the dash, beneath the hood. The Lamborghini surged. The road came toward me faster, the car answering like a living thing that had waited for the last demand. I passed beneath the helicopter before the gunman could follow my movement. Ahead, a low stone ramp rose beside the road, part of an old loading terrace. I took it.

The car lifted. For a second the palace gates filled the windshield. Then the Lamborghini landed beyond the line of concrete barriers the duke had placed across the entrance, tires striking the courtyard road with a sound that made every guard turn. Blue lights flashed over white marble. My siren filled the palace. Nigga, the finish line had been trying to hide in a fortress.

I brought the whole police department to its door. The official race arch still stood in the courtyard, ribbons moving in the helicopter's wind. Lord Orlan waited beneath it with the seventh key in a velvet case, pale and sweating. The duke stood on the palace steps beside the stranger in the dark uniform.

Shawna was at the far side of the courtyard with my people. I saw her before I stopped. Just like she asked. I crossed the line first. The bells began ringing. The crowd outside the gates heard them and roared. Inez came through the opening the guards had finally cleared. Cassius followed. Sabine. Adrian. All five of us reached the courtyard alive.

I sat in the car for one breath while the thunder's cost rolled through my shoulders. One breath. Then I opened the door and stood. Shawna ran to me. I caught her with both arms, lifted her and kissed her in front of the palace, the drivers, the guards, the duke and every camera that had followed me across a month of his bad decisions.

Nigga, she whispered against my mouth, you did it. I looked over her shoulder at Aurelio. His face had gone empty. No smile. No threat. Nothing left to arrange between himself and what everybody had seen. I put Shawna gently on her feet. Then I walked toward the seventh key. Lord Orlan held it out. The duke drew a gun. Nobody moves, he said.

Nigga, the race was over. Now he wanted to meet me.
