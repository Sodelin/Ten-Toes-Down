# 10. The Strongest Man in the Room Met Me

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

The night after I won the Sea Crown, Mercedes and Porsche opened a club on somebody else's yacht. That somebody was Rocco Silvar. He insisted it was a temporary partnership. Porsche told him every partnership was temporary if he kept talking through the women explaining it to him.

The yacht had three bars, a glass dance floor and a bedroom upstairs whose ceiling opened to the stars. Rocco showed me the bedroom personally. Very popular, he said. With who? Guests. Nigga, that's a wide category. He spread his hands, smiling. We respect discretion. From downstairs, Mercedes shouted his name. His smile vanished. We also respect punctual payment, he added, and hurried away.

I brought Shawna flowers that night. Actual flowers, purchased without destroying the place they came from. She examined the bouquet like she suspected a car might be hidden inside it. No fountain? she asked. No. No injured driver? No. You just went to a shop? Nigga, I am a complicated man. She kissed me beside the yacht's rail while the party built around us.

The harbor shone. Music rose through the deck. Beyond the lights, Isla Gloria lay somewhere in the dark, holding men who should have been sitting at tables like ours. We hadn't forgotten them. We also hadn't promised the duke we'd stop living while we came for him. Mercedes's club was called Blue Hour.

The women working it had chosen their clothes, their prices and who they wanted near them. Some danced. Some hosted tables. Some took men upstairs and came back smiling at their phones rather than at the men. Nobody's passport disappeared into an office.

Porsche kept the door with two huge attendants and the calm expression of a woman who had made an excellent list of people she wasn't allowing inside. Tank stood at the bar beside Rosa. A woman in silver leaned toward him, touched his sleeve and asked if he wanted company. He pointed to Rosa. I got company. The woman looked at her.

Then back at him. Oh. Good company. The best. Rosa pretended not to hear. Failed so badly she had to look down into her drink. I nudged Tank. Nigga, you glowing. That's the lights. Nigga, them lights don't have feelings. Mind your marriage. My marriage upstairs choosing what to take from the stars. He laughed and pulled Rosa closer.

Cassius arrived with Inez half an hour later. No sling. Just a dark jacket and the cautious way he held his shoulder. She wore red and carried the silver horse cane because, she said, it had grown on her. They weren't holding hands when they came aboard. They were by the time they reached our table. Nobody said a word for almost three seconds.

Then Tank leaned forward. Nigga. Cassius closed his eyes. Please don't. Nigga, your hand got a girlfriend. Inez burst out laughing. Cassius tried to keep his face straight and lost immediately. Shawna kissed Inez's cheek. You look happy. I am annoyed in a new direction. Cassius lifted their joined hands. Very committed annoyance. We drank to that. Rosa sang first.

The band gave her something slow and deep, a rhythm that moved through the floor before anybody decided to dance. She stood in the light with one hand on the microphone, looking at Tank while she sang about a man who kept arriving at her door pretending he had forgotten something. Tank looked at me. Nigga, that ain't about me. Of course not.

I really left my jacket. Seven times? Different jackets. When the song ended, Rosa walked straight off the stage into his arms. He lifted her without thinking. The whole club cheered, and for once the man who could carry a car looked embarrassed by the attention. Then Mercedes announced the evening's guest. Bruno Bastion.

Thirty-eight years old, six feet eight, shoulders like a doorway, champion of a dozen strength contests whose trophies looked too small in photographs because he was holding them. He came aboard wearing a sleeveless black shirt, white trousers and a gold belt large enough to keep a small nation together. Rocco had hired him for an exhibition. Guests could challenge him at the strength table.

Anybody who beat him would receive a gold bar and a year's membership at the Coral Crown. Bruno looked around the club and smiled. Who wants to try? Four men did. Four men regretted it. The first lasted two seconds. The second lasted one. The third tried using both hands and lost more publicly.

The fourth sat down, looked at Bruno's arm and decided he had always preferred dancing. Bruno laughed with him, not at him. I noticed that. Then he saw me. Captain Aiden. Nigga, you know me? I watched the boat race. Which boat? The one where the car flew out of it. Oh. Regular boat race. He laughed. A good big sound. Come sit down.

The crowd began chanting before I moved. Shawna looked at Bruno's arm. Then mine. Then my face. You're going to do it. Nigga, they got a chair ready. Aiden. I can't disrespect furniture twice in one night. Bruno tested the table with one hand. "This thing sturdy?" Rocco nodded. "Very." Tank looked at me.

"Nigga, they still believe in furniture." "Let the niggas believe," I said. "We ain't touched it yet." Bruno grinned. "You always bring this many niggas to watch?" "Nigga, they follow the noise." Rosa pointed at Tank. "That nigga makes half the noise." "Supportive noise," he said. Shawna rested her hands on my shoulders.

"Win clean." "Nigga, I bathed." "Your mouth didn't." Bruno laughed so hard we had to wait for him to get his arm back on the pad. I sat opposite Bruno. Up close, his hand was enormous. He wrapped it around mine carefully, checking that I was ready instead of trying to surprise me.

That made me like him more, which was unfortunate for his record because now I wanted him to have a memorable defeat. No powers? he asked. No thunder. Good. No excuses? I asked. He grinned. None. Porsche stood beside the table. Ready? Bruno nodded. I nodded. Go. The table creaked immediately. Nigga was strong. Not famous strong. Not gym-picture strong.

Actual strength that arrived all at once and expected the world to make room. His forearm tightened. His shoulder rolled forward. My elbow pressed into the pad, and for half a second my hand moved toward the table. The crowd screamed. Tank slapped both hands over his head. Nigga, don't let that mountain take you! I looked at Bruno. He looked at me.

We both smiled. Then I pushed back. Slowly. His eyes changed first. Then his breathing. Then the grin disappeared because his face had found more urgent work. I brought our hands upright, held them there for a beat and heard the whole club go quiet enough for the band to stop playing. Shawna stood behind me. Come on, baby. Nigga, that was unfair to Bruno.

I put his hand down. The table broke. Not exploded. Not shattered into a weapon. It simply gave up beneath the amount of greatness it had been asked to host, one leg folding, the top dropping, both of us standing at once to keep from landing in our own victory. For a second Bruno stared at the broken wood. Then at his hand.

Then he threw his head back and laughed so loudly the people on the neighboring yacht started cheering without knowing why. You are a fucking monster. Nigga, I'm a husband. Shawna came around the table and kissed me. The club lost its whole mind.

Bruno took off his gold belt and put it over my shoulder before Rocco could explain that it wasn't part of the prize. I gave him my gold wheel from the harbor qualifier in return. Marcellus had fixed the clasp. It could handle a big nigga. Now you raced, I told him. He held it up, laughing. Now you lifted.

We became friends over broken furniture. It happens. Later, Shawna went onstage. She asked for the pianist to stay, then looked toward me. Aiden. I froze with my drink halfway to my mouth. Tank saw my face. Oh, nigga. What? She called you by the whole name. Nigga, that's the only name. She used all of it. Shawna held out one hand. Come here.

I went. The pianist stood up and offered me the bench. I looked at him. Nigga, don't get generous. Shawna leaned close. You played for me in Vegas. That was a room. This is a bigger room. I sat. I knew enough piano to make a woman stay beside it. I did not know every song in the world.

My wife chose one I knew, and when I touched the first chord, she began singing before I could get distracted by the fact that an entire yacht had decided to watch my hands. Her voice settled over the notes. I followed it. That was the trick, if you can call loving somebody's voice a trick. I didn't try to outrun her.

Didn't fill every space because I was afraid the room might notice silence. I gave her a place to step, and she stepped into it so beautifully that the people closest to the stage stopped holding their drinks halfway through their mouths. Then she changed the melody. Looked at me. Dared me. Nigga, I caught it.

The pianist, standing beside the stage, put both hands on his head. Shawna laughed into the next line. I answered her with a bright run of notes that surprised me enough to make me grin. She came closer, one hand on my shoulder, singing now like we were alone again, except three hundred people were finding out what alone with her could sound like.

When we finished, nobody moved for a breath. Then the room stood. Bruno lifted his new gold wheel above his head. Tank shouted so hard Rosa covered one ear. Mercedes climbed onto a chair. Porsche kept filming, steady as a woman who understood the value of proof. Shawna bent and kissed me at the piano. You keep surprising me, she whispered. Nigga, I got to.

You married me. Her eyes shone. Upstairs, later, with the ceiling open and the stars above us, she laid her head on my chest and listened to the sea. You liked them cheering, she said. Yeah. I liked them cheering too. I looked down at her. For you? For us. She traced the edge of the cross at my chest. DBM. Damn, boy. Magnificent.

Shawna had put those ridiculous words on Dante's cross years before I knew either of them, and now the gold lay warm against my skin like the dead nigga had handed me a compliment with instructions to keep earning it. Do you ever get tired? she asked. Of you? Of needing to be the greatest thing in the room. I considered lying beautifully. Didn't. Sometimes.

She kissed my chest. You can sleep in this room. So I did. For three hours, nigga, the fastest king alive didn't go anywhere. At dawn, somebody knocked. It was Bruno, now wearing a shirt large enough to suggest he had borrowed a sail. Beside him stood Lark, holding a message written on the back of a shipyard meal ticket. Mateo's handwriting.

She was smiling and crying at the same time. He got my letter, she said. He knows we're coming. I took the message. Most of it was for her. Ordinary things. Their apartment. The plant he had finally bought instead of flowers. The way he missed her swearing at the kettle. At the bottom he had added a line for me.

The duke is moving the old captains before the final race. There is a road under the opera house. Celadon says the third key opens more than a trophy. I looked at the jeweled sea key on the bedside table. Bruno folded his huge arms. I heard you need help on an island. I smiled. Nigga, bring a bigger table.
