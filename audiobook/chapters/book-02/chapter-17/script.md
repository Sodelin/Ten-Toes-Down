# 17. Blue Thunder Looked Good on a Nigga

Single narrator: the funeral ticket man. Verbatim chapter body.

I drove off the broken bridge because the bridge had stopped being useful. Nigga, I gave that bridge several chances. The Lamborghini left the stone ledge and landed on a maintenance platform hanging below it. The suspension slammed hard enough to make my teeth click.

Shawna caught the handle over her door and shouted something that began with my name and ended with a detailed opinion of my judgment. I agreed with the passionate parts. Ahead, a blue service lane flickered between the platform and the stadium's outer ring. It was narrower than a proper road and less confident about existing.

The Heart's unstable field kept making pieces of it fade. I watched the light pulse. Waited one breath. Then drove. The wheels caught the lane at its brightest point. We raced through open air while the path went dim behind us. Below, clouds climbed toward the academy's falling edges. Above, Voss's aircraft crossed toward the central tower. Shawna stared through the windshield. Aiden.

I see it. You see all of it? Nigga, I'm trying to focus on the helpful portion. The lane ended short of the stadium ring. I didn't brake. The Lamborghini flew the gap and struck the ring road with its front wheels first. The rear swung wide. I corrected, straightened, and heard a roar from the field below. People had seen us. People were cheering.

Nigga, I needed those people to evacuate, but I understood the interruption. Knox looked up from beside a helicopter and put both hands on her head. That's my fucking captain, she said over the radio. Pike answered from the control entrance. He's technically ours this week. Commander, don't get greedy. I took the last turn toward the central chamber.

Voss's aircraft had landed through its glass roof. Not on it. Through it. Broken panels lay across the terrace. Smoke rose from the aircraft's nose. He had abandoned any interest in returning school property in good condition. Shawna and I got out. Inside, Storm stood between Voss and the Heart. Her hands were white with restrained lightning.

She looked exhausted enough to fall but remained exactly where she had chosen to stand. Behind her, Bell's assistants sheltered near the wall. Lena crouched beside one whose leg was trapped under a fallen beam. She was speaking in a calm voice that belonged entirely to her now. Voss raised his staff. You cannot hold it forever. Storm's eyes burned.

You keep mistaking exhaustion for agreement. He struck. White light met blue. The room shook. I ran toward the trapped assistant while Shawna moved around the opposite side. Together we lifted the beam enough for Lena to pull him clear. The assistant looked at me, recognized me, and began thanking me while he was still being dragged across the floor.

Nigga, thank me in the hallway. Move. He moved. Voss saw me. His face did something beautiful and ugly at once: astonishment because I had arrived again, hatred because apparently arriving was a talent he could not buy. What are you? I stepped into the open. Consistent. He turned the staff toward me. Shawna fired. The staff broke near its head.

Blue force burst sideways into the Heart's housing. Glass cracked. The core's light flared so bright every shadow in the room vanished. Storm threw both hands toward it. The surge struck her and drove her to her knees. Bell's voice came through the radio. The transfer conductor is open. Get away from the housing. Voss's armor began drinking the loose current.

He smiled through the pain. The nigga was still trying to win with a mouthful of the disaster he had made. Then the ceiling support above Shawna began to fall. I saw it. Saw her look up. Saw the distance between us.

There was no time for a clever route, no car, no faculty member with a gift ready to do the part my body could not reach. I ran. The broken transfer conductor lay across the floor between us, black metal wrapped in blue light.

It was part of Voss's attempt to bond a human body to the Heart's charge, a system he had forced Bell's designs into and Reiss had warned him not to use. I stepped over it. It caught my leg. Current tore through me. Nigga, I had never known pain could be a color. Blue filled my hands. My chest. My mouth.

Every muscle locked, then opened into a terrible fast clarity. The falling beam seemed to move slowly enough for me to see the chips of paint leaving its edge. It wasn't slow. I was moving. I reached Shawna. Caught her around the waist. We crossed the room in a flash that left blue fire crawling along the floor behind us.

The beam struck where she had stood. I hit the wall with her in my arms and fell to one knee. The light went out. For a moment I could not breathe. Shawna held my face. Aiden. I tried to answer. Nothing came. My hands shook violently. Tiny blue threads moved beneath the skin, then faded. The old wound still hurt.

My bruised ribs still hurt. Whatever had happened had not repaired a single fucking thing. It had simply made me do something impossible and sent the bill directly to my body. Storm stared at me from beside the broken housing. Voss stared too. Bell arrived at the doorway with Miriam behind him and stopped so abruptly she nearly walked into his back.

You bonded with the conductor, he said. I finally found enough air for words. Nigga, was there a cheaper option? Shawna laughed once, half relief and half terror. Then she pulled me against her so tightly my ribs filed another complaint. Voss raised his armored hand. That power is mine. Tank came through the doorway behind Bell. He looked at my glowing fingers.

Looked at Voss. Nigga, ain't nothing about this man ever belonged to you. He fired. Voss ducked behind the Heart's damaged housing. Storm used the moment to pull the loose current back into a contained arc. Bell and Miriam reached the emergency braces. Shawna helped me stand. My legs felt like somebody had replaced the bones with hot wire.

Victor arrived through the roof opening and looked straight at me. He could feel it. I saw that in his face before he spoke. You didn't have that before. Nigga, I had several other advantages. He smiled despite everything. Of course you did. Voss tore a black coupling from the housing and leaped back toward his aircraft. The core itself remained inside the emergency braces.

He had taken a concentrated store of charge, enough to keep his armor alive while the Heart continued supporting the school. The aircraft's engines whined. Tank fired at its rising nose. Victor forced it away from the open roof with a burst of lightning. Voss escaped through the gap anyway, his damaged craft dragging sparks into the morning air. The stadium shuddered again.

Bell shouted from the braces. We have to reset the outer support ring. Now. The western section is breaking loose. I looked toward the windows. A quarter of the stadium's outer structure had begun sliding away from the island. People crowded the stairs leading off it. A helicopter hovered too far away to reach everyone before the stone separated. My Lamborghini waited on the terrace.

Blue threads flickered in my fingers. Shawna saw both things at once. Can you control it? Don't know yet. Then listen to me. One thing. One burst. You don't spend yourself like you're infinite. I looked at her. The word infinite had never sounded more like a trap than it did in her mouth. I nodded. One thing.

Bell showed us the outer support arch from the window. Its emergency return point sat along the stadium road, just beyond the widening gap. A powered vehicle could carry a short charge across it and wake the local field long enough for the engineers to secure it. Yesterday that would have meant Victor's car.

Victor's car had burned because Voss wanted to turn a man into fuel. Today my hands glowed. I wasn't going to let Voss decide what that meant too. I got into the Lamborghini. Shawna got in beside me before I could start an argument we had already settled a hundred ways. Tank leaned through my window. Nigga, you got lightning now? Little bit.

That nigga was already too much. They gave that nigga electricity. Victor stood behind him, smiling with exhausted wonder. Blue Thunder, Shawna said. We all looked at her. She touched my hand on the wheel. That's what it looked like. I smiled. Baby, you naming my greatness? Somebody has to keep it organized. I started the engine.

The new charge moved when I wanted it to. Not easily. Not painlessly. I felt it gather in my arms, felt my heartbeat grow hard and quick, felt the Lamborghini answer with a low blue shimmer across its dark paint. Then we moved. The car shot along the stadium ring faster than it had ever moved under its own engine.

The world sharpened around the windshield. Every broken edge, every loose stone, every opening in the road seemed to arrive with just enough time for my hands to answer. The gap opened ahead. I held the wheel. Blue light crossed the hood. We jumped. For one brief burst the car carried my charge through the empty space between the two pieces of road.

The local field caught it, flared, and held. The Lamborghini landed beneath the support arch in a thunderclap that rolled across the stadium. The sliding section stopped. People on the stairs caught one another instead of falling. The engineers' lights turned green. My hands went dark. So did the edges of my vision. Shawna caught the wheel while I fought to keep my head up.

She guided us into the safe bay beside the arch and brought the car to a stop. I leaned forward, breathing hard enough to hurt. One burst, she said, holding the back of my neck. I nodded. Could not speak yet. Below us, the stadium began cheering. Above us, Voss's aircraft circled back toward the tower.

Inside my car, my wife kept her hand on my neck until my breathing slowed. Blue Thunder had arrived. Nigga, I still needed a minute.
