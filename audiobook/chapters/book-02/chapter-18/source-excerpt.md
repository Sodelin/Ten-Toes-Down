For the first minute after Blue Thunder, my greatest achievement was keeping breakfast inside my body.

Nigga, nobody had put that part on the poster.

Shawna held a bottle of water to my mouth. My hands were still shaking too hard to trust with it. Through the windshield I could see students crossing the restored stadium road, helping one another toward the helicopters. Every few steps somebody looked at my car and raised a hand.

I raised mine back.

The fingers trembled.

That did not make the wave less important.

Tank reached us first. He leaned through the window, saw my face, and lost the joke he had brought with him.

“How bad?”

“Nigga, electricity got a terrible cancellation policy.”

He looked at Shawna.

She shook her head once: tired, hurt, awake.

He understood.

Victor landed beside the car and sat on the curb without pretending he had meant to sit there. His blue uniform was torn at one shoulder. The glow around his hands had faded to a faint pulse.

“The first time your body holds a charge,” he said, “it feels like it should be able to hold all of it.”

I looked at him.

“It can't,” he said.

“Nigga, my stomach already submitted that finding.”

He smiled tiredly.

“Then listen to your stomach.”

Above the tower, Voss's damaged aircraft circled once. Smoke trailed from its left engine. The coupling he had stolen from the Heart glowed through the cockpit like a little captive sun.

Bell spoke over the radio. The newer support chamber was stable. The stadium's western ring was holding. Miriam had isolated the old tower's first two locks. One remained, and Voss still controlled it through the stolen coupling.

“We have to separate him from it,” Bell said.

Tank looked up.

“Nigga, separate how thoroughly?”

Bell paused.

“Enough that he stops using it.”

“Now that's a specification I can respect.”

The aircraft descended toward the stadium.

Voss was not fleeing.

He had seen people cheering beneath my car. Seen his guards stop firing. Seen the academy move at the sound of my voice instead of his. The nigga could have left with a flying machine and a stolen store of power. It wasn't enough. He wanted the people who had refused him to watch somebody take me apart.

He landed on the stadium's central platform.

The place where they handed out trophies.

Some men never understood how thoroughly location could insult them.

His aircraft's canopy opened. He stepped out in the black armor, the coupling fixed against his chest. Blue force moved around him in ugly jerks. The staff was broken, but he had turned the remaining length into a handle for the charge.

“Aiden,” he shouted.

His voice carried through the stadium speakers, still linked to the old tower.

People stopped moving.

I sat up.

Shawna put her hand against my chest.

“One more does not mean unlimited.”

I covered her hand with mine.

“I know.”

“Say what you're actually going to do.”

I looked down at the field. At Voss standing in the center of a school he had nearly destroyed because it had stopped admiring him.

“Finish with help.”

She nodded.

Then she opened my door.

Tank took the driver's seat. I moved into the passenger side. Shawna climbed onto the rear platform of a nearby rescue cart with Victor, and Petra joined them from the stairs. Knox's officers kept the evacuation lanes clear. Rook and the men who had abandoned Voss put their weapons down in a visible line and helped the last spectators leave the lower seats.

Voss saw them.

“Traitors.”

Rook looked up at the platform.

“You dropped our bridge.”

“I gave you purpose.”

“You gave us a long fall.”

Tank started the Lamborghini.

“Nigga, even his employees reviewing him badly.”

We drove down the stadium access road toward the field. The tires rolled over grass as green and perfect as if nobody had spent the morning trying to turn it into wreckage. I looked at the finish arch from the race. It stood above the far entrance, bent but intact.

Two days ago I had arrived beneath it in first place.

Today I intended to leave the same way, even if I had to let my brother drive.

Voss lifted his armored hand.

Tank swerved before the blast struck. Blue force tore a long groove through the turf behind us. The Lamborghini slid sideways and came around facing the platform.

“Nigga got no respect for landscaping,” Tank said.

“Harlan gonna be furious.”

Voss struck again.

Petra stepped in front of the rescue cart and took the edge of the blast across her marble shoulders. It drove her back three feet. Mercedes caught the cart before it rolled into the stairs. Victor sent a brief answering bolt at the coupling, making Voss turn away from them.

I got out of the Lamborghini.

Voss smiled.

“At last.”

“Nigga, you been looking at me all week.”

“Without the car.”

I glanced at Tank.

He revved the engine.

“Car still here,” I said.

Voss lunged.

He crossed the platform in one enormous step and struck down with the broken staff. I moved sideways, slower than I wanted, fast enough to live. The blow smashed the edge of the trophy pedestal.

Gold-painted stone burst across the grass.

I picked up a broken piece and hit him in the face with it.

His head snapped back.

Tank shouted through the open window.

“Nigga used the award!”

“It's educational.”

Voss hit me across the shoulder. I fell onto the platform steps, caught the rail, and dragged myself up before he could strike again. Pain flashed through my ribs. I had no fresh body waiting under the damaged one. Blue Thunder had not given me a replacement.

He saw the weakness and came closer.

That was useful.

Shawna moved behind him.

Lena's voice suddenly rang across the stadium, clear as a bell.

“Voss.”

He turned toward the sound, searching the seats.

Her gift carried the word from one speaker to the next, moving his attention away from the woman approaching his blind side. Rosa stood beside her in the upper gallery, one hand on her back. Two singers, no longer reaching through prison walls, using the whole open sky.

Shawna reached the coupling.

She struck its exposed latch with the broken staff head she had recovered from the support chamber.

The black casing shifted.

Voss spun toward her.

I caught his arm.

Current tore through my hand.

Blue woke beneath my skin.

This time I knew it was limited. Knew the pain that came after. Knew I was choosing a few seconds, not becoming a god with the whole afternoon available.

I spent them on his hand.

The charge moved through me and into the ground, a hard bright arc that made my teeth ache. Voss's grip loosened. Shawna pulled the coupling free of its chest bracket.

The armor went dark.

I went down on one knee.

Shawna ran the coupling toward Victor and Bell's waiting transport case.

Voss followed her with a gun drawn from inside his coat.

Nigga, after all that machinery, he still came back to the same ugly decision every other coward had made in front of my wife.

I drew first.

“Drop it.”

He froze.

For a moment the stadium held its breath.

The gun remained in his hand.

He looked at me kneeling on the grass, shaking, bruised, tired enough that staying upright had become work.

He thought he had found the part of greatness that could be outwaited.

Then he looked at Shawna.

His weapon moved.

I fired.

Voss fell backward against the broken pedestal.

The gun dropped from his hand.

He tried to rise once.

Failed.

I kept my weapon steady until Knox reached him with two officers and Vega came behind them. The doctor knelt, checked, and looked up at me.

One small shake of her head.

Finished.

The chancellor was dead.

The Heart's last old lock opened under Bell's control. The stadium settled with a long, deep sound that ran through the ground beneath my knee. Above us, the towers stopped leaning. The clouds resumed their proper position below the windows.

For a second nobody cheered.

We listened to the school stay in the air.

Then the sound began.

From the upper gallery. From the evacuation stairs. From the helicopter crews hovering beyond the rail. From the people who had been hidden beneath the academy and the people who had arrived through a door that would never open again.

Aiden.

Shawna reached me before the chant could become too large to hear her voice inside it.

“Look at me.”

I did.

“You done?”

I looked at Voss. At the steady towers. At Tank climbing out of my car with tears in his eyes and a grin too big to hide them.

“Yeah.”

My wife put both hands on my face.

“Then come back to me.”

I tried to stand.

My legs said no.

Tank caught me under one arm. Victor caught the other. Between them they hauled me upright while the stadium roared louder at the sight of us together.

“This nigga finally heavy,” Tank said.

Victor laughed, breathless.

“He has always been heavy.”

“Nigga, emotionally ain't a lifting category.”

Shawna walked in front of us, making room, keeping her eyes on mine every few steps. I could not produce one more spark. Could barely lift my hand to wave. Every joint hurt. Every old injury had returned to remind me it had paid rent before the new power moved in.

But the sky stayed up.

My people stayed alive.

And when we passed the broken race arch, Tank stopped and looked at the sign still hanging above it.

FIRST PLACE.

He nodded toward me.

“Nigga, even when we carrying him.”

I laughed so hard my ribs nearly ended the celebration.

They carried me through anyway.
