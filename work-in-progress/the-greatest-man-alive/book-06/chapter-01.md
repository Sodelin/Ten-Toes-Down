## 1. The City That Had Never Met Me

The first thing Emperor Zero sent to welcome me was a gold coffin strapped to a Ferrari.

The Ferrari came out of the desert with nobody driving it. I watched it through the windshield of my police Lamborghini while Shawna put on lipstick beside me. Behind us, Tank led a convoy long enough that the last car was still having a different morning. We had driven out of Midnight City with kings owing us money, queens waving from balconies, and my wife wearing sunglasses that cost more than my first apartment building. Now some nigga had sent funeral furniture down the highway.

"Baby," Shawna said, "is that for you?"

"Depends what's inside."

"A coffin, Aiden."

"Could be champagne. Rich niggas get weird with presentation."

The Ferrari slowed ahead of us. On its rear window, gold letters lit up.

WELCOME TO YOUR FINAL DESTINATION.

Shawna considered the message, rubbed her lips together, and snapped her mirror shut.

"Spelling's right. He's already smarter than the last one."

I pulled alongside. The coffin lid opened by itself. There was a suit inside, black silk with a white rose on the chest. A photograph of me stood where the head should have been. This nigga had chosen a bad photograph. I looked good in it, obviously, but it was from before I bought my current watch.

"He wants to bury me without my badge," I said.

"You'd make the other dead people nervous."

A loudspeaker crackled.

"Captain Aiden. Your journey ends here."

I leaned toward Shawna's window.

"Nigga, which here? It's a big desert."

A man appeared on the Ferrari's dashboard screen. White suit. Black gloves. Hair swept back like it had its own chauffeur. I knew Emperor Zero's face. Seeing it that small made him look like a man waiting for somebody to answer the doorbell.

"You have mistaken a succession of fortunate accidents for greatness."

"And you mistook a Ferrari for a mail truck. We all learning."

Shawna laughed so hard she had to hold the mirror again.

Zero looked directly at her. That stopped my laughing before it started.

"Mrs. Aiden. I have rooms prepared for both of you. Separate rooms, should you discover a more intelligent attachment."

My wife lowered her sunglasses.

"Nigga, look at your own wife."

"I have no wife."

"We can see that."

The screen went dark.

Behind us, Tank's voice burst over the radio.

"What that nigga want?"

"Me to die dressed like a waiter."

"Tell him you don't do afternoon funerals. Rosa got a headache."

I was still smiling when the hills beside the highway opened fire.

The first bullets knocked sparks from the coffin. The second burst took out the Ferrari's rear tire. Its beautiful ass swung across our lane with death sliding around on the roof, and Shawna caught my arm before I could turn too hard. A black shape came over the ridge. Then another. Six trucks were dropping toward us through curtains of sand.

"Aiden."

"I see them, baby."

"The coffee."

She lifted our cups out of the holders. I hit the accelerator.

We shot beneath the Ferrari's raised coffin lid. Wood split over the windshield. A sleeve from my funeral suit slapped the glass and blew away. I had been dressed for my own death for three seconds and already ruined the outfit.

Tank's Mercedes swerved around the wreck behind us. Rosa leaned out his passenger window with a pistol and a silk scarf holding her hair down. This woman had packed seven evening gowns for a desert war and was already defending all seven.

"Get your niggas off my luggage!" she yelled.

A bullet punched through a truck's mirror. Its driver ducked. Tank bumped the truck hard enough to push it sideways, and the other convoy cars poured past him.

I saw Vesper's car in my mirror, black and long, moving with the smoothness of a hearse that had decided to pursue a music career. Mercedes drove. Porsche was standing through the sunroof holding two phones and one gun. I did not know which item she considered most dangerous.

"Left side clear!" Porsche shouted over the radio. "And whoever keeps calling me from a blocked number, your timing is fucking disgusting!"

The road ahead ended at a bridge suspended over a dry riverbed. Beyond it rose the Last City.

Nigga, I had seen rich. I owned rich. I had slept in rooms where the curtains required their own employee. This city looked like somebody had robbed the sun and built a neighborhood with the evidence. Gold towers shone behind a wall of black stone. A casino shaped like a giant crown sat on a hill. A white palace floated above the center on enormous pillars, with highways running underneath it and planes banking past its windows. Even from miles away, I could see a waterfall falling off a hotel into a swimming pool big enough to have weather.

Shawna stared.

"I want that pool."

"Which one?"

"Yes."

The bridge began to rise.

It split in the middle, its two halves opening like a mouth. On the other side, armored cars appeared beneath the city's gate. Somebody had figured on trapping us between the trucks and the gap. It was an elegant plan for people who did not know who the fuck was driving.

"Tank, bring everybody close."

"How close?"

"Family close."

He understood. The Mercedes moved up behind us. The other cars tightened into two lines. Our enemies were still firing from the ridges, but the loose convoy had become a single glittering animal, and I was the head of that motherfucker.

Shawna looked at the bridge. Then she looked at me.

"You aren't glowing."

"I'm saving it."

"Good. I'd hate to marry a man who needs lightning every time he leaves the house."

I kissed her hand and shifted down.

A garage stood beside the bridge approach. Its roof was covered in solar panels and old racing signs. A woman in grease-stained white trousers ran out carrying a red flag. She had a silver braid, brown skin, and the kind of arms that could fix an engine or break the owner.

"Not the bridge!" she shouted. "The old spillway!"

She pointed right.

I had half a second to decide if the woman was saving us or selling us.

Then she threw a wrench through the window of a truck trying to ram the garage, and I liked her judgment.

"Everybody right!"

We dropped off the highway.

The Lamborghini hit the sloping concrete of the old spillway, bounced hard enough to make the badges on my uniform slap my chest, and tore downhill. Shawna's coffee left its cup in one perfect brown crown. She watched it land on my trousers.

"That's why I bought you two pairs."

Behind us, thirty engines roared into the riverbed.

A truck followed too fast. Its front end struck the lip of the spillway. It pitched forward, bounced twice, and rolled onto its side. Another stopped at the top. Rosa stood on Tank's passenger seat and fired until the niggas inside remembered an urgent appointment somewhere else.

The garage woman jumped onto a motorcycle and came down after us. She caught my side, one hand on the bars, the other pointing toward a narrow channel beneath the city wall.

"That tunnel goes through!" she yelled.

"How low?"

"Lower than your ego!"

"That don't tell me shit!"

She laughed. I liked her more.

We reached the tunnel with half the desert following us. It was an old service passage, wet at the bottom, with rusted gates standing open. My Lamborghini fit with inches to spare. Tank folded his mirrors against the Mercedes with a curse. The long black car scraped sparks down both walls.

Porsche's voice came through the radio.

"Vesper, your driver is exceptional!"

"I am aware," Vesper said.

"I meant me!" Mercedes snapped.

"I am aware of that also."

The tunnel bent. Daylight flashed ahead. I could hear something above us that sounded like thunder, but it was too regular, too heavy. Engines. Hundreds of them.

We exploded out of the tunnel into the middle of a parade.

Gold motorcycles. Red supercars. Black limousines with flags. A marching band scattered as my police Lamborghini slid across a boulevard and stopped beside a float carrying a fifty-foot statue of Emperor Zero. His statue held the world in one hand. Some nigga had given him very generous shoulders.

The whole street froze.

I got out of my car, coffee on my trousers, dust on my shoulders, and looked up at the statue.

"You build that yourself?"

Nobody answered.

Then Tank came out of the tunnel sideways and knocked the float's tow bar loose.

The statue started rolling.

A brass player screamed. The float drifted toward a fountain, slow and majestic, while six men tried to stop it by pushing the same side. They only helped it turn. Emperor Zero's golden ass hit the fountain wall. The world fell out of his hand and bounced down the boulevard.

Shawna stepped out beside me.

"Well," she said. "We're here."

An old woman on a balcony began to clap.

One clap. Then another. Then a hundred.

A man in a red racing jacket pointed at my badge.

"That's Aiden!"

Nigga, my name traveled down that street faster than the gold globe. People came out of shops. Waiters abandoned tables. Two women jumped off a stationary party bus and ran toward us waving champagne. Somebody started a chant. The marching band, having survived, began playing something triumphant without being asked.

I had entered Zero's city through a drain and received a parade he had already paid for.

"Captain!" a young man yelled.

He was grown, broad-shouldered, maybe twenty-six, wearing a chauffeur's cap under a motorcycle helmet. He reached me holding a bouquet that had probably been intended for a royal mistress.

"My brother saw you race in Monaco. He said you made Cassius Chrome look like he was parking."

"Cassius is my nigga. Don't disrespect him."

"Sorry, Captain."

"But your brother got eyes."

The man laughed and thrust the flowers into my hands. I passed them to Shawna. She smelled them, looked at the fountain, and smiled like a woman deciding where her new city ought to put the furniture.

The garage rider stopped beside us and removed her helmet.

"June Mercury," she said. "Thirty-two. Mechanic. Owner of that garage your enemies tried to turn into a fucking window."

I shook her hand.

"Aiden."

"Nigga, I know."

Tank came up behind me, looked at June, then at the motorcycle.

"You the one put us in that little-ass tunnel?"

"You the one driving a house?"

Rosa stepped out of the Mercedes and pulled him away before he could fall in love with arguing.

Vesper emerged from the long black car. The applause changed when people recognized her. It got deeper, less surprised. Some bowed. Some said her name as if they had not expected to say it without a guard hearing. She stood beside Shawna and let the city look.

"You know these people?" I asked.

"Some. The others know stories."

"Good stories?"

"Ask after lunch."

A siren shrieked from the far end of the boulevard.

Black armored cars turned the corner. The crowd broke apart, doors opening, hands pulling friends into shops. The band stopped in the middle of a note. Zero's men spread across the street beneath flags with a silver circle on them.

June took one look and swore.

"Ruin's guard."

"Who the fuck is Ruin?"

"A man who thinks engines should kneel."

"That nigga's about to have a terrible day."

The lead armored car stopped. A tall man stepped out wearing a white driving suit and black sunglasses. There was a silver slash through one eyebrow. He carried no visible gun, which meant he either believed in conversation or had enough niggas around him to outsource it.

"Captain Aiden," he said. "Roland Ruin. His Imperial Majesty welcomes you."

I pointed at the wrecked statue.

"Tell him thanks for the ride."

His jaw moved once. He looked past me at Shawna, Vesper, the convoy, and the people hiding behind shop windows. I watched him count the cost of shooting in front of all those witnesses.

"You will accompany me to the palace."

"We need breakfast."

"The emperor does not wait."

"Then the emperor should've eaten before we got here."

Behind me, Tank made a noise so disrespectful it should have required a second mouth.

Roland took off his sunglasses.

"You have a reputation. I suggest you enjoy its final hour."

"Nigga, I been enjoying it since yesterday. You late."

His men raised their weapons.

Mine raised theirs.

For a second the whole boulevard held still. A flower petal slid off Shawna's bouquet and landed on my shoe. June's motorcycle ticked as it cooled. Somewhere inside a shop, a childless wedding display kept playing a love song to mannequins while forty grown men considered dying outside it.

Shawna touched my wrist.

I knew that touch. Not fear. A request. There were people in those shops, people who had clapped for me before knowing if I could keep them alive.

I took my badge off my chest and held it up.

"Roland. Look at this."

He did.

"You know what this means?"

"It means Las Vegas has poor judgment."

"It means I'm on duty. And right now you're obstructing my honeymoon."

"Your honeymoon?"

"It's a long marriage. We take breaks."

I stepped forward.

His men tightened their grips. Mine moved with me. Roland's expression changed, just a little, when he understood that I was going to walk right up to him with a whole war waiting behind my shoulders.

"You can have your palace meeting," I said. "Tomorrow. Tonight my people eat, sleep, and wash the desert off. Your niggas follow us into our hotel, I'm arresting every last one of them in front of whatever emperor you're renting your spine to."

"You cannot arrest an imperial guard."

"I never attended college either. Life's full of surprises."

June laughed before she could stop herself.

Roland looked at her. I moved half an inch, enough to take his eyes back.

"Me, nigga. We talking."

He held my stare. Then he smiled, slow and cold.

"Tomorrow, then. The emperor enjoys a guest who remains entertaining."

"Buy him a television."

He put his sunglasses on and stepped into the armored car.

They withdrew without firing. The crowd did not immediately return. People waited until the last black vehicle vanished before they dared believe we had stayed alive.

Then the old woman on the balcony clapped again.

I looked up.

"Ma'am, you started all this."

She threw me a white handkerchief.

"For the coffee, handsome!"

Shawna caught it first.

"Thank you! He needs supervision!"

June led us toward the Crownless Hotel, an enormous sandstone place built around a courtyard full of date palms and black cars. Its owner came running down the steps in slippers, kissed Vesper's hand, shook mine twice, and offered us the entire top floor before learning how many niggas we had brought.

When he learned, he offered the second floor too.

"Captain," he said, looking at my Lamborghini, "would you permit us to display your arrival photograph in the lobby?"

"Make sure my good side in it."

He stared at me, honestly troubled.

"How would I choose?"

Tank bent over laughing.

I liked this fucking city.

June caught me before I reached the elevator. She had washed her hands, but black grease remained under her nails. In the lobby light I could see a scar on her chin and a tiny gold engine hanging from her neck.

"My garage," she said. "You should know what that was."

"A garage. I been to one."

"My father's. Then mine. Zero wants the ground under it for another road to his palace. He offered me a room in a hotel and a job repairing his cars."

"You tell him no?"

"I told him where to park the hotel."

"That's my kind of negotiation."

She smiled, then stopped. "My partner Adrian is still there. He won't leave the lifts. Says nobody steals a man's wrench while he's breathing."

I turned to Tank.

"Send two cars. Bring him and whatever he can carry."

"The nigga's gonna try to carry a lift," June said.

"Then send three."

Tank put his phone to his ear. Just like that, Adrian had more friends than he had possessed five minutes earlier.

June looked at me as if I had done something strange.

"You don't know us."

"I know you put my wife through a tunnel instead of a grave. That's plenty."

For the first time since the road, she had no answer. She rubbed the little engine between her fingers and looked away at the lobby fountain.

The hotel owner approached with a velvet box. Inside was a gold room key the size of a dagger.

"The imperial suite, Captain. We have renamed it."

"Already?"

"The engraver was having lunch downstairs."

AIDEN'S SUITE shone on the handle.

"Nigga," Tank whispered, "we been here eleven minutes."

I took the key. "People recognize quality."

June shook her head, smiling now with all her teeth. "If you live through this, I want to build you a car."

"If you build it right, living through shit comes included."

She held out her greasy hand again. I shook it again. The hotel owner watched a black mark land on his golden key and looked so honored I thought he might frame that too.

Our suite looked over the boulevard and, beyond it, the emperor's palace. Shawna walked through the rooms with the flowers in her arms. She checked the bedroom, the bath, the balcony. Then she came back to where I stood and opened my shirt.

"Any new holes?"

"Only emotional ones. That nigga picked the wrong funeral suit."

Her fingers found the old scar in my side. She kissed my chest above the cross and stayed there for a moment.

Outside, our people were unloading bags. Tank was arguing with a bellhop about whether a gun case counted as luggage. Rosa wanted a piano. Mercedes wanted a better parking space. Porsche wanted whoever had called from a blocked number to have the courage to do it again.

My wife looked up at me.

"One last city."

"Then Vegas."

"Then the wedding."

"Then you."

"You already have me."

I held her tighter.

"Nigga, that's how I know I'm winning."

She laughed against my mouth, and I kissed her while the emperor's palace glittered outside like a place that had never learned what happened when a man came for his people.

Down in the street, somebody had dragged the fallen gold globe to the hotel entrance.

They had painted my name across it.
