## 1. First Night in Vegas

The night I met Shawna, I had cocaine on my shirt and another man's blood inside my watch. Nigga, I was twenty-four years old, and I hadn't owned a car since the last one caught fire outside my ex-boyfriend's apartment. Before sunrise I would steal three million dollars, take a woman every killer in Vegas wanted back, and catch a bullet that would stay in me for the rest of my life.

But a nigga had to survive the parking lot first.

First I had to get jumped in.

Big T took me behind the club while the bass was still shaking the walls. There were twelve niggas waiting by the cars. Blue rags. Gold chains. Guns under their shirts. I knew some of them from the block, but nobody was smiling now. This was family business, and I was the last motherfucker without a seat at the table.

T put his hand on my shoulder.

"Once a nigga in, that nigga in. You understand me?"

"Nigga, I came here to understand."

"Niggas say that. Summer ends, the first body drops, suddenly the nigga got somewhere else to be."

"Nigga, I said I'm here. You need me to stand closer?"

He hit me so hard I went against the side of a Cadillac.

Then every nigga came at once.

I caught a fist in the mouth and an elbow behind my ear. Somebody kicked my leg out. I went down on the hot concrete, got up, went down again. Blood ran into my eye. I couldn't see which nigga I was swinging at. I just kept swinging. I had been waiting my whole life for somebody to tell me I belonged somewhere, and if this was what it cost, I was paying it. Take the whole fucking payment.

Tank caught me under the ribs. Big nigga, twenty-eight, arms hanging out his shirt like he'd been born carrying furniture. I grabbed his shirt and drove him backward into a car. The alarm started screaming. He was twice my size, but that night there wasn't a motherfucker alive who was going to make me stay down.

"Get this nigga off me!" Tank shouted.

Nobody moved. I heard Big T laughing behind me. That laugh put another man's strength inside my hands. Nigga, I could've carried Tank into the sky.

I hit him until T pulled me off.

"Enough! Look at this nigga. Twelve of you and the nigga asking for more."

I stood there with my fists up. My hands were shaking so hard I couldn't open them.

T looked at the blood on my face. Then he took a gold chain from his pocket and put it over my head.

A little cross hung against my chest.

"My brother wore that," he said. "Real nigga."

I looked up at him.

"Where my nigga at, then? I should thank him."

"Dead."

Nobody said anything.

"Nigga, don't make me bury you too."

I closed my hand around the cross. I would've walked through fire for that man right then. I would've put a gun in the devil's mouth if T told me the devil owed us money. Nigga, I would've collected interest.

"Not this nigga," I told him.

He kissed my forehead like a father and pointed toward the club.

"Come get your money. Niggas, make room for my son."

Inside, everything was blue light and smoke. The back room was packed with niggas counting stacks of cash. Bricks of cocaine covered a glass table. Bottles of Hennessy stood between them. Three women in diamonds were drinking with a man who had two pistols tucked into the same belt. One of them lifted her glass when she saw my face.

"Damn, baby. You survive a war?"

"Yeah," I said. "And I won."

They cheered. Somebody pushed a bottle into my hand. Somebody else handed me a blunt already burning. I drank, smoked, wiped the blood off my lip, and sat down beside a pile of money bigger than anything I had ever seen in my life.

"This the nigga beat Tank?" one of the women asked.

"That's the nigga," T said.

She pushed a folded bill into my shirt pocket. A thousand dollars. I'd been back inside for ninety seconds and a woman was paying me to be handsome with a broken face. That's the kind of night it was.

Tank came in carrying a bag of ice against his jaw. He took the bottle out my hand, drank, and gave it back.

"Nigga, who taught you to fight dirty like that?"

"Same nigga taught you to fight unconscious."

The room went crazy. Tank stared at me long enough I thought we was going again. Then that big nigga started laughing too, blood on his teeth, and pulled a chair up beside mine. We drank from the same bottle. I would've trusted him to hold my heart while I fought with both hands.

T threw me the keys to a black Mercedes.

"That's yours."

I looked at the keys.

"Nigga, these keys real? You serious?"

"West side need a nigga. West side got you."

An hour earlier I hadn't even been in the crew. Now the west side was mine. That's how fast life moved when you were ready for it. Some men spent their whole lives waiting on a chance. I took mine with blood still coming out of my mouth.

T opened a photograph on his phone.

A woman stood beside a swimming pool at night. Black dress. Diamond earrings. Dark hair over one shoulder. She wasn't smiling. Behind her, the whole Strip was lit up, but I was looking at her.

"Shawna," T said. "Twenty-six. Works for Red."

"Nigga, working for Red doing what?"

"Whatever Red wants."

Tank watched me from across the table.

T kept talking.

"She's at the Saint tonight. Penthouse. She's bringing a case down at eleven. You take her and the money to my house in Henderson. That's all you gotta do."

"Why she leaving him?"

"Because she finally got smart."

I looked at the picture again.

"Nigga, does Red know about this little family pickup?"

"If he knew, we wouldn't be having this conversation."

T gripped the back of my neck and pulled me close enough that nobody else could hear.

"Don't fall in love with her."

I laughed.

"I'm serious. Look at me."

"Nigga, look at me. Do I look like the nigga falls in love?"

He let go of me.

I believed that shit when I said it. Nigga, remember that. I meant it with my entire chest.

***

The Saint had a gold fountain outside and bulletproof glass on the top floor. Red owned the penthouse, the nightclub underneath it, and enough people in the building that nobody asked questions when his men dragged somebody into a service elevator. Nigga had built himself a palace where the carpets could swallow a scream.

I parked the Mercedes right in front.

The valet looked at the blood drying on my collar.

"You need somebody to call a doctor?"

"Nigga, my car needs you first. Nothing happens to it."

I gave him a hundred and went inside.

Upstairs, the penthouse was going crazy. Music pounding. Cocaine all over the tables. Niggas gambling with money that was still wrapped from the bank. Hookers in dresses that cost more than the cars outside, laughing with men who never gave their real names. Everybody was rich. Everybody was armed. Every nigga thought the night belonged to him.

A man in a white fur coat was doing coke off a trophy. It was September in Vegas. I didn't know what the trophy was for, but that nigga had brought it with him and was guarding it like the whole room wanted his championship.

I knew a woman by the bar, Mercedes, thirty, who had once left me naked in a hotel room and taken my shoes because I'd called her by her sister's name. She looked down at my new keys.

"You finally got a Mercedes you can afford, baby?"

"Come see it."

"Nigga, you still owe me flowers."

I gave her five hundred out the money in my pocket. "Buy the whole fucking garden."

She kissed my cheek. "You bleeding on the good side of your face."

"Both sides good, baby."

She laughed, and I left her with it. I was shining. I had a dead man's gold against my chest, a living man's keys in my hand, and no idea how much that shit could cost.

I had been with men and women all over that city. Big men who put their guns down gentle when they came into my room. Women who left lipstick on the champagne glasses and somebody else's diamonds on my pillow. I thought I knew every kind of trouble a beautiful person could bring into a room.

Then I saw Shawna.

She stood by the window with a cigarette in one hand and a silver case beside her ankle. She wore the same black dress from the picture. There was a little scar above her eyebrow. Somebody was talking to her, but she was looking straight at me.

I walked over. My mouth hurt, my ribs hurt, and every other woman I'd ever wanted fell clean out of my fucking head.

"T sent me."

"I know."

"How?"

She looked at the cross on my chest.

Her cigarette stopped halfway to her mouth.

"Where'd you get that?"

"T."

"Tonight?"

"Yeah."

She reached out and turned the cross over.

On the back were three little letters I hadn't noticed.

D. B. M.

Her fingers tightened around it.

"You know what those mean?"

"No."

She let it fall against my shirt.

"Drive before you find out."

I took the case. She took my hand. We walked through the party together while men who had been smiling a second earlier started reaching for their phones.

At the elevator, one of Red's guys stepped in front of us.

"Nigga, where the fuck you taking her?"

"With this nigga right here. You need my picture?"

"Nigga, I'm asking her."

I hit him in the throat. He folded against the wall, and I shoved him down before he could get his hand inside his jacket. Shawna pressed the elevator button. The doors opened. Nigga was still looking at me like he couldn't believe I'd touched him. I stepped over his shoes.

Another man came around the corner.

She drew a little silver pistol from her purse and pointed it at his chest.

"You get one chance," she said.

He stopped.

The doors shut between us.

She didn't put the gun away until we reached the lobby.

I looked at her.

"Niggas chasing you out of every party, or I got the special one?"

"Only the ones I survive."

I was already in love with her. I just didn't know how bad it was going to get.

***

The first car came after us before I had learned where the fucking seat warmer was.

Black Charger. No lights. Two men in it and a third leaning across the back seat with something long in his hands. Shawna saw them in her mirror before I did.

"Aiden. Don't look."

Naturally I looked. Nigga, I had owned a Mercedes for eleven minutes. I was checking everything that got close to it.

The first shots came through the rear glass.

Shawna dropped against the silver case. I ducked and the steering wheel jerked under my hands. We clipped a fountain with the right fender, threw white water over three gambling executives, and hit the street with the horn stuck screaming.

"Your niggas?" I shouted.

"You think I'd ride with you if those were my niggas?"

"You met me tonight!"

"I'm trying to meet you tomorrow! Drive!"

I drove.

A yellow Ferrari came sideways out of the next street and joined the Charger. The man driving the Ferrari had an expression like he'd paid extra to kill somebody comfortably. He came level with my window and smiled while his passenger raised a pistol.

I looked at Shawna.

"Baby, move your hand."

"What?"

"The horn."

She lifted her wrist. The screaming stopped. For one beautiful second all I could hear was that Ferrari and my own heart getting ready to become somebody's problem.

I turned toward the yellow car.

The passenger's face changed first. His gun went up. The driver tried to go with it. Yellow metal struck a street pole, and the Ferrari spun across a valet entrance spraying sparks over a hundred thousand dollars' worth of men's shoes.

"Nigga!" Shawna said.

"That's one."

"That's a Ferrari!"

"That's one Ferrari. Keep up."

The Charger stayed on us. I could see the long gun moving behind its windshield. Then a red convertible shot out of a hotel drive between us, and Mercedes stood up in the passenger seat with her heels in one hand.

"I told you about my fucking flowers, Aiden!"

Porsche was driving. Twenty-nine, dark red hair, red dress, eyes fixed on the road as if she had personally laid it. I'd met her once at a party where a rich man had bought her a sports car and cried when she actually drove away in it. She had kept both the car and his apology.

"Sit down, Mercedes!" she shouted.

"He owes me!"

"Nigga, he owes the whole street right now!"

Mercedes dropped as the red car swept across the Charger's nose. The black car braked hard enough to lift at the back. Shawna watched the women go by with her mouth open.

"Your friends?"

"Good people."

"Your good people have terrible timing."

"Look behind us."

She looked. The Charger was trying to pass Porsche. Porsche wouldn't let it. Every time its nose appeared, that red convertible slid over with her, carrying two furious beautiful women who had decided nobody was dying until their night got better.

Shawna laughed once. It was the first happy sound I'd heard from her. It went through me harder than the gunshots.

Then the Charger hit their rear bumper.

Porsche's car swung. Mercedes grabbed the windshield frame. I saw her heels fly into the air, one silver shoe catching a streetlight before it disappeared into somebody's outdoor dinner.

I braked.

"Aiden!"

"Not leaving them."

The Charger filled my mirror. I could see the driver now, big white scar across his eyebrow, teeth showing as he tried to decide whether he wanted me or the women more.

"Nigga, choose me," I said.

I dropped behind the red car, and the Charger hit us instead.

Metal folded. My head struck the seat. Shawna's hand found my arm and held. The Mercedes kept rolling, its rear bumper screaming along the ground, and Porsche caught her car before it reached the wall. She lifted one finger without looking back. Not thank you. Something stronger.

The boulevard opened ahead. A transport truck crossed the intersection carrying a silver speedboat high above its cab. Beside it, the delivery ramp of a closed hotel pointed up toward a black ribbon of roof.

I looked at the ramp.

Shawna looked at me.

"No."

"You don't know what I'm thinking."

"Nigga, I know exactly what you're thinking."

"Then hold the case."

We hit the ramp so hard the sound disappeared.

My stomach went cold. The street fell below the windshield. For a second the whole fucking Strip lay under us, beautiful and useless, all those men spending their lives trying to buy a view while I flew through theirs with a woman on my arm and a three-million-dollar suitcase on her knees.

Shawna screamed my name.

I would've stayed in the air forever to hear it like that.

We cleared the speedboat by less than the height of its flag. The Mercedes landed on the far side with a crash that drove my teeth together. A tire exploded. The car leaned, caught, kept moving. Behind us the Charger tried the same turn too late and went sideways into the truck's empty rear trailer.

I heard the collision through our broken windows.

Shawna twisted around. A black wheel rolled through the intersection by itself.

The nigga wasn't following us anymore.

I slowed. My hands stayed steady on the wheel, though my whole chest was jumping. Porsche came alongside at the next light. Mercedes had one shoe left and looked ready to beat God with it.

"You jumped a fucking boat!" Porsche shouted.

"Had to get over it somehow."

"Aiden, nigga, you jumped the boat with a car!"

Behind her, a man on the sidewalk started clapping. Then the couple beside him. Then a whole row of people waiting for taxis, strangers putting down luggage to salute the greatest driver Vegas had ever fucking seen.

I lifted my hand.

Shawna caught it and put it back on the wheel.

"They can admire you moving."

Mercedes pointed at her. "Keep him. He's expensive, but nigga, he comes with a show."

Porsche blew me a kiss and turned away, her convertible carrying one silver heel and two women who'd just made themselves family. I took us toward the desert on a damaged tire and the kind of pride a man could drive forever on.

Shawna reached across and touched my cheek.

"You could've kept going when they hit those girls."

"Wouldn't have been me."

She watched me another second. Then she put her hand on my leg and left it there.

Nigga, I could feel that hand all the way down to the tire.

***

We got out past the last casinos before she told me to pull over.

"T's house is farther out," I said.

"I know where his fucking house is. Pull over."

I took the Mercedes off the road behind a shuttered wedding chapel. Half the sign was burned out. The parking lot was empty. Shawna opened the case on her lap.

Money filled it from one end to the other.

"Three million," she said.

I stared. Three million fucking dollars. That afternoon I'd been eating a gas-station sandwich in a borrowed car. Now this woman was opening a fortune in my Mercedes.

"T said—"

"T says a lot."

She took an envelope from beneath the cash. Inside was a photograph, bent across the middle.

Shawna stood in front of the same chapel, three years younger, wearing a white dress. A man stood beside her with his arms around her waist.

The cross on his neck was the one T had put on mine.

"Dante," she said. "His brother. My husband."

I looked from the photograph to her face. My nigga, I'd been carrying her dead husband against my chest and talking like the whole world started when I walked in.

She took the cigarette from behind her ear, tried to light it, and couldn't get the lighter to work.

I did it for her.

She watched the flame for a second before she leaned into it.

"I was twenty-three when I came out here. Thought I was going to sing. I had a suitcase, eight hundred dollars, and a number some girl gave me in Phoenix."

She looked out at the chapel.

"The number was Valentino's."

I knew the name. Everybody did. Valentino ran women through half the hotels on the Strip. He wore white suits and had a diamond in one of his teeth. Niggas laughed around him like he was funny. Women watched his hands.

"He bought me clothes. Got me a room. Told me he had people in music. By the time I knew what the fuck was happening, everything I had belonged to him."

She blew smoke against the windshield.

"Dante came into the club on my birthday. Everybody else wanted something. He asked what kind of cake I liked."

Her mouth moved like she was going to smile.

She didn't.

"He came back with it the next night. Carrot cake. Whole fucking cake. Put it on the table between the bottles. Valentino laughed at him. Dante knocked out that diamond tooth."

"My nigga," I said before I could stop myself.

"That's what I said." She looked at the photograph. "That's exactly what I fucking said."

She wiped one eye with her thumb, angry that she had to.

"We got married here six weeks later. T stood beside him. Red paid for the room. They all smiled in the pictures."

I looked at the gold cross in my hand.

"What happened?"

"Dante took money out of a deal. Three million. Told me we were going to leave. Said he had a brother, so he didn't have to be scared."

She turned the photograph over.

There was writing on the back.

*Damn, boy. Magnificent.*

"That's what the letters mean," she said. "Damn, boy. Magnificent. He looked in the chapel mirror after we got married and said it like God had just finished him. I laughed so hard I nearly dropped my flowers. Next morning I had it put on the cross."

"Nigga was right about you."

"He was talking about himself."

I looked down at the gold against my chest.

"Then the nigga finally got it on the right neck."

She laughed before she could stop herself. One bright, startled sound in the middle of all that hurt. She covered her mouth, still holding the photograph, and looked at me like I had just brought something she thought was gone into the car with us.

She held the photograph against her chest.

"They left him in the desert. Valentino brought me his watch. Told me Dante died begging for somebody to take care of me."

I wanted to kill a man I had never even met. Nigga was already dead, and I wanted another turn.

"What did you do?"

"Waited."

Her voice got quieter.

"For eight months, I waited. Valentino thought I came back because I had nowhere else to go. He thought I loved him by the end."

She looked straight at me.

"Last Tuesday, I shot him in his car. He was talking when I did it. He kept talking after the first shot, so I shot him again. Then I took Dante's watch out of his glove compartment."

She showed me the watch on her wrist.

It was too big for her.

"Red took me in. Said he'd protect me. Tonight I took the three million back."

"And T?"

"T says he's going to get me out of Vegas."

I thought about the way he'd told me not to love her. Nigga knew what she was carrying besides the case.

I thought about the cross.

"You believe him?" I asked.

"I believed his brother."

She put the photograph inside her dress and closed the case.

Then she leaned across the seat and kissed me.

I forgot about the money. I forgot whose car I was driving. All I knew was her hand against my face and the taste of smoke on her mouth. When she pulled back, she was still holding on to me. My nigga, I could feel her trying to decide if I was another room she couldn't leave.

"Don't take me to that house," she said.

"Then this nigga ain't taking you. Give me somewhere else."

Just like that. My first night in the family, and I was ready to go to war with every fucking one of them.

***

I took her to a motel outside town and paid for the whole top floor.

The clerk looked at the stack of money in my hand.

"All six rooms?"

"All six. Nobody comes up."

Upstairs, Shawna put the case on the bed and her gun on the nightstand. I locked the door. We could hear trucks on the highway and somebody arguing with a woman in the parking lot below.

She unbuttoned my shirt to look at what Tank had done to my ribs.

"Jesus, Aiden."

"I'm good."

"You're bleeding."

"I've been bleeding all night."

She got a wet towel from the bathroom and cleaned my face. Every time she touched the cut on my mouth, she said she was sorry. Every time, I told her not to be.

I took off the cross and put it in her hand.

She started crying then. Hard. Like she'd been holding it in since the day somebody handed her that watch.

I held her. I didn't know anything worth saying to a woman who had carried that much pain into a room, so I just held her until she stopped shaking. Nigga, I was ready to threaten God for putting her through it. There wasn't anybody else left in the room to threaten.

Then she asked me to stay.

"I'm here, baby."

"Tonight. I mean tonight."

"I'm right fucking here."

She pulled me down to kiss her. I could taste salt at the edge of her mouth. All that blood and money downstairs, all those niggas looking for us, and she wanted my hands on her. I let the shirt fall off my shoulders. She shut the lamp off herself.

We spent the night together in that room with the money on the other bed and our guns within reach. Afterward, she lay against my chest while the blue light from the motel sign moved across the ceiling.

"What do you want?" she asked.

"You."

"You don't know me."

"Then tell me more."

She told me about the songs she'd wanted to sing. About her mother in Phoenix, who thought she was working in a restaurant. About a house Dante wanted near the water, even though neither of them knew how to swim.

I told her we'd buy it.

I told her I'd take her anywhere she wanted.

I had known her less than three hours. I meant every fucking word. Give me a country and I would've put her name on it. Give me a year and I'd have fought every man in it to keep her.

Then my phone lit up.

T.

I let it ring.

A picture came through.

My Mercedes outside the motel.

Then a message.

*Come down, son.*

***

T stood beside the empty pool with Tank and two niggas I didn't know.

He wore the same shirt he'd been wearing when he brought me in. There was still a little of my blood on the cuff.

I came down the stairs with my hands where he could see them.

"Where's the money?" he asked.

"Upstairs."

"The girl?"

"Upstairs."

He nodded slowly.

"Nigga, one thing. I gave you one fucking thing to do."

"She told me about Dante."

Tank looked away.

T kept looking at me.

"Dante stole from his family."

"Nigga, he was your brother."

"Then the nigga should've known better."

The words hit harder than the first punch.

I had wanted there to be something else. A lie. A misunderstanding. Some way he could say he hadn't done it and I could let myself believe him.

He didn't even try.

"That woman put him in the ground," T said. "Now you standing here ready to follow him."

"You put him in the ground."

T stepped close.

"Nigga, who made you? Who put you in that car?"

I could smell his cologne. The same cologne I'd smelled when he put the chain around my neck.

"I gave you money. A car. A fucking name in this city. You think she would've looked at you without all that?"

I didn't answer.

"She's upstairs with my three million. That's what she's in love with."

Behind him, Tank shifted his weight.

T saw me notice.

"Last chance. Go get her."

Upstairs, a door opened.

Shawna stood on the balcony holding the silver case.

"You want your money, T?"

He looked up.

She threw the case over the railing.

It hit the bottom of the empty swimming pool and burst open. Cash flew across the cracked blue tiles.

"There," she said. "Now tell him what I love."

T pulled his gun.

I hit his arm as he fired.

The shot shattered a window above us. Tank grabbed me from behind. One of T's men came in swinging, and I caught him with my forehead. My vision went red. I twisted free and drove Tank backward against the pool fence.

Shawna fired from the balcony.

The man beside T went down.

T shoved his gun against my side.

I felt the shot before I heard it.

For a second, I couldn't breathe. I couldn't feel the ground. I had blood running under my shirt and T's face right in front of mine.

"Should've listened," he said.

I grabbed his wrist with both hands and held on.

I wasn't letting that motherfucker point the gun at her. Nigga could empty it into me first.

Tank hit me again. I barely felt it. I drove my knee into T, tore one hand free, and got the pistol out of my waistband. My nigga, I loved him. Right up until that second, I still wanted him to let go.

I fired.

T fell against the fence.

I fired again.

He went down with his hand still reaching for me.

All the music in my head stopped.

Tank stared at him.

Then he looked at my gun.

"Aiden—"

"Nigga, back—Tank, back the fuck up!"

He did.

Shawna came down the stairs barefoot, still holding the little silver pistol. She saw the blood on my shirt and ran to me.

"Baby. Oh, fuck. Baby, look at me."

I tried to tell her I was good.

Nothing came out.

She pressed both hands against my side and looked at Tank.

"Get the fucking car!"

Tank looked down at T one more time.

Then he went for the Mercedes.

That's how the west side became mine. With a dead father at my feet and another man's wife holding me together.

***

We left the money in the pool. All three million. Not a fucking dollar could've bought me another minute standing there.

Shawna drove with one hand on the wheel and the other holding a towel against me. The sky was getting light over the desert. Behind us, Vegas kept shining like nothing had happened.

"Stay with me," she kept saying.

"I'm here."

"Keep talking."

I told her about the house. About the water. About how we'd have a room just for her music. I told her we'd learn to swim. I said all that shit with blood in my mouth because she was crying again, and I couldn't stand it. Nigga, I was bleeding through a towel and promising waterfront property. She kept asking about the windows. I kept telling her they'd face the sunrise.

At the edge of town, she pulled over long enough to put the gold cross back around my neck.

"Don't," I said. "That's his."

"I know."

She closed my fingers around it.

Then she kissed me and got back behind the wheel.

I watched the sun come up beside her face. My phone wouldn't stop ringing. T's people. Red's people. Niggas I'd never met calling to ask who was running things now.

I answered the third call.

"Aiden," I said.

On the other end, somebody went quiet.

Then he said, "What you want us to do?"

I looked at Shawna.

She was watching the road, still wearing Dante's watch.

"Some nigga better find me a doctor," I said. "And tell everybody the girl is with me."

Ten toes down. All the way to the end.

## 2. A King with a Hole in Him

Nigga, the doctor asked Shawna who she was to me.

"His wife," she said.

Nigga, I tried to live harder when I heard that. I had been slipping in and out the whole ride, seeing my mother's kitchen and the motel swimming pool, hearing Big T call me son like he still had the right. But I heard wife clear as a church bell.

"Name?" the doctor asked.

"Shawna."

"His name."

"Aiden. His name is Aiden."

"Let go of him, Shawna."

She wouldn't. They were trying to move me onto another bed and she had both hands caught in my shirt. Her black dress had gone brown where my blood dried. One of her earrings was missing. She was barefoot in a hospital that smelled like flowers and cost more every minute than my old apartment cost every month.

"Baby," I said.

She looked down.

"Don't let them cut my chain."

She took it off me herself. Kissed the cross. Put it around her neck.

Then everything went dark.

***

When I woke up, a woman in a white coat was looking at me like she knew exactly how much bullshit I was capable of.

"Dr. Marisol Vega," she said. "You're alive."

"Shawna?"

"Sleeping."

"Where?"

She pointed.

Shawna was in a chair by the window, curled around my bloody shirt. Somebody had put hospital socks on her. She had my cross around her neck and Dante's watch hanging loose on her wrist.

"Don't wake her," I said.

"She'll wake herself. She has been doing it every eleven minutes."

I tried to sit up. Pain hit me so hard I forgot what I was trying to do.

"Fuck."

"Yes," Vega said. "That. You will be doing quite a lot of that if you continue."

I stayed down.

"Where's the bullet?"

"Still inside you. Removing it would add risks you do not need. We treated the damage. You need time."

I looked at the ceiling. Big T was dead and the nigga had still managed to move in with me.

"How much time?"

"More than you want."

"Niggas waiting downstairs, Doc. A nigga can't run a whole west side horizontal."

"They can keep waiting."

The man below looked up. He actually answered.

"Who you think?"

"I think your taste is fucking awful."

He didn't fire. Maybe his gun was empty. Maybe he thought a wounded man standing inside a coffin would finally understand the invitation.

He nodded.

"Nigga, use your words."

"Finished."

"Then stay fucking finished."

"Nigga was in my way."

She tilted her head.

"And that solved it?"

The gunman in the fountain groaned.

Knox smiled.

"Apparently."

"Aiden."

"Everybody's alive."

"You were in bed."

"Didn't have wheels."

Porsche lifted her hand. "I tried a chair."

"You saved my nurse."

"She saved my ass first."

"This nigga?"

## 3. The Palace of the Woman I Just Met

The club opened for me the following night.

"They waiting on you."

"Let them."

Inside, the whole west side had dressed like it was getting married to money. Women in diamonds. Men in velvet. Champagne sweating in silver tubs. Cocaine on mirrored tables in the private rooms. Hookers I knew from bad months wearing shoes that cost more than those months. Niggas who owed everybody suddenly buying bottles with both hands.

One of the dancers came down from a platform and kissed my cheek.

"Look at my nigga! Baby, you made it."

"We made it," I told her.

I put a stack of cash in her hand. She held it against her chest and went back up laughing.

Shawna saw. She moved closer.

At the back table, a pretty man in a silver shirt stood up when he saw me. I'd spent a reckless weekend with him the year before, broke and drunk and swearing on his balcony that one day I'd own the view.

"Look at you," he said.

"You too."

He kissed the corner of my mouth before I could turn.

Shawna's arm went stiff through mine.

"Nigga, come here. Congratulations," he said. "Always knew you had something."

"Nigga, he still has it," Shawna said.

He looked at her and smiled.

"Keep it, girl. Cost me a weekend's sleep."

We moved on. Shawna waited until we reached our table.

"How many niggas in here have you fucked?"

I looked across the room.

"Don't look around. Answer me."

"You want me counting niggas I only kissed?"

She hit my good shoulder with her purse.

"Nigga, I want you sitting your ass down."

I sat. Nigga, I had survived T, but I wasn't trying to survive her in those earrings.

My nigga, she sat sideways on my lap until my face told on me, then moved into the chair beside mine. Her hand stayed on my thigh. Every time some nigga approached the table, she pulled me closer by the collar before letting go.

I loved that shit. I had women flirting with me, men remembering me, the whole room learning which side of her I belonged on. Nigga, let them look. I'd spent my whole life feeling like people could leave me wherever they found me.

Then the music stopped.

Rosa stood onstage in the red velvet coat. Under it she wore gold from her throat to the floor.

"Somebody owes me a song," she said.

Shawna shook her head.

"No."

"I ain't said your name yet, bitch."

"Rosa."

The room started clapping. Rosa held out her hand.

Shawna looked at me.

"You set this up?"

"No, baby."

I meant it. My nigga, I would've bought her an orchestra and scared her right out the back door. Rosa just stood there with a microphone and waited for her friend.

Shawna got up.

The first few steps looked harder than the walk I'd made out of the hospital. She kept one hand against her stomach. When she reached the stage, Rosa kissed her cheek and gave her the microphone.

"You know the one," she said.

Shawna closed her eyes.

The piano started.

Nigga, I had heard people say a voice could break your heart. I thought it meant somebody sang pretty while you remembered somebody ugly. Then Shawna opened her mouth and I stopped knowing where I was.

She sang about leaving a porch light burning. About a car going past the house. About getting dressed for somebody who wasn't coming home. Her voice shook on the first line. By the second verse, men stopped pouring drinks. A woman by the bar put her face in her hands.

I watched that watch slide down Shawna's wrist when she lifted the microphone.

"Dante's song," somebody behind me whispered.

Nigga, I turned so fast my side lit up.

The nigga was staring at the stage. He hadn't said it to hurt me. That almost made it worse.

All these niggas were listening to my woman sing for her husband while my name glowed outside.

I drank what was in front of me. Then I remembered what Vega had said and put the glass down, furious with myself for needing a fucking doctor in my head while another man had a song.

Shawna opened her eyes during the last verse. She looked straight at me.

I couldn't tell which of us she saw.

When she finished, the whole club got up. Niggas banged on tables. Rosa cried openly. I stood too, slow and angry, clapping until my hands hurt.

Shawna came down smiling like a girl who had found something under the bed she thought she'd lost forever.

"Did you like it?"

I kissed her before I answered.

Long. In front of everybody.

"Beautiful," I said.

She touched my face.

"Why you looking at me like that?"

"Like what?"

Her smile faded.

"Never mind."

***

Later, a man from one of the cash tables grabbed Rosa by the coat when she told him she was done working.

"I paid," he said.

Rosa pulled away.

"You paid for last time."

He grabbed her again.

Nigga, I was standing before Tank got to me.

"Aiden," Shawna said.

The nigga looked over his shoulder, still holding Rosa. That was his mistake. Not knowing me could've been fixed. Holding on after he saw somebody coming was the part that put him on the floor.

I hit him once. Hard enough that I felt something pull under my bandages. Tank caught my elbow before I could hit him again.

"Nigga, whose hand you think that is? Take your fucking hands off her," I said.

The man wasn't holding anything now.

"You hear me?"

He nodded from the carpet.

Rosa pulled her coat straight. Her hands shook. She hated that we could see them.

"Get him out," I told Tank.

"You sit down."

"Get that nigga out first."

The room had gone still around us. All these niggas had wanted a new boss to buy champagne. Now they had one bleeding into a white suit because somebody hadn't let go of Shawna's friend.

Tank handed the man to two others and walked me into the back office. Shawna followed. So did Rosa.

"You dumb motherfucker," Shawna said.

She unbuttoned my jacket. A little red had come through the shirt.

"I'm okay."

"Nigga, you don't know another fucking sentence?"

Rosa stood by the door with both hands inside her coat.

"I didn't need—"

"I know," I told her. "I wanted."

She pressed her lips together. Then she came over, bent down, and kissed my head again.

"Stupid nigga," she whispered.

But she stayed beside me until Shawna got a clean shirt.

***

At home, the lake was black under the windows.

Shawna took off her earrings and laid them on the bathroom counter. I watched her unclasp the watch.

"Let me buy you one that fits," I said.

She stopped.

"A watch?"

"Yeah."

"I have one."

"It hangs off you."

"I like it."

"I can see that."

She turned around. The silence got ugly fast.

"Say it, Aiden."

I stood in the doorway, tired enough that my legs shook.

"That song about him?"

"Yes."

She hadn't even tried to soften it. Nigga, I would've taken one little lie right then. Just something I could lay down beside.

"You look at me when you sing to him?"

"I looked at you because you were there."

"I'm always there. You wearing his watch, my earrings, got his chain on my fucking neck—"

"You want it off?"

She reached toward the cross.

I caught her wrist.

We stared at each other.

Then I let go.

"Don't," I said.

She rubbed where my fingers had been. I hadn't hurt her. I could still see that I'd done something I didn't want between us.

"Nigga, what do you want from me?" she asked.

I looked down at the marble. I'd bought the whole floor and couldn't find a place to stand.

"I want one thing that's only mine."

Her face changed.

She came close and put her palm over my heartbeat, clear of the wound.

"This is you," she said. "I know who this is."

"Then why'd you say his name in the hospital?"

She closed her eyes.

I wished I'd kept it. Held it in until it killed me by itself. But it was out now, standing between us in our new fucking bathroom.

"I dreamed he was there," she said. "And I was trying to tell him you were hurt."

Nigga, I didn't know what to do with that.

She took my hand and led me to bed. We didn't settle it. She didn't put the watch away. I didn't take off the cross. She kissed me until I stopped asking questions, and when we lay down, she kept her head on my good side.

I woke once before dawn.

She was still there.

For that morning, that was enough.

## 4. Flowers for the Man I Killed

Nigga, Big T's coffin was blue.

Not dark blue. Not respectful blue. That deep candy blue you put on a car when you want other niggas to pull up beside you and hate their lives. Somebody had ordered gold handles. His picture stood at the front of the chapel, six feet tall, smiling over a room full of people who'd been scared to smile wrong around him.

Your nigga paid for all of it.

The flowers. The cars. The musicians. The lunch afterward that half the guests would be too nervous to eat. T had called me son, then put a bullet in me. I couldn't make those things fit, so I bought him the biggest funeral that place could hold.

Tank came downstairs at the house wearing black.

"Nigga, you ain't gotta go," he said.

"Nigga, I killed him."

"That's why you ain't gotta go."

I adjusted my cuff in the mirror. My hands weren't steady yet. The suit hid the bandages. The medicine made everything feel a half second farther away.

"He had nobody else?" I asked.

"Plenty people."

"People that loved him."

Tank looked toward the window.

I left the cuff alone.

"You did."

He kept looking out.

"Nigga, you can say it."

"He gave me work when I came out."

"Nigga gave me a car."

"Ain't the same."

"I know."

For a minute we just stood there, the two men who'd left T by a swimming pool, trying to get dressed for what came after.

Shawna came downstairs in white.

Tank turned.

"Nigga, oh, hell no."

She stopped on the last stair.

White dress. White coat. White gloves in one hand. Her dark hair pinned up. Dante's watch shining like she'd polished it with her rage.

"What?" she said.

"Nigga's funeral, Shawna."

"I know whose funeral it is."

"Nigga, you can't wear that."

She looked at me.

I could've asked her to change. Could've told her this was already hard enough without her walking in looking like she'd come to get married on his grave.

But nigga, she looked beautiful.

"Nigga, get the car before she decides on a veil," I told Tank.

***

My nigga, nobody knew what to do when we walked in.

Some niggas stood. Some stayed seated so hard it looked like work. A woman near the front started crying louder when she saw me. I didn't know if that was grief or hate. I kept going.

Shawna's hand was in mine.

Tank walked behind us.

Every nigga in that chapel knew the story had changed. T had sent me to collect a woman and some money. A week later he was in a blue box, I had his men at my back, and the woman was holding my hand in front of his face.

I didn't feel proud until somebody whispered that I shouldn't have come.

Then I felt proud enough to scare myself.

"Which nigga said it? Say it loud so I know who to look at," I said without turning.

Nobody did.

We sat in the second row. Shawna kept her knees together and looked straight ahead. I could feel her pulse through her fingers.

The preacher talked about a generous man.

That part was true. T had been generous. He'd put money in people's hands, found rooms for niggas who had nowhere to sleep, paid somebody's mother's bill without letting anybody say thank you twice. I knew men who'd lived on that kindness for years.

The preacher talked about a man who loved his family.

Shawna let go of my hand.

I looked at her.

Her eyes had gone flat. She was staring at the picture, and her white glove was balled so tight in her fist I could see a seam giving way.

I put my hand on her knee. She didn't move.

Then Tank got up.

He hadn't told me he planned to speak. He walked to the front carrying a folded paper, opened it, looked at it, and put it back in his pocket.

"T knew my mother," he said.

His voice sounded too big for the microphone.

"Knew she worried about me. Every time I came by, nigga made me call her first. Didn't matter what we was doing. Call your mother, Tank. Tell her you ate."

A woman near the aisle laughed through her crying.

Tank looked at the coffin.

"She died while I was inside. T paid for everything. Told me don't owe him for that."

He rubbed one hand down the front of his suit.

"I owed him for it anyway."

I could hear Shawna breathing beside me.

"He did some shit," Tank said.

The preacher shifted.

"Some shit I can't stand up here and fix for him. I ain't gonna try. But he picked me up when nobody was answering. And I remember that."

He stepped away from the microphone.

On the way back, he looked at me once.

I nodded.

Nigga, it hurt. I needed to let it.

***

Red had sent flowers taller than the coffin.

White roses, blue ribbon, his name on a card big enough to read from the aisle. He hadn't come himself. Every time the door opened, your nigga looked. I hated that he could make me wait for him at a dead man's funeral.

When people went up to the coffin, Shawna stayed seated.

"You don't have to," I said.

"I know."

Then she stood.

We walked up together.

T lay there wearing a dark suit and the ring that had cut my mouth. Somebody had made him look peaceful. I remembered him saying he'd made me. I remembered being happy when he said son.

Nigga, a dead man shouldn't be able to embarrass you. But I stood over him ashamed of how much I'd wanted him to mean it.

Shawna put a folded photograph on his chest.

I recognized the chapel in the background. Her wedding. Dante smiling with his arms around her. This was another print, with T still visible at the edge, one hand on his brother's shoulder.

"You can take your fucking picture with you," she whispered.

I thought she was done.

Then she bent close.

"He loved you. You knew he did."

Her voice broke on loved.

I looked away before she saw me watching.

Behind us, the preacher cleared his throat. I wanted to turn around and tell that nigga to swallow it. Let her have a minute. Let her tell one dead man what the other couldn't hear.

Shawna straightened.

I touched T's cuff once. I don't know why. Maybe because I couldn't touch his hand. Maybe because he'd never get to pull me close again, and some stupid little part of me wanted that settled properly.

"Nigga, all you had to do was let us go," I said.

Then I took my woman's hand and walked out.

***

Outside, Rosa waited beside the cars in black sunglasses and a coat the wind kept trying to take.

She'd come for Shawna. She said that three times to everybody who asked.

"I ain't crying for that nigga," she told me.

"Nobody asked you to."

"I know. I just want the dead to be clear."

Shawna leaned against her. Rosa wrapped the coat around both of them and let her stay there while the pallbearers brought the coffin down.

Mercedes and Porsche stood by the Mercedes with a tray of sandwiches between them. They'd come dressed for church and carrying enough food to keep a war from becoming rude. Leon had sent a new white Bentley with them because, according to the card, seeing the last one die had changed his feelings about ownership.

"That's love," Tank said, taking two sandwiches.

"That's a nigga with insurance," Porsche told him.

I saw the black SUV before the first shot. It came around the chapel too slowly. There was another one behind it. Both had dark windows. Both had men leaning forward as if whatever they wanted was about to become available.

Tank stopped chewing.

"Down."

The first bullet shattered the tray in Porsche's hands.

I caught Shawna around the waist and drove us behind the Mercedes. Rosa hit the ground beside us. Somebody screamed. Gunfire ripped through the chapel doors, and the beautiful blue sky filled with glass.

A pallbearer dropped beside the hearse. Rico. Thirty-two. I'd learned his name that morning because he'd told me the gold cuff links were a gift from his daughter, who was grown and living in another state, and he'd wanted me to notice she had taste.

He wasn't moving.

Tank fired over the hood. The sound went through my chest. Porsche pulled Mercedes down behind the rear wheel as another burst took the car's mirror off.

"Everybody inside!" I shouted.

The chapel doors were open, but the drive between us and them was full of bullets. A woman stood frozen halfway down the steps holding the hand of her seventy-year-old husband. He was trying to pull her down. She kept looking at Rico.

"Nigga, get her!" I shouted to Tank.

"You stay here!"

"I heard you the first time!"

He ran bent low across the drive. Shots tore the flowers off a standing wreath behind him. He caught the old man under one arm and pushed the woman ahead of him through the doors. The whole front of the chapel swallowed them.

The first SUV came farther into the lot. Its passenger leaned out with a gun.

I fired from beside the wheel. The man jerked back inside. The SUV clipped the fountain, turned, and struck the empty wheelchair someone had left for me at the bottom of the steps.

That pissed me off personally.

"That nigga just hit my chair."

Shawna had her pistol out. She fired twice toward the second SUV and pulled back beside me.

"You weren't using it."

"Still mine."

Across the drive, the hearse began rolling.

Its driver was on the ground behind the open door. I couldn't tell whether he was hurt or scared. The engine was running. The car moved forward, slow at first, down the slight slope toward the street. T's coffin gleamed inside it through the rear window.

The first SUV turned to follow it.

"They're taking him," Rosa said.

"Taking who?"

"T!"

For one second I couldn't believe any nigga alive had found a way to complicate that man's death further.

Then I saw the shooter get out of the SUV and run toward the driver's door of the hearse.

Shawna grabbed my jacket.

"Aiden."

"They don't get him."

"Baby, he's dead."

"Then he ain't driving himself home."

I kissed her once and went.

The driveway tilted under me. Pain shot across my ribs. Somebody shouted my name from the chapel doors, and somebody else shouted, "Look at that nigga," like the first person had been announcing a miracle and the second wanted everybody to see it before it left.

I hit the hearse as the gunman reached it.

He had one hand on the wheel. I caught his jacket and drove him against the open door. His gun went off into the sky. We slammed into the side of the moving car, my shoulder against the window, his elbow against my throat.

He was bigger than me and healthy. Nigga, that was his whole fucking argument.

I put my forehead into his nose.

He fell back. I shoved him clear and jumped into the driver's seat. The hearse hit the curb with both front wheels. Behind me T's coffin thumped against its restraints like the nigga had an opinion.

"You can shut up too," I told him.

The first SUV came after us.

I drove the hearse through the open cemetery gate across the road. Gravel burst beneath the tires. White stones flashed past on both sides. I kept to the broad center drive, flying between the trees while the men behind me fired through their own windshield.

The hearse's rear glass shattered. A gold handle on the coffin caught the light.

"Nigga, they're scratching your box."

I turned hard around the stone circle at the center of the cemetery. The hearse leaned. The coffin stayed where it was. I could hear the SUV behind us lose its grip on the gravel, tires screaming while its driver tried to copy a man who was presently doing the best driving at his own enemy's funeral.

We came out beside a second gate. A groundskeeper stood next to a watering truck, hose in his hand, mouth open. I lifted two fingers. He dropped the hose and lifted two back.

Respect knew what to do even when nothing else did.

I took the hearse onto the service road. The SUV followed, front bumper bent, one headlight gone. A second engine roared somewhere ahead of us.

For a second I thought they'd boxed me in.

Then the white Bentley appeared across the intersection with Shawna driving.

Her coat was off. Her white gloves were on. Porsche sat beside her holding her purse against the dash and grinning like she'd just gotten invited to the best part of her own fucking life.

Shawna turned the Bentley toward the SUV.

"Baby, don't—"

Too late. She struck its front corner and knocked it sideways across the road. The Bentley spun, white smoke pouring off the tires. Porsche's door flew open and slammed shut again. The SUV climbed the curb and hit a stone wall hard enough to throw its hood up like a surrender flag.

I braked the hearse beside them.

Shawna looked through her broken window at me.

"Nigga, your wife drives too."

We weren't married. I didn't correct her. I was busy falling in love with her for the fourth fucking time before lunch.

A man came out of the SUV with a gun.

I fired through the hearse's open door. He dropped behind the engine and his weapon clattered into the gutter. Another man tried to pull him back inside. Tank's Mercedes arrived from the far end of the road, and that nigga took one look at Tank getting out and raised both hands so high I thought he was asking God to pull him through the sunroof.

"Who sent you?" Tank shouted.

The man didn't answer.

Tank took another step.

"Nigga, this is the easy question."

"T had friends!"

I got out of the hearse.

"Then where the fuck were you when they passed the collection plate?"

The man's eyes widened. He looked from me to the coffin behind the shattered rear window.

"You killed him."

"And I'm burying him. You coming between those things again, you can ask him how he feels about it yourself."

He stared at the pistol in my hand. Then at the blood beginning to show through my shirt. Then at Shawna stepping out of the Bentley in a white dress with glass shining around her shoes.

"You don't stop," he said.

It came out almost admiring. Like he'd found the problem and hated that it was beautiful.

"Nigga, you should've asked somebody before you came."

Knox's cruisers arrived behind Tank. I hadn't called her. Half the city had. Men got put on the ground, guns got kicked away, and the chief got out with her eyes already on me.

"Please tell me that's an empty hearse."

"Family in the back."

She looked through the window.

"You brought the deceased into a pursuit?"

"Nigga wasn't walking."

Knox took off her glasses.

For three seconds I watched her try to decide whether she'd seen anything better in thirty years of police work.

Then she laughed. Loud. Hands on her hips, face turned toward the sky, laughing while the men in handcuffs looked at her like she had personally betrayed the law by appreciating me.

"Aiden," she said, "you are wasting your fucking talents in civilian clothes."

"You keep saying that."

"I'm going to keep saying it until somebody gives me what I want."

Shawna reached me. She had a shallow cut on her hand, nothing worse, and I kissed it before she could tell me the blood wasn't all hers.

"I asked you to stay with the car," I said.

"I did. I moved the car."

Porsche came around the Bentley and held out its keys to Tank.

"Tell Leon this one had a meaningful life too."

Tank looked at the crushed front fender.

"Nigga gonna start sending bicycles."

We brought T back to the chapel in the same hearse, with a police escort in front and Shawna in the seat beside me. People came out when they heard the engines. Somebody started applauding. A woman on the steps raised both hands and shouted my name so hard her hat moved.

I got out slowly. My side had finally started collecting everything I owed it.

The old man Tank had rescued came down the steps with his wife. She put both hands around my face.

"You brought everybody home."

I looked past her at the white sheet beside the curb.

Not everybody.

Rico was still there. Vernon, forty-one, had died inside the chapel after they carried him in. Two of my people gone while I was trying to keep one dead man from being taken again.

I held the woman's hands for a second, then let them go.

"Help me finish this," I told Tank.

We put our hands on T's blue coffin. Four other men came beside us. The funeral workers found their places. The musicians began the hymn again, shaky at first, then strong enough that the whole parking lot listened.

This time when we carried him to the waiting car, nobody stopped us.

***

They buried T later that afternoon.

I didn't go to the graveside. Vega was back at the house, furious enough that everybody found a reason to leave the room when she walked in. She examined me behind closed doors while Shawna sat on the bed, still in the dirty white dress.

"You were told to rest," Vega said.

"I was sitting at a funeral."

"You are bleeding in a house full of armed men. I am interested in what happened after the sitting."

I looked away.

She made arrangements for what I needed, repeated herself slowly enough to insult me, and left with the promise that I was going back under her care if my condition worsened. I believed her. So did Shawna. That meant I was trapped in my own fucking palace.

Two of our men were dead. Others were hurt. Tank told me their names and made me repeat them. I said I'd pay for everything. He said their mothers needed to hear a human being first.

So I called.

Nigga, there wasn't a thing on earth I could say that made that conversation right. I didn't try to make a speech. I told them who I was. Told them I was sorry. Stayed on the line through whatever they needed to call me.

When I finished, I threw the phone across the room.

Shawna watched it hit the wall.

"I want Red," I said.

"You don't know it was him."

"Who the fuck else, Shawna?"

"People loved T."

"Nigga, I paid for the funeral."

"You also killed him."

I hated hearing it in her voice. Hated that she could say something true while I wanted her angry beside her nigga.

"You defending Red now?"

Her face went cold.

"Say that again."

I didn't.

She stood up, took Dante's watch off, and set it beside me on the bed.

"You think I forgot what those men did?"

The watch lay face up between us.

"You think because I got a house and some fucking earrings I forgot where they left him?"

"No."

"Then don't talk to me like I'm sleeping through this."

She picked it back up. Her fingers were shaking at the clasp. I reached for her hand, and she let me help her fasten it.

"I'm scared," I said.

She looked at me.

Nigga, I'd rather have taken another punch than said that. But I'd seen her under that car, and I couldn't get the glass out of my head.

"I'm scared they get you while I'm laying here."

She sat down.

"Then stop trying to stand up every time somebody says your name."

I put my forehead against hers.

Outside the room, Rosa was yelling at Tank to eat something. He told her he wasn't hungry. She told him she hadn't asked for his fucking feelings about the sandwich.

Shawna laughed once, through her nose.

I held on to her.

***

Red's invitation came at sunset.

A man from the Saint left a black envelope at the gate. Tank brought it upstairs on a silver tray he must've taken from the kitchen. I stared at it.

"Nigga, why you serving me a threat?"

"Didn't wanna touch it more than I had to."

I took the envelope.

Inside was an invitation printed in gold. Breakfast at the Saint. Tomorrow morning. Penthouse. My name and Shawna's, together like we were anybody's idea of welcome guests.

There was another card behind it.

I read it once.

Then I gave it to Shawna.

She sat on the bed so suddenly I thought her legs had gone.

"What?" Tank said.

She didn't answer. She was staring at the handwriting. I watched her thumb move to Dante's watch and stay there.

Red had written one sentence.

*Dante didn't die for three million.*

## 5. Forty Million Reasons to Die

I went to see Red the morning after the funeral with a bullet in my body and a diamond pin covering the hole in my coat.

Tank drove. I sat beside him with the note open on my knee.

*Dante didn't die for three million.*

The nigga had written it in red ink. He wanted me looking at it all night, wondering what my woman hadn't told me. Nigga, I had done exactly that. I wasn't giving him the pleasure of knowing.

“You look tired,” Tank said.

“You look unemployed if you keep talking.”

He reached into the console and handed me the little bottle Dr. Vega had left for me.

“Take what she said to take.”

“Nigga, I heard the doctor.”

“Then listen to her.”

I put the bottle in my pocket. He was wearing black again. The nigga had carried a coffin yesterday and was driving me toward another one today. I looked at his hands on the wheel. There was a cut across his knuckles I didn't remember putting there.

At the Saint, two men approached my door before the valet could. I stepped out holding Red's note.

“Your boss sent for me.”

One of them put his hand out for my gun.

“Nigga, you can have the note.”

He looked toward the entrance. Red stood inside the glass doors in a cream suit, watching.

He lifted one finger. The men backed off.

“Aiden,” he said when I reached him. “Glad you came.”

“You would've been sad if I didn't?”

“I had breakfast made.”

“Then let's eat.”

He put his arm around me like we were cousins. I let him. Nigga, I wanted to know how close this motherfucker would come to a man who had killed his friend.

My nigga came close enough to squeeze my shoulder.

Before the waiters brought the food, Red took me past the white piano.

He did it slowly. Let me see the polished lid, the flowers beside it, the photograph on the wall of Shawna singing with her hair pinned up. The nigga had paid a fortune to make a room look like she was still going to walk into it.

"You play?" he asked.

"You asking me to?"

"I wondered what you brought to a woman who already sings."

Tank looked at me. I could feel him begging me to keep walking. He'd known me a week and already understood that asking what I could do was an expensive way to start a morning.

I sat down.

There was sheet music on the stand. A song with a long introduction, full of black notes pressed together like the niggas had arrived in the same car. Somebody had written Shawna's name at the top.

I put my hands on the keys.

The first chord filled the room. Then the next. My fingers remembered a church piano from when I was broke enough to play for supper, an old woman with strong hands correcting my wrists, hours by myself in an empty room when nobody wanted me around except an instrument that couldn't leave.

Red stopped walking.

I played the introduction once. Then I played it again with the melody in my right hand and something meaner moving underneath it. The waiters stood still with the breakfast trays. One of the men at the door turned around. Tank leaned against the wall, arms folded, smiling like a nigga watching his brother come home with a stolen moon.

"You read that just now?" Red asked.

"Yeah."

"Then come on. Niggas graduating."

He put his cap back on and fell in beside us like I'd just personally admitted him into the morning.

***

They took me through the science hall on the way to the auditorium because Principal Mercer wanted me to see what my name had done to a wall.

There it was. AIDEN HALL. Gold letters. Taller than my head. I stood under them while Tank took a picture and Shawna laughed into her hand.

"Nigga, I got a hall."

"You got several," Mercer said.

"Don't tell him that," Rosa said. "He'll start naming the doors."

Inside, three professors stood around a long table covered in papers. They had been arguing when we came in. I could see it in the way they all stopped speaking at slightly different times.

Professor Lorne looked down at a page and then up at me.

"Perhaps you could settle something."

Halcyon made a little warning noise.

"He has a ceremony."

"One minute."

"Nigga, everybody thinks they need a minute until I get here," I said. "Show me."

He turned the paper around. A drawing of the new auditorium roof. Lines crossing. Numbers beside them. Somebody had used a red pen to circle the same span three times.

"The model gives us two possible answers," he said.

I looked at the drawing. Then through the window at the actual auditorium.

"You got the old roof in here."

He blinked.

"The dimensions—"

"Changed when they moved the stage. Look at that window. It's eight panels across. Your drawing got seven."

All three professors turned toward the window.

I took the pencil and moved the line.

"Now your load goes there. Those two numbers stop fighting."

Lorne looked at what I'd written. His mouth moved without sound.

The professor beside him pulled another sheet from the pile, compared them, and sat down so suddenly his chair rolled backward.

"He's right."

"Nigga, say it like you enjoyed finding out."

"He's right!"

The room laughed. Mercer started crying again.

I looked at Shawna.

"Your man got a roof."

She hooked her fingers through my robe.

"My man better get under it before they name another building."

As we left, Lorne caught my arm gently.

"What do you do for a living?"

Tank answered before I could.

"Nigga improves transportation."

***

The auditorium was packed. A choir stood above the stage. Blue banners hung from the balcony. The youngest graduate was twenty-two and the oldest was sixty-eight, and every one of those grown people had brought somebody who wanted to see their name become something they could hold.

Our people took the front row. Shawna sat in the middle. Tank sat at the aisle because every chair in that room looked like it had been built to underestimate him.

Chief Gloria Knox stood beside the stage.

I stopped when I saw her.

"Nigga, you following me to school now?"

"I was invited."

"You graduating?"

"I was hoping to recruit the valedictorian."

Behind her, under a dark blue cloth, stood something low and wide enough to make my pulse change.

A car.

I could see the nose through the fabric. I knew what kind of nose that was. Nigga, a Lamborghini could wear a bedsheet and I'd still recognize its ass in a church parking lot.

Knox watched me looking.

"Finish your speech first."

"Chief, that's cruel."

"Motivation."

She smiled and took her place beside Mercer.

The ceremony began. Names. Applause. People walking across the stage trying not to cry before their family did. I stood behind the curtain listening, the robe hanging open enough to let my cross shine.

Shawna kept looking back at me. Every time she did, my chest got bigger. I had a bullet in my body, three nights until a man tried to take her from me, and a whole college waiting to find out how I sounded when somebody gave me a microphone on purpose.

Then the lights flickered.

I looked up.

A man stood in the upper aisle wearing a gray suit. He wasn't watching the stage. He was watching the doors.

Another man stood beside the exit under the balcony.

Their jackets hung wrong.

Nigga, I knew that shape better than I knew my own fucking curriculum.

I caught Knox's eye. Looked toward the balcony.

She saw him.

The man under the balcony pulled his gun as the first flame ran up the curtain behind the choir.

***

The fire moved fast.

Blue fabric turned orange. Somebody screamed from the choir. The gunman by the exit fired into the ceiling, and the whole auditorium tried to stand up at once.

I grabbed the microphone.

"Stay off the stairs! Niggas on the floor, heads down!"

My voice filled the room louder than his gun.

Knox fired from beside the stage. The man at the exit fell back behind a row of seats. Her officers moved toward him while Tank got the people in our row down and Shawna pulled Rosa away from the aisle.

Above us, the balcony gunman raised his weapon.

I fired.

He fell against the rail and dropped behind it. His gun hit the empty aisle below. The choir was coming down from its platform, adults in white robes stumbling through smoke, and I ran up to meet them before the first person fell.

"This way!"

A woman with a cane had stopped halfway down. I caught her under the arm. A man behind her took her other side. We carried her to the stage while the curtains burned overhead.

Mercer stood by the lectern holding my diploma against his chest.

"Principal! Move!"

He looked at me through the smoke.

"Your degree—"

"Nigga, I need somebody alive to sign it!"

He moved.

A beam fell across the side exit. Sparks burst upward. The doorway vanished behind fire and broken wood.

The main aisle was jammed with seats somebody had overturned while trying to run. People at the back were getting out, but those closest to the stage couldn't reach them. The fire was between us and the loading doors behind the choir.

I looked at the covered car.

Then at Knox.

She was already pulling the cloth away.

Dark blue. Gold shield on the doors. Low black wheels. A light bar shining across the windshield. The most beautiful fucking police Lamborghini a criminal could ever deserve.

She threw me the keys.

"Don't scratch it."

"Nigga, don't limit me."

Tank reached me as I opened the door.

"You driving in the school?"

"I'm improving the exit."

"Nigga, I went to the wrong fucking college."

He pulled the overturned seats clear of my bumper. I got behind the wheel. The engine woke up with a roar that cut through the fire and the screaming, and every head in front of me turned.

Shawna stood at the edge of the stage holding Mercer's arm.

She looked at the car. Looked at the fire. Looked at me.

Then she smiled.

Not because she wasn't scared. Baby was scared enough to shake. But she knew which nigga she'd come to watch that morning, and she wasn't looking away while I became him.

I drove.

The Lamborghini crossed the stage, took the shallow steps in one hard drop, and hit the blocked aisle with the whole weight of my morning behind it. Seats flew aside. Wood snapped. Smoke closed over the windshield.

I kept going.

Flame washed across the glass as a curtain fell over the hood. For a second I couldn't see anything but orange. Then it slid away, and the far doors appeared directly in front of me.

I put my foot down.

We broke through into daylight.

Both doors came off their hinges. The Lamborghini shot down the broad front steps and landed in the college courtyard with its siren screaming. Behind me, fresh air rushed through the opening I'd made.

People came out after it.

The choir first. Then graduates helping graduates. Then Tank carrying the woman with the cane while Rosa and Mercedes pushed two overturned benches out of the way. Porsche had a fire extinguisher in each hand and was shouting at niggas like she personally owned oxygen.

I got out and ran back up the steps.

Knox met me at the opening with Mercer beside her. Shawna came behind them, one hand over her mouth, the other holding my graduation cap.

"Baby!"

I caught her.

She hit my shoulder with the cap, then put it on my head herself.

"You almost forgot something."

"Nigga, I was making an entrance."

"You made an exit."

"A great man does both."

She kissed me while the whole courtyard watched.

Behind us, the fire crews arrived. Knox's people brought the surviving attackers out in handcuffs. One of them looked at the Lamborghini and shook his head.

"We burned the building."

"And the nigga still came through it," his partner said.

"With a police car."

"Nigga, I saw."

I heard them. Of course I heard them. They'd come to ruin my graduation and now they were reviewing the best part of it together.

I straightened my cap.

"Principal. You got a microphone out here?"

***

They brought the lectern into the courtyard.

Mercer stood beside it still crying. Halcyon put a hand on his back. Professor Lorne looked at the smoke coming out of the auditorium and then at me with the expression of a man whose roof calculation had just acquired a personal reason to live.

The graduates gathered in front of us. Some had soot on their gowns. Some were holding shoes. Everybody had somebody by the hand.

I stepped up to the microphone.

"Aight. Where we at?"

The laughter rolled across the courtyard, wild and relieved. Tank stood behind Shawna with his arms folded and his face shining. Marcellus had one hand on Rosa's shoulder. Mercedes lifted her camera.

"Niggas, I ain't gonna lie. I wasn't here much."

The laughter got louder.

"You were not here at all!" somebody shouted.

"And look what happened the morning I came. You welcome."

They cheered. A man in the third row took off his cap and waved it over his head.

I looked out at them. All those people. All those grown men and women who'd come back to a classroom after life had given them plenty of reasons to leave it alone. They were looking at me like I could tell them something worth taking into tomorrow.

Nigga, I could do that too.

"You came for your name," I said. "Some of you worked all night and came anyway. Some of you had people telling you this wasn't your kind of room. Some of you got somebody missing today who should've been here making too much fucking noise."

Shawna looked down at her watch. Then back at me.

"But you here. And when the doors caught fire, niggas started carrying each other. I saw that. That part belongs to you. Keep it. Don't let anybody make you little enough to forget it."

I put my hand on the cross.

"Three letters on this. D. B. M."

Tank's eyebrows lifted. Shawna began smiling before I said the words.

"Damn, boy. Magnificent."

The courtyard erupted.

"That's you too," I told them. "Men, women, every grown motherfucker standing here with a cap and a dream. You look in the mirror tomorrow, say it properly. Don't mumble greatness in your own house."

Mercedes shouted, "Say it again!"

"Damn!"

The whole courtyard answered, "Boy!"

I spread my arms.

"Magnificent!"

They shouted it back so loudly the pigeons left the roof of Aiden Hall together.

Mercer put the diploma in my hand while he was still crying. I had driven through a burning auditorium and finished my valedictorian speech before the principal finished his first fucking breakdown.

Nigga, education was going beautifully.

***

Knox stepped up beside me before anybody could start the next hymn.

Two officers brought a blue velvet box. Another brought a dark uniform jacket with gold braid on its shoulders. The whole courtyard went quiet in stages as people realized the police had come to offer me more than a ride.

"Aiden," Knox said, "you've been making this offer difficult to postpone."

"Chief, I been busy graduating."

"You've also saved my officers' lives, protected this city's people, and demonstrated a relationship with automobiles I have never seen in any man who still has all his limbs."

Porsche applauded first.

Knox opened the box.

A gold captain's badge lay inside.

Not honorary. Not a souvenir. The chief told the entire courtyard that before any nigga could get confused and make me explain it with a siren later.

"Captain Aiden," she said. "Las Vegas Police Department. Actual command. Actual badge. Actual trouble for anyone who thinks otherwise."

Tank bent forward with his hands on his knees.

"Nigga."

Rosa caught his arm.

"You all right?"

"This nigga's a cop."

"I heard."

"My nigga's the police."

"Stand up before he arrests your posture."

Knox held out the badge.

"Raise your right hand."

I did.

The oath she gave me was short. Keep faith with my people. Stand when somebody needed standing for. Use the authority of the badge with my whole name behind it. I spoke every word in front of the chief, the officers, the graduates, and the woman I wanted beside me for the rest of my life.

Then Knox pinned the badge inside my jacket.

"Captain."

I looked down at the gold.

Nigga, my mother would've had to sit down twice.

Shawna came up the steps. Took my face in both hands. Kissed me slowly enough that the police officers started applauding before the students did.

"My man," she whispered.

"Your captain."

"Still my man."

I could've stood there forever letting her settle the rank.

Knox gave me the Lamborghini's keys again. One door had a scratch from the auditorium. The hood smelled like smoke. The siren still worked, because God wasn't going to give a nigga that much greatness and then get petty over wiring.

"It's yours," she said.

"I noticed."

She leaned close enough that only I could hear.

"Red's people used to believe they owned every uniform that mattered in this city. Let him see yours."

I looked at her.

She smiled.

"I know exactly who I recruited, Captain."

We left the college with my diploma on Shawna's lap and the badge inside my jacket. Tank followed in the original Mercedes. The rest of the convoy came behind him, horns sounding all the way to the gates.

Every graduate turned to watch the Lamborghini pass.

At the fountain, Marcus raised his cap and shouted, "That's our nigga!"

His friend shouted, "That's our captain!"

I hit the siren once for both of them.

Shawna put her hand on my leg as the campus disappeared behind us.

"What you doing tomorrow?"

I looked at her. Looked at the road. Let the car stretch its legs beneath us.

"Baby, niggas better not schedule anything they can't afford."
