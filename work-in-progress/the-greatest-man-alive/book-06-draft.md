## 1. The City That Had Never Met Me

The first thing Emperor Zero sent to welcome me was a gold coffin strapped to a Ferrari.

The Ferrari came out of the desert with nobody driving it. I watched it through the windshield of my police Lamborghini while Shawna put on lipstick beside me. Behind us, Tank led a convoy long enough that the last car was still having a different morning. We had driven out of Midnight City with kings owing us money, queens waving from balconies, and my wife wearing sunglasses that cost more than my first apartment building. Now some nigga had sent funeral furniture down the highway.

"Baby," Shawna said, "is that for you?"

"Depends what's inside."

"A coffin, Aiden."

"Could be champagne. Rich niggas get weird with presentation."

The Ferrari slowed ahead of us. On its rear window, gold letters lit up.

WELCOME TO YOUR FINAL DESTINATION.

Shawna considered the message, rubbed her lips together, and snapped her mirror shut.

"Spelling's right. He's already smarter than the last one."

I pulled alongside. The coffin lid opened by itself. There was a suit inside, black silk with a white rose on the chest. A photograph of me stood where the head should have been. This nigga had chosen a bad photograph. I looked good in it, obviously, but it was from before I bought my current watch.

"He wants to bury me without my badge," I said.

"You'd make the other dead people nervous."

A loudspeaker crackled.

"Captain Aiden. Your journey ends here."

I leaned toward Shawna's window.

"Nigga, which here? It's a big desert."

A man appeared on the Ferrari's dashboard screen. White suit. Black gloves. Hair swept back like it had its own chauffeur. I knew Emperor Zero's face. Seeing it that small made him look like a man waiting for somebody to answer the doorbell.

"You have mistaken a succession of fortunate accidents for greatness."

"And you mistook a Ferrari for a mail truck. We all learning."

Shawna laughed so hard she had to hold the mirror again.

Zero looked directly at her. That stopped my laughing before it started.

"Mrs. Aiden. I have rooms prepared for both of you. Separate rooms, should you discover a more intelligent attachment."

My wife lowered her sunglasses.

"Nigga, look at your own wife."

"I have no wife."

"We can see that."

The screen went dark.

Behind us, Tank's voice burst over the radio.

"What that nigga want?"

"Me to die dressed like a waiter."

"Tell him you don't do afternoon funerals. Rosa got a headache."

I was still smiling when the hills beside the highway opened fire.

The first bullets knocked sparks from the coffin. The second burst took out the Ferrari's rear tire. Its beautiful ass swung across our lane with death sliding around on the roof, and Shawna caught my arm before I could turn too hard. A black shape came over the ridge. Then another. Six trucks were dropping toward us through curtains of sand.

"Aiden."

"I see them, baby."

"The coffee."

She lifted our cups out of the holders. I hit the accelerator.

We shot beneath the Ferrari's raised coffin lid. Wood split over the windshield. A sleeve from my funeral suit slapped the glass and blew away. I had been dressed for my own death for three seconds and already ruined the outfit.

Tank's Mercedes swerved around the wreck behind us. Rosa leaned out his passenger window with a pistol and a silk scarf holding her hair down. This woman had packed seven evening gowns for a desert war and was already defending all seven.

"Get your niggas off my luggage!" she yelled.

A bullet punched through a truck's mirror. Its driver ducked. Tank bumped the truck hard enough to push it sideways, and the other convoy cars poured past him.

I saw Vesper's car in my mirror, black and long, moving with the smoothness of a hearse that had decided to pursue a music career. Mercedes drove. Porsche was standing through the sunroof holding two phones and one gun. I did not know which item she considered most dangerous.

"Left side clear!" Porsche shouted over the radio. "And whoever keeps calling me from a blocked number, your timing is fucking disgusting!"

The road ahead ended at a bridge suspended over a dry riverbed. Beyond it rose the Last City.

Nigga, I had seen rich. I owned rich. I had slept in rooms where the curtains required their own employee. This city looked like somebody had robbed the sun and built a neighborhood with the evidence. Gold towers shone behind a wall of black stone. A casino shaped like a giant crown sat on a hill. A white palace floated above the center on enormous pillars, with highways running underneath it and planes banking past its windows. Even from miles away, I could see a waterfall falling off a hotel into a swimming pool big enough to have weather.

Shawna stared.

"I want that pool."

"Which one?"

"Yes."

The bridge began to rise.

It split in the middle, its two halves opening like a mouth. On the other side, armored cars appeared beneath the city's gate. Somebody had figured on trapping us between the trucks and the gap. It was an elegant plan for people who did not know who the fuck was driving.

"Tank, bring everybody close."

"How close?"

"Family close."

He understood. The Mercedes moved up behind us. The other cars tightened into two lines. Our enemies were still firing from the ridges, but the loose convoy had become a single glittering animal, and I was the head of that motherfucker.

Shawna looked at the bridge. Then she looked at me.

"You aren't glowing."

"I'm saving it."

"Good. I'd hate to marry a man who needs lightning every time he leaves the house."

I kissed her hand and shifted down.

A garage stood beside the bridge approach. Its roof was covered in solar panels and old racing signs. A woman in grease-stained white trousers ran out carrying a red flag. She had a silver braid, brown skin, and the kind of arms that could fix an engine or break the owner.

"Not the bridge!" she shouted. "The old spillway!"

She pointed right.

I had half a second to decide if the woman was saving us or selling us.

Then she threw a wrench through the window of a truck trying to ram the garage, and I liked her judgment.

"Everybody right!"

We dropped off the highway.

The Lamborghini hit the sloping concrete of the old spillway, bounced hard enough to make the badges on my uniform slap my chest, and tore downhill. Shawna's coffee left its cup in one perfect brown crown. She watched it land on my trousers.

"That's why I bought you two pairs."

Behind us, thirty engines roared into the riverbed.

A truck followed too fast. Its front end struck the lip of the spillway. It pitched forward, bounced twice, and rolled onto its side. Another stopped at the top. Rosa stood on Tank's passenger seat and fired until the niggas inside remembered an urgent appointment somewhere else.

The garage woman jumped onto a motorcycle and came down after us. She caught my side, one hand on the bars, the other pointing toward a narrow channel beneath the city wall.

"That tunnel goes through!" she yelled.

"How low?"

"Lower than your ego!"

"That don't tell me shit!"

She laughed. I liked her more.

We reached the tunnel with half the desert following us. It was an old service passage, wet at the bottom, with rusted gates standing open. My Lamborghini fit with inches to spare. Tank folded his mirrors against the Mercedes with a curse. The long black car scraped sparks down both walls.

Porsche's voice came through the radio.

"Vesper, your driver is exceptional!"

"I am aware," Vesper said.

"I meant me!" Mercedes snapped.

"I am aware of that also."

The tunnel bent. Daylight flashed ahead. I could hear something above us that sounded like thunder, but it was too regular, too heavy. Engines. Hundreds of them.

We exploded out of the tunnel into the middle of a parade.

Gold motorcycles. Red supercars. Black limousines with flags. A marching band scattered as my police Lamborghini slid across a boulevard and stopped beside a float carrying a fifty-foot statue of Emperor Zero. His statue held the world in one hand. Some nigga had given him very generous shoulders.

The whole street froze.

I got out of my car, coffee on my trousers, dust on my shoulders, and looked up at the statue.

"You build that yourself?"

Nobody answered.

Then Tank came out of the tunnel sideways and knocked the float's tow bar loose.

The statue started rolling.

A brass player screamed. The float drifted toward a fountain, slow and majestic, while six men tried to stop it by pushing the same side. They only helped it turn. Emperor Zero's golden ass hit the fountain wall. The world fell out of his hand and bounced down the boulevard.

Shawna stepped out beside me.

"Well," she said. "We're here."

An old woman on a balcony began to clap.

One clap. Then another. Then a hundred.

A man in a red racing jacket pointed at my badge.

"That's Aiden!"

Nigga, my name traveled down that street faster than the gold globe. People came out of shops. Waiters abandoned tables. Two women jumped off a stationary party bus and ran toward us waving champagne. Somebody started a chant. The marching band, having survived, began playing something triumphant without being asked.

I had entered Zero's city through a drain and received a parade he had already paid for.

"Captain!" a young man yelled.

He was grown, broad-shouldered, maybe twenty-six, wearing a chauffeur's cap under a motorcycle helmet. He reached me holding a bouquet that had probably been intended for a royal mistress.

"My brother saw you race in Monaco. He said you made Cassius Chrome look like he was parking."

"Cassius is my nigga. Don't disrespect him."

"Sorry, Captain."

"But your brother got eyes."

The man laughed and thrust the flowers into my hands. I passed them to Shawna. She smelled them, looked at the fountain, and smiled like a woman deciding where her new city ought to put the furniture.

The garage rider stopped beside us and removed her helmet.

"June Mercury," she said. "Thirty-two. Mechanic. Owner of that garage your enemies tried to turn into a fucking window."

I shook her hand.

"Aiden."

"Nigga, I know."

Tank came up behind me, looked at June, then at the motorcycle.

"You the one put us in that little-ass tunnel?"

"You the one driving a house?"

Rosa stepped out of the Mercedes and pulled him away before he could fall in love with arguing.

Vesper emerged from the long black car. The applause changed when people recognized her. It got deeper, less surprised. Some bowed. Some said her name as if they had not expected to say it without a guard hearing. She stood beside Shawna and let the city look.

"You know these people?" I asked.

"Some. The others know stories."

"Good stories?"

"Ask after lunch."

A siren shrieked from the far end of the boulevard.

Black armored cars turned the corner. The crowd broke apart, doors opening, hands pulling friends into shops. The band stopped in the middle of a note. Zero's men spread across the street beneath flags with a silver circle on them.

June took one look and swore.

"Ruin's guard."

"Who the fuck is Ruin?"

"A man who thinks engines should kneel."

"That nigga's about to have a terrible day."

The lead armored car stopped. A tall man stepped out wearing a white driving suit and black sunglasses. There was a silver slash through one eyebrow. He carried no visible gun, which meant he either believed in conversation or had enough niggas around him to outsource it.

"Captain Aiden," he said. "Roland Ruin. His Imperial Majesty welcomes you."

I pointed at the wrecked statue.

"Tell him thanks for the ride."

His jaw moved once. He looked past me at Shawna, Vesper, the convoy, and the people hiding behind shop windows. I watched him count the cost of shooting in front of all those witnesses.

"You will accompany me to the palace."

"We need breakfast."

"The emperor does not wait."

"Then the emperor should've eaten before we got here."

Behind me, Tank made a noise so disrespectful it should have required a second mouth.

Roland took off his sunglasses.

"You have a reputation. I suggest you enjoy its final hour."

"Nigga, I been enjoying it since yesterday. You late."

His men raised their weapons.

Mine raised theirs.

For a second the whole boulevard held still. A flower petal slid off Shawna's bouquet and landed on my shoe. June's motorcycle ticked as it cooled. Somewhere inside a shop, a childless wedding display kept playing a love song to mannequins while forty grown men considered dying outside it.

Shawna touched my wrist.

I knew that touch. Not fear. A request. There were people in those shops, people who had clapped for me before knowing if I could keep them alive.

I took my badge off my chest and held it up.

"Roland. Look at this."

He did.

"You know what this means?"

"It means Las Vegas has poor judgment."

"It means I'm on duty. And right now you're obstructing my honeymoon."

"Your honeymoon?"

"It's a long marriage. We take breaks."

I stepped forward.

His men tightened their grips. Mine moved with me. Roland's expression changed, just a little, when he understood that I was going to walk right up to him with a whole war waiting behind my shoulders.

"You can have your palace meeting," I said. "Tomorrow. Tonight my people eat, sleep, and wash the desert off. Your niggas follow us into our hotel, I'm arresting every last one of them in front of whatever emperor you're renting your spine to."

"You cannot arrest an imperial guard."

"I never attended college either. Life's full of surprises."

June laughed before she could stop herself.

Roland looked at her. I moved half an inch, enough to take his eyes back.

"Me, nigga. We talking."

He held my stare. Then he smiled, slow and cold.

"Tomorrow, then. The emperor enjoys a guest who remains entertaining."

"Buy him a television."

He put his sunglasses on and stepped into the armored car.

They withdrew without firing. The crowd did not immediately return. People waited until the last black vehicle vanished before they dared believe we had stayed alive.

Then the old woman on the balcony clapped again.

I looked up.

"Ma'am, you started all this."

She threw me a white handkerchief.

"For the coffee, handsome!"

Shawna caught it first.

"Thank you! He needs supervision!"

June led us toward the Crownless Hotel, an enormous sandstone place built around a courtyard full of date palms and black cars. Its owner came running down the steps in slippers, kissed Vesper's hand, shook mine twice, and offered us the entire top floor before learning how many niggas we had brought.

When he learned, he offered the second floor too.

"Captain," he said, looking at my Lamborghini, "would you permit us to display your arrival photograph in the lobby?"

"Make sure my good side in it."

He stared at me, honestly troubled.

"How would I choose?"

Tank bent over laughing.

I liked this fucking city.

June caught me before I reached the elevator. She had washed her hands, but black grease remained under her nails. In the lobby light I could see a scar on her chin and a tiny gold engine hanging from her neck.

"My garage," she said. "You should know what that was."

"A garage. I been to one."

"My father's. Then mine. Zero wants the ground under it for another road to his palace. He offered me a room in a hotel and a job repairing his cars."

"You tell him no?"

"I told him where to park the hotel."

"That's my kind of negotiation."

She smiled, then stopped. "My partner Adrian is still there. He won't leave the lifts. Says nobody steals a man's wrench while he's breathing."

I turned to Tank.

"Send two cars. Bring him and whatever he can carry."

"The nigga's gonna try to carry a lift," June said.

"Then send three."

Tank put his phone to his ear. Just like that, Adrian had more friends than he had possessed five minutes earlier.

June looked at me as if I had done something strange.

"You don't know us."

"I know you put my wife through a tunnel instead of a grave. That's plenty."

For the first time since the road, she had no answer. She rubbed the little engine between her fingers and looked away at the lobby fountain.

The hotel owner approached with a velvet box. Inside was a gold room key the size of a dagger.

"The imperial suite, Captain. We have renamed it."

"Already?"

"The engraver was having lunch downstairs."

AIDEN'S SUITE shone on the handle.

"Nigga," Tank whispered, "we been here eleven minutes."

I took the key. "People recognize quality."

June shook her head, smiling now with all her teeth. "If you live through this, I want to build you a car."

"If you build it right, living through shit comes included."

She held out her greasy hand again. I shook it again. The hotel owner watched a black mark land on his golden key and looked so honored I thought he might frame that too.

Our suite looked over the boulevard and, beyond it, the emperor's palace. Shawna walked through the rooms with the flowers in her arms. She checked the bedroom, the bath, the balcony. Then she came back to where I stood and opened my shirt.

"Any new holes?"

"Only emotional ones. That nigga picked the wrong funeral suit."

Her fingers found the old scar in my side. She kissed my chest above the cross and stayed there for a moment.

Outside, our people were unloading bags. Tank was arguing with a bellhop about whether a gun case counted as luggage. Rosa wanted a piano. Mercedes wanted a better parking space. Porsche wanted whoever had called from a blocked number to have the courage to do it again.

My wife looked up at me.

"One last city."

"Then Vegas."

"Then the wedding."

"Then you."

"You already have me."

I held her tighter.

"Nigga, that's how I know I'm winning."

She laughed against my mouth, and I kissed her while the emperor's palace glittered outside like a place that had never learned what happened when a man came for his people.

Down in the street, somebody had dragged the fallen gold globe to the hotel entrance.

They had painted my name across it.

## 2. Two Queens and a Stolen Ballroom

By six o'clock, our hotel had become the most expensive bad decision in the Last City.

There were race cars in the courtyard, guns on the billiard tables, and three women arguing with a florist over whether white roses were romantic or evidence that a nigga had given up. Rosa stood on the piano in the ballroom wearing a green dress and directing furniture like she had conquered the building herself. Tank held her shoes. The pianist, a grown man with gray hair, kept looking at her feet as if God had finally answered a very specific prayer.

"Nobody sits with their back to that window," Rosa announced.

"Why?" the owner asked.

"Because I just paid to have my hair done and we in a city full of shooters."

He moved the chairs personally.

I came downstairs in black trousers, a white shirt, my shoulder holster, and the captain's badge hanging against my chest. The blood money years had taught me how to dress for dinner. The police years had taught me that the gun should match.

Shawna came down behind me wearing blue.

Nigga, some colors belong to everybody until the right woman walks into them. Then you realize the rest of the world had only been borrowing. Her dress moved like water around her legs. Dante's watch sat on her wrist. My ring shone on her hand. She looked at the ballroom, then at the crowd looking at her, and smiled without asking any of them for permission.

I forgot the thing I had been saying.

"Captain?" the hotel owner prompted.

"Give me a minute."

"Of course."

"Maybe two."

He understood. Every nigga in the room understood.

Vesper stood by the doors in burgundy silk, speaking to a silver-haired woman whose black gown looked capable of inheriting property. The older woman watched us approach with a small smile. She carried a cane topped by a pearl. I had seen enough royal women to know the cane was either a weapon or an excuse to hit somebody with a weapon.

"Aiden," Vesper said. "Magnolia Saint-Clair. My old tutor."

"Not that old," Magnolia said. "Sixty-four is an excellent age for disapproving of emperors."

She offered me her hand. I kissed it because my mother had raised me intermittently but effectively.

"A captain, a king, and he remembers manners," Magnolia said. "Vesper, you should have brought him years ago."

"I was busy," I said.

"Doing what?"

"Becoming all that shit."

She laughed. A clean, wicked laugh. I liked her immediately.

Shawna took her other hand. "I'm his wife."

"Yes, dear. He looked at you as if somebody had opened the sun. I worked it out."

Magnolia led us toward a table under a chandelier shaped like falling rain. Vesper sat beside her. Shawna sat beside me. Two queens and a woman who had probably slapped a king with a dictionary. I had entered the room thinking I was in charge of dinner.

Then Mercedes arrived carrying a bottle of champagne in each hand.

"The women downstairs want to know if this is a private party."

"Who downstairs?" Shawna asked.

"The women Zero paid to welcome his motor champions. Their bus driver saw Aiden's cars and came here instead."

I looked toward the door.

A line of grown women in glittering dresses stood behind Porsche, who was arranging them by who needed a drink most urgently. Some were dancers. Some were escorts. One carried a violin. Another carried a little dog wearing diamonds, and the dog had the expression of a nigga who knew he would never pay rent.

"We're happy to be confused," the violinist said.

"Then welcome," I told her.

They came in cheering.

The pianist found a rhythm. Rosa came down from the piano and took the microphone. Tank put her shoes at her feet as if presenting a queen with two very small thrones.

"My man," she said into the microphone, "has spent this whole trip claiming he can't dance."

Tank looked at me.

"Don't help her."

"Nigga, I'm the police. I can't obstruct an investigation."

Rosa held out her hand.

Tank went to her.

He danced like a refrigerator being taught to trust love. Slowly at first. One hand too high. One foot trying to move to a song nobody else could hear. Rosa whispered something in his ear. He laughed, relaxed, and found her rhythm.

The room changed around them. People still drank and talked, but they watched those two with the kind of attention you give something that nearly did not survive long enough to happen.

Shawna laid her head against my shoulder.

"He's practicing."

"For the wedding?"

"For being happy without looking over your shoulder every second."

I looked over mine.

She pinched my leg.

"Aiden."

"Somebody gotta do it."

Magnolia waited until Rosa began singing before she leaned toward us.

"Zero has asked every hotel in the city to report your visitors."

"This one gonna need more paper," I said.

"He also recalled the airport landing permissions. Your friends may find arriving difficult."

I took out my phone.

There were eleven messages from Cassius Chrome. The first said he had landed. The second said customs wanted his cars. The third was a photograph of him standing on a crate with his arms folded. The fourth read: BROTHER I HAVE BEEN PATIENT FOR ALMOST NINE MINUTES.

I called him.

"Where you at?"

"A warehouse beside the airport. They say my fleet is imperial property."

"You show them your paperwork?"

"I showed them my face. They remained confused."

"Who with you?"

"Inez."

In the background, I heard a man yell.

Cassius sighed.

"She has begun explaining."

"Nigga, tell her leave something for me."

I stood up. Shawna stood with me.

Magnolia smiled into her glass.

"Dinner before the battle would have been conventional."

"Save our plates," Shawna told her.

Vesper rose. "I know the airport director."

"That good?" I asked.

"For us."

Tank was still dancing when I reached him. He saw my face, kissed Rosa, and stepped back into his shoes like somebody had rung the bell on a champion.

"Who we getting?"

"Cassius and Inez."

"What they do?"

"Arrived too rich."

"Nigga, that's discrimination."

June came out of the courtyard with Adrian, her partner. He was thirty-five, lean, with a shaved head and a little beard that made him look like he had strong opinions about engines. Our men had brought him safely through the old service route, along with three tool chests and, somehow, half a motorcycle.

"You going to the airport?" he asked.

"Apparently."

"Take the eastern road. Main gate has an armored checkpoint."

June shook her head. "Eastern road has two."

I looked between them.

"Which one got better scenery?"

They stared.

Shawna picked up my jacket. "Main gate. He likes an entrance."

We took four cars. Me and Shawna in the Lamborghini. Tank and Rosa in the Mercedes because she had refused to stay behind while her friends were detained. Vesper rode with Mercedes and Porsche. June and Adrian brought a vicious little silver coupe that looked like it had been built during an argument.

At the airport gate, a man in a black uniform held up his hand.

I stopped close enough that he could admire the badge on my hood.

"The airport is closed."

"Then open it."

"To unauthorized arrivals."

"I'm a departure from your usual bullshit. Move."

He looked at Shawna, then at the cars behind us. A second guard approached holding a tablet. My photograph was on the screen with a red border around it.

"You are expected," he said.

"See? Nigga got the invitation."

The barrier rose.

Shawna watched the first guard step aside.

"Too easy."

"Maybe my smile working."

"It works on me. I still know when you're lying."

Inside the cargo yard, a dozen supercars sat under floodlights. Red, silver, black, blue. Cassius stood on the hood of a white truck in a purple racing jacket, applauding slowly as I drove in. Inez leaned against a yellow car with her arms folded. Three guards lay on the ground near her, awake and reconsidering their employment.

"My brother!" Cassius called. "You brought the police!"

I got out.

"Nigga, I am the police."

"Exactly what I told them. They asked for a local authority."

Vesper stepped out of her car.

The airport director, a round man with a red face, stopped speaking before he finished turning toward her.

"Your Majesty."

"Director Pell."

"I had no idea these were your guests."

"You had several ideas. Most of them were poor."

She held out her hand for the keys.

Before he could move, the warehouse doors behind him slammed shut.

Floodlights snapped on across the roof. A voice rolled over the yard.

"Captain. I hoped you would bring the whole family."

Roland Ruin stood on a balcony above the loading bays.

Of course that nigga had found a balcony. Men like him believed height was a personality.

He wore his white driving suit again, now open at the throat. A line of Black Mantle guards appeared beside him. Their rifles pointed down at us.

Cassius looked up, then at me.

"I dislike this airport."

"One star," I said.

Roland smiled.

"You may leave. Your vehicles remain."

"You can keep that ugly truck," I told him.

Cassius looked down at the hood beneath his feet.

"It is a rental."

"Even better."

A shot struck the pavement by my shoe.

The whole yard moved.

Shawna pulled Vesper behind the Lamborghini. Tank shoved Rosa toward a concrete pillar. I drew and fired at the balcony as Cassius jumped off the truck. The guards ducked. Inez was already moving between the cars, low and fast, keys flying from her hand to mine.

"Silver one!" she shouted.

I caught them without looking.

June's little coupe roared across the yard and struck a stack of empty pallets. Wood scattered. Adrian leaned out and fired toward the warehouse windows. The distraction gave Cassius enough time to pull a door open on the nearest race car.

"Everybody drives!" I yelled.

"Aiden!" Shawna called.

I turned.

She had taken the Lamborghini's wheel. My wife looked at the silver car, then at me.

"Keep up."

Nigga, she drove away before I could fall more in love.

I jumped into the silver car. It smelled of new leather and expensive gasoline. The engine woke like a predator hearing its own name. Across the dashboard, a small plaque read CHROME SPECIAL NUMBER ONE.

"Cassius!" I shouted into the radio. "This yours?"

"Was."

"What?"

"I brought you a gift. Try not to put bullets in the seats."

I laughed and hit the gas.

The car shot forward so hard I felt my stomach stay in customs. Bullets cracked off the yard around me. I swerved behind a cargo loader, came out beside the white rental truck, and saw Roland descending a staircase at the far end of the warehouse.

He had a black racing car waiting below.

That nigga thought he was leaving his own party early.

I swung the silver car around the truck, followed Shawna beneath the balcony, and cut toward the staircase. Roland fired through his open door. A bullet took my mirror. I returned two shots through the empty stretch ahead of him, enough to make him drop into his seat and pull away before his guard could join him.

Tank rammed the front barrier with a cargo tractor.

I did not know where he had found a cargo tractor. I had stopped asking where Tank found heavy things. Some men had guardian angels. Mine had industrial equipment.

The barrier folded. Rosa stood beside him in the little cab, still holding the microphone she had carried out of the ballroom by mistake.

"Ladies and gentlemen," she shouted into it, "we will be leaving this raggedy-ass airport!"

We left in a river of stolen-back luxury.

Roland took the outer service road. I followed. Shawna came up on my left, blue police lights spinning over the Lamborghini. Behind her, Cassius and Inez moved together, two bright blades in the night.

A plane rolled across the taxiway ahead of us.

Roland accelerated toward it.

"He going under the wing," Cassius said.

"Then so am I."

"Your roof is higher."

"Nigga, don't make me nervous after giving me the car."

I lowered my head anyway.

The black car slipped beneath the plane's wing. My silver roof cleared it by so little I could hear the air change. On the other side, Roland swung sharply onto a narrow ramp. I followed, tires singing. Shawna took the wider route around the plane and came out ahead of us.

She blocked the exit.

Roland braked.

I closed behind him.

For one second we had the nigga between husband and wife, which is where all his decisions had been trying to put him since morning.

He looked from her car to mine.

Then he drove through a glass terminal wall.

The crash flashed white under the lights. Glass burst into the empty lounge. His car tore between rows of seats, climbed a shallow staircase, and vanished through another window into the passenger pickup lane.

I could have followed.

Then I saw the cleaner standing inside the terminal, frozen beside a cart, with a falling metal sign swinging above him.

I stopped.

Shawna stopped too.

I ran through the broken wall, grabbed the man by the shoulder, and pulled him clear as the sign came down hard enough to fold his cart in half. He clung to my shirt, shaking. A grown man, fifty maybe, with tired eyes and rubber gloves.

"You all right?"

He looked at the wreckage. Then at me.

"My cart."

"We'll get you a new one."

"It was a good cart."

"Nigga, I'm buying you an excellent cart."

He laughed once, shocked to hear himself do it.

Cassius came through the doorway and surveyed the destruction.

"Roland escaped?"

"Yeah."

He looked at the man in my arms, then nodded.

"Good brakes on that car."

"Best part so far."

"Omar," she said, "don't let that handsome captain leave before I get there."

Shawna raised her glass at the phone.

"You can look."

"I have been married thirty years. Looking is my vacation."

The ballroom roared.

I sat beside my wife with dust in my hair and a silver car outside that had been a gift for less than twenty minutes before surviving an airfield gunfight. Inez stole a piece of bread off Cassius's plate. He pretended to object, then moved the whole basket toward her.

Magnolia studied them over her wine.

"Another wedding?"

"Drive slowly," Inez told her.

Cassius smiled into his glass.

The violinist approached our table with her instrument under one arm and a plate of food in her hand. Up close she had a little silver scar through one eyebrow and a look that said she had heard men promise things in several languages.

"Sable," she said. "Thirty-one. I play violin, drive boats, and occasionally make rich men believe they're interesting."

"Busy woman," Shawna said.

"Depends how rich."

She sat when Shawna moved a chair for her.

"Those other women are my friends. We worked the emperor's private parties. The motor champions paid, but the palace decided when we could leave. Tonight I told the driver the wrong address."

"He had," Sable said. "For two weeks. His sister plays cello. She's upstairs changing."

One of the dancers was teaching Tank a turn while Rosa laughed at both of them. Everybody had arrived carrying a different reason for wanting to stay.

"Palace coming for you?" I asked.

"Probably."

"Then they know where to find me."

Sable studied me. "You always say shit like that?"

"Only when I'm awake."

She smiled, but her fingers tightened around the violin's neck. Shawna noticed. She set her glass down and put a hand over Sable's.

"There's enough room upstairs," my wife said. "You and your friends decide what you want tomorrow. Tonight you eat."

Sable looked at her for a moment. Then she began eating, fast at first, until she remembered nobody was taking the plate.

The pianist asked whether she would play with Rosa.

She swallowed, wiped her mouth, and stood.

"One song. Then I want cake."

Nigga, that woman put the whole desert inside four strings. The ballroom quieted. Her sister came down with the cello and joined before anyone could ask. Rosa listened for a verse, then found a melody above them, her voice low and rough and full of everything she did not usually explain.

Tank stopped moving.

I watched him watch her. His big hands rested on the back of a chair. He looked almost frightened by how much he loved the woman singing under that chandelier.

"You good?" I asked when he came beside me.

"Yeah."

"Your face look otherwise."

He rubbed his jaw. "I'm just thinking about that day, man. Her in the dress. All these niggas watching me try not to cry."

"Cry, then."

He stared.

"I'm bringing sunglasses," I added. "For both of us."

Tank laughed and leaned his shoulder against mine. Across the room, Rosa saw us standing together and sang the next line straight at him.

My brother smiled like the whole war could wait until she finished.

Vesper returned from the terrace carrying a folded note. She laid it beside my plate.

"From Roland."

I opened it.

TOMORROW. THE EMPEROR'S TABLE. COME WITHOUT YOUR ARMY.

Underneath, in a different hand, someone had added:

I HAVE NEVER SEEN ANYONE DRIVE LIKE THAT.

I passed it to Shawna.

She read both lines and shook her head.

"Your enemies keep writing you fan mail."

"Niggas recognize a man doing his job."

She kissed me in front of the whole room.

Outside, the Last City lit up another thousand windows. Somewhere behind them, Zero was planning my death. Inside our ballroom, my friends began arguing about which car should carry Tank and Rosa away from their wedding.

I listened to them like a man listening to his future arrive early.

## 3. Captain of the Whole Fucking Sky

Victor Voltage called while I was in the bath.

I answered because some niggas only call from the sky when the sky is about to become a problem.

"Aiden," he said, "how attached are you to that hotel roof?"

I sat up.

Shawna looked over from the mirror. She had one earring in and the expression of a woman whose husband had just heard the opening sentence of another emergency.

"What happened?" I asked.

"Minor difficulty with the aircraft."

Behind him, somebody shouted, "WE HAVE LOST THE OTHER ENGINE!"

Victor covered the phone badly.

"I am talking to Aiden!"

I stood, water running off me.

"Nigga, what other engine?"

"The second one."

"How many did you start with?"

"Two."

Shawna threw me a towel.

"Tell him breakfast is getting cold."

I heard Seraphine Storm's voice over the phone, calm enough to make the shouting around her sound rude.

"Captain, we are over the western quarter. The emperor's men have fired upon our transport. Victor has kept us aloft longer than the pilot believed possible. He has approximately three minutes of strength left."

"What you flying?"

"A university transport."

"How big?"

"We brought gifts."

Nigga, I knew then it was a big fucking plane.

I dressed in pieces while running. Shawna buttoned my shirt in the elevator. Tank met us in the lobby carrying two coffees and a gun bag. He looked at my wet hair.

"You shower during that phone call?"

"Other way around."

June was already outside, staring west.

A white aircraft hung above the city with one wing trailing smoke. Lightning flashed around its nose. Victor was somewhere up there putting his whole body between a school full of presents and the ground.

Seraphine came back on the phone.

"There is an unfinished exhibition hall south of your hotel. Its roof is broad enough for an emergency landing, but there are cranes in the approach."

I looked at June.

She pointed before I asked.

"Glass building. Half mile."

"Get everybody out of its way."

"Everybody?"

"Nigga, the plane got gifts."

We took the silver Chrome Special because it was closest and because a nigga should occasionally thank a friend by putting his gift through terrible stress. Shawna came with me. Tank and June followed. The morning traffic scattered around our lights, drivers leaning out to cheer until they looked up and remembered the sky could fall.

The exhibition hall stood beside a dry canal. Its enormous roof shone beneath three construction cranes. Workers ran across the forecourt, pointing at the plane. A security guard tried to stop me at the entrance, saw the aircraft, and decided his barrier did not pay enough.

I drove straight into the hall.

The place was empty except for scaffolding, marble crates, and a ridiculous gold model of Zero's palace. My tires squealed across polished stone. We went through a set of unfinished doors and onto a sloping service ramp that climbed toward the roof.

Shawna held the dashboard.

"Does this go all the way?"

"About to."

"Aiden."

"Baby, I got confidence."

"I would love some information to go with it."

The ramp ended on an open terrace one floor below the roof.

For a second, I had too much information.

Then I saw the scaffolding bridge connecting the terrace to the crane platform.

"There."

"That isn't a road."

"Nigga, it is today."

We hit the narrow bridge. Metal shuddered beneath the tires. A board snapped behind us. I kept the car straight while Shawna watched the far end with her jaw set. We climbed onto the crane platform just as the plane dropped low enough to blot out the sun.

Victor hung beneath its nose, bright with electricity, one hand braced against the fuselage. His whole body shook. I could see him now. The nigga looked like a man trying to bench-press a terrible decision made by an airline.

"Victor!"

He turned his head.

Even from up there, I saw him grin.

"You brought the new car!"

"Cassius gonna be pissed if you land on it!"

The plane lurched. Victor's grin vanished.

I could not hold a plane. Blue Thunder did not make me a god. It gave me a few fierce moments when my body could conduct something stronger than it ought to survive. I had learned the price at Sky High. Every time I spent it, my hands shook afterward like the strength had taken the bones with it.

But I could give my friend a moment.

I looked at Shawna.

She already knew.

"Enough to help him," she said. "Then you let go."

I kissed her once.

"Get down behind the wall."

She got out, took the emergency flare from the car, and ran toward the roof's far edge to guide the pilot. I stepped onto the hood.

Blue light crawled between my fingers.

Nigga, it hurt before it became beautiful. The old scar in my side tightened. My teeth pressed together. I reached up as Victor brought the plane over the crane platform, low enough that its belly filled my world.

For one breath our hands met.

His lightning met mine.

The air cracked blue.

The plane lifted just enough to clear the first crane. Victor shouted. I shouted back. There was no clever shit in it, no king speech, just two niggas refusing to let go of their people at the same time.

Then I pushed the last of my charge through him and dropped to one knee on the hood.

"Go!"

Victor moved with the aircraft. Its wheels struck the broad roof hard enough to throw dust from the seams. Shawna stood at the far end waving the flare. The pilot turned toward her light. One wing clipped a hanging sheet of construction fabric. The fabric wrapped around it like a bride's veil on the ugliest wedding of all time.

The plane slid.

A crane cable snapped.

I heard Tank yelling below.

The aircraft stopped with its nose hanging over the far edge of the roof.

For a second, nothing moved.

Then its door opened.

Dean Seraphine Storm stepped out wearing a white traveling suit and carrying a bouquet.

She looked across the roof at me kneeling on a silver car.

"Captain," she called, "your practical work remains exceptional."

I tried to answer. What came out was mostly breathing.

Shawna ran back to me. She caught my elbow before I could pretend I did not need help.

"Sit."

"I am sitting."

"On the ground. You keep choosing expensive things to collapse on."

She got me off the hood and into the passenger seat. My hands trembled so badly she had to open the water bottle for me. I hated that. I loved that she did it without turning it into a speech.

Victor stumbled across the roof toward us. His uniform was scorched. His hair stood up in seven directions, six of which appeared physically impossible.

He bent and hugged me through the open door.

"My nigga."

"You heavy as shit."

"That is gratitude you feel."

"Feels like a grown man in my lap."

Shawna laughed, and Victor laughed, and for a moment the fact that we were all alive was loud enough to drown out the broken plane.

Then bullets struck the roof wall.

Of course. Zero's niggas could not allow a reunion to have decent lighting without firing at it.

Black Mantle guards were climbing the service stairs on the far side of the building. More vehicles stopped in the forecourt below. They had watched the plane come down and come to collect whatever survived.

Seraphine set the bouquet carefully inside the plane's doorway.

"Victor," she said, "rest."

"Dean, I can—"

"That was not a question."

She raised one hand.

The air above the roof tightened. Loose cloth lifted. Dust spun into a pale circle around her shoes. The first guard reached the stair top and found the wind had opinions about his arrival. He flew backward into the men behind him. Their guns clattered down the steps.

Tank appeared at the other staircase with Rosa and June behind him.

"Aiden! How many fucking floors you need?"

"Ask the architect!"

He ran to the plane. Rosa followed with a medical bag she had taken from Vega downstairs. I had not even seen the doctor arrive; apparently she had been summoned by the universal signal of me doing something stupid at altitude.

Passengers began coming out of the transport. All grown, all rattled. A Sky High music instructor carried two instrument cases. A faculty cook held a covered tray against his chest. An enormous instructor in a purple coat emerged with a wedding cake box so large it needed handles.

"That better not be our cake," Rosa said.

"A sample," the cook replied, offended. "The final cake would never travel in these conditions."

Tank stared at the box.

"Nigga, that's a sample?"

I laughed, then regretted laughing because every muscle in my chest had joined a union against me.

The guards fired again from a neighboring rooftop. Seraphine turned, keeping the wind between the passengers and the shots. She could protect the roof or help unload the plane, but not both forever. Victor tried to stand. Shawna pushed him back beside me.

"You two saved it once. Let somebody else have a fucking turn."

Then she took my gun bag and went to Tank.

My wife and my brother moved to the far wall together. Tank covered the neighboring roof. Shawna fired at the light above its doorway, bursting the glass and forcing the guards back into the dark stairwell behind it. June found a construction cart and began loading cases onto it. Rosa got the shaken passengers moving toward our stairs, speaking to them like they were late for rehearsal and she would not accept excuses.

I watched the people I loved handle the world while my hands shook in my lap.

Victor sat beside me on the pavement, leaning against the tire.

"It is difficult," he said.

"What?"

"Being temporarily less impressive."

"Speak for yourself. I'm still handsome."

"Yes. It is extremely irritating."

I held out the water. He drank, wiped his mouth, and passed it back.

"You knew I'd come?" he asked.

"To the desert?"

"To the end."

I looked at him. Under the scorched uniform and ridiculous hair was the same proud nigga who had once wanted to beat me so badly he could barely stand beside me. Now he had crossed an ocean in a plane full of wedding presents because he knew I was walking into the last fight.

"Yeah," I said. "I knew."

He nodded once.

That was all either of us needed.

Down below, an armored truck crashed through the exhibition hall's front doors.

The floor shook.

June looked over the edge and swore. "They're inside."

The stairs were our way out. The guards were coming up them. Seraphine's wind lashed the roof, but she could not reach inside the building without letting the shooters across the street have us.

I flexed my fingers.

Nothing. No blue light. Only pain and a weak twitch.

Shawna saw.

"Don't."

"I wasn't."

"I know your face."

"Nigga, everybody knows my face. It's famous."

She ignored that and pointed at the silver car.

"Can you drive?"

I looked from the car to the scaffolding bridge.

"With you yelling directions?"

"Always."

We loaded the most shaken passengers into the faculty transport carts that had come out of the cargo hold. Of course Sky High had brought little electric cars inside its big electric emergency. Seraphine covered their crossing as June drove the first one onto the scaffolding bridge. Tank carried the cake sample himself, because some priorities survive war.

Shawna got behind the wheel of my silver car.

"Move over."

I obeyed. Nigga, loving a woman gives her certain privileges over your most expensive possessions and all your least sensible ideas.

She drove us across the swaying bridge while bullets chipped the concrete behind us. Victor lay across the back seats muttering that he would never complain about legroom again. At the terrace, Shawna turned sharply down the ramp.

Below, the armored truck blocked the exhibition hall.

Its driver saw us coming and swung toward the ramp.

Shawna did not slow.

"Baby."

"I see him."

"Baby."

"I married you. I know a bad approach when I see one."

She drove up the sloping side of a marble crate. The silver car tilted onto two wheels. For one impossible second we balanced against the crate while the armored truck passed beneath our raised side. I could see the driver's astonished face through his windshield.

He looked at my wife like he had just discovered a second Aiden and did not have enough bullets for the information.

Then we landed on all four tires and tore through the hall's broken doors.

Victor sat up just enough to shout.

"Mrs. Aiden!"

"Seat belt!" she shouted back.

We reached the hotel with our people, the gifts, the cake sample, and a piece of the plane's fabric still caught in Tank's collar. The courtyard filled with cheers. Cassius ran toward the silver car, saw the new scratches, and put a hand over his heart.

"My gift."

"Your gift saved a plane," I told him.

He looked at Shawna.

She raised an eyebrow.

"And I drove it home."

Cassius bowed so deeply Inez slapped the back of his head.

Vega made me sit in a quiet room with Victor for an hour. She examined my hands, checked my pulse, and told me that if I tried to summon more lightning before I could hold a glass steadily, she would have Tank sit on me.

"That medically necessary?" I asked.

"For my peace of mind."

Seraphine came in carrying the bouquet from the plane. Somehow it had survived with only three petals missing. She placed it in Shawna's hands.

"For your wedding renewal. I wished to bring them personally."

Shawna looked down at the flowers.

"You could have mailed them."

"My dear, your husband would have found that insufficiently dramatic."

I lifted a finger. "She's right."

The dean turned to me. Her expression softened.

"You have assembled a remarkable family, Captain."

Through the open door, I could hear Tank arguing that a sample cake should be sampled by somebody. Rosa was defending it. Mercedes was asking the music instructor if he knew anything filthy. Porsche was telling the hotel owner that a rooftop piano would improve the mood. June and Adrian were describing Shawna's two-wheel stunt to Inez, who kept looking into the room at my wife with growing professional respect.

"Yeah," I said. "I know."

Seraphine took a sealed envelope from her coat.

"The academy sent something else."

I opened it. A photograph slid out: Sky High floating above the clouds, our whole class on the steps, me in the center wearing the captain's badge like the school had been built around it.

On the back, every instructor had signed a message.

At the bottom, Victor had written one line larger than the rest.

IF HE GETS HIMSELF KILLED, I AM GOING TO BE VERY ANGRY.

I looked at him across the room.

The nigga shrugged.

"Motivation."

I folded the photograph carefully and put it inside my jacket, beside the cross.

A siren sounded in the courtyard.

Not the emperor's siren. Mine had a different kind of arrogance.

I went to the window with Shawna's hand on my elbow. Six dark-blue vehicles rolled through the hotel gates, their lights spinning over the palms. The lead car stopped beside my Lamborghini. Chief Gloria Knox stepped out wearing a white uniform, a gold badge, and sunglasses that reflected an entire hotel full of her favorite mistakes.

She looked up at the window.

"Captain!"

"Chief!"

"Why is there an airplane on a shopping center?"

"Exhibition hall!"

"That clears it right up!"

She came upstairs with two officers carrying suitcases. One case held clothes. The other held a ceremonial sword that Las Vegas apparently believed I needed because regular weapons had failed to express its affection.

Knox embraced Shawna first. Then she took my face between both hands and examined me like a mother who had raised a department instead of a child.

"You look tired."

"Saved a plane."

"I saw. Somebody filmed you from a crane. The city council watched it six times."

"They should've been helping."

"They voted to name a road after you."

"I already got one."

"They voted for a better road."

Victor laughed from the couch.

Knox looked at him. "And you're the young man who held it up."

He sat straighter. "Victor Voltage."

"Excellent work. We are considering a roundabout."

"I would accept a tasteful one."

She nodded seriously, then turned back to me.

"I brought twelve volunteers. All grown, all armed, all aware you attract trouble like a free bar attracts off-duty police. They are downstairs helping your people secure the hotel."

"Zero let you through?"

"He tried to charge an arrival tax."

"You pay it?"

She stared at me over her sunglasses.

Nigga, I had forgotten who I was talking to.

"The border captain now owes me money," she said.

Shawna laughed and hugged her again.

Knox set the sword on the table. Its scabbard was blue velvet. The hilt had my initials worked into gold. I lifted it halfway, saw my reflection in the polished blade, and understood why old kings had difficulty being reasonable.

"The city wanted you to have something dignified," she said.

"Nigga, this is a sword with my name on it."

"I argued for two. Budget hearing was difficult."

She waited until the others began discussing where to put the sword before lowering her voice.

"I didn't come for a photograph, Aiden."

I looked at her.

"I know."

"You made my department the most famous in the world. Half the applicants want to be you. The other half want to arrest you and get promoted. I would prefer to keep the original alive."

"Working on it."

She touched the badge on my chest.

"Work harder. Your wife has plans."

Across the room, Shawna was showing Seraphine a photograph of the dress she wanted for Vegas. Rosa had joined them. Three women leaned over a phone while a whole criminal empire waited outside, and none of them looked remotely willing to postpone their celebration.

I looked back at Knox.

"Yes, ma'am."

She smiled.

"There. Best officer I ever hired. Follows instructions when properly threatened."

Outside, the sky was empty again.

Our people had all come down safely.

## 4. Dinner with the Wrong Motherfucker

Nigga, I arrived at the emperor's dinner with my wife, two queens, a police chief, and a sword.

He had said come without my army. I had listened. Tank was in the car park being a completely different problem.

The palace stood on white pillars above an artificial lake. Black boats moved beneath it. Gold windows burned in the evening sun. A staircase wide enough for a cavalry charge climbed from the water to the front doors, and some nigga had put a red carpet down the middle like I might have trouble finding the building.

Shawna stood beside me at the bottom, looking up.

"That's a lot of stairs."

"Nigga don't believe in arriving relaxed."

"You could carry me."

I picked her up.

She laughed so suddenly that the nearest guard turned. Two more guards turned with him. Then half the staircase was watching me carry the most beautiful woman alive into the most dangerous nigga's house.

"Captain," one of them said, "the formal entrance requires—"

"Nigga, this is formal. Look at my wife."

He did. His mouth stayed open.

"Close that," I told him. "You on duty."

Shawna hid her smile against my neck.

At the top, a row of palace servants bowed. I put her down and straightened my jacket. The sword hung at my side in its blue scabbard. Knox had insisted I wear it because she wanted to see the emperor's face when he realized Las Vegas was now sending medieval problems abroad.

Vesper walked beside Magnolia. Nobody asked either woman for a weapon. The niggas at the door had clearly met them before.

"Chief Gloria Knox."

"His Majesty's table admits guests by invitation only."

"I'm supervising an officer."

"That is not—"

"You want him unsupervised?"

The chamberlain looked at me.

I smiled.

The nigga moved aside.

Inside, the dining room stretched across the palace like somebody had tried to feed a whole dynasty without letting them speak privately. A long table shone under gold lamps. Beyond the windows, the city glittered far below. At the end of the room, Emperor Zero stood beside a throne that had no business being anywhere near soup.

He wore black. No crown. No mask. Just a hard, handsome face that had spent too many years hearing yes from frightened people. Roland Ruin stood behind his chair. Another man sat near the middle of the table, broad and blond, with a diamond pin in his tie and nervous fingers.

Zero watched us approach.

"Captain. You have expanded my invitation."

"Nigga, your dining table long enough to land the plane you shot. I figured you had room."

His eyes narrowed at the mention of the transport.

"An unfortunate misunderstanding."

"Two engines worth."

Shawna took my hand before the room could feel how badly I wanted to put that nigga through his own window.

Zero looked at her hand in mine.

"A devoted marriage."

"You said that like you're allergic," she replied.

Magnolia laughed softly. Roland stared at the floor. I watched him trying not to smile and decided the nigga might possess one redeemable muscle.

We sat.

The blond man rose to greet us.

"Gideon Gold. Transport and hospitality."

"Aiden. Bad timing and excellent cars."

"I have heard."

"Nigga, everybody heard. You live under an emperor, not a rock."

He laughed too quickly. His eyes moved toward Zero before he sat. I noticed. Shawna noticed me noticing. Under the table, her thumb pressed once against my knuckles.

Dinner began with silver plates and food arranged so carefully I suspected it had been threatened.

"Depends what the nigga asking me did that morning."

Knox drank her wine without looking up.

Vesper set her fork down.

"You could end this tonight."

"And what would you have me end, Vesper?"

"The theater. You know what we found in Midnight City. You know who signed the promises you broke."

For the first time, the nigga did not have an answer ready.

She did not take out the ledger pages. She did not need to. She named three people and two dates. Magnolia watched him over the rim of her glass. Gideon's fingers stopped moving.

"Paper travels poorly in the desert."

"Copies travel beautifully," Vesper said.

Nigga, I loved when somebody else at the table brought ammunition that did not stain the curtains.

Zero leaned back.

"You imagine my people will abandon me because you show them figures?"

"Your people will make their own choices," Shawna said. "You should try letting them."

He looked at her as if she had reached across the table and slapped him with his own napkin.

"And you chose this man."

"Every day."

"Despite what he is."

"Nigga, especially knowing what he is."

I looked at my wife.

She kept her eyes on Zero.

"You keep saying captain, king, criminal like you hope one word cancels another. I know who I married. He knows who I married before. None of that is your dinner conversation."

Magnolia set down her glass with a tiny click.

"Excellent," she said.

Zero's jaw hardened.

"You are a singer, are you not?"

"I am."

"Perhaps you would perform."

I started to stand.

Shawna tightened her hand.

"I'd love to."

She rose before I could speak. The room watched her cross to a piano near the window. Its player stood immediately, bowing like he had been waiting his whole life for an opportunity to stop working for that nigga.

Shawna sat.

She did not sing to Zero.

She sang to me.

The first note changed the room's air. Her voice moved low over the table, warm and sure. No begging. No trembling little performance for a man with a throne. She gave me the song as if all those guards and gold lamps had been invited to witness something they could never buy.

I forgot the sword against my leg. I forgot Roland. For a minute I forgot that a nigga at the other end of the table wanted to kill us.

Then Gideon leaned toward me.

"My fiancée is in the western tower," he whispered.

I did not turn my head.

"Name."

"Liora. Thirty-three. She manages the airship berths. Zero accused her of allowing an unauthorized departure."

"She do it?"

"Yes. Her sister was aboard."

"Then your woman sounds smart."

His breath caught.

"He said if I helped you, he would never let her leave."

Nigga, fear had made the man shiny. Sweat at his temples. Fingers crushing his napkin. He had brought his whole heart to a table where somebody else had put it on the menu.

"Listen to the song," I said.

"Captain—"

"Nigga. Listen. Then watch what happens."

Shawna finished on a note so gentle the room stayed quiet after it was gone.

The first person to applaud was a guard by the window.

He caught himself too late.

I stood and applauded with him.

Then Knox. Magnolia. Vesper. Gideon. The pianist. The servants. Roland tapped two fingers against his palm before Zero looked at him.

Shawna rose, took my hand, and returned to her chair with the whole room watching like the emperor had invited the sun indoors and discovered he could not tell it where to shine.

"Beautiful," Zero said.

"Yeah," I said. "Nigga, I know."

He smiled without warmth.

"A pity such things are fragile."

The doors closed.

Every guard in the room moved his hand toward a weapon.

Magnolia sighed.

"At dinner, really?"

Zero stood.

"Captain Aiden. You will place the Crown of Midnight under my protection and depart this city by morning. Your friends may accompany you."

"Nigga, I didn't bring the crown."

"Then send for it."

"Didn't bring a desire to obey you either."

Roland drew his pistol.

Knox drew hers beneath the table.

Vesper stood so smoothly her chair barely moved. Magnolia lifted her cane. Shawna's hand disappeared behind her dress and came back holding the little pistol she had selected that afternoon while asking me whether the silver earrings looked better than the blue ones.

I put one hand on the sword.

Zero looked at it.

"You cannot be serious."

"Nigga, they gave me a sword. What you think I was gonna do, point at dinner?"

The first shot shattered a lamp.

Shawna pulled Magnolia behind the table. Knox fired toward the doorway. I drew the sword and cut the hanging cord above the side curtains. Heavy blue fabric crashed down between us and the guards by the windows. The niggas fired into silk while I kicked my chair into Roland's knees.

He stumbled. I grabbed his wrist and drove it against the table. His gun skidded through a plate of untouched fish.

"That was expensive," he snapped.

"Nigga, so am I."

He hit me hard in the mouth.

I respected the punch. Then I returned it with interest.

Roland went backward into a serving cart. Silver dishes crashed around him. Zero had vanished through a panel behind the throne. Of course the nigga had a private exit built into his personality.

Gideon crawled beneath the table.

"Western tower!" he shouted.

"I heard you!"

Tank's voice broke over the small radio in my pocket.

"Brother, your dinner loud as shit!"

"We arguing over the check!"

"I got the car!"

"Get to the western side!"

Shawna and Vesper moved toward the side door while Knox covered them. Magnolia struck a guard's wrist with her cane, hard enough to make him drop his gun and reconsider what sixty-four meant. I caught the gun before it hit the floor, fired toward the main doorway, and followed my wife into a corridor lined with portraits of Zero looking emotionally unavailable in different outfits.

No argument. No question about why the night had acquired another person. This was why that nigga at the table could never understand us.

The corridor opened onto a glass bridge between the palace and the tower. Below us, black water gleamed under the moon. Above, security lights snapped on.

Gideon caught up, gasping.

"She's on the third landing."

"You go with Vesper and Magnolia," I said. "Knox, take them down."

"Captain—"

"Chief, I'm being excellent. Give me room."

She looked at my bleeding lip, then at Shawna's gun.

"Bring your wife back. She is the sensible officer."

Shawna kissed my cheek.

"Hear that?"

"Nigga, I hear everything."

We crossed the bridge together.

Halfway across, the lights went out.

Then the bridge moved.

Not shook. Moved. The whole glass walkway began withdrawing toward the palace, opening a gap between its far end and the tower.

Shawna looked down.

"Aiden."

"I see it."

Behind us, guards appeared in the doorway. Ahead, the gap widened over the lake. We had come to save a woman and discovered the emperor could put a moat in the middle of a hallway.

"Nigga got money," I said.

"So do you. Jump."

We ran.

I fired once behind us. The guards ducked. Shawna reached the end first, launched herself across the gap, and caught the tower railing. I followed, boots leaving the glass as the last panel slid away beneath me.

For a second there was only water below and my wife's hand ahead.

I caught the railing.

She caught my wrist.

"You heavy," she said through her teeth.

"That's greatness, baby."

"Greatness needs to pull its ass up."

I did.

We rolled onto the tower landing together. A guard came around the corner. Shawna put her gun on him before he finished inhaling.

"Door," she said.

The nigga pointed.

"Key."

He handed it over.

"Excellent employee," I told him.

Liora stood inside a locked room wearing a gray dress and a face full of furious patience. She was thirty-three, dark-haired, with a gold ring on a chain around her neck. When Shawna opened the door, she looked from us to the broken bridge outside.

"Gideon finally did something?"

"Nigga did enough," I said. "Come on."

She picked up a small bag.

"I've been packed for six days."

We went down the tower stairs instead of back toward the palace. At the bottom, a boat dock opened onto the artificial lake. Tank stood on a black launch, holding a gun in one hand and the wheel in the other. Rosa sat behind him wearing his jacket over her green dress.

"Get in!" he yelled.

I looked at the boat.

"Nigga, where the Mercedes?"

"Parked! I got hobbies!"

We jumped aboard.

Tank drove away from the dock as the tower doors burst open behind us. Bullets struck the water. Rosa fired back. Shawna put Liora behind the seats, then stood beside me as the launch tore beneath the palace's pillars.

A second boat came after us.

Roland drove it.

That nigga had recovered from the fish quickly.

He fired as he came. Tank cut between two pillars. The boat leaned hard. I caught Shawna around the waist and held her against me while she fired toward Roland's windshield.

Glass burst.

He ducked but kept driving.

Ahead, a low service arch opened beneath the palace gardens. Tank aimed for it.

"How low?" I shouted.

"Lower than the last fucking tunnel!"

We ducked.

The arch scraped the top of our launch. Metal screamed. We shot through in a shower of sparks, emerged on the far side, and heard a much louder crash behind us.

Roland's boat had been taller.

Nigga, geometry finally arrested somebody for me.

We reached the western dock where Knox waited with the cars. Gideon ran to Liora before the launch had stopped moving. He caught her as she stepped up, held her so tightly she struck his back with the bag.

"I can breathe or I can marry you," she told him. "I would prefer both."

He laughed against her hair.

I watched them for a second. Shawna stood beside me, her hand warm in mine, the hem of her dress wet from the boat.

"You okay?" she asked.

"Nigga punched my mouth."

She examined my lip.

"Still pretty."

"That's what I was worried about."

She kissed the uninjured side and led me toward the car.

Behind us, the palace lights blazed over the lake. Zero remained inside, alive and furious, with his dinner cold and his prisoner gone.

At the hotel, Magnolia insisted on dessert.

"We survived an emperor's manners," she said. "We deserve sugar."

Tank sat beside Rosa with his boat keys on the table. I looked at them.

"You keep those?"

"Nigga, I stole a boat for love. I'm not giving back the keys."

I raised my glass.

"To love."

Every nigga at that table raised a glass with me.
