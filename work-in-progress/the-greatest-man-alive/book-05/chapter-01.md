## 1. The City That Stayed Up for Me

Midnight City had built a throne at the airport.

I thought that was considerate as hell until a nigga in a silver cape told me it wasn't mine.

"It's for the victor of the tournament."

"Then why you making me walk past it?"

He had a tall hat, a little mustache, and twenty men behind him holding gold rifles. Every rifle turned toward me at the same time. Shawna stepped out of the police Lamborghini, stretched after the flight, and took one look at the situation.

"Baby, we just landed."

"I know. These niggas already hiding my furniture."

The silver cape made a noise in his throat. Tank got out of the next car carrying a garment bag in one hand and the expression he usually wore before putting somebody through a closed door. Rosa was still in the car doing her lipstick. She rolled down the window to see who was about to interrupt her vacation.

"Is that your king?" she asked the silver cape.

"I am Lord Octavian Rusk, master of ceremonies."

"Then do a ceremony where you get out of the way."

Behind us, the whole airport had stopped pretending it was busy. Pilots watched from the stairs of their jets. Baggage men stood on their carts. A woman in a white flight uniform took her sunglasses off slowly, like her eyes deserved this without anything between them and me.

I had come to Midnight City because Emperor Zero thought the world was something he could buy, and General Solomon Saint had died proving that a nigga can have a whole army and still pick the wrong island. We had found enough money under Isla Gloria to make rich men look poor. We had also found enough of Zero's secrets to make him want all of us dead.

He had sent an invitation anyway.

Black card. Silver letters. My name bigger than the address.

COME AND CLAIM WHAT YOU THINK YOU DESERVE.

Shawna had read it twice, then asked if there would be dancing.

That was why I loved my wife. A man could threaten the future of everybody we knew and this woman would still make sure I packed my good shoes.

"Lord whatever," I said. "I'm tired. My wife looks beautiful. My brother's got a suit in that bag that cost more than your first apartment. Either you show us to our rooms or you move that throne closer so I can sit down while you keep talking."

Rusk's face tightened. A few of the riflemen looked at each other. That little look meant they had already realized the same thing I had: their boss was standing too close to my hood.

Then somebody started clapping.

Slow. Three times.

A woman came down the airport steps in a dark green coat with nothing timid about it. Black hair pinned high. A gold snake around one wrist. Two guards carrying her luggage like they were transporting the last clean water on earth.

She was thirty-seven, I found out later, and she had the kind of face that could make a man apologize for a crime she had committed.

"Aiden," she said.

"That's usually who it is."

"Queen Vesper Vane."

"Queen of what?"

"You ask that before you kiss my hand?"

"Nigga, I'm married before I ask either."

Shawna laughed. Vesper looked at her, then at the wedding ring, then back at me with a smile that got sharper instead of smaller.

"How unfortunate for the city."

"The city's gonna survive," Shawna said. "I'm less sure about your master of ceremonies."

Vesper turned one finger and the gold rifles lowered.

"Lord Rusk mistakes anticipation for authority. Your rooms are ready. The opening procession is in forty minutes."

"We just flew in."

"The kings wait for nobody."

A jet engine started somewhere behind me. My police badge shone in the sun. I was looking at a queen, a throne, a thousand windows full of nosy rich people, and a city that thought forty minutes was enough time to put my wife in a dress.

"They finna learn," I said.

Our hotel had once been a palace. You could tell because getting to the bathroom required crossing land somebody had probably died over. Marble stairs, red carpets, chandeliers thick as trees. Two young men tried to take Shawna's bags and accidentally competed for the same handle until she assigned them separate suitcases.

Our suite overlooked the harbor. Modern yachts sat under stone walls older than everybody's grudges. Ferraris crawled along the waterfront between flower stalls and armed men drinking coffee. Above the city, on a black hill, Zero's palace stood with every window shut against the afternoon.

Shawna walked out onto the balcony and let the sea wind move her hair.

"That's pretty."

I looked at her instead.

"Yeah."

"You didn't even look."

"I looked where I wanted."

She turned with that smile I had crossed whole cities to keep, and I caught her around the waist before she could say something that would have had me canceling the tournament. For a minute there were no kings. No invitations. No bullet scars. Just my wife with her hands at my collar, straightening something that hadn't been crooked until she decided it needed attention.

"You gonna behave tonight?"

"Beautifully."

"Aiden."

"I'm gonna misbehave beautifully."

She kissed me once, hard enough to make that answer official.

A knock came at the door. Tank carried in a black case with the hotel crest on it.

"Somebody sent you a welcome present."

I opened it. Inside was a pistol plated in white gold, a key to a red Ferrari, and a note.

FOR THE MAN WHO NEEDS HELP ARRIVING ON TIME.

"Who sent it?"

"Vesper."

Shawna read the note over my shoulder.

"She better send me something."

Another knock. Rosa came through holding a long green box.

"That queen sent you emerald earrings," she told Shawna. "I was gonna steal them, but Tank looked disappointed before I even did it."

"I was looking at your shoes."

"You were looking with judgment."

"Baby, one heel is a weapon."

"Both of them are weapons. Keep up."

Shawna opened the earrings, admired them, and set them beside the mirror without putting them on. She chose a blue pair I had given her instead. Nigga, my whole chest went stupid. People kept trying to give me cities, but my wife choosing my earrings could still knock the sense out of me faster than any man alive.

By the time we came downstairs, the procession had started without us.

There were drums outside. Horns. Men on black horses. Women in gowns riding in open cars. Every balcony on the avenue had a banner hanging from it. We were supposed to join at the front, behind the ceremonial carriage carrying an empty cushion where the Crown of Midnight would eventually sit.

The Ferrari was waiting at the curb.

So was a young hotel manager who looked ready to start a new life somewhere else.

"Captain Aiden, the avenue has been closed."

"Open it."

"I cannot. The procession route is sealed."

I looked at the route. I looked at the Ferrari. I looked at the elevated tram line running down the middle of the street.

Tank saw me looking.

"Nigga."

"What?"

"That's a train track."

"It's a road with confidence."

Shawna was already getting into the passenger seat.

"Don't scratch my side."

Rosa grabbed Tank's sleeve before he could climb in behind us.

"We taking the normal road. I want to arrive with my hairstyle still believing in God."

I drove the Ferrari through the hotel courtyard, past a fountain, up a service ramp, and onto the low platform where the trams came through. A bell rang. People screamed. A tram driver leaned out and saw me coming toward him in a car that cost enough to buy his entire argument.

I took the outside rail.

The Ferrari tilted.

Shawna held her hair with one hand and my thigh with the other.

"Aiden."

"I got it."

"I know. I'm saying your name because I like it."

Nigga, I almost missed the turn.

We dropped from the platform into the procession between two marching bands. One band stopped. The other started playing louder like they were trying to survive through talent. My front tires landed inches from a drummer's shoes. He looked at me, looked at the Ferrari, and kept the beat.

I threw him a stack of money.

"That's professionalism, my nigga."

The crowd recognized the badge first. Then my face. Then the red car where no red car was supposed to be.

"Aiden!"

Somebody yelled it from a balcony. Another voice caught it. By the time we reached the empty royal carriage, the whole avenue was saying my name.

Shawna stood just enough in her seat to wave. Her blue dress caught the sun. Cameras turned away from kings who had spent years preparing to be looked at and pointed at my wife arriving late with me.

A man in a purple limousine slammed his fist against his window.

"You have broken the order!"

"Nigga, I improved it!"

That was when the first shot hit the ceremonial carriage.

The empty cushion jumped. A horse reared. The music tore apart into separate frightened noises.

I saw three black vans pull out from a side street. Men in white masks stood through their roof openings. They weren't firing into the crowd. Their guns tracked the cars carrying the visiting kings. One burst shattered the purple limousine's windshield. Another took the mirror off my Ferrari.

Shawna dropped into her seat.

"Now you can scratch my side."

I hit the gas.

The Ferrari crossed in front of the carriage as the next burst came. Glass broke above us. I pulled the white-gold pistol from its case and fired toward the nearest masked man while Shawna grabbed a fallen parade banner and threw it across the windshield of the van coming alongside.

The driver saw nothing but the city's royal crest.

He hit a flower cart so hard roses filled the air like the street had exploded romantically.

"Beautiful throw, baby!"

"Beautiful gun! Who gave you that?"

"We can argue after!"

The second van came at the carriage. Its driver wanted the road open and didn't care how many horses had to disappear to make that happen. I cut between them and caught his bumper with the Ferrari's back corner. The whole car jolted. My shoulder slammed the seat. Blue heat sparked inside my arms, that thunder I could call for a little while if I was willing to pay for it afterward.

I didn't spend it yet.

A little power was good. Knowing when a nigga needed to keep some was better.

I drove up onto the broad stone steps of the opera house. People scattered behind columns. The van followed until its front end struck a statue base and stopped hard enough to throw the masked gunman against his own roof.

The third van turned toward the harbor.

I went after it.

"Seat belt," I said.

Shawna pulled it tight.

"You say that every time we're already flying."

"Because you listen beautifully under pressure."

We passed fish stalls, a wedding party, and a priest who lifted his hands at exactly the right moment for our car to miss his sleeves. The van cut onto a loading pier. A cargo crane stood over the water with a steel container hanging from it. Beyond the pier, Vesper's black speedboat waited with its engines running.

The masked men were not trying to escape the city.

They were trying to reach her boat.

I saw Vesper on the upper dock. She saw me see her.

Neither one of us smiled.

The van braked beneath the crane. Three men jumped out. One carried a narrow silver case that must have come from the carriage during the confusion. The cushion had been empty. Something beneath it hadn't been.

I hit the blue thunder.

For three seconds the whole world became sharp.

My hands, the wheel, the engine, the air in my teeth. The Ferrari leaped forward with blue fire running across the hood. Shawna shouted my name and it sounded like a song somebody had turned all the way up.

We went off the end of the loading ramp.

The car struck the hanging container with its front wheels, rode the metal side at an angle no mechanic on earth would have signed his name under, and dropped onto the dock between the men and the boat.

Every window on the pier shook when we landed.

My arms went dead-heavy. That was the price. I let the thunder go before it took more than I had to spend.

One masked man raised his weapon.

Shawna opened her door into him.

The second slipped on the roses still caught beneath our tire. I put him down before he could stand. The third held the silver case over the water.

"Stop or I drop it!"

I got out slowly, my legs arguing about the landing.

"Nigga, you keep threatening me with stuff I don't know the price of."

"It belongs to the emperor!"

"That explains why you're scared."

He threw it.

I caught the case against my chest with one hand and caught him by his coat with the other. For a second we were both leaning out over the harbor. He stared at the water. I stared at him.

"Can you swim?"

"No!"

"Then stop wearing shoes that expensive near the edge."

I dragged him back and dropped him on the dock. Harbor police were finally arriving, breathless, uniforms immaculate enough to prove they'd missed most of the trouble.

Their chief took one look at my badge.

"Captain Aiden?"

"Arrest these niggas. And somebody get my wife a different car. This one got opinions now."

The chief saluted.

Nigga, he saluted so hard his hat nearly left him.

Vesper came down the steps without hurrying. Her guards stayed above. She looked at the destroyed Ferrari, the captured attackers, my wife standing on the dock with one emerald box still in her handbag, and the silver case in my hands.

"You are very difficult to welcome."

"You sent the car."

"I expected you to use the street."

"You should have said that."

I opened the case. Inside lay a strip of black velvet and a heavy gold seal marked with a crown. No jewels. No magic. Just an old piece of authority a whole lot of men had nearly died trying to move from one place to another.

Vesper's eyes changed when she saw it.

"The signet of invitation," she said. "It admits one claimant to the private rounds."

"I thought I was already invited."

"You were invited to watch."

Shawna came beside me and closed the case.

"That's embarrassing for whoever sent the invitation."

By evening the city had put my face on the side of a building.

I hadn't asked. Nigga, I had been taking a shower. I came out in a towel and there I was outside our balcony, forty feet tall, stepping out of the wrecked Ferrari with the silver case in my hand. Underneath it, somebody had written THE KING FROM THE WEST.

Tank stood at the balcony rail with a drink.

"They got your good side."

"I ain't got a bad one."

"Your left shoulder looks tired."

"Nigga, that's history."

Shawna sat at the vanity putting on my blue earrings. Her dress for dinner was black, fitted, and personally disrespectful to every other plan I had made that night.

I went behind her and put my hands on her shoulders.

"We can stay in."

"You just stole a king's invitation."

"I can steal dinner too."

She looked at me in the mirror.

"I want to see what they do when you walk in."

So did I.

Downstairs, Lord Rusk waited by the throne from the airport. It had somehow reached the hotel before our luggage. The silver cape was gone. His new coat was black, and there was a bandage across one eyebrow where the parade had given him a different opinion about the afternoon.

"Captain," he said carefully. "The council has acknowledged your claim."

"Good."

"There are rules."

I sat down on the throne to adjust my cuff.

Every conversation in the lobby stopped.

Shawna stood beside me, her hand resting on my shoulder like she had been born knowing where a crown would eventually fit.

"Aight," I told Rusk. "Start with the interesting ones."
