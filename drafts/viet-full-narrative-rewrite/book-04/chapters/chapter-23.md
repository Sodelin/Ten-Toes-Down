## Chapter 23: The Man in the Wrong Lane

Long hit me before I finished the breath after my invitation.

I slipped his first fist. The second drove my raised forearm into my face and lifted both feet off the grass. I landed against the tire stack by the service opening. Long reached me before the upper tire finished wobbling.

I kicked it at his knees. He stepped through it and punched the place my head had occupied. Rubber split against the post behind the stack. The post bent, dragging wire through the grass. I rolled left, came up under his next arm, and hit his stomach twice.

The second made him grunt. I went to his damaged nose with my right. Once, twice, three times. Blood came over his lip. On the fourth he caught my wrist and squeezed until the bones seemed to be discussing a merger.

“Enough,” Long said.

“Your turn,” I told him.

I stepped beneath the trapped arm and drove my elbow up under his jaw. His teeth cracked together. He loosened his grip; I tore free, shoved his face sideways, and kicked the side of his knee. He dropped an inch and became much angrier without becoming sufficiently shorter.

Long caught my shirt and threw me through the service gap. My back struck the lower barrier. A section of plastic tore from its mountings. I landed on the banking, slid downhill, and stopped against the inside curb with all my ribs speaking at once.

He stepped onto the track behind me. Smoke drifted across the infield lights. Engines and shouted instructions came from the east. I couldn't see the Crown Wagon anymore. I hoped that meant it had gone where I wanted.

Long picked up the broken barrier. Sand poured from its split end. He raised it over one shoulder as though preparing a badly organized beach holiday.

“Too much talking,” Long told me.

“People pay for appearances,” I said.

He threw it. I dived uphill. The barrier burst where I'd been, spraying sand over the lower lane. I rolled and pushed into a crouch. My left arm shook. Long walked through the broken plastic, his shoes slipping on what he'd scattered.

I ran higher onto the banking. He followed. The slope made his upper foot travel farther than his lower one. I watched three uneven steps, waited until his weight settled, and turned into him.

My right foot hit his outside ankle. My shoulder hit his chest. We slid downhill together with his larger body underneath. He caught my belt before the curb and twisted.

I hit concrete first. Breath vanished. Long landed on top with a knee across my thigh. His forearm pressed beneath my jaw, forcing my head over the curb edge. The lights broke into white flecks around his face.

I pushed against his wrist with both hands. It didn't move. Blood dripped from his nose onto my shirt.

“There,” Long said quietly.

I stopped pushing upward. My thumb went into the scarred fold of his flattened ear while I twisted my hips. He roared. The ear tore under my nail; hot blood slicked my thumb. His weight shifted off my leg. I dragged it free, hooked a heel against the curb, and shoved sideways. His arm slipped off my throat. Air came back painfully. I struck the damaged ear again before he could reestablish the hold.

Long rolled away and stood. I crawled three feet before my knees would go underneath me. The hand dressing hung in two strips. I pulled one away with my teeth, wound the other across my palm, and tightened it until my eyes watered.

He watched. Blood ran down his neck now, soaking his collar. A little piece of white lay on the track between us. Tooth or paint, I couldn't tell. I didn't intend to examine it closely.

“Could have kept your building,” Long said.

“Still planning to,” I said.

“You won't own tomorrow,” Long said.

My left knee nearly folded when I stood. I flexed it twice. “Does Thành rehearse you?”

Long punched my mouth. I'd seen the shoulder turn and moved too late. My lower lip split against my teeth. I stumbled uphill, swallowed blood, and tasted the expensive water from the harbor as though my stomach had filed an objection too.

His next fist passed beside my head. I caught his sleeve and drove my right elbow against the back of his arm. The joint bent beyond its useful range. He grunted and swung the other hand into my left shoulder.

Something inside the joint made a short, wet sound. The arm dropped. Pain reached my fingertips, then disappeared into numbness that frightened me more.

I kept his sleeve in my right hand, stepped across his feet, and pulled his injured arm over my shoulder. Momentum brought him up. Only a moment, only because he was moving and I'd got underneath, but both his feet left the ground.

I turned and drove my legs straight. Long went downhill and hit the inner barrier. Its mounts tore free; a whole white section folded beneath him. I dropped onto one knee.

He rolled onto his hands. His right elbow no longer straightened. He looked at it with the same offended concentration Hậu had given his shoulder. That pleased me more than it should have.

An engine rose behind us. The black Meridian racer entered through the western access gate, bumper hanging from its cinema damage. A different driver had it now. He saw Long down and me kneeling on the banking, then accelerated straight toward me.

I watched the front wheel wobble. I watched his hands. Distance disappeared with no consideration for the condition of my legs.

One heartbeat. Two.

I jumped.

For an instant my shoes were level with the driver's face. Then above his windshield. I cleared the roof without touching it and struck the upper barrier feet-first, high enough for my head to rise above the catch fence. My right hand caught the mesh. It shook all the way to the next post.

“More than intended,” I told the empty air.

Below, the racer hit Long.

He caught the hood. Its front crumpled around his hands, folding into an open metal mouth. Tires drove him backward through the sand until his heels met the broken barrier. He roared, lifted the nose, and swung it sideways. The rear wheels kept spinning while the car rolled onto a flank. The windshield burst. The driver fell sideways, then began clawing out through the empty front.

Long let the car settle. Its engine coughed once and died. His hands bled across the buckled hood. He looked up at me hanging on the fence.

I let go and landed in the upper lane. My knees bent too far; I caught myself with my right hand and stood carefully.

“Nice parking,” I told Long.

He came uphill carrying a torn metal support from the barrier, longer than my arm, jagged at one end. Behind him the driver crawled free. I pointed toward the access gate and told him to keep crawling. He did.

Long swung the support across my chest. I stepped back. Its tip opened my shirt and cut a hot line across the upper arm. Blood ran toward my elbow. The next swing struck the fence rail, throwing sparks along the narrow upper lane.

Fence to my left, banking down to my right, Long in front. Behind me the lane narrowed at the pedestrian bridge support. I could see him working out the same limits. He smiled through the blood on his teeth.

I ran at him.

The smile disappeared. His metal bar descended. I slipped inside its long arc, caught his injured right elbow in the crook of my good arm, and drove my forehead into his nose again. Something crushed flatter. He shouted, caught the back of my neck with his left hand, and slammed my head into the concrete bridge support.

The world shifted sideways. I lost his elbow. He drove a knee into my ribs. I folded around it and couldn't stop the sound coming out of me.

Long lifted my face by my hair. The jagged support rose in his other hand.

I caught it with my right palm. Metal bit deep. Blood ran between my fingers and down his wrist. He pressed toward my throat. My good arm shook so hard I could hear my sleeve scrape the concrete.

His injured elbow stayed tucked against his side. His upper foot stood beside a loose cover at the bridge base. I stamped its near edge. The far edge rose under his heel. His foot slipped.

I turned my head, let the jagged end pass beside my neck, and drove into him. The bar struck concrete, glanced away, and jammed between two rail lengths. His hand remained closed around it.

I hit his injured elbow with my knee. Something broke decisively. He released the bar. I did too before it took more palm with it.

Long struck with his left. I caught the blow on my shoulder and went into the fence. Links opened behind me. My belt and shirt caught me halfway through. He staggered toward the loose cover while I worked myself back onto the track one bloody grip at a time.

We stopped for a moment. Floodlights hummed. A siren began far away on the ridge road. His breathing rasped. Mine came short and wet through my split mouth. His right forearm hung wrong.

“Stop,” I told Long.

He looked at me. I stood clear of the broken mesh, unable to raise my left arm.

“You won't get another chance,” I said.

He bent, lifted the loose cover in his left hand, and threw it at my legs. I jumped back, but its edge caught my ankle and folded the foot beneath me. I fell.

Long crossed the space and seized my throat. He lifted me into the torn fence. My feet left the track. Wire cut my back while his thumb pressed beneath my jaw. He pushed me through the gap toward the drop beyond the outer wall.

I caught the upper rail with my right hand. It bent. Long pushed harder. I tore the rail out of its bracket and swung the loose end into his left wrist.

His hand opened. I dropped to the track and dragged air into my throat. Turning beneath his arm, I hit the rail across his knees. He fell forward. I struck the side of his head once more before he could catch himself.

Long rolled down the banking. I followed with the bent rail dragging in my right hand, leaving red marks across the painted lines. At the lower curb he tried standing. His knee folded. He tried again and got one foot beneath him.

I threw the rail away. He saw my empty hand and came with his left fist raised.

I stepped inside it, caught his wrist, and drove my knee beneath his ribs. Then I turned him into the exposed end of the broken barrier post.

His back hit steel. The jagged post went through his jacket and into him. His body stopped while his hand remained clenched around my sleeve.

I stepped back. He tried to pull free. His good hand slid in the blood beneath him. His legs gave way, leaving him settled against the post with his head turned toward the lights.

I watched until his chest stopped moving.

The ring on his little finger rested against torn fabric. A strip of clean skin remained beneath it. I looked at that for a second, then looked away and pressed my forearm across my ribs. The siren was closer now. I waited for my breathing to become something I could use.

Tess called. It took two attempts to get the phone out.

“Western bend,” I told her.

“Long?” Tess asked.

I looked back at him. “Finished.”

She exhaled. “Stay. I'm coming.”

Across the infield a door opened under the northern stand. Thành emerged with Coulter and two men. One carried a little case. They turned toward the tunnel to the Ducal. Thành glanced at the blocked road, then the smoke. He hadn't seen me.

I pushed away from the barrier.

“I see him,” I told Tess.

“You sound as if you shouldn't see anything standing up,” Tess said.

“Car at the north end,” I said.

“Doctor,” Tess replied.

“They can share,” I said.

I put the phone away, tightened the bloody strip around my palm, and tested my ankle. It held badly. I took a breath that reached below the pain and started across the infield.
