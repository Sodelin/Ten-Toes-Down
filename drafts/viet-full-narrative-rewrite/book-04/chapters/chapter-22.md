## Chapter 22: Everybody Gets a Ride

Rafa stood at the bottom of the apron with both arms raised, refusing the Crown Wagon entry into his understanding of vehicles.

“No!” Rafa shouted.

I leaned from its side door. “We discussed this route.”

“With a car! You've brought a lounge!” Rafa said.

Bullets rang against the rear armor. Louis ducked beside the prize cases and swore with unexpected range. Rafa looked past us toward the gunfire and lowered his arms.

“Fine,” Rafa shouted. “Bring your fucking lounge.”

Khải cleared loose barriers from the lower lane. Tess held the utility truck away by presenting different lengths of Argent across its path. Every time it tried turning around her, she reversed far enough to offer another few yards of silver hood. Its driver couldn't find an angle he wanted to pay for.

Nessa knelt beside Eddie. “Straight until axle two comes over. Then a little left. A little, Eddie. You aren't driving your old bus.”

The front wheels dropped onto the first step. The display cabin pitched. A cash case slid into Louis's boot; he stopped it and made a face at the practical disadvantages of feet. I gripped the brass rail. The second axle crushed the next stone edge. Rafa backed away from the rubble.

Then the wagon stopped. Its rear skirt caught the upper landing. Eddie touched the accelerator; wheels spun without useful contact.

Nessa looked back. “Rear travel frame.”

I climbed out into a narrow strip between the armor and a broken rail. Shots struck the far side. Stone chips jumped beside my shoes. Tess drove forward, forcing the utility truck away from our rear and into the covered pit entrance. For a few seconds its shooters lost their view.

I crouched beneath the wagon's back corner. Its crossmember rested on the top step, lifting weight off the tires. I found a grip with each hand and told Eddie to move when Nessa called it.

I lifted. My left shoulder burned. The rear frame rose half an inch, then more. The step under my right foot cracked. I shifted to its lower edge before it broke away completely and lifted again.

The wagon crawled forward over me. I took short steps beneath it, bent low, its weight traveling through my arms and back. Blood made the steel slippery. I tightened my fingers until its edge bent. Nessa shouted clear.

I dropped sideways. The rear wheels hit the lower lane. The whole cabin bounced; a shelf broke inside and expensive water flew through the luxury accommodations.

Rafa slapped the armor and shouted for Eddie to keep moving. I lay flat for one breath, looking at the floodlights. Saint appeared above me.

“Do you want a picture?” Saint asked.

“I'd like a new nervous system,” I said. “Can you arrange that?”

He offered a hand instead. I stood slowly enough to meet each injury individually. His smile went when he saw my dressing.

“Are you all right?” Saint asked.

“Ask me after we get everyone to the hotel,” I told him.

The lane narrowed under a pedestrian bridge. Moses's red transporter backed carefully toward the eastern court, full of staff and wounded crew, with June's security chief guiding it by flashlight. At the bridge opening Hậu had met three men trying to close a portable barrier.

Two lay on the ground. The third hung from Hậu's right arm, kicking at his knee. Hậu stopped, head-butted him, and sat him firmly on the barrier's base. The man fell off.

“Near enough,” Hậu said.

His left shoulder stayed stiff against his body while he dragged the barrier aside. Mara would have opinions. Hậu saw me running beside the wagon and asked where Long was.

“Somewhere ugly,” I told him.

“He owes me a window,” Hậu said.

“I'll add it to the list,” I said.

Hậu shook his head. “I've got a list of my own.”

He stepped onto the base, bent his knees, and shoved with his good shoulder. The barrier rolled clear. His sling caught a bolt. He tore it free, swore, and kicked the base the final foot so Eddie could pass.

Saint stayed with Hậu to direct the next group toward transport. A woman in a chef's jacket put wrapped sandwiches into Hậu's good hand. He looked at her as though somebody had finally understood the emergency.

“Proper support,” Hậu said.

I climbed back aboard the wagon as it entered the infield. Louis knelt among the sealed cases. The trophy was a silver crown on black stone. Irene's red seals remained intact on the money and property stakes. Felix's folding chair leaned against the wall with PROPERTY OF FELIX DUNN. NOT PART OF WAGER taped to its back.

“He labeled it,” Louis observed.

“He knows us,” I said.

Nessa returned from the cab. Both tunnel ends were blocked now. The eastern road was our remaining exit, but the connecting lane filled with people moving toward the court. We couldn't drive the wagon through them without pushing them into open gunfire.

Eddie eased to a walking pace and watched a cook limping through the lane with another worker supporting her. A wheel from a broken catering trolley lay across their path. She tried stepping over it, couldn't, and began turning back.

“Hold,” I told Eddie.

I stepped down, kicked the wheel into the grass, and gave the cook my right hand. She gripped the bandage before she saw the blood. I kept my face still until she had her weight over the obstruction. Her colleague took the other arm.

“Your hand,” the cook said.

“Still attached,” I told her. “Red transporter. Almost there.”

She nodded and continued. Behind them a young porter stopped with two cases, looked at the wagon's open cabin, and asked whether these were ours. They held hotel table linen. Louis reached toward him, stopped before taking another piece of luggage, and pointed him to the exit instead.

“Leave the cases,” Louis said. “We'll tell the manager.”

The porter hesitated. I put a boot on one case so he could see it had acquired an owner.

“I'm developing an interest in management,” I told him. “Go.”

He left both and ran to catch his colleagues. I climbed back aboard. Eddie watched the gap close behind the last of that group and touched the wheel, waiting for room without demanding anyone hurry faster than their legs allowed.

Irene called for the prizes' location. I told her they were enjoying the infield. She instructed us to stay there until the road cleared. Celeste and June held the southern garages. Hale had collected enough extinguishers to declare a republic. Thành and Coulter had gone beneath the north stand.

Then Irene's voice sharpened. “Long's coming.”

I looked through the rear window. A maintenance tug crossed the grass, Long on its running board, two armed men behind him. It cut between abandoned banners and came up fast.

“Move, Eddie,” I said.

We rolled along the inner service road. The tug drew level. Long stepped onto our rear platform, caught the rail, and hauled himself up. His first blow bent the door inward. Louis grabbed its handle. I dragged him aside before the second blow tore the locking pins free.

Long filled the doorway against the moving lights. He looked at the cases, then at me.

“Whole thing?” Long said.

“Buy smaller prizes,” I told him.

I kicked an empty pedestal into his knees. He stopped it with a shin, seized its brass upright, and swung it across the cabin. I ducked. It struck the armored window and cracked the inner pane. Louis went flat beside the cases. Nessa pulled the cab door shut, keeping the flying pieces away from Eddie.

Long threw the pedestal down and reached for my throat. I drove my shoulder into his chest. We struck the rear frame. His fist hit my ribs, folding me forward; the next blow struck my left shoulder. My arm went numb.

With the right I caught his wrist and drove my forehead into his damaged nose. Blood sprayed my cheek. He shoved me across the cabin into the cases. A lid sprang open, spilling cash around my shoes.

The tug drew alongside the open doorway. One rider raised his pistol. Louis saw him first and dragged the fallen pedestal upright as a shield. The bullet passed through its thin base and into the outside of his thigh. He fell backward with a sharp cry.

I took Felix's chair and threw it through the doorway. It struck the gunman across the face and swept him off the tug. Chair and man bounced into the grass. His weapon fell under a rear wheel. The other rider ducked as Eddie turned sharply away.

Long's balance changed for one instant. I hit him with everything my right arm retained. His head struck the doorframe, denting steel. I caught his lapel, stepped onto the threshold, and pulled us both out.

We fell from the moving wagon. I hit the grass on my side. Long rolled across the service-road edge into a stack of tires. Black circles burst away from him, one bouncing over my legs before continuing toward the track.

The wagon kept moving. Nessa appeared in the doorway pressing cloth to Louis's thigh. His eyes were open; his mouth moved angrily, though I couldn't hear him over the engine.

I raised my good hand and shouted for them to go. Khải ran beside the front, guiding Eddie toward the eastern lane as the last staff cleared it. Tess swung the Argent across the maintenance tug's path. Its driver turned into the grass to avoid her.

Long stood between me and the wagon, stripping his ruined jacket from one arm. Blood covered his mouth. His little-finger ring caught the light as he threw the jacket away.

I rose. My left hand tingled as sensation began coming back. My ribs hurt with every breath. Grass stuck to the blood on my cheek. The bent crown badge hung against my chest.

Long looked at it. “Wanted to be king.”

Behind him a service gap opened onto the banked western track. The tug's remaining gunman tried standing nearby. Long turned and told him to leave. When the man hesitated, Long took one step toward him. The man ran.

Tess stopped twenty yards off and opened her window. She said my name.

I looked east. The wagon had reached the court. Nessa remained beside Louis. Khải had climbed aboard to help. They had the prizes and a way out.

“Get them clear,” I told Tess.

“I'll come back,” Tess said.

“Do that,” I said.

She held my eyes a second, then followed the wagon. Long flexed his hands.

“No father?” Long asked.

“Couldn't afford that fight,” I said.

I took off the badge, put it in my pocket, and tested my left fingers. They opened and closed. Enough for now. I stepped toward him.

“You get this one,” I told Long.
