## Chapter 22: Rook's Last Bid

The recovery truck carried the tractor to the freight terminal with its blade over the rear edge and its foam cannon pointing up. Tú sat in the hatch wearing a bicycle helmet found in the garage. Leon looked at him from the hospitality vehicle and asked whether it helped.

“Safety,” Tú said, tapping it.

“Appreciate the gesture,” Leon replied.

Beyond the terminal gate, the Monarch lay beside a loading pier with its upper decks glaring white. Men moved along the rails. Trucks blocked the approach to the bow ramp. Bách kept his vessel out on the dark water with the cabin lights low. Moss waited on the other side. Farther out, harbor officers watched the ship's windows from their boats.

Minh stood on the recovery platform as Rook called. The old man had seen the tractor.

“Wedding present,” Minh explained.

Rook accused him of treating the situation as a joke. Minh looked back toward Carter's line on the road. The bakery owner had come to identify the men who shot through her window. Her sleeve was singed; Mai held a water bottle against the small burn beneath it.

“No,” Minh told Rook.

After a pause, Rook offered seven million additional dollars. Return what had been taken and there would be seven more.

“You shot Leon,” Minh said.

Rook pointed out that Leon was alive.

“He had a clean shirt,” Minh said.

“Ten million,” Rook answered, the number coming harder now.

Minh looked toward the bridge. “Should've started with the shirt.” He hung up.

The first gate opened before the tractor reached it. A guard came out with both hands raised and an empty holster. He announced he was leaving. Minh nodded toward the road. The man passed the truck, then paused beside Leon to ask whether he was hiring.

Leon established that he could drive and avoid shooting an employer. He gave him a business card.

Mai arrived in time to see it. “You have cards?”

“Curtis,” Leon said.

She held out her hand. The card advertised LEON PRICE, LUXURY TRANSPORTATION, FAMILY-ADJACENT. Mai read the last line twice.

“Specific service,” Leon explained.

Minh stepped through the gate. A shot struck the road in front of his shoes, ending the conversation behind him. The gunman crouched inside a parked truck. Minh moved toward the driver's side. As the shooter leaned across to follow him, Minh pulled the door open and put his other hand through the windscreen.

Glass cut against his sleeve. He caught the rifle forward of the trigger guard and pulled. The man came with it until his chest hit the steering wheel. Minh bent the rifle across the frame and dropped it.

“Get out,” Minh said.

The gunman obeyed. Minh turned him toward the gate and pushed between his shoulders. Behind the first truck, three engines started. Drivers moved to close the gaps along the pier. Men climbed onto their roofs with guns.

Minh called Sơn on the radio. The recovery deck lowered. The tractor rolled down. Minh climbed into its cab while Tú scrambled out of the hatch, protesting that he thought he was operating.

“You operated the helmet,” Leon called. “Come here.”

The tracks took hold of the old pier. Minh drove the blade under the first truck's front guard and lifted. Wheels rose; metal folded; the men on top jumped down. He turned and pushed the truck sideways into the next. Both slid until their tires struck the pier's raised edge beside a pallet stack.

The third driver tried reversing. Minh caught its rear with the blade and shoved it into the vacated gap. Its front wheels climbed a loading platform and hung beyond the edge. The driver jumped from the cab before Minh stopped pushing.

Minh fired the foam cannon across the passage between the trucks. The men running through it disappeared waist-deep in white. One fell; another tripped over him; guns vanished beneath the spreading foam.

Leon hoped aloud that the tractor would stay away from the reception. Mai, watching the cleared approach, said the photographs would be spectacular.

The Monarch's ramp stood open, wide enough for two vehicles. A truck loaded with steel plates occupied the middle. Dense machinery had been chained beside it, fastened directly to the ramp's structure. Its outer lip rested on the pier. At the shipward end, two immense hinges and hydraulic arms held it to the bow.

Minh stopped the tractor short of the lower lip. He looked at the added load, the chains, the men beside the controls. Above, Rook watched from the bridge.

Mai and the others had reached the shelter of the last truck. Minh told them to stay on the pier. Mai looked from the heavy load to the open vehicle deck.

“You need someone outside the door,” Mai said.

“Several,” Minh replied.

“Then stop choosing for all of them.” Mai held his gaze until he nodded and gave her the radio.

“Ramp moves, everybody off,” Minh said.

She clipped the radio to her belt. Minh went up the ramp alone, passing the truck's chocked wheels. Rook spoke through the ship's loudspeaker, offering him a place in the city.

“Got a garage,” Minh called back.

Rook said he meant power. Minh stopped beside the steel plates and looked down the pier. Rook's trucks were shoved aside. Men were surrendering at the gate or trying to find their feet beneath the foam.

“You mean permission,” Minh said, and walked on.

Three men waited inside with steel poles. The first thrust at Minh's chest. He caught the shaft, pulled the man toward him, and drove the far end into the second man's knee. As that man fell, Minh turned the pole and swept the third man's ankles. The first let go and tried running. Minh caught his jacket, held him for a second, then shoved him toward the open ramp.

“Still time for that half,” Minh told him.

The man ran. Minh followed the passage between two rows of crates. A gunman stepped from the left. Minh pushed the nearest crate into him. It broke against the opposite stack, spilling rope. A second shooter raised his weapon farther down. Minh dropped behind a coil, threw it around the man's legs, and pulled. His chin hit the deck. Minh took the gun and drove it beneath a steel support until the grip broke.

Mai entered with Sơn at her shoulder. Minh turned toward them. She raised the radio and said Carter had the pier; she was watching this side. Sơn took up one of the loose poles and positioned himself at the crate passage.

“I can do doors,” Sơn said.

“Already do driving,” Minh told him.

“Expanding,” Sơn replied.

A motor began turning overhead. The ramp lurched. Duy shouted from the pier that the support was going. Minh ran back to the opening.

The hinge housings were tearing away from the bow. The outer lip still rested on the pier, but the truck and chained machinery pulled the inner end down toward the water. Leon and Reggie were halfway across, helping a wounded guard toward shore.

Mai ran to the threshold and shouted for them to get off. Duy and Tú reached from the pier. Leon pushed Reggie toward their hands.

The shipward edge dropped another foot. Minh passed Mai, planted his feet on the fixed threshold, caught the torn edge beneath both palms, and lifted.

The ramp stopped falling. Above his hands, the truck's wheel chocks split. Its loaded tires began rolling toward his face.
