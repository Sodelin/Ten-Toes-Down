## Chapter 19: Dinner on Moving Water

Lights hung between the Monarch's upper rails. Through the saloon windows, Minh could see white tablecloths and flower arrangements prepared by people who had been forbidden to leave. A banner over the bow ramp welcomed Bell Street.

At its foot, a guard looked from Minh's case to Mai's canvas bag.

“Only him,” the guard said.

Mai looked up at the banner. “You invited the street. I'm on it.”

The guard began explaining Rook's instructions. Behind the saloon glass, Rook lifted one hand. The guard stepped aside before Minh had to help him understand the correction.

They walked up together. The vehicle deck smelled of diesel, paint, and food kept warm upstairs. Laundry carts lined one wall. Guards occupied the doors to the lower service rooms. Minh counted six visible guns before the stairs.

“Nobody wants trouble,” the man behind him said.

Minh glanced at his pistol. “You packed for it.”

At the top, another guard tried taking Mai's bag. She opened it herself and showed the flashlights, cloths, and batteries. He looked disappointed. Mai asked what he had hoped for, shut the bag, and continued into the saloon without waiting for an answer.

Rook stood beside a table set for two. Mai borrowed a third chair and put it beside Minh's. Rook watched the legs drag over his carpet.

“You take liberties,” Rook told her.

“You keep buying the wrong number of chairs,” Mai said.

Minh put the case on the table. Rook's eyes went to it immediately. He ordered Minh to open it. Minh asked for the workers. Rook began explaining that it was his ship. Minh clicked the lid open just far enough to expose the money, then shut it again when the explanation stopped.

“Thirty-one,” Minh said.

Rook told the guard to bring them up.

They entered in groups. A woman in a stained apron, a man carrying his shoes, two musicians clutching instrument cases. Denise came with one sleeve torn where someone had grabbed her. She saw Mai, stopped, and asked for Felicia.

“Waiting at the shop,” Mai told her.

Denise cried, wiped her face angrily, and said she had promised to handle the delivery herself. Felicia would have something to say.

Mai took her hand. “Several things. You'll have chances to answer.”

Minh matched each person to Carter's list while Mai asked names. Some answered immediately. Others stared until she repeated the question. Rook grew impatient as the line lengthened beside his unused tables.

“All here,” Rook announced.

Minh counted once more. “Thirty.”

Rook said thirty-one. His guard counted with one finger and stopped at the final man. He looked toward Rook.

“Marine crew,” Rook said. “He's attending the machinery.”

“Gus Navarro,” Minh read from the list. “Bring him.”

Rook explained that the machinery could not be left unattended. Minh closed his hand around the case handle. Rook reached across the table and stopped when Minh looked at his fingers.

The engines hummed beneath the floor. Nobody in the line moved. Mai kept Denise beside her and watched Rook's hand withdraw.

Rook said the others could leave while Navarro's replacement was arranged. Minh looked at Mai and nodded toward the stairs.

“Take them to Carter,” Minh said.

She pressed his wrist once. “Thirty.”

He nodded. She led them out in a line, asking the musicians to watch the people ahead instead of their cases. Minh stood at the saloon window while Rook remained beside him.

On the pier, Sơn and the two gym men met the first group. Duy opened the hospitality vehicle's doors. Reggie filmed from the rail with his camera low; Leon handed out bottles from a crate.

“Take two,” Leon told an apron-clad woman when she reached him.

She said she only needed one. Leon said she could surprise herself later and tucked a second into the bag she carried.

“Safe,” Rook said, watching the first ten move away.

“Twenty still on the stairs,” Minh replied.

Rook accused him of making everything laborious. Minh looked from the ramp to the case.

“Rubber doorstop,” Minh reminded him. “Still cheaper.”

Mai helped Denise down the final slope. A musician stumbled; Sơn caught the case and passed it back unopened. Twenty ashore. Rook left the window and lifted a telephone from the wall.

Minh watched the reflection of his mouth in the glass. Rook told someone to keep the engines at idle. Their note changed under Minh's shoes.

Minh opened the cash case and removed one bundle. Rook turned immediately. Minh laid the bundle beside the case and let him come back toward it while the final group descended. Outside, Mai waited until the last man stepped onto the pier. Then she looked up, raised three fingers, and closed her hand.

Thirty. Minh nodded to her.

The guard shut the stair door behind him.

Rook put the loose bundle back on top of the money and announced that they could finish. Minh asked again for Navarro. Rook wanted to discuss the other seven million.

Minh took the bundle back and put it in his jacket. “Deposit for my time.”

The guard drew his pistol. Minh kicked the table into his hips and pinned him against the wall. The gun fired into the ceiling. Plates fell. The case slid, and Rook caught it with both hands while Minh crossed the room, stripped the guard's pistol, and bent it against the door handle. One blow under the ribs left the man folded over the table.

A second guard came through the service doorway. Rook shouted about the money. The guard glanced at the case, and Minh threw a chair across his chest, driving him back through the opening.

Rook disappeared through a narrow door behind the bar carrying the case. Minh reached it as a steel panel dropped across the passage. From a speaker beyond it, Rook told him he should have accepted a place.

“Keep getting offered furniture,” Minh said, taking the panel's lower edge.

It rose six inches and jammed. He pulled outward until the bottom bowed, shoved a shoulder beneath it, and stood. The steel folded across his back. He pushed until the top tore out of the housing and the entire panel fell into the saloon behind him.

Two stairs led from the empty passage: up to the bridge, down toward the machinery. A man was shouting below. Minh went down.

He found Navarro striking a maintenance-room door with a wrench. Minh pulled the door off. Navarro's next swing stopped an inch from his face.

“You're the guy?” Navarro asked.

“Which one?” Minh said.

“The one they say can't be killed.” Navarro lowered the wrench and leaned against the wall. One ankle bulged above his shoe.

“Not testing it tonight,” Minh told him. “Come on.”

Navarro explained that they had ordered him to disable the ramp. He had removed a fuse and claimed the entire mechanism was broken. He showed Minh the fuse from his pocket.

“I repair things,” Navarro said. “I can make a convincing problem.”

Minh smiled and took his weight under one arm. “You'll enjoy my fiancée.”

The engines deepened. Through a porthole at the end of the passage, the pier started sliding backward. Minh brought Navarro up to the vehicle deck. The bow ramp had risen halfway. Across the narrowing opening, Mai stood at the pier rail.

Navarro looked at the moving ramp and swore. “Auxiliary circuit. They found it.” He tried putting weight on his swollen ankle to reach the controls, and Minh lifted him clear again. There would be no repairing it from where the guards had left him.

She saw them and called Gus's name. Minh lifted him high enough for her to see his face.

“Thirty-one!” Minh called.

Footsteps hit the stairs behind him. Six armed men came down together. Minh set Navarro behind a laundry cart, took off Felicia's gray jacket, and placed it over his knees.

“Hold that,” Minh said.

Navarro looked at the approaching guns, then at the jacket. He folded the sleeves inside it with the care the assignment seemed to require.
