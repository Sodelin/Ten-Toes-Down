## Chapter 27: The Practice Dinner

Seven days after the Monarch lost its ramp, Leon came to the garage in a tuxedo carrying a live chicken.

Minh lowered the wrench in his hand. “Why?”

“Before you judge, not my first choice,” Leon said. The bird shifted beneath his good arm. He adjusted it and looked down as if it had violated their agreement. “Curtis knew a man with white doves. That man knew another man. By the time I arrived, the story had feathers.”

The wedding was tomorrow. Minh's palms had healed enough to work, though a narrow dressing remained across the left. His shoulder only complained if he slept on it. Mai had caught him testing it by lifting the Rolls's front end and made him lower the car before she finished her sentence.

The garage contained no gunmen, no sinking machinery, and one chicken. Minh considered this progress.

“Take it to Hương,” Minh suggested.

Leon drew the bird closer. “Named him already.”

“Tell her the name,” Minh said.

“Reggie,” Leon replied.

The human Reggie lowered his camera in the doorway and demanded an explanation. Leon held up the bird, pointing out the way it kept looking around as though it had missed something. Reggie studied the chicken. The chicken studied him.

“Not filming your speech,” Reggie said.

Leon nodded toward the camera. “Weren't getting my good side anyway.”

Bách's banquet hall stood three blocks inland from the harbor. Above its entrance, Minh and Mai's modest approved portrait had expanded to include a gold border, two painted lions, and A UNION OF GREATNESS in letters visible from the next intersection.

Felicia stood beneath it staring at the lions. Minh came beside her and said these had the correct number of fingers. She reminded him lions had paws. He said they were improving.

Inside, the room smelled of polish, flowers, and broth. Tables circled a clear dance floor. A piano and two microphones waited on the stage before the backdrop Mai had printed herself. All the names were spelled correctly.

Bách met them in a white dinner jacket and invited admiration. Minh noticed he had moved the portable bar closer to their table and congratulated him. Mai took Bách's arm, turned him toward the former position, and told him to put it back.

“Gesture,” Bách explained.

“So is walking,” Mai said.

Two employees began wheeling the bar across the room. Moss entered carrying a long box and watched its departure with pleasure. He handed the box to Minh. Inside was a brass key mounted on a wooden plaque.

“Boat's yours,” Moss said. “Papers have your name.”

Minh checked the key. “Both engines?”

Moss asked if he intended to take one back. Minh said he knew people. Moss laughed and clapped his uninjured shoulder. Bách disappeared into his office.

Mai closed the box and looked at Moss. “You know what you've started.”

Moss watched Bách's office door. “Absolutely.”

The officiant began rehearsal by asking everyone to stand where they would stand tomorrow. Twenty minutes passed. Leon believed the best man belonged beside the groom even when the groom went to the bathroom. Tú had claimed security at a doorway nobody planned to use. Duy kept drifting behind Sơn whenever the photographer looked in their direction.

Mai put Duy back in his assigned place. “Invited,” she reminded him.

Duy said he knew. Sơn looked over his shoulder and explained that Duy was also watching for food. Duy admitted both could be true.

The officiant waited, glasses on a chain against her blouse. She explained that when she requested the rings, Leon would hand them over. Leon patted his jacket confidently. Mai reminded him these were practice rings. The real ones remained with her because he had put a parking receipt in the ring box yesterday.

“Proved I was present,” Leon said.

The officiant removed her glasses. “Will you talk during the actual ceremony?”

Leon looked at Minh. Minh inspected the ceiling. Mai said he would try.

“Then I'll wait,” the officiant said. “Thirty years. I've outlasted louder men.”

Hương applauded once from the front row. After that, the rehearsal acquired forward movement.

Minh started too quickly on their practice walk down the aisle. Mai slowed him with two fingers against his wrist and explained that they were leaving a ceremony, not responding to the garage alarm. He shortened his stride. At the dance floor, the pianist began their song. Mai put a hand on Minh's shoulder.

“This next,” she said.

He looked at their feet. They had practiced at home, where he had moved the sofa into her path and called the collision a turn. Mai reminded him of that account while taking his hand.

Two steps went well. On the third, she moved left and he right. They stopped together, laughed, and tried again. Leon advised larger steps. Mai told him no. Leon explained that he was addressing Minh. Mai said she was too.

Tín rose from his chair. Bích watched him go with the patient recognition of someone who had seen the same decision repeatedly over many decades. He offered Minh a hand.

Minh took it. Tín placed his other hand at Minh's upper arm and turned him through half a circle. Minh tried bracing his feet. His soles slid anyway. Another half circle returned him neatly to the starting position. Tín's jacket had not shifted.

“Follows beautifully,” Tín said.

Minh looked at the pale marks his shoes had left. “Wasn't following.”

“Noticed,” Tín said. “Rude.”

Bích stood and offered her hand, suggesting he try somebody who knew where her feet were. The pianist adjusted the pace as they went onto the floor. Bích corrected Tín's collar while they turned.

Mai stood beside Minh with a hand over her smile. He told her that Tín had dragged him. She said he had looked graceful. He accused her of lying; she advised practice hearing it because tomorrow everyone would.

The piano's front caster broke as the song ended. A crack, a tilt, the pianist jumping from the bench. Minh stepped toward it, but Hòa was already there. He put one hand beneath the lower edge and held the instrument level while picking up the broken caster with the other.

Bách ran from his office holding a red envelope. “Don't scratch it!”

Hương asked whether he meant the man or the piano. Bách chose both.

Minh offered to take the weight. Hòa asked him to find support for the corner instead. Minh brought a solid wooden riser from beside the stage. Hòa lowered the piano onto it without making the strings sound.

Tín said the instrument could have gone farther from the wall. Hòa replied that the suggestion would have been useful before he set it down. Father and son looked at each other until Bích came between them and told both to move themselves toward dinner.

They went. Minh stayed a moment beside the piano. The caster's steel stem had twisted into two clean spirals before snapping. Hòa put it in his pocket and found Minh looking.

“Bad wheel,” Hòa said.

“Saw,” Minh replied.

“Then eat,” Hòa told him.

Mai took Minh's arm and guided him away before he could ask a question the family had been answering all week by lifting furniture.

Bách's red envelope contained a key to the hall. Moss lowered his fork as Bách put it beside Minh's plate. Mai asked whether he was giving them the building.

“Access,” Bách said quickly. “Whenever you want. Family, business, private occasions.”

Moss picked up his fork again. “So a key.”

Bách said it was a key to an institution. Minh thanked him and put it in his pocket. Bách relaxed until Moss mentioned that his key started engines. Bách's fed three hundred people. Moss pointed out the workers still required.

Mai passed them a platter. “Compete quietly.”

Across the table, Tú refilled water wearing a dark suit over the gold lettering. His mother beckoned Minh close to report that he had been getting up on time and paying for things. There remained the problem that he called himself Master.

Minh looked at Tú struggling with a bottle cap. “Developing,” Minh said.

The cap came loose. Water splashed Tú's sleeve. He looked around to see who noticed and found his mother already offering a napkin.

After dinner, Minh found Leon outside beside the convertible. The chicken had gone home with a kitchen assistant who kept birds. Reggie had demanded his name be removed. Leon renamed the bird Curtis, satisfying everybody except the bird.

Leon stood alone with his good hand on the roof. Minh asked whether he was rehearsing his speech. Leon said no. Minh called that a relief and leaned beside him.

A car slowed to examine the giant wedding portrait. Somebody pointed through a window. Minh heard his name from the street, then the engine carried it away.

Leon tapped the car roof. “Sometimes I hear the shot again.”

Minh turned toward him. Leon kept his eyes on traffic.

“When it's quiet,” Leon said. “Had a plate, then I didn't. Keep thinking about the plate.”

“Dropped it,” Minh said.

Leon nodded. “Good food.”

The hall door opened. Music came out with Bách's voice explaining that bars had wheels for a reason. The door shut again.

“You don't have to come on the next one,” Minh said.

Leon looked at him. “Didn't request retirement, nigga.”

Minh nodded.

“Wanted to say it,” Leon said. He rubbed the sling's edge. “You showed up.”

“You were loud,” Minh replied.

Leon said he would stay loud. Minh asked him not to. This time Leon laughed properly. Minh squeezed his uninjured shoulder once and told him tomorrow he would stand beside him.

“Where else?” Leon asked.

“Bathroom, apparently,” Minh said.

Leon laughed again, opened the car, and retrieved a folded sheet from the seat. He asked Minh to read the speech. The opening declared that he had known this beautiful nigga since he was poor enough to listen. Minh laughed before the second line. Leon took the paper back, satisfied with the test.

Inside, most guests had gone. Mai waited by the stage with her shoes in one hand. Hòa and Tín argued about stacking folding tables while Hương and Bích moved the chairs without submitting a system.

Mai put her free hand on Minh's chest. “Once more.”

The pianist had left. Kitchen radio supplied the music. Minh took her hand and waited for her first step. She moved close; he followed their practiced pace. They crossed the empty floor without meeting a sofa, the stage, or each other's toes. At the far side, she turned beneath his arm and came back laughing.

“Tomorrow?” Minh asked, drawing her close.

“Tomorrow,” Mai said.

Leon opened the hall door and began to speak. Felicia caught the back of his jacket and pulled him outside before he finished the first word. The door closed. Minh and Mai kept dancing.
