## Chapter 5: The Arcade King

Benny had prepared for Minh's arrival by declaring himself closed for maintenance. The notice covered the glass front door of Crown Arcade. Behind it, someone was moving furniture.

Leon parked his own brown sedan at the curb, its blue passenger door protesting when Minh opened it. “I told him you were coming,” Leon said. “He answered the phone enthusiastic, then had to become absent halfway through.”

A young man in red trainers approached the window before Minh could stand. MASTER CHIGGA shone across his black shirt in gold.

“You're Minh?” the young man asked.

Minh looked at the lettering. “Depends what your shirt wants.”

The shirt wanted recognition as a brand. Its owner played pool here, was building something, and eventually admitted that his name before the gold letters had been Tú. Minh asked him to move. Tú stepped sideways into the curb; Leon caught his elbow and suggested mastering sidewalks next.

Minh knocked. A man inside held up a second notice: READ THE SIGN.

Minh pulled the handle. The lock came out of the frame. He caught it before it hit the pavement and handed it to the man.

“Maintenance,” Minh said, walking in.

The carpet smelled of years concealed under one recent bottle of cleaner. Arcade screens flashed along two narrow aisles leading to a roped-off pool room. At the change counter, Veronica looked over her magazine and announced that Benny wasn't there.

Leon placed both hands on the counter. “Veronica. How you been?”

He angled himself between the magazine and the pool room, smiling with the practiced ease he brought to women who remembered earlier versions of the smile. Veronica let him wait while she finished a paragraph, marked her place with one finger, and looked at the white shoe he had scraped under the Rolls.

“New shoes?” Veronica asked.

“Recent history,” Leon said. “You look good.”

“Thirty dollars short,” Veronica said.

Minh paid the thirty. Veronica put away the magazine and pointed toward the back-left office. As they walked away, Minh learned that Leon had borrowed her money to buy her earrings.

“He'll say he left,” Veronica added, turning toward the office. “Benny! You left with company!”

There was a scrape from beyond the rope. Leon followed Minh between the machines, trying to conduct his defense without Tú hearing it.

“You didn't have to pay her,” Leon said.

“You didn't have to borrow it,” Minh answered.

“A gift requires preparation,” Leon said. “Sometimes capital.”

Minh stopped at the rope and looked at him. “You borrowed her money to buy her a present?”

“She liked them,” Leon said.

Behind them, Tú coughed into his sleeve. Leon turned to examine the cough until it stopped being laughter.

Benny stood at the nearest pool table in a gold waistcoat with a watch heavy enough to explain his posture. Eight men occupied the room. They stopped pretending to play as Minh came under the green lamps.

“You sent flowers,” Minh said.

Benny denied it. Minh took his cue and set the white ball before the racked triangle. He bent, sighted, and broke.

Balls cracked across felt. Two went into opposite corners together. A stripe leaped onto the adjoining table, knocked its cue ball into a pocket, and dropped back onto the floor. The last red circled, nudged the eight, and followed it into the side.

Minh laid down the cue. “Table leans left.”

Tú stared at the emptied felt. Leon assured him that the word forming in his mouth was the correct technical term.

Benny suggested a man could make money with that skill. Minh explained that he was trying to make money with the conversation. Benny looked at his friends instead of answering.

“I don't hire every driver in the city,” Benny said. He leaned one hip against the table and tried looking comfortable without bringing his watch into range of Minh's hands.

Minh described the green van, the apron, the flowers, and the gun. Benny's eyes moved to the man behind Minh before the description was finished. The cue carrier changed his grip. Minh could hear the narrow tip scraping along the floor as the heavier end came up behind him.

The first cue came at Minh from behind. He caught it under his arm, backed into its owner, then lifted the man by his belt onto the table. His face struck the felt hard enough to rattle the return tray. A stool followed from Minh's right. He ducked beneath it and pushed its owner in the stomach.

The man crossed the rope, hit a racing machine, and broke its steering wheel with his back. GAME OVER lit above him while he settled into the seat.

“One quarter,” Leon observed. “Excellent mileage.”

The third man had a knife out. Minh told him he could keep it if he stayed where he was. The man considered Benny's gold waistcoat, then set the knife down.

Benny ran for his office. Tú tried blocking him and was shoved backward over a chair. He came up with it raised, but Leon took it from him while Minh lifted the near end of the pool table. Minh turned the table between its neighbors and drove its far edge into the office door just as Benny reached it. The door slammed into Benny's face. He stumbled back into the new barricade.

“I was trying to keep this professional,” Tú told Leon, following his chair with both hands as Leon moved it safely out of reach.

“First you need the profession,” Leon said.

Minh stepped onto the tilted table's edge. It settled another inch against the door. Benny backed until the office handle pressed into his spine, then looked up at Minh occupying the route back to the room.

“This yours too?” Minh asked, tapping his heel against the felt.

Benny nodded.

“Good table,” Minh said. “Very good at staying closed for maintenance.”

“Benny owns those!” Veronica called from the counter.

“Good,” Minh called back.

“Machines are mine,” Veronica added.

Leon pointed toward the broken racer. “That customer already paid for his ride.”

In the office, Benny sat under a photograph of himself standing beside a boxer who looked as though he had been detained. Minh put the broken lock on the desk. Leon occupied the doorway. Tú stood behind him, trying to see without looking assigned to the back.

Benny admitted the flowers were meant to frighten Minh. Dante Rook had paid for the message.

“The cake was terrified,” Minh said. He handed Benny the desk telephone. “Call your florist.”

Dante answered after six rings. Benny began an explanation and stopped when Minh took the receiver.

“Got your flowers,” Minh told Dante.

The line clicked dead. Minh put it down and opened Benny's drawer. Cash, watches, photographs, keys. One brass tag read LOT 7.

Benny claimed the white Cadillac outside. He had won it fairly from Duy that morning.

Tú pushed into the room. “On the short table. Don't tell him fairly. I lose here. I know the equipment.”

Duy had arrived at six with keys to three cars and a bag he would not put down. Benny had offered him a game. Leon leaned against the frame and asked how a man could fall into a vault and emerge owing the hinges.

Minh spread the photographs over the desk. The Cadillac appeared in the Meridian loading court beside Dante and Duy. Dante's arm rested over Duy's shoulders. Minh looked at that arm long enough for Benny to volunteer more information.

Duy had used the phone. Benny had heard Blue Palm Car Wash. He hadn't listened to anything else.

“You didn't listen,” Minh repeated, keeping his hands on either side of the photographs.

Benny spread both palms. “People make calls.”

“And you recognized the place without hearing it,” Minh said.

Benny looked at the barred office window. Minh let him finish the calculation. Outside was an alley, above the alley a small drop, across the room a man who had just moved a pool table to discourage a walk of six feet.

“Blue Palm,” Benny said again. “He asked for the back. I didn't hear a name.”

“Selective ears,” Minh said, pocketing the keys and photographs.

Outside, the pearl-painted Cadillac waited beneath a canvas awning. Leon sat in the passenger seat and sighed at the upholstery. Tú leaned through the other doorway and asked whether recovery counted as stealing professionally.

Minh lifted the driver's cushion. Under it lay an envelope containing thirty thousand dollars. He checked it and put it back.

“Duy didn't know,” Minh said.

Leon asked how he could be sure.

“He sold his keyboard for two hundred.” Minh dropped the cushion back into place.

Veronica appeared at the arcade door with a grocery bag and the broken lock. She called after them to take Benny's security tapes before he erased them. Benny materialized behind her, heard his own proposed future, and vanished toward the office.

Minh nodded to Leon. Leon got out of the Cadillac, followed by Tú, who immediately wanted to know what helping paid.

“A chance to be Master Outside,” Leon told him. “Carry the tapes.”

Minh watched them go, then called Mai to say he would make her fitting. He looked down at the Cadillac's expensive seats while the phone rang. There was a million-dollar recovery fee ahead, thirty thousand under him, and Duy somewhere in the city behaving as if all the money in the world had already been spent.
