## Chapter 9: Lunch With the Wrong Old Man

The day after Blue Palm, Rook arrived at the Lê house with a tree and six pastries. Two men pushed the tree up the path on a velvet-covered cart. Rook carried the pastry bag away from his trousers as if it contained an uncertain chemical.

Hương opened the door and pointed toward the garden. “Plant stays outside.”

“It's a gift,” Rook explained.

“Still a plant,” Hương said. She took the bag, counted its contents, and asked whether he was eating.

Minh stood behind her with a bowl of peeled garlic. He had come for Mai and acquired work before finding her. Rook looked to him for help. Minh peeled another clove.

In the living room, Tín was repairing the handle of a kitchen knife. A small clamp held the wooden halves together. Rook stopped beside the visitor's chair and announced that his limousine remained on the cross street, outside Bell Street's boundary.

“The car sent its lawyer?” Tín asked, looking up at him.

“Eight years, Tín,” Rook said.

“You counting because you owe an anniversary present?” Tín tightened the clamp a quarter turn and examined the joint.

Rook sat. His guard, a thick-necked man with a shaved head, remained standing behind the chair. The man inspected the safe on the rug and commented on its commercial rating. Tín said it should contribute, then resumed ignoring him.

Mai came downstairs carrying a roll of proofs. She saw Rook and stopped at the bottom step.

“You followed him to my parents' house?” Mai asked.

Rook explained that he wished to speak to Minh. Mai put down the proofs and turned toward the garlic bowl.

“He appears occupied,” she said.

Minh held up a freshly peeled clove. Rook waited until Mai went to the kitchen, then said Dante had called.

“He found trousers?” Minh asked.

The guard's mouth moved before he could stop it. Rook turned. The man developed a more serious interest in the safe.

Rook reported that his son claimed Minh had attacked him. Minh described Duy bound inside the car and the hydraulic frame descending onto its roof. Rook sat very still. Tín stopped turning the clamp.

“Duy who sings?” Tín asked.

Minh nodded and said he was at Leon's.

“Tell him my radio is fixed,” Tín said. “He can stop helping.”

Rook placed his hands on his knees. The family had again wandered out of his conversation without permission. He brought them back by asking for the convoy and Dante, alive.

“Then tell him to stop sending guns,” Minh said.

“He's frightened,” Rook replied.

“He should enjoy the novelty,” Minh said, setting the garlic bowl on the side table.

The guard told Minh to watch how he spoke. Tín stopped working again. The guard noticed; Rook noticed him noticing; Rook ordered him outside before the old man had to find a use for the clamp. On his way out, the guard struck his knee against the safe and stumbled. He looked back at it with offended suspicion.

“House-trained,” Tín observed.

Hương appeared with plates. She put the stack in Rook's hands, sent him toward the porch, then gave Minh another stack. Rook carried his with the cautious dignity of a man whose possessions usually traveled ahead of him.

From the kitchen, Mai watched the shipping boss lay out their table. “He brought armed men,” she told her mother.

Hương tasted the broth. “They can wash after.”

“Mom,” Mai said.

“Nigga, I asked if they eat,” Hương replied. “I didn't invite their guns.”

Minh smiled into the garlic. Mai caught him and took the bowl away.

“Two refrigerators,” Mai counted, pushing him toward the porch. “Now Calvin Rook setting the table. This started with you answering a telephone.”

“He can do the other side too,” Minh suggested.

Bích went behind Rook and corrected every plate. He watched silently. Beside the porch steps, Hòa crouched by the tree's cart. One caster caught at the same point on every turn. Hòa spun it once, put two fingers against its bent bracket, and pressed. The metal straightened. The wheel ran freely.

Rook offered to replace the cart only after the repair was finished.

“There,” Hòa said, standing.

His eyes moved to Minh's bandaged arm as he passed. He asked about the glass, learned Mai had cleaned it, and suggested Minh stop discovering new places to put it. Through the doorway, Tín lifted one eyebrow at Minh. Minh kept the question behind his teeth.

They sat down to eat. Rook arranged his napkin across his knee. He seemed to have recovered some composure when the limousine sounded its horn from the cross street.

The second blast lasted longer. Minh looked beyond the garden wall. A pickup carrying a metal drum had stopped behind the limousine. Three men were getting out. One was Blue Palm's tire washer.

Rook rose. Hương told him to sit because she had just put the food down. His guard reached inside his jacket outside, and the tire washer struck his wrist with a club. The gun fell beneath the limousine. A companion rolled the drum from the truck. Liquid splashed over the pavement.

Minh laid down his chopsticks.

“Yours?” he asked Rook.

Rook shook his head.

Mai came beside Minh. “Get them off the street.”

Minh went down the steps. The tire washer saw him first and ran back to the pickup, abandoning the assault so quickly that his companions were left following his eyes. One had already struck a lighter beside the spilled fuel.

Minh caught the lighter hand, pulled it away from the ground, and bent the man chest-first over the limousine's hood. The flame went out. With his free hand he lifted the nearly empty drum and lowered it over the man's head. It caught at his elbows. Minh pushed him sideways into the gutter, where the drum stopped against the curb with the man shouting inside it.

The club came toward Minh's head. He caught it, broke it, and struck its owner across the right thigh with one piece. The man's knee folded. Minh pulled his belt free, looped it around his wrist and the limousine's door handle, and tightened it.

“Wait for your friend,” Minh told him.

Behind them, the pickup reversed. The tire washer looked over his shoulder and found Hòa's blue sedan parked across the far exit. Hòa got out and shut his door. The pickup stopped, turned hard, and tried climbing the curb.

Hòa put one hand on its tailgate.

The back wheels spun. The truck shuddered against his palm. A hubcap came loose and circled his shoe before falling flat. Hòa looked down at it, then back at the driver.

“Off,” Hòa said.

The driver switched off the engine. Hòa opened the passenger door and took the keys without climbing into the cab. When Minh reached him, Hòa indicated something beneath the seat. Minh pulled the driver out and found a pistol beside a paper bag of cash.

The tire washer stared at Hòa. “Who the fuck are you?”

“Late for lunch,” Hòa told him.

Rook's guard had retrieved his pistol and was now threatening the drum. Minh took the weapon away before the man could turn recovering his dignity into a neighborhood problem. Rook came down the path with his napkin still in his hand.

The tire washer started talking immediately. Dante had hired them. He had said his father would be in the car. He had said to frighten him.

Rook hit the man hard enough to drop him against the truck. His second punch stopped in Minh's hand.

For a moment, the shipping boss looked nothing like the man who had brought pastries. His arm strained against Minh's grip. Minh held his fist between them and waited until Rook looked up.

“At your house,” Minh told him.

Rook stopped pulling. Minh released him.

From the porch, Tín called Calvin's name. He stood beside the tree, cutting its red ribbon with the repaired kitchen knife.

“Take your boys,” Tín said. “Leave the pastries.”

Rook handed Minh his folded napkin and went to the limousine. His guard helped turn the man in the drum upright, then steered him by rotating the metal a few inches at a time. Leon arrived with takeaway containers and stopped at the gate.

“Why the lunch walking?” Leon asked.

Rook waited at the car window for Minh. He admitted that his son was out of control.

“He learned somewhere,” Minh said.

Rook looked forward for several seconds. Then he offered an invitation to tomorrow's Harbor Crest dinner. Minh recognized the card from Dante's trousers. Rook promised to discuss the recovery before the owners of the missing property, ending any suspicion about Minh's involvement. He suggested bringing Mai.

Minh took the invitation without promising anything. When the damaged limousine left, he returned to his place beside Mai. She had kept his plate.

“Dinner?” she asked, looking at the card.

“Tomorrow. I'll take Leon,” Minh said.

Mai raised an eyebrow. “Felicia finished your suit. Leon isn't wearing my dress.”

Hương laughed from the other end of the table. Minh unfolded his napkin and reached for the rice.
