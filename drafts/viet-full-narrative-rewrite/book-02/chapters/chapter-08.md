## Chapter 8: Three Minutes at the Car Wash

The tire washer was still at work when they returned to Blue Palm. He brushed the same immaculate wheel while water ran beneath the curb.

“Forty minutes,” Leon told Minh. “That's a relationship.”

The receiver put lot three directly ahead. Minh shut it off, left it beneath the Cadillac's seat, and asked Leon to leave a car's worth of space beyond the wash exit. Leon requested a definition of car. Minh said they would find out.

Sơn and Tú followed him under the sign advertising a three-minute wash for seven dollars. The tire washer dropped his brush and disappeared through a side door. Tú picked it up, considered establishing cover, then put it down under Minh's gaze.

The office was empty. A baseball game murmured on the television above an open cash drawer. Beyond the counter, water hissed in the wash bay.

Minh went through. A black luxury sedan sat halfway along the conveyor, blue cleaning strips hanging over its roof. Dante stood at a control cabinet near the far door. Duy sat behind the windshield, wrists tied to the steering wheel.

“Move away from that cabinet,” Minh told Dante.

Dante rested a hand on it. He said they could begin with hello. Minh reminded him about the flowers at his home. Sơn drew Tú behind his right shoulder as Dante noticed that his hired fighter had arrived on the wrong side of the discussion.

“I paid you,” Dante said to Sơn.

“For a car,” Sơn replied. “It's outside. It doesn't need supervision.”

Duy shook his head against the headrest. Above the sedan, a heavy brush frame hung from steel arms. Someone had chained it to a service ram bolted into the overhead beam. Minh followed the fittings with his eyes. The alteration would drive the frame through the roof.

Dante demanded the two recovered cars, the receiver, and a report to his father that Minh had found nothing. In return, Duy could leave.

“Then he goes home,” Dante repeated when Minh kept looking at Duy.

“His hands are tied to a steering wheel,” Minh said. “You've made leaving difficult.”

“You give me what I want, I untie him.” Dante tapped the cabinet with two fingers. “This is simple.”

Water pattered on the sedan's roof. Duy tried forming words behind the closed glass. Minh watched his mouth and could not make them out, but he could see the red marks where the bindings entered his skin. He moved toward the conveyor while Dante talked. By the time the young man noticed the change in distance, Minh was close enough to touch the car.

“He doesn't believe you,” Minh said, watching Duy shake his head harder.

Dante pressed the switch. The ram drew the chain tight. Bristles flattened on the roof; then the metal began to crease.

Minh stepped between the conveyor rollers and put his right palm beneath the brush frame. It stopped descending. He pushed upward, water running into his collar, until the hydraulic pipe jerked against its mount.

“Turn it off,” Minh said.

Dante took a pistol from behind the cabinet. With his free hand, Minh tore the sedan's door off and threw it the length of the bay. Its edge struck the cabinet. The shot went into the wall. Dante disappeared behind the metal door, legs caught beneath it.

Minh tore the cords from Duy's wrists. Duy tried moving and discovered his ankles were still tied beneath the dash. Minh bent the steering column up, grabbed his jacket, and pulled. The ankle cord broke. Duy fell into the wash water and crawled toward the office.

The hydraulic line burst overhead. Minh shoved the frame up until its chain snapped, ducked the whipping end, and hurled the swinging assembly through the partition. The office wall shook. Its television fell onto the counter.

A shot cracked from the office. Sơn had his good arm around a man's throat. Tú stood over another man holding the broken television.

The weapon lay beneath the change counter, beyond the man Sơn was restraining. Sơn had turned the man's elbow up until his heels lifted. His braced wrist stayed clear of the struggle. Minh saw him glance toward Tú, then toward the open front door, keeping both in view instead of chasing another blow.

Tú had television glass in the fold of his shirt. The gold lettering read MASTER through a film of wash water. He lifted the set another inch when the man on the floor tried moving an elbow.

“He might get up,” Tú told Sơn.

“Then hit him again,” Sơn advised.

The man on the floor made a visible decision to remain there. Tú called it negotiation.

Behind the control cabinet, Dante had escaped through the rear door. Minh followed the sound of an engine and found him driving a tow truck. Its cable was attached to the black sedan's rear frame. As the truck moved, the damaged car tore against the conveyor guides inside.

Minh jumped onto the towing platform, feet on either side of the winch. He caught the cable and pulled backward. The tow truck slowed; Dante accelerated. The sedan broke free of its guides and came through the exit sideways, taking its front bumper off against the building.

Beyond the gate, Leon retreated toward the Cadillac. “You said a car!” he shouted.

Minh let the cable feed one turn, caught it, and pulled upward. The truck's rear rose while its front bumper touched the pavement. Dante hit the brakes. Minh lowered the wheels, jumped down, and opened the driver's door.

Dante was already crawling across the cab. Minh caught the back of his trousers. The belt broke. Dante slid out the passenger door in his underwear and sprinted into the warehouse gap, leaving Minh holding the trousers.

“Collateral,” Leon said, arriving beside him.

Minh started after Dante, then heard Duy shouting from the office. Smoke had begun pouring through its doorway.

The broken television lay across the open drawer, its wiring burning against paper. Duy was dragging an injured guard away while Sơn pushed another outside. Minh lifted the metal drawer with the television balanced on it, carried the fire out, and set it under the hose. Tú turned on the water. Steam rose; the screen cracked; the flames went out.

Leon bound a gunman's bleeding forearm with the florist's apron he had kept as emergency cloth. Sơn asked if he was a medic.

“Ten years being friends with him,” Leon said, tightening the knot. “Continuing education.”

Duy sat on the curb with raw wrists and trousers torn from knee to ankle. He looked at the pair in Minh's hand and asked if he could borrow them.

Minh stared. Leon made it behind the Cadillac before his laughter became unmanageable.

Minh searched the pockets first: cash, a room key, an invitation to a private dinner at Harbor Crest. He kept the last two and gave Duy the trousers.

The dinner was two evenings away. Calvin Rook's name occupied the top of the invitation in expensive type. Minh held it under the edge of the awning until a drop ran from his sleeve rather than onto the card. Then he folded it into his pocket beside the room key.

Duy looked at the broken belt loop. “He going to want these back?” he asked.

“Ask him,” Minh said. “You can describe where he left them.”

Duy shook water from one cuff and began pulling the trousers on behind the open Cadillac door. Leon's laughter returned on the other side of the car, muffled unsuccessfully in his sleeve.

The car-wash owner arrived while Duy dressed. Minh offered Dante's name, the printed address, and money toward the counter. The owner asked whether Minh planned another visit. When Minh said no, the owner decided the relationship was improving.

At a noodle shop two blocks away, Duy got three mouthfuls down before explaining the job. Dante had called it an insurance arrangement approved by his father. Duy had been paid to drive. After they left the Meridian, the route changed and Dante put a gun in his lap, telling him to look employed.

“So you gambled the Cadillac,” Minh said.

Duy said he needed cash to escape. Tú pointed out that he could have driven away in the car. Duy asked who he was, read the answer on the shirt, and decided to continue eating.

Sơn moved a bowl closer to Duy's bruised wrist. Duy thanked him without making eye contact. It was a small crowded table; shoulders touched each time Leon reached for the condiments. Minh kept the empty seat beside himself clear with his foot until the waitress brought another bowl.

“Dante said everybody knew,” Duy said after a while. “A man from Rook's office paid me before we left. It wasn't just the boy talking.”

Minh stopped turning his spoon. “You knew the office man?”

“From routes. Deliveries. He had the paperwork,” Duy said. “Then Dante changed the route and the paperwork stopped helping.”

Minh put that claim beside the invitation in his mind. He wanted Duy fed, dry, and somewhere he could find him before pursuing the parts that still did not fit.

The remaining trucks, Duy said, had gone to cold storage near Pier Nine. Cash cases went into a blue container. Dante took the diamonds personally.

Minh waited until Duy looked up. “Why didn't you call?”

“Thought you were busy,” Duy said.

Even Tú put down his spoon. Duy looked around the table and admitted the explanation had sounded better inside his head.

Minh let him sit with it. Then he pushed the bread plate toward him.

“You know a yard and a container,” Minh said. “You know the man who tried folding you into a car. Finish eating. Show me the roads.”

Duy nodded. Sơn raised his good hand slightly and asked whether he was still driving.

“Yes,” Minh told him.

The fighter picked up his spoon. Leon pointed at him for Tú's benefit.

“Profession,” Leon said. “Try that on a shirt.”
