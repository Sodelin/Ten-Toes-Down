## Chapter 21: A Street Full of Presents

Rook's men blocked Bell Street with trucks and immediately encountered stronger opinions about parking than their briefing had prepared them for. By the time Duy brought the hospitality vehicle around the corner, the bakery owner was shouting at one driver with a broom in her hand. Marisol photographed another driver's number plate, then his face when he told her to stop.

At the Lê gate, Hòa held a truck door by its window frame. The truck's driver sat inside without it, staring straight ahead.

Minh got out and looked at the detached door.

“Across the gate,” Hòa explained.

“You asked him to move?” Minh said.

“We had a conversation.” Hòa leaned the door against the wall and returned to the courtyard.

Minh continued toward the garage. Mai had parked the Rolls diagonally across its front. Behind it, Felicia and Hương led neighbors through the rear passage to the next building. Carter's car stood sideways at the intersection with its lights flashing. Six gunmen occupied the opposite pavement.

Their leader wore a tan coat and held a radio. When he saw Minh, he took a step back, checked his men, and stopped retreating.

“Where's the money?” he demanded.

Minh looked at the trucks. “You bring receipts?”

The man said Rook wanted what belonged to him. Minh told him Rook could come ask. The leader raised his radio. Before his thumb reached the button, Minh crossed the road, caught his wrist, and pressed the man's own knuckles into his cheek.

“Tell him that,” Minh said.

The others raised their guns. Minh lifted the leader by the back of his coat and held him between them. The leader ordered them to shoot. The nearest gunman pointed out the person currently occupying the line of fire.

“I can see that!” the leader shouted.

From the hospitality vehicle, Leon praised the strength of the briefing.

The gunman by the bakery fired. Brick broke beside Minh's shoulder. Minh threw the leader into the shooter, caught the bakery owner's motorbike by the handlebars, and swung its rear wheel across the next man's legs. He fell flat. Minh set the bike back on its stand before pursuing the others.

The owner examined a new scrape. “Keeping track!” she called after him.

Two gunmen ran toward the garage. Mai waited behind the Rolls with a flashlight and the tire iron. As the first came around the car, she shone the beam in his eyes. He raised a hand. She hit his gun wrist, kicked him into his companion, and stepped aside as the pistol fell beneath the Rolls.

Minh reached them before they recovered. He caught the first man's shirt and put him headfirst into the washing machine standing by the door. The man's shoulders jammed in the opening. His legs kicked against the polished front.

His companion looked at the appliance and set down his gun.

“I quit,” the man said.

Mai took his keys and pointed him toward Carter. “That man wants to hear about it.”

He went. Carter looked from him to Mai, then opened his handcuffs. Inside the washer, the other man shouted that he couldn't breathe. Minh pulled him out by the belt and sat him on the ground.

“Then stop shouting,” Minh said.

Leon arrived with a bottle of water and asked whether the man wanted a rinse. Mai sent Leon back to a chair before his sling could become part of another scene.

At the intersection, a truck turned its steel front guard toward Carter's car and accelerated. Carter moved his prisoners behind a building and raised his radio. Minh ran toward it.

The tractor came around the corner first.

Sơn drove. Tú stood in the hatch wearing his life jacket over the gold-lettered shirt. The blade met the truck's guard with a crash that shook the shop windows. The truck stopped with its nose compressed beneath it. Its driver disappeared into an air bag.

Tú looked down into the cab. “Was that the brake?”

Sơn answered something Minh couldn't hear. The foam cannon fired. A solid white stream struck the windshield, spread over the cab, and engulfed the driver as he struggled with his door. Tú vanished into the hatch. The stream stopped.

For a moment Bell Street watched a truck become a wedding cake.

Leon called from his chair that somebody had ordered the full wedding package.

Minh waded through the foam, pulled the truck door open, and lifted the coughing driver out. Carter reached them with his radio still in his hand.

“I said keep it off the street,” Carter told Minh.

Minh pointed toward Sơn. “Different driver.”

Sơn raised his wrapped wrist from the cab and said he had followed instructions. Tú shouted from beneath the hatch that they were not his. Carter closed his eyes briefly before calling for another unit.

At the far end of the block, the remaining men decided to leave in one van. Three scrambled through its side door as the driver reversed toward the cross street. Tín stood near the curb holding a covered dish. The rear corner came toward him.

Minh started running and called Grandfather.

Tín turned his head, put one palm on the rear panel, and stopped the van. Metal folded around his hand. All three passengers fell toward the front seats. The back wheels spun against the pavement.

Tín looked at the dish in his other hand. He set it on a parked car, took the van's bumper with both hands, and lifted until the rear wheels reached his chest. The driver stopped accelerating. Tín tipped the vehicle toward its open side. Three guns slid into the road. One man followed, landing on his hands and knees.

The old man lowered the van and retrieved the food.

“They can wait,” Tín told Minh. “This gets cold.”

Minh stopped running. Across the street, Hòa watched from the courtyard gate. He caught Minh looking, shrugged once, and went to help Carter collect the van's occupants.

More police cars arrived. The surrendered men sat along the curb with their hands behind them, periodically looking toward Minh as if a better relationship with him might improve the seating. Hương insisted on reheating food once the shooting stopped. The street smelled of exhaust, foam, and broth.

Mai stood by the Rolls examining a scratch along its front wing. Minh offered to repair it.

“I know,” Mai said. “Deciding what you owe while you do.”

He put a hand at her waist and drew her close. She looked up. He checked her breathing, the steadiness of her hands, the clean skin beside her temple. Then he kissed her.

Leon looked away with elaborate politeness and announced support for public affection. Mai told him to stop narrating without letting go of Minh.

Bách called from the water. The Monarch had tied up at the old freight terminal. Men were loading crates, steel cages, heavy equipment. Pier lights pointed toward Bách's boat, and the boat had acquired bullet holes.

“Keep watching,” Minh told him.

Bách repeated the part about the holes more slowly. Minh looked toward the tractor.

“I'll bring thicker sides,” Minh said.

When he put away the phone, Mai watched him look from the captured men to the family inside the garage. Rook had sent men to the home, the shops, the street. Leaving him on the ship would only give him more time to hire replacements.

“Going back?” Mai asked.

Minh nodded.

“Finish it,” she said.

He went to the tractor and climbed onto its track. Sơn opened the hatch. Minh asked whether he could drive the recovery truck and told him to load the tractor on it. Tú raised his head from inside.

“Taking the tank?” Tú asked.

Brown paper had torn away from one gold dollar sign. Minh looked at it, then toward the harbor road.

“Returning a call,” Minh said.
