## Chapter 16: The Party Is on Television

Rook bought an hour of local television the next day and spent the first six minutes as a concerned parent. On the television above the garage bench, he sat opposite Joyce Dean wearing a charity ribbon and a face selected for public suffering.

Minh watched from the workbench. Mai printed wedding place cards beside him. Leon lay on the sofa with his injured arm in its sling and a bowl balanced on his stomach. Dante ate a sandwich he had been permitted to make for himself.

“My son has fallen in with dangerous men,” Rook said on the screen.

Dante looked up. “He bought my first gun.”

Leon pointed his spoon at the television. “Tell the host, nigga.”

Behind Rook hung a photograph of a fishing boat. Its name had been painted out, but Mai recognized the little dollar sign left on the cabin door. Before she finished saying it was Bách's, the garage phone rang.

“That is my fucking boat,” Bách shouted through the receiver.

Reggie arrived six minutes later with a padded bag of tapes and the address of Channel Six. The interview was live. He had bought the ballroom footage from Sơn's camera crew, kept the flower attack, filmed Peel's confession, and recorded the convoy recovery. He placed the convoy tape aside.

“Maybe that stays home,” Reggie said.

Leon nodded. “Excellent editorial judgment.”

Mai called Felicia and told her that her clothes were on television. Minh took his jacket from its hook. When Dante stood to join them, the room paused. Minh walked over and removed the receipt from his shirt.

“Keep that one,” Minh said.

At Channel Six, the receptionist looked from Minh to Rook on the waiting-room television. Onscreen, Rook explained his repeated unsuccessful attempts to discuss the matter privately.

“He's expecting me,” Minh told her.

“Apparently,” she said.

Reggie displayed his community-access badge. It had expired the previous year. He said the work hadn't. Mai told the receptionist that Rook was using a boat photograph he did not own and its owner was already on his way to correct him. Dante raised a hand and introduced himself as the concerned son.

The receptionist picked up her telephone.

Joyce Dean was discussing civic responsibility when the studio door opened. Minh came in with Mai beside him, followed by Dante and Reggie. Leon remained outside conducting a dispute with a vending machine.

Joyce looked at Minh, then at Rook. The producer behind the glass leaned forward. Joyce invited Minh into the spare chair.

“Scheduled interview,” Rook said, one hand gripping his armrest.

“And you were discussing him,” Joyce replied.

Minh sat. Mai took a chair behind the cameras where she could see both men. Dante stayed standing in his father's sight.

Joyce asked about the property Rook claimed Minh was holding. Minh described the hundred-thousand-dollar door job, Dante closing the shutters, and men shooting at his home.

“Accusations,” Rook interrupted.

Reggie raised his bag. “With pictures.”

The producer pointed at a technician, who came into the studio and took the first tape. Rook watched it change hands and announced that he would not participate in an ambush.

“You purchased the hour,” Minh reminded him.

On the monitor, the florist's gun emerged from the lilies. The cake table flew. The van rose until its rear wheels spun over Minh's head. Joyce watched without consulting her notes. When it ended, she asked if Rook knew the men.

He said no. Dante laughed once.

“You should be home,” Rook told his son.

“Which home?” Dante asked. “The one you sent men to, or the one you're saying I can't leave?”

Joyce asked whether Minh was detaining him. Dante glanced at the man beside her.

“Mostly feeding me,” Dante said.

Mai held up the receipt. “Clothing too.”

The nearest cameraman made a choking sound. Rook stood. His guard came in through the studio door and put a hand beneath his jacket.

Minh slid an empty chair across the floor with one foot. It caught the guard behind the knees. The man sat involuntarily and traveled backward until the chair struck the wall. A pistol fell into his lap. Every camera turned.

Joyce looked into the nearest lens. “We're staying with this.”

Behind the glass, the producer nodded hard enough to dislodge his headset.

The second guard went for the control room. Reggie saw him push the technician away and reach across the console. The studio monitor jumped, then went black.

Leon appeared behind the guard carrying an unopened bag of chips and the vending machine's coin box. He brought the box down across the man's head. The guard folded over the controls. The technician pulled his arm clear and restored the picture.

Through the glass, Leon held up the box. “Machine owed me money.”

In the studio, Dante stood between Rook and the exit. He asked why Peel had hired him, why his father had promised him part of the business. Rook ordered him aside and said they would discuss it elsewhere.

Dante stayed. Rook slapped him hard enough to knock him against the wall.

Minh stood between them before the second blow. He caught Rook's wrist and turned him toward the camera. The red light above its lens remained on.

“Concerned parent,” Minh said.

Rook looked into the lens, straightened his jacket with his free hand, and waited until Minh released him. Then he walked out. His guards followed only after they could stand.

Joyce stayed seated. She looked at the empty chair, then at Minh.

“Twenty-two minutes left,” Joyce said.

Leon entered with his chips and coin box. “You got food sponsors?”

Mai laughed behind the camera. The producer asked Minh to stay. Reggie already held the next tape.

They played Peel's confession in the cake. On the monitor, the sailor's cap sat crooked while he described the false theft and the intended dependency. Joyce followed with three short questions. Minh answered two. When she asked where the cash was, he said Rook had already demonstrated what he did with an address.

Joyce accepted that boundary and turned to Dante. He described the loading order, the stolen jewelry, and his father's plan. He also described his own part. He had stolen from Rook, hired the men, threatened Duy. The account came in starts, with places he plainly wanted to make himself sound better and failed. Mai did not nod him through them.

When he finished, Joyce let a second of silence remain. Then she asked whether he had told police.

“Not yet,” Dante admitted.

Minh looked toward the entrance. Carter stood there with two officers.

“He can now,” Minh said.

Bách arrived in the last commercial break carrying a framed photograph with the boat's name still visible. Moss followed with a photograph of his own, concerned that the audience might think Bách possessed the biggest available vessel.

Mai sat beside Minh on the waiting-room sofa while the men took their corrections into the studio. Joyce let them explain ownership, length, engines, and the bar until both had made useful contributions to their own embarrassment. Leon watched it on the television and praised the programming.

The receptionist handed Reggie a new badge application. He accepted it with both hands, as if the year had finally promoted him.

Outside, Carter spoke to Dante beside the police car. Dante looked at Minh over its roof. Minh nodded once. The young man turned back and continued answering.

Mai leaned against the Rolls. “He won't get quiet because people saw him.”

“No,” Minh agreed.

“Good. I want to hear him coming.” Mai opened the passenger door.

Leon stopped beside them with his phone at his ear. His face had acquired the brightness Minh associated with prices that came with consequences.

“Convertible,” Leon whispered, covering the receiver. “Guy saw me on television. Knows my cousin.”

Mai closed her eyes. Minh held out his hand until Leon surrendered the phone.
