## Chapter 14: The Prize Convoy

Leon arrived at the packaging yard the next morning in a sling and his best black shirt. Mai sat behind the hospitality vehicle's wheel. She told him he was not driving before he had asked.

“Appreciating the equipment,” Leon said, climbing into the passenger seat. He opened the little refrigerator and found that Bách had stocked it. Mai handed him water before his appreciation became selective.

Across the yard, Duy sat at the recovery truck's wheel with Dante beside him. The arrangement pleased neither man.

“You put me in a car the other day,” Duy said.

“Five days,” Dante corrected.

Duy stared at him. “Improving the crime with a calendar?”

Minh checked a heavy chain and laid it on the towing platform. Tú asked about code names. Leon called him Ready, explaining that his job was lifting when someone said lift. Tú tried Ready Chigga aloud. Mai covered her face for a second before returning to the mirrors.

Reggie checked the camera battery in the hospitality vehicle's rear seat. Sơn waited beside the Cadillac. He had taken his championship belt out and left it on a bench. When Minh looked at it, Sơn shrugged and said it caught on things.

Minh handed him a radio. “Stay in front. If they stop, keep moving until I call.” He looked at the others. “Container. Nobody hunts for Rook.”

Dante said his father would not be there. Minh told him that should make the instruction easier.

At eight thirteen, the white-roofed lead van came out of Pier Nine. Dante pointed through the windshield. Behind it rolled the truck carrying the blue container, then black and silver escorts and a rear van. The white patch on the container's corner was visible as it turned. Its number ended forty-six.

Minh stood on the recovery platform holding the rail behind the cab. Sơn put the Cadillac into traffic ahead of the convoy. Duy pulled out two vehicles behind it. Mai followed at a distance in Bách's black hospitality car, its gold dollar sign defeating some of its discretion.

“Subtle uncle,” Leon remarked over the radio.

At the low bridge, Sơn slowed. The lead van sounded its horn. He raised a hand in acknowledgment and maintained the same speed. The container crawled around the bend. Behind it, the black escort moved outward; Duy put the recovery truck into the gap before it could pass.

Its passenger looked up at Minh standing above him. Minh nodded, took his hands from the rail, and jumped.

He landed with both feet on the container's rear locking bar. Steel bent beneath him. He caught an upper hinge and brought one foot higher until he stood beside the door. The road moved beneath his heels.

The black escort's passenger raised a pistol. Minh tore the lower locking bar free and dropped it across the vehicle's hood. It bounced through the windshield and lodged across the front seats. The car swerved into the curb and stopped with one wheel turned inward.

Peel's key opened the lock on the first turn. Minh pulled the right door out. A shotgun fired from inside. He swung behind the steel, heard pellets strike its inner face, and kicked it back. The edge hit the shooter across the shoulder, throwing him along the floor beside a parked car.

Inside the container, two cars sat nose to tail. A third stood on a steel rack above the farther one. Cash cases filled the narrow spaces around them. Four guards occupied what room remained.

The nearest drew a knife. Minh took his wrist, turned him toward the open rear, and pushed his head between the doors. He tore the remaining locking bar from its bracket and bent it around the man's waist and the door handle, securing him sideways against the steel.

Another guard climbed a hood with a pistol. Minh put his hand through the side window, caught the steering wheel, and dragged the car's front end sideways. Tires squealed. The guard fell between the car and the container wall, firing into the ceiling. Minh stepped onto the hood and pressed the gun hand flat against its roof until the weapon dropped.

The last standing guard looked out at the road passing behind them and set his pistol on the floor.

“I got a daughter,” he said.

“Lie down,” Minh told him.

The man lay down with unusual care, as if good performance might qualify him for an earlier exit.

The driver accelerated out of the bend. A loose cash case slid toward the opening. Minh caught its handle, saw hundreds through the split lid, and shut it with his heel before wedging it beneath a car.

The silver escort pulled alongside. A gunman rose through its roof. Minh picked up the surrendered pistol, emptied the magazine out of it, cleared the chamber into his palm, and threw the unloaded weapon. It struck the roof gunman's forehead. He folded back inside. His long gun bounced from the roof onto the road.

Mai swerved around it. Leon complained over the radio about littering; Reggie asked whether he wanted to collect it. Leon reminded him he had retired from bending for the day.

Minh climbed onto the container roof. Wind caught his wet collar as he ran forward and dropped onto the cab. The roof dented. He bent to the passenger window and told the driver to pull over.

The driver reached beneath his seat. Minh pulled the passenger door off and held it outside.

“Better view?” Minh asked.

The driver brought his hand back and braked.

From the cab, Minh saw the rear van closing on Mai. Its sliding door opened; a barrel came up toward the hospitality vehicle's back window.

“Mai,” Leon said over the radio, voice suddenly sharp.

Mai braked. The van surged alongside before the gunman corrected his aim. She turned into its rear wheel with the hospitality vehicle's armored running board. The van swung sideways. Its gunman fell inside as the sliding door slammed on his arm. Mai straightened, accelerated, and pushed it through the open entrance of a tire yard. Its nose disappeared in a stack of old tires.

“Tell him about that when he complains about my parking,” Mai told Leon.

The container truck stopped by the refrigeration plant. Duy brought the recovery truck behind it. Tú jumped down with the chain, tripped over its last loop, recovered, and presented it as if the stumble had been a delivery flourish.

Minh attached the chain to the container's rear corner fitting and the recovery boom. Duy lowered the boom until it took tension. Minh went to the front, checked the trailer's released locks, got both hands beneath the steel edge, and lifted.

The container came free. Inside, a guard shouted and a car alarm began. The man tied to the rear door shut his eyes.

“Move the truck,” Minh called.

The original driver stared through his missing door. Dante jumped down from Duy's cab, pulled him out by the sleeve, and drove the tractor unit and empty trailer forward. For a moment the blue container hung between Minh's hands and the recovery boom.

Tú stood with his mouth open. Minh told him to extend the deck. He ran.

Minh ducked beneath the load, moved his grip toward the center, and straightened until the entire container rested across his shoulders. The chain slackened. Tú unhooked it. Duy swung the recovery truck around, backed its extended deck beneath the suspended weight, and waited while Minh stepped sideways to line up the edges.

Minh lowered it. Steel hit steel. The truck settled on all six tires. Inside, the car alarm stopped.

Leon arrived beside them and looked out through the hospitality window. “Generous wedding registry.”

The lead van returned as Minh closed the container. Sơn followed in the Cadillac, horn sounding continuously. The escort driver had escaped by crossing a curb and an empty parking lot. Now he accelerated toward the recovery truck, saw Minh, and swerved toward the plant.

Minh caught the open side-door rail as it passed and pulled sideways. The van spun around him, hit the curb broadside, and tipped. He let go before its roof struck the road. Glass burst beneath it; the horn remained on.

Sơn stopped twenty feet away. “I kept moving,” he told Minh.

“I saw,” Minh said.

Sirens sounded in the distance. Minh released the man fastened to the door and called the other guards out. He left all four beside the overturned van, clear of traffic, then climbed onto the recovery truck's rear step. Duy drove before the sirens reached the junction.

At Bách's yard, eleven people had found reasons to attend the opening. Moss stood closest until Bách corrected the arrangement. Mai held the silver key. Leon lowered himself into a folding chair and watched Duy open the first case.

Hundreds filled it edge to edge. The next case looked the same. Minh checked bundles, counted cases, and examined the cars behind them. Eight million in cash sat on the deck of his newly acquired truck.

Leon removed his sunglasses. “Nigga.”

“Technical term,” Tú said quietly.

Mai closed the nearest case and pointed toward the gate. “Doors. Before the city comes shopping.”

Tú ran to shut them. Minh looked at Mai, still holding the little silver key.

“Large enough?” he asked.

She smiled at the container. “We're getting another press.”
