Chapter 18: The Wrong Suit

Felicia took the wedding suit out of Minh's hand the next morning and put it behind her counter.

“Absolutely not,” she said.

“I'm not negotiating,” Minh told her.

“That strengthens my answer.” Felicia pointed toward a rack. “Threaten people in something else. You get married in that.”

Mai chose a dark gray suit. Felicia said it was expensive too. Mai told her to charge him. Dangerous men should support local business.

Minh stepped onto the fitting platform while Felicia pinned a small correction at his shoulder. Her fingers worked more sharply than usual. The cancellation calls had reached most intended passengers. The barber and his wife had stayed home. Curtis had stopped selling tickets after Mai asked how he planned to refund them. Bách's people had carried warnings through the restaurants and shops.

Thirty-one workers were already aboard.

They had arrived the previous afternoon to prepare food, tables, instruments, cabins, flowers. Denise, Felicia's assistant, had delivered table runners in her brother's van and stayed to help. Felicia reached her once. Denise said they had been told to remain below for a security inspection. Then the ship's phone stopped answering.

“She doesn't like boats,” Felicia told Minh, smoothing the jacket with an angry little pull. “I said leave the things and come straight back.”

Minh found her eyes in the mirror. “I'll bring her to the shop.”

Felicia held his gaze. “Bring the jacket too,” she said. “I like both.”

Carter met them at the packaging yard in an unmarked car. The younger officer with him could not stop looking at the tractor. Mai had covered its dollar signs with brown paper, but the tracks remained difficult to explain as anything else.

The harbor unit knew about the Monarch, Carter said. Its crew answered Rook's calls and ignored theirs. Dante was cooperating with a lawyer present. He had supplied a layout, the names of the workers, and the location of the stolen jewelry: a steel case in the bridge safe.

“His father brought a second crew aboard,” Carter said. “Men who weren't at the hotel or the yard.”

Minh spread the layout on the recovery truck's hood. Vehicle space below, dining saloon above, narrow stairs, bridge at the top. A broad bow ramp joined the ship to the pier.

“He told them you can't be hurt,” Carter said.

Mai came beside Minh. “Then they should enjoy the evening.”

Carter looked at her. “He told them to shoot everybody else.”

The yard fell quiet. Leon lowered the cloth he had been using on the convertible's mirror. Duy stopped by the truck's open fuel compartment. Minh kept his palms against the ship's plan until the paper stopped moving in the breeze.

“Names,” Minh said.

Carter handed him the list. Minh read every one. He did not assume he would recognize the people Rook had selected for him.

Before leaving for the harbor, he and Mai went to the Lê house. Mai had called ahead. Bích ironed a tablecloth while Hương argued about which wedding dishes could be prepared early. Tín ate by the window. Hòa had the doorbell taken apart on newspaper.

Tín inspected the gray suit. “Wrong day.”

“Wrong suit,” Mai said. “Wedding one's safe.”

Hương put down the dish she was holding and asked how many people. Minh told her thirty-one. Nobody made him repeat the number.

Hòa stood, went to the cupboard, and brought out a canvas bag. He put it in Mai's hands. Flashlights, batteries, clean cloths. She opened it and looked inside.

“You always need something after everybody else leaves,” Hòa said.

Mai hugged him. He held the back of her head briefly, then let her go. Minh looked at the small bag, remembering the way Hòa's hand had stopped the pickup. He had come with a plan full of vehicles and keys; Hòa had brought the things people would need after the plan.

Tín set down his bowl. “Calvin still thinks it's about money?”

“He wants his back,” Minh said.

“He can ask.” Tín rose, and the room paused with him. Then he carried the empty bowl toward the kitchen. “Finish before the wedding. Bích paid for flowers.”

Minh smiled despite the tightness in his chest. Hương caught his sleeve and inspected the healing cut. New shirt, she observed. He should try not bleeding through it immediately.

At the door, Hòa tested the repaired bell. It rang once. Tín shouted from the kitchen that it was too loud. Hòa explained that it announced people; Tín said he could hear people. Mai laughed as she took Minh down the steps.

Back in the yard, Reggie had taped PRESS across his camera bag. Leon asked if bullets could read. Reggie hoped somebody in charge could, and requested that Minh stop walking out of frame.

Sơn brought two men from his old training gym. Their attention moved from Minh to the tractor to Sơn's brace. He told them to stay with the rescued people, help them walk, and chase nothing. One looked surprised. Sơn touched the brace and said he had chased enough for the month.

Duy checked the recovery truck's fuel. Tú counted the flashlights twice and placed them in the hospitality vehicle. He took the task seriously enough that nobody interrupted his second count.

Bách and Moss argued beside the gate over which boat could reach the outer piers faster. Moss had two engines. Bách had a kitchen. Each treated the other's advantage as a failure of imagination.

“Both,” Minh told them. “Stay outside the pier until I call.”

Bách agreed first. Moss agreed harder. Leon watched them leave and praised competitive donors.

At sunset, Rook called. Minh stood beside the gray suit hanging from the recovery truck's open door. Mai had packed one million dollars into a case and set it on the seat.

“You're coming,” Rook said.

“With a million,” Minh told him. “Thirty-one people walk down your ramp. Then we discuss what you don't like.”

Rook demanded eight. Minh held the phone at his ear without answering while the old man explained whose conditions governed the ship.

“Enjoy your evening,” Minh said, beginning to lower it.

Rook called his name. Minh waited.

“The million comes aboard first,” Rook said.

“People leave,” Minh answered.

“We'll see,” Rook said.

Minh hung up. Mai lifted the case with both hands and gave him the handle.

“He's keeping them,” she said.

“I know,” Minh replied.

She checked the canvas bag again, closed it, and slung it over her shoulder. Minh put on the gray jacket. Leon whistled from beside the convertible, calling it an expensive bad decision. Mai straightened Minh's collar, then stepped back to inspect him as Felicia had.

“That's why we're bringing it home,” Mai said.

Minh checked Carter's list once more before folding it into his inside pocket. Thirty-one names. The cash had weight in his hand. The list weighed nothing he could demonstrate by lifting it.

Chapter 19: Dinner on Moving Water

Lights hung between the Monarch's upper rails. Through the saloon windows, Minh could see white tablecloths and flower arrangements prepared by people who had been forbidden to leave. A banner over the bow ramp welcomed Bell Street.

At its foot, a guard looked from Minh's case to Mai's canvas bag.

“Only him,” the guard said.

Mai looked up at the banner. “You invited the street. I'm on it.”

The guard began explaining Rook's instructions. Behind the saloon glass, Rook lifted one hand. The guard stepped aside before Minh had to help him understand the correction.

They walked up together. The vehicle deck smelled of diesel, paint, and food kept warm upstairs. Laundry carts lined one wall. Guards occupied the doors to the lower service rooms. Minh counted six visible guns before the stairs.

“Nobody wants trouble,” the man behind him said.

Minh glanced at his pistol. “You packed for it.”

At the top, another guard tried taking Mai's bag. She opened it herself and showed the flashlights, cloths, and batteries. He looked disappointed. Mai asked what he had hoped for, shut the bag, and continued into the saloon without waiting for an answer.

Rook stood beside a table set for two. Mai borrowed a third chair and put it beside Minh's. Rook watched the legs drag over his carpet.

“You take liberties,” Rook told her.

“You keep buying the wrong number of chairs,” Mai said.

Minh put the case on the table. Rook's eyes went to it immediately. He ordered Minh to open it. Minh asked for the workers. Rook began explaining that it was his ship. Minh clicked the lid open just far enough to expose the money, then shut it again when the explanation stopped.

“Thirty-one,” Minh said.

Rook told the guard to bring them up.

They entered in groups. A woman in a stained apron, a man carrying his shoes, two musicians clutching instrument cases. Denise came with one sleeve torn where someone had grabbed her. She saw Mai, stopped, and asked for Felicia.

“Waiting at the shop,” Mai told her.

Denise cried, wiped her face angrily, and said she had promised to handle the delivery herself. Felicia would have something to say.

Mai took her hand. “Several things. You'll have chances to answer.”

Minh matched each person to Carter's list while Mai asked names. Some answered immediately. Others stared until she repeated the question. Rook grew impatient as the line lengthened beside his unused tables.

“All here,” Rook announced.

Minh counted once more. “Thirty.”

Rook said thirty-one. His guard counted with one finger and stopped at the final man. He looked toward Rook.

“Marine crew,” Rook said. “He's attending the machinery.”

“Gus Navarro,” Minh read from the list. “Bring him.”

Rook explained that the machinery could not be left unattended. Minh closed his hand around the case handle. Rook reached across the table and stopped when Minh looked at his fingers.

The engines hummed beneath the floor. Nobody in the line moved. Mai kept Denise beside her and watched Rook's hand withdraw.

Rook said the others could leave while Navarro's replacement was arranged. Minh looked at Mai and nodded toward the stairs.

“Take them to Carter,” Minh said.

She pressed his wrist once. “Thirty.”

He nodded. She led them out in a line, asking the musicians to watch the people ahead instead of their cases. Minh stood at the saloon window while Rook remained beside him.

On the pier, Sơn and the two gym men met the first group. Duy opened the hospitality vehicle's doors. Reggie filmed from the rail with his camera low; Leon handed out bottles from a crate.

“Take two,” Leon told an apron-clad woman when she reached him.

She said she only needed one. Leon said she could surprise herself later and tucked a second into the bag she carried.

“Safe,” Rook said, watching the first ten move away.

“Twenty still on the stairs,” Minh replied.

Rook accused him of making everything laborious. Minh looked from the ramp to the case.

“Rubber doorstop,” Minh reminded him. “Still cheaper.”

Mai helped Denise down the final slope. A musician stumbled; Sơn caught the case and passed it back unopened. Twenty ashore. Rook left the window and lifted a telephone from the wall.

Minh watched the reflection of his mouth in the glass. Rook told someone to keep the engines at idle. Their note changed under Minh's shoes.

Minh opened the cash case and removed one bundle. Rook turned immediately. Minh laid the bundle beside the case and let him come back toward it while the final group descended. Outside, Mai waited until the last man stepped onto the pier. Then she looked up, raised three fingers, and closed her hand.

Thirty. Minh nodded to her.
