Outside Bách's hall, guests had filled the pavement before the doors opened. Polished cars stood among flower vans. A man Minh had once helped recover a stolen truck arrived carrying a roasted pig. Marisol brought an envelope and said she had deducted nothing, making Leon visibly suspicious. Veronica arrived with her husband and reported that the arcade had never earned as much as during the week people came to see the damaged pool table.

Leon asked whether the information was her gift. She handed Minh a wrapped box and explained which part belonged to him and which part was information for Leon's broke ass. Leon protested that he was no longer broke. Veronica told him to pay Marisol. Marisol held out her hand without turning around.

Minh watched Leon pay with the satisfaction of a man attending a second ceremony.

Sơn and Tú stood at the entrance. Felicia had fitted Sơn's sleeve around the brace. Tú's mother had kept all three branded shirts at home as insurance. He wore a plain suit and held a guest list with the seriousness of an immigration official.

Minh told them to eat when the food arrived. Tú asked what to do if someone tried entering. Ask the name, Minh said. What if the man lied? Then Tú would have met somebody.

Leon put a hand on Tú's shoulder and announced that he had listed the cousins. Minh took the pages. Half the last sheet consisted of men called Curtis.

“Different branches,” Leon explained.

Mai called Minh's name from the dressing room. He returned the list without attempting to prune it and went inside.

She stood before a mirror with Hương beside her. The dress he had seen unfinished now had its proper neckline, fitted waist, and train. Her earrings moved when she turned. Minh stopped inside the door.

Hương watched his face and smiled. “Good. He noticed.”

Mai asked for a minute. Her mother kissed her cheek and touched Minh's arm on the way out. Felicia checked the hem once more before following.

Minh approached. Mai warned him that everyone had already used the good words. He took her hand.

“I want to take you home,” Minh said.

“We paid for the food,” Mai reminded him.

“They can eat without us,” Minh said.

Mai glanced toward the hall. “Leon can. The others might wonder why we've disappeared.”

Minh kissed her forehead carefully, avoiding anything Felicia might have arranged under threat. Mai touched the tie bar beneath his chin.

“That's Dad's,” Mai said.

“He gave it to me this morning,” Minh said.

Her finger found the dent. “When I was little, I'd stand on his shoes when he got home,” Mai told him. “He'd walk me around the room, and I'd try to reach this.”

Minh looked down at the silver, then back at her face. “I'll take care of it,” he promised.

“He'll ask,” Mai said.

Leon knocked and announced that it was time. He had told the officiant they were probably discussing taxes. Mai shut her eyes laughing. Minh opened the door and instructed Leon to stand exactly where the woman had told him.

Leon looked at them. For once, the next joke did not arrive.

“Yeah,” he said. “All right.”

The ceremony followed rehearsal until the rings, when Leon produced the real box, opened it correctly, and handed it over without mishap. The officiant looked surprised. Several guests laughed before he could explain.

“I can grow,” Leon said, raising his good hand.

She waited. Leon lowered the hand. Minh looked at Mai and found her trying not to laugh. He was doing no better.

Mai spoke first when it was time for their vows. She held Minh's healing hand between hers.

“You brought groceries when there was a hundred thousand dollars in the car,” Mai said. “The milk still got put away first.”

Laughter went through the room. Leon nodded from beside Minh, claiming the refrigeration department without interrupting.

“I know what your days look like,” Mai continued. “How many people want part of them. I want a home with you, and work I'm proud of, and dinner where you tell me what happened before someone arrives with a refrigerator.”

Bách glanced toward Moss. Moss faced forward.

Mai squeezed Minh's hand. “I love being with you. Even when you're impossible to dress. I choose you.”

Minh drew a breath. One written version of his vows remained in his pocket. Another had suffered Leon's demand for more jokes and Hương's demand for fewer. The words he wanted now had arrived that morning with tea and a small silver bar.

“First time we went out, I thought I needed a better car,” Minh said. “Better table. Spent half the evening trying to look like I knew what I was doing.”

Mai smiled at him.

“Then you said you liked my motorcycle,” Minh continued. “Still do. I'll keep a place for you, whatever I'm driving. I'll come home. I'll listen when you tell me I'm an idiot. Might need it twice.”

“I've had practice,” Mai said softly.

He laughed, steadied his breath, and went on. “I want the ordinary days too. Shop busy. Sink leaking. You asleep before the story's finished. I love you, Mai. I choose you.”

She put his ring on with care around the healing skin. He put hers on without assistance. When the officiant finished, the room stood before being invited. Leon shouted first. Reggie filmed while shouting through the side of his mouth. Hương cried openly. Hòa stood with a hand on her shoulder. Bích held Tín's hand in both hers. Tín smiled without trying to conceal it.

Minh kissed his wife. Somewhere beyond the applause, Tú announced that he had secured the whole door. Nobody asked which one.

Before the meal, Minh and Mai went to the family table. Hòa rose. Minh bowed his head before him, deliberately and without a hand on his neck. Then he did the same for Tín. The old man straightened one cufflink with his thumb.

“Better when I don't help,” Tín said.

Minh laughed. Hòa drew him into a short embrace, careful of his shoulder despite Minh's repeated assurances. Hương already had Mai in her arms. Bích reached for Minh next and advised him to sit before someone gave a speech.

Leon had reached the microphone several seconds earlier.

He opened with the line about knowing Minh when he was poor enough to listen. The room laughed. Encouraged, Leon unfolded a second page nobody had approved. Felicia sat forward.

“One jacket and three plans,” Leon said. “Every plan involved making that jacket look more expensive. Then he met Mai. Jacket got cleaned. Suddenly he knew when the printshop closed. Suddenly I couldn't borrow the motorcycle because love had transportation needs.”

Mai leaned against Minh's shoulder as the room laughed around them. Minh covered his eyes.

“Now cars, boat, refrigerator bigger than my first apartment,” Leon continued. “People send furniture because they're scared to send opinions.”

Moss raised his glass. Bách raised his higher.

Leon lowered the paper. “Still answers when I call. That's my brother. Mai, you know what you got. Wanted everybody else to hear.” He swallowed, looked briefly annoyed by it, and lifted his glass. “Be happy. Stay rich. Buy something with a back seat.”

They drank. Mai wiped the corner of her eye and whispered that Leon could keep the speech. Minh said he would frame its first page.

Food arrived in quantities sufficient to stop the argument about the two keys. Duy sat beside Sơn and explained the judgment required to drive a recovery truck. Sơn listened for three minutes, then asked whether judgment happened before or after backing into the trailer. Tú's mother made him remove his jacket before sauce reached it. He obeyed without mentioning rank.

Carter sat off duty beside Navarro, whose injured ankle occupied a spare chair. Leon accused the sergeant of hiding money because his suit fitted. Navarro described the Monarch's damaged machinery to the red-awning cook until the cook established that nobody planned to serve the engine and changed the subject to lunch.

When Minh thanked Carter for coming, the officer mentioned Rook remained in custody. His lawyer said he was uncomfortable. Minh suggested sending a chair brochure. Carter reminded him he was off duty, raised his glass, and returned to his food.

Minh enjoyed every table. Men stood to shake his hand. Chairs opened for Mai. Envelopes disappeared into their palms. People called them back for photographs. The jeweler whose necklace had been returned gave Mai a small velvet box and refused to discuss its price. Inside were earrings. She thanked him and put the box beside her plate.

Marisol informed Minh that his security photograph would remain in her shop. He suggested using the wedding portrait.

“You look available for conversation in that one,” Marisol said. “Useless.”

The only problem at the door arrived after dessert. Ernie wore the same red jacket that had ignored four years of fashion and carried a bottle with a ribbon. Tú inspected the clipboard while Ernie complained that he had provided the car for Minh's first date.

“Which name is that under?” Tú asked.

Minh heard them on his way toward the bathroom and came outside. Ernie spread his arms in greeting. Minh looked at the bottle and asked whether it was paid for.

“Never letting that go,” Ernie said.

“Tow truck did,” Minh replied.

Tú looked between them with growing interest. Ernie offered the bottle and congratulations, saying he meant them. Minh accepted it. Through the open entrance, Mai watched from their table with one eyebrow raised.

“Something for her?” Minh asked.

Ernie looked into the hall. He apologized for the restaurant. Minh told him to say it louder. Ernie drew a breath and apologized directly to Mai loudly enough that several nearby guests turned.

She studied him, then pointed toward the empty seat beside the cook. “There's food left, Ernie.”

Ernie nodded and went inside before the invitation changed. Tú lowered the clipboard, disappointed that nothing else would happen. Minh gave him the bottle and told him the next event was cake. He held the door open while Tú went through, then followed him back to Mai.

Their first dance went well. Minh waited for her step. She moved close, her hand resting lightly on his shoulder, and they crossed into a slow turn. Glasses still touched tables, people still spoke, but he followed the music and Mai's small laugh when his foot missed the edge of her train.

“See?” Mai said.

“Always good,” Minh told her.

“There he is,” Mai said, coming back beneath his raised arm.

Leon applauded early. Felicia pulled his hand down. Reggie sidestepped with the camera and nearly backed into Bách, who was trying to occupy the background of every important photograph. The song ended. Minh kept Mai close another moment and asked about the motorcycle.

“Later,” she said. “Another dress.”

They left after dark. Mai changed into a shorter white dress and low shoes. Minh removed the tie and placed Hòa's bar in its box. He gave the jacket to Felicia with a formal declaration that it had encountered no fish.

The motorcycle waited beside the hall, cleaned the previous morning before Leon and the chicken arrived. Two helmets sat on the seat. Hương put food in the rear case. Bích added more. Tín waited until Minh latched it, then tested the fastening with one finger and approved.

Hòa shook Minh's hand. “Get some sleep tomorrow.”

“That's the plan,” Minh said.

Mai kissed her mother and grandmother, hugged her father, and touched Tín's cheek. She fastened her helmet and settled behind Minh. Leon began offering assistance for any possible need. Mai specified tomorrow. He stepped back and announced respect for a healthy boundary.

Minh started the engine. Mai's arms came around his waist. Her hands met over his shirt where they had on rides home four years earlier. He pulled away slowly enough for everyone to wave.

At the corner, a red light held them while a bus passed. Mai rested her helmet against his back.

“Husband,” she said.

He turned his head a little. “Wife.”

She squeezed him. The light changed.

He took the long road along the harbor, city lights to their left and dark water to the right. The motorcycle ran cleanly. At the familiar uneven patch near home, he slowed so she would not bounce against his shoulder.

They parked under the shop light. Mai took the food from the rear case and followed him upstairs. Minh unlocked the apartment, opened the door wide, and stepped aside while she carried the packages into the kitchen.

She set them down, came back, and took his hand.

“You too,” Mai said.

Minh went inside.
