## Book I, Chapters 1–4

### Narrative and listening

First-person Aiden remains the narrator. Speaker changes are attributed with speech tags or actions in the same paragraph. Each scene gains movement through a located room or street; character opinions remain Aiden's impressions rather than unexplained access to other minds. The rewriting is scene-by-scene authored prose, not a global dialogue-tag replacement.

### Chapter 1

Preserved: mistaken identity, cash and gifts, Flores's landlord story, banner nickname, Saint's microphone/bus reception, beef wedding gift, Shawna's machines and agency, four attackers, shot through van roof, wrist and window injuries, return to noodles, invitation and check.

Example: the original moves directly from the mistaken-identity opening to the gift total. The rewrite places Aiden beside the car with the suitcase between his ankles; the dog tangles its leash around the luggage while the giver requests a photograph. This gives the crowd business to perform before the next speaker arrives.

Example: the source's brief clipboard fold becomes a grasp, downward pull, release, knee brace, break, and return. The two versions preserve the same outcome; the rewrite lets a listener locate hands and bodies without inventing the movement.

Tradeoff: the comic welcome takes longer. Distinctive existing punchlines are retained where they still earn their place; the new connective prose is not attributed to Nolan.

### Chapter 2

### Chapter 3

Preserved: Honest Work, publicist invention, Bảo's haircut/grapes, 20-to-40-percent bargain and protected deliveries, Voss's chain-breaking demonstration, Hậu's elbow warning, extraordinary jump, Voss defeated but alive, gifts/phone call, signed agreement, Vinh's threat/watch, Hậu invited for food.

The ring, balcony, spectators, and betting tables are established before the jump. The shrimp now falls near the fighters and briefly interrupts their attention. The source's remark about a nonexistent referee is made explicit through Saint shouting into a vacancy in the event's organization. The danger remains real: Aiden is trapped, bloodied, and hurt before winning.

Tradeoff: retaining Aiden's limited point of view reduces access to spectators' motives; uncertain motives are presented as his interpretation.

### Chapter 4

Preserved: shirts/revenue split, Hậu breakfast and wrestling name, Pearl tour and advance revenue, borrowed bronze statue, lift failure and worker rescue, split shoe, payment, Sunday/Leon Mercer with gifts and sister referral, rejected shoe obligation, Loan's acceptance of statue, Shawna's stage inspection, theft and red-box threat.

The lift failure now locates the endangered worker, trapped shoe, ramp, truck, plinth, and Aiden's weight transfer. The later move to the barber shop is staged with moving equipment. No discovery of the red box is added prematurely. Hậu's work history is identified as information Aiden learned over the previous night's food rather than an unexplained biographical insert.

The source's explicit “twenty-six hours” after a next-morning opening was replaced by “We've barely unpacked.” This preserves the rapid accumulation without asserting incompatible elapsed time.

Tradeoff: the statue's spectacle is longer and more physically specific. Sunday's threatening courtesy still arrives after an actual rescue, without the narrator claiming to know his private intentions.
