## Chapter 11: The Man Under the Table

Vinh offered Hậu a ten-percent raise. Hậu asked for twenty.

We sat in Dad's blue sedan behind the cinema while the argument continued. My left arm throbbed beneath the strip of jacket Shawna had tied around it. Rafa had brought a clean shirt from the tow truck. Saint had brought five suggestions for what to call the evening on social media.

“Call it Saturday,” I told Saint. “Put the phone away.”

He lowered it. Beside me, Hậu listened to Vinh for another minute, then raised a finger as if the man could see him.

“Dental too,” Hậu said.

He waited, looked at me, and shrugged.

“Because I have teeth,” Hậu explained into the phone.

Saint turned toward the window and shook silently against the seat. Hậu waited for Vinh to finish before hanging up. He put the phone on the dashboard with care.

“Back entrance,” Hậu told me. “Fifteen minutes. Alone.”

“You don't have to go in,” I said.

He looked over. “I know.”

“He'll search you,” I said.

“I know,” Hậu repeated.

“He might already know you're with me,” I told him.

Hậu rolled his shoulders against the seat. “Then he'll be expecting a better performance.”

His hands lay loose on his knees. Neither shook. I looked at the scar across his knuckles, then back at his face.

“What do you want from him?” I asked.

Hậu rubbed his thumb over the scar before answering.

“Four years ago he gave me work,” he said. “I was sleeping in my cousin's laundry room. I thought carrying money in a nice car meant I had a nice job.”

“And now?” I asked.

He flexed the hand and closed it again. “Now I want him to hear me quit.”

We parked two blocks from Crown Billiards. Rafa came behind us in the tow truck and stopped farther along the curb. Above the building's blacked-out windows, a gold crown had been painted over the entrance. An actual crown would have looked less suspicious.

Saint stayed with the sedan while Rafa waited beside his truck. Voss arrived in his own car and parked across the street. He still looked annoyed that I wouldn't let him run anywhere with his jaw half-healed. I pointed toward his position until he settled against the fender.

Hậu went around the building to the back. I waited long enough for him to reach it, then walked through the front door.

Six pool tables filled the long room in two rows. Green lamps hung low over the felt. The bar ran along the left wall, and at the far end saloon doors separated the public room from whatever required more privacy.

Four men looked up. One held a cue beside the first table. Another sat on its edge counting money. At the saloon doors, two men stood with their jackets open and their hands conspicuously empty.

The man with the cue recognized me.

“Fuck,” he said.

“People keep greeting me like that,” I told him.

He laid the cue on the felt carefully. The man counting money swept his bills into his jacket, slid off the table, and went toward a side door without speaking. I let him go and continued between the rows.

At the far end, one of the guards raised a hand.

“Private event,” he said.

“I'm invited,” I told him.

“Not through here,” the guard replied.

I stopped beside the last table. “Then show me the red carpet.”

His hand went inside his jacket and came out with a pistol. I shoved the table sideways. Its legs scraped across the floor; the far edge struck his knees before he could settle his aim. He folded over the felt and fired into the ceiling. The lamp above him shattered.

The second guard reached into his jacket. A cue lay across the table beside me. I picked it up and drove its butt into his mouth. His head snapped back. As he dropped to one knee, I caught the wrist inside his jacket and pulled his gun free. I threw it behind the bar.

The first guard tried to crawl backward from the table. I kicked his pistol out of reach and stepped over his legs. Behind the saloon doors, someone shouted Hậu's name.

I pushed through.

The private room held another pool table, a leather couch along the right wall, and a television showing a silent horse race. Vinh stood at the far end of the table with a pistol aimed at Hậu's stomach. Hậu faced him, hands open at his sides.

Two men flanked Hậu. The one nearer the couch pressed a knife beneath his ribs. The other held a short shotgun across his body. On the couch behind them, Kenny Vale sat with his wrist in a dirty bandage.

Kenny saw me and closed his eyes. “Again?”

Vinh kept the pistol on Hậu. “Stop there.”

I stopped beside the near corner of the table. “You're hard to get on the phone.”

“You're easy to find in a crowd,” Vinh said.

He raised his pistol toward Hậu's chest.

“He didn't come alone,” Vinh told him.

“I told you,” Hậu said, “I wanted a better position.”

Vinh struck him across the face with the pistol. Hậu's head turned with the blow. He spat blood onto the floor, looked back, and smiled with red teeth.

“That's not it,” Hậu said.

The shotgun man glanced at Hậu's mouth. I took the table's near corner in my right hand and heaved upward.

The slate top rose between us. Balls rolled down the felt and spilled off the far edge around Vinh's feet. The shotgun fired into the exposed underside. Wood burst toward the floor beneath my hand.

Beside the couch, Hậu caught the knife man's wrist. He stepped across him, turned, and threw him over his hip. The man landed on the cushions hard enough to launch Kenny off the other end.

I drove the raised table forward. Its far edge struck the shotgun man in the chest and pinned him against the back wall. His weapon dropped. I let the table fall onto its legs again, then climbed across it as Vinh fired.

The shot passed beneath my right arm and tore a strip from the felt. I landed on his side, caught his gun wrist, and shoved the pistol down into the corner pocket. He pulled back. The gun snagged in the leather netting.

I struck his elbow with the heel of my hand. His arm bent the wrong way. He screamed and let go of the pistol.

Hậu had seized the knife man by both ankles. He swung him off the couch into the shotgun man, who'd just staggered free of the wall. They hit the floor together. Broken plaster sifted onto their jackets.

Kenny crawled toward the door. I put a foot in his path.

He looked up from the floor. “I just drive trucks.”

“Badly,” I told him.

I stepped past him to reach Vinh. With his uninjured hand, Vinh drew another knife and slashed at my left arm.

The blade cut through Shawna's bandage and into the skin beneath. Pain flashed so hot that I pulled back before I knew I'd moved. My hip struck the table edge. I caught my balance as he came at me again, driving the point low toward my stomach.

I turned sideways. The blade scraped across my clean shirt. I clamped his wrist beneath my right arm and hit him in the ear with my left fist.

Pain shot through my own arm. I hit him again anyway.

He sagged toward me. I hauled him down and drove his head into the table cushion, once, then twice. His knees failed. The knife fell, and I kicked it beneath the couch. He hung from my grip with blood running over his mouth.

“You sent men into my wife's shop,” I said.

Vinh spat blood at my shoes. “You walked into our business.”

“Now I'm staying,” I told him.

I let him fall.

At the wall, the other two men had tried to untangle themselves. Hậu took a cue from the rack and brought it down as the shotgun man's hand moved toward his weapon. The cue broke. Hậu stood over them holding the remaining length, and neither tried to get up again.

Kenny sat against the wall with both hands visible. He breathed rapidly through his nose and watched me take out my phone.

Bảo answered before the second ring.

“Is he alive?” he asked.

“He's complaining,” I said.

“Bring him,” Bảo told me.

“Have the keys,” I said, and hung up.

Vinh laughed from the floor, a wet sound that made him cough afterward.

“You think he gives things away?” he asked.

I crouched beside him. “He gave me you.”

His eyes moved past me to Hậu.

“You stupid fuck,” Vinh told him. “He'll use you the same.”

Hậu came over and knelt on Vinh's other side. Neither spoke for a moment. Then Hậu took Vinh's phone out of his pocket and placed it on the table above them.

“I quit,” Hậu said.

Vinh tried to lift his head. Hậu put one hand against it and lowered it gently to the floor.

“You can keep the dental,” Hậu told him.

We wrapped Vinh in the leather cover stripped from the couch and carried him outside. Saint looked from the bundle to my bleeding arm. He opened the sedan's rear door without making a joke.

Rafa brought the tow truck around. Kenny volunteered to remain available after Voss explained that walking home might become tiring very suddenly. He climbed into the truck between Voss and a box of chain. Saint drove us to Bảo's house, with Rafa following.

Bảo met us in his garage wearing a silk robe over his trousers. The sling was gone, but he held his left arm close to his body. Three men waited behind him. Baojun hovered in the doorway to the house with his phone in his hand.

We lowered Vinh into a folding chair and pulled the couch cover clear of his feet. Bảo studied his face for a long time.

“Fourteen years,” Bảo said.

Vinh stared at the concrete floor.

“You ate at my table,” Bảo told him.

“You charged me for the chair,” Vinh said.

Bảo slapped him with his good hand. Vinh's head snapped sideways. He laughed again, showing blood on his teeth.

I held out my hand. “The keys.”

Bảo turned toward me. “We're having a conversation.”

“You can have it after you pay,” I told him.

Baojun lowered his phone. “You don't talk to my father like that.”

Hậu looked at him. Baojun started to raise the phone again, paused, and put it in his pocket.

Bảo walked to a cabinet. He opened a drawer with more force than it needed, took out a ring of keys, and threw it to me. I caught them in my right hand.

“Documents?” I asked.

He stared at me. “You want a ribbon too?”

“Only if it's tied around the building,” I said.

Phương appeared behind her son in the doorway.

“They're on the kitchen counter,” she told Bảo. “I told you he would ask.”

Bảo's jaw tightened. Hậu followed Phương into the house while I stayed beside Vinh. Behind the chair, the three men moved closer. One held a length of electrical cord. Another removed his jacket and laid it over the cabinet.

I looked at the cord, then at Bảo. “Ask what you need to ask.”

“This isn't your concern,” Bảo said.

“He knows Mercer,” I replied. “That part is.”

Bảo held my gaze before turning back to the chair.

“What did Sunday offer you?” he asked.

Vinh wiped his mouth against his shoulder. “Your rooms.”

“My rooms,” Bảo repeated.

Vinh looked up. “And a piece of the ferry.”

Bảo went still at the last word. I looked from him to Vinh, who followed my glance. A smile pulled at Vinh's damaged mouth.

“There it is,” Vinh said. “The boy doesn't know about the water.”

Bảo hit him again. Vinh fell sideways out of the chair. Bảo moved after him, his good hand rising, and I caught his wrist.

The garage went quiet. The man with the electrical cord stopped winding it around his palm.

Bảo looked at my hand on his arm. “Careful.”

I let go slowly. “He's answered one question.”

Hậu returned with a folder. Behind him, Phương took one look at Vinh on the floor and drew Baojun into the house by his sleeve. I heard the door close as I opened the papers.

Trinh's name appeared beside mine on the operating agreement. The building transfer was ready for signatures. I turned each page, checking what Bảo had prepared against what I'd asked him for beside the pool. He'd come far enough toward his promise. I still wanted to see the last part happen.

“Tomorrow night,” Bảo said. “At Honest Work. We finish it in front of the Circle.”

I closed the folder. “I'll be there.”

Hậu came with me toward the driveway. From the garage floor, Vinh called after us.

“He'll make you kneel,” Vinh said.

I turned. Bảo stood over him, the three men gathered behind his shoulder.

“He can buy a ticket,” I told Vinh.

Saint opened the car door. Hậu went around to the other side, and I sat between them in the back because neither intended to let me pretend my arm was fine. Rafa had parked the tow truck beside the garage, leaving Voss with its keys to keep Kenny available. Now he leaned through the sedan's driver's window and handed me a clean towel.

“For the seat,” Rafa said.

I pressed it against my arm. “My dad's car.”

“Exactly,” Rafa told me. “I know who frightens me.”

He opened the driver's door and got behind the wheel.
