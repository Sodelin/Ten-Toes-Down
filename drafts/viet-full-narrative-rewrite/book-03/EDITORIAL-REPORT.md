## Scope and result

All 29 chapters of *The River’s Weight* were read in full before drafting. The source is the Book III file at repository commit `4f38948aa5fa97f294adfa74a0b7055c399b2826`. Any truncated retrieval was explicitly reread. The preceding book's ending and the following book's opening were also consulted. The final narrative has **41,428 words across 29 chapters**, compared with **50,700 source chapter words**. The comparison includes chapter headings and excludes the book heading and editorial front matter. See `chapter-word-counts.csv` for every chapter. The first pass compressed too many reactions and exchanges. The subsequent passes restored 8,526 words of actual scene work; they did not try to manufacture parity with the source. The remaining reduction comes primarily from combining repeated explanations and making exchanges carry more than one beat. Nolan may prefer more of the original's breathing room in particular scenes.

## Narrative and dialogue decisions

- Close third-person narration stays around Tín. His observations, guesses, and memories are distinguished from facts known privately by others. The 1987 furniture memory in Chapter 13 and the brief recalled departure in Chapter 28 have explicit returns to the current scene.
- Every spoken paragraph identifies a speaker by name or by an unambiguous role such as the doctor, guard, or crane operator. Conversational attribution does not depend on a second audiobook voice. The final scan covered 1,236 quoted paragraphs; manual corrections included pronoun-only tags and collective speech.
- Comedy follows physical and social actions: rental shoes and a boat wager, stolen truck parts becoming evidence, useful work assigned to armed men, a wedding continuing on a repair bench, a rescued sandwich, reluctant tug lessons, and the crown becoming a spoon holder. The broad influence is permission for affection, absurdity, danger, and sincerity to share a scene. No external franchise characters or scenes were imported.
- Injury and recovery remain cumulative. Tín's arm is grazed, reopened and burned before later wounds; Bình's shoulder stays impaired; Mateo and Boots receive treatment; the deaths do not become reversible punchlines. The aftermath includes fatigue, other people's fear, medical care, and three weeks of recovery.
- The new coalition remains compromised. Duke profited by not asking what passed beneath his ballroom. Sable kept Rook's accounts and withheld the fact that she recovered her wages from the chest. Bishop takes Rook's advance while changing sides. Tín takes cash for repairs before any formal settlement. Iris's late cooperation does not erase her earlier participation. Rook's two-million-dollar first payment does not prevent arrest or close every claim.

## Continuity decisions

The story remains summer **1992**, Tín's seventieth birthday, five years after the family's arrival in Eastbridge. Tín and Bích have been married forty-five years. Hòa and Hương are Mai's parents; Boots and Loretta are Marcus's parents. Rosa is Mateo's sister. The older family story is distinct from Mai's adult wedding in Book II.

The two-week calendar preserves the original announced “Saturday next week”: birthday Monday; truck/ring Wednesday; printer Thursday; wedding Saturday; hospital Sunday; cannery Monday; market Tuesday; Bishop Wednesday; date Thursday; kidnapping through crane rescue Friday; hospital/festival Saturday. The school visit is Monday afterward; the final walk follows three weeks of recovery. These anchors repair compressed source references without reordering events.

**Leon distinction:** Book I's Sunday is Leon Mercer (source Book I, introduction); Book II's friend is Leon Price (source Book II, opening). Book III contains no Leon appearance. Sunday's claim that he was four at Tín's 1992 river party does not create an age contradiction with the adult Leon Price in Book II. No earlier party was invented to reconcile it.

Hòa's feats remain observed rather than measured or ranked. Tín sees the trunk damage, the bollard, and the safe; he does not gain access to Hòa's private thoughts or define a hierarchy of powers.
