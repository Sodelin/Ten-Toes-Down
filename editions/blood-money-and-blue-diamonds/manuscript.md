## 1. First Night in Vegas

The night I met Shawna, I had cocaine on my shirt and another man's blood inside my watch. I was twenty-four years old, my nigga, and I hadn't owned a car since the last one caught fire outside my ex-boyfriend's apartment. Before sunrise I would steal three million dollars, take a woman every killer in Vegas wanted back, and catch a bullet that would stay in me for the rest of my life.

But that shit came later, nigga.

First I had to get jumped in.

Big T took me behind the club while the bass was still shaking the walls. There were twelve niggas waiting by the cars. Blue rags. Gold chains. Guns under their shirts. I knew some of them from the block, but nobody was smiling now. This was family business, my nigga, and I was the last motherfucker without a seat at the table.

T put his hand on my shoulder.

"Once you in, you in, nigga. You understand me?"

"I understand."

"This ain't some shit you try for a summer, nigga."

"I said I understand, nigga."

He hit me so hard I went against the side of a Cadillac.

Then every nigga came at once.

I caught a fist in the mouth and an elbow behind my ear. Somebody kicked my leg out. I went down on the hot concrete, got up, went down again. Blood ran into my eye. I couldn't see which nigga I was swinging at. I just kept swinging. I had been waiting my whole life for somebody to tell me I belonged somewhere, and if this was what it cost, I was paying it, nigga. Take the whole fucking payment.

Tank caught me under the ribs. Big nigga, twenty-eight, arms hanging out his shirt like he'd been born carrying furniture. I grabbed his shirt and drove him backward into a car. The alarm started screaming. He was twice my size, but that night there wasn't a motherfucker alive who was going to make me stay down.

"Get this nigga off me!" Tank shouted.

Nobody moved. I heard Big T laughing behind me. That laugh put another man's strength inside my hands. Nigga, I could've carried Tank into the sky.

I hit him until T pulled me off.

"Enough, nigga. He earned it."

I stood there with my fists up. My hands were shaking so hard I couldn't open them.

T looked at the blood on my face. Then he took a gold chain from his pocket and put it over my head.

A little cross hung against my chest.

"My brother wore that," he said. "Real nigga."

I looked up at him.

"Where he at?"

"Dead."

Nobody said anything.

"Don't make me bury you too, my nigga."

I closed my hand around the cross. I would've walked through fire for that man right then. I would've put a gun in the devil's mouth if T told me the devil owed us money. Nigga, I would've collected interest.

"Never," I told him.

He kissed my forehead like a father and pointed toward the club.

"Come get your money, nigga."

Inside, everything was blue light and smoke. The back room was packed with niggas counting stacks of cash. Bricks of cocaine covered a glass table. Bottles of Hennessy stood between them. Three women in diamonds were drinking with a man who had two pistols tucked into the same belt. One of them lifted her glass when she saw my face.

"Damn, baby. You survive a war?"

"Yeah," I said. "And I won."

They cheered. Somebody pushed a bottle into my hand. Somebody else handed me a blunt already burning. I drank, smoked, wiped the blood off my lip, and sat down beside a pile of money bigger than anything I had ever seen in my life.

"This the nigga beat Tank?" one of the women asked.

"That's the nigga," T said.

She pushed a folded bill into my shirt pocket. A thousand dollars. I'd been back inside for ninety seconds and a woman was paying me to be handsome with a broken face. That's the kind of night it was, my nigga.

Tank came in carrying a bag of ice against his jaw. He took the bottle out my hand, drank, and gave it back.

"You fight dirty, nigga."

"You fight unconscious."

The room went crazy. Tank stared at me long enough I thought we was going again. Then that big nigga started laughing too, blood on his teeth, and pulled a chair up beside mine. We drank from the same bottle. I would've trusted him to hold my heart while I fought with both hands.

T threw me the keys to a black Mercedes.

"That's yours, nigga."

I looked at the keys.

"You serious, my nigga?"

"You handling the west side for me now."

An hour earlier I hadn't even been in the crew. Now the west side was mine. That's how fast life moved when you were ready for it, nigga. Some men spent their whole lives waiting on a chance. I took mine with blood still coming out of my mouth.

T opened a photograph on his phone.

A woman stood beside a swimming pool at night. Black dress. Diamond earrings. Dark hair over one shoulder. She wasn't smiling. Behind her, the whole Strip was lit up, but I was looking at her.

"Shawna," T said. "Twenty-six. Works for Red."

"Doing what, nigga?"

"Whatever Red wants."

Tank watched me from across the table.

T kept talking.

"She's at the Saint tonight. Penthouse. She's bringing a case down at eleven. You take her and the money to my house in Henderson. That's all you gotta do."

"Why she leaving him?"

"Because she finally got smart."

I looked at the picture again.

"Red know, nigga?"

"If he knew, we wouldn't be having this conversation, nigga."

T gripped the back of my neck and pulled me close enough that nobody else could hear.

"Don't fall in love with her, nigga."

I laughed.

"I'm serious, nigga. Look at me."

"I don't fall in love, my nigga."

He let go of me.

I believed that shit when I said it. Nigga, remember that. I meant it with my entire chest.

***

The Saint had a gold fountain outside and bulletproof glass on the top floor. Red owned the penthouse, the nightclub underneath it, and enough people in the building that nobody asked questions when his men dragged somebody into a service elevator. Nigga had built himself a palace where the carpets could swallow a scream.

I parked the Mercedes right in front.

The valet looked at the blood drying on my collar.

"You need somebody to call a doctor?"

"You need to make sure nothing happens to my car, nigga."

I gave him a hundred and went inside.

Upstairs, the penthouse was going crazy. Music pounding. Cocaine all over the tables. Niggas gambling with money that was still wrapped from the bank. Hookers in dresses that cost more than the cars outside, laughing with men who never gave their real names. Everybody was rich. Everybody was armed. Every nigga thought the night belonged to him.

A man in a white fur coat was doing coke off a trophy. It was September in Vegas. I didn't know what the trophy was for, but that nigga had brought it with him and was guarding it like the whole room wanted his championship.

I knew a woman by the bar, Mercedes, thirty-one, who had once left me naked in a hotel room and taken my shoes because I'd called her by her sister's name. She looked down at my new keys.

"You finally got a Mercedes you can afford, baby?"

"Come see it."

"Nigga, you still owe me flowers."

I gave her five hundred out the money in my pocket. "Buy the whole fucking garden."

She kissed my cheek. "You bleeding on the good side of your face."

"Both sides good, baby."

She laughed, and I left her with it. I was shining, my nigga. I had a dead man's gold against my chest, a living man's keys in my hand, and no idea how much that shit could cost.

I had been with men and women all over that city, my nigga. Big men who put their guns down gentle when they came into my room. Women who left lipstick on the champagne glasses and somebody else's diamonds on my pillow. I thought I knew every kind of trouble a beautiful person could bring into a room.

Then I saw Shawna, nigga.

She stood by the window with a cigarette in one hand and a silver case beside her ankle. She wore the same black dress from the picture. There was a little scar above her eyebrow. Somebody was talking to her, but she was looking straight at me.

I walked over. My mouth hurt, my ribs hurt, and every other woman I'd ever wanted fell clean out of my fucking head.

"T sent me."

"I know."

"How?"

She looked at the cross on my chest.

Her cigarette stopped halfway to her mouth.

"Where'd you get that?"

"T."

"Tonight?"

"Yeah."

She reached out and turned the cross over.

On the back were three little letters I hadn't noticed.

D. B. M.

Her fingers tightened around it.

"You know what those mean?"

"No."

She let it fall against my shirt.

"Drive before you find out."

I took the case. She took my hand. We walked through the party together while men who had been smiling a second earlier started reaching for their phones.

At the elevator, one of Red's guys stepped in front of us.

"Where she going, nigga?"

"With me, nigga."

"I wasn't asking you, nigga."

I hit him in the throat. He folded against the wall, and I shoved him down before he could get his hand inside his jacket. Shawna pressed the elevator button. The doors opened. Nigga was still looking at me like he couldn't believe I'd touched him. I stepped over his shoes.

Another man came around the corner.

She drew a little silver pistol from her purse and pointed it at his chest.

"You get one chance," she said.

He stopped.

The doors shut between us.

She didn't put the gun away until we reached the lobby.

I looked at her.

"You always leave parties like this?"

"Only the ones I survive."

I was already in love with her, my nigga. I just didn't know how bad it was going to get.

We got out past the last casinos before she told me to pull over.

"T's house is farther out," I said.

"I know where his fucking house is. Pull over."

Money filled it from one end to the other.

"Three million," she said.

I stared. Three million fucking dollars, nigga. That afternoon I'd been eating a gas-station sandwich in a borrowed car. Now this woman was opening a fortune in my Mercedes.

"T said—"

"T says a lot."

She took an envelope from beneath the cash. Inside was a photograph, bent across the middle.

Shawna stood in front of the same chapel, three years younger, wearing a white dress. A man stood beside her with his arms around her waist.

The cross on his neck was the one T had put on mine.

"Dante," she said. "His brother. My husband."

I looked from the photograph to her face. My nigga, I'd been carrying her dead husband against my chest and talking like the whole world started when I walked in.

She took the cigarette from behind her ear, tried to light it, and couldn't get the lighter to work.

I did it for her.

She watched the flame for a second before she leaned into it.

"I was twenty-three when I came out here. Thought I was going to sing. I had a suitcase, eight hundred dollars, and a number some girl gave me in Phoenix."

She looked out at the chapel.

"The number was Valentino's."

I knew the name. Everybody did. Valentino ran women through half the hotels on the Strip. He wore white suits and had a diamond in one of his teeth. Niggas laughed around him like he was funny. Women watched his hands.

"He bought me clothes. Got me a room. Told me he had people in music. By the time I knew what the fuck was happening, everything I had belonged to him."

She blew smoke against the windshield.

"Dante came into the club on my birthday. Everybody else wanted something. He asked what kind of cake I liked."

Her mouth moved like she was going to smile.

She didn't.

"He came back with it the next night. Carrot cake. Whole fucking cake. Put it on the table between the bottles. Valentino laughed at him. Dante knocked out that diamond tooth."

"My nigga," I said before I could stop myself.

"That's what I said." She looked at the photograph. "That's exactly what I fucking said."

She wiped one eye with her thumb, angry that she had to.

"We got married here six weeks later. T stood beside him. Red paid for the room. They all smiled in the pictures."

I looked at the gold cross in my hand.

"What happened?"

"Dante took money out of a deal. Three million. Told me we were going to leave. Said he had a brother, so he didn't have to be scared."

She turned the photograph over.

There was writing on the back.

*Don't bury me.*

"That's what the letters mean," she said. "He used to say it whenever I talked about getting old together. Don't bury me, baby. You go first. I can't do that without you."

She held the photograph against her chest.

"They left him in the desert. Valentino brought me his watch. Told me Dante died begging for somebody to take care of me."

I wanted to kill a man I had never even met. Nigga was already dead, and I wanted another turn.

"What did you do?"

"Waited."

Her voice got quieter.

"For eight months, I waited. Valentino thought I came back because I had nowhere else to go. He thought I loved him by the end."

She looked straight at me.

"Last Tuesday, I shot him in his car. He was talking when I did it. He kept talking after the first shot, so I shot him again. Then I took Dante's watch out of his glove compartment."

She showed me the watch on her wrist.

It was too big for her.

"Red took me in. Said he'd protect me. Tonight I took the three million back."

"And T?"

"T says he's going to get me out of Vegas."

I thought about the way he'd told me not to love her. Nigga knew what she was carrying besides the case.

I thought about the cross.

"You believe him?" I asked.

"I believed his brother."

She put the photograph inside her dress and closed the case.

Then she leaned across the seat and kissed me.

I forgot about the money. I forgot whose car I was driving. All I knew was her hand against my face and the taste of smoke on her mouth. When she pulled back, she was still holding on to me. My nigga, I could feel her trying to decide if I was another room she couldn't leave.

"Don't take me to that house," she said.

"Then I won't."

Just like that, nigga. My first night in the family, and I was ready to go to war with every fucking one of them.

I took her to a motel outside town and paid for the whole top floor.

The clerk looked at the stack of money in my hand.

"All six rooms?"

"All six, nigga. Nobody comes up."

Upstairs, Shawna put the case on the bed and her gun on the nightstand. I locked the door. We could hear trucks on the highway and somebody arguing with a woman in the parking lot below.

"Jesus, Aiden."

"I'm good."

"You're bleeding."

"I've been bleeding all night."

She got a wet towel from the bathroom and cleaned my face. Every time she touched the cut on my mouth, she said she was sorry. Every time, I told her not to be.

"I'm here, baby."

"Tonight. I mean tonight."

"I'm right fucking here."

She pulled me down to kiss her. I could taste salt at the edge of her mouth. All that blood and money downstairs, all those niggas looking for us, and she wanted my hands on her. I let the shirt fall off my shoulders. She shut the lamp off herself.

"You."

"You don't know me."

"Then tell me more."

She told me about the songs she'd wanted to sing. About her mother in Phoenix, who thought she was working in a restaurant. About a house Dante wanted near the water, even though neither of them knew how to swim.

I told her we'd buy it.

I told her I'd take her anywhere she wanted.

I had known her less than three hours, my nigga. I meant every fucking word. Give me a country and I would've put her name on it. Give me a year and I'd have fought every man in it to keep her.

Then my phone lit up.

T.

I let it ring.

A picture came through.

My Mercedes outside the motel.

Then a message.

*Come down, son.*

## 2. A King with a Hole in Him

Nigga, the doctor asked Shawna who she was to me.

"His wife," she said.

Nigga, I tried to live harder when I heard that. I had been slipping in and out the whole ride, seeing my mother's kitchen and the motel swimming pool, hearing Big T call me son like he still had the right. But I heard wife clear as a church bell.

"Name?" the doctor asked.

"Shawna."

"His name."

"Aiden. His name is Aiden."

"Let go of him, Shawna."

She wouldn't. They were trying to move me onto another bed and she had both hands caught in my shirt. Her black dress had gone brown where my blood dried. One of her earrings was missing. She was barefoot in a hospital that smelled like flowers and cost more every minute than my old apartment cost every month.

"Baby," I said.

She looked down.

"Don't let them cut my chain."

She took it off me herself. Kissed the cross. Put it around her neck.

Then everything went dark.

***

When I woke up, a woman in a white coat was looking at me like she knew exactly how much bullshit I was capable of.

"Dr. Marisol Vega," she said. "You're alive."

"Shawna?"

"Sleeping."

"Where?"

She pointed.

Shawna was in a chair by the window, curled around my bloody shirt. Somebody had put hospital socks on her. She had my cross around her neck and Dante's watch hanging loose on her wrist.

"Don't wake her," I said.

"She'll wake herself. She has been doing it every eleven minutes."

I tried to sit up, my nigga. Pain hit me so hard I forgot what I was trying to do.

"Fuck."

"Yes," Vega said. "That. You will be doing quite a lot of that if you continue."

I stayed down.

"Where's the bullet?"

"Still inside you. Removing it would add risks you do not need. We treated the damage. You need time."

I looked at the ceiling. Big T was dead and the nigga had still managed to move in with me.

"How much time?"

"More than you want."

"I got niggas waiting."

"They can keep waiting."

She checked what she needed to check. I couldn't lift my head without the room going sideways. There was nothing kingly about it. A woman I barely knew had to help me drink water. Nigga, I could own the whole city and still drown trying to take a fucking sip.

I dropped the cup, my nigga, and Shawna woke.

She came out of the chair like somebody had called her name in a burning house.

"Aiden?"

"Right here."

She touched my face, my shoulders, the hair above my ear. She wouldn't look at the bandages.

"You scared me," she said.

"I'm sorry, baby."

"Don't be sorry. Don't do it."

She kissed my forehead. Then she slapped my arm. Then she kissed me again, crying so hard I felt the tears on my mouth.

Vega went out and closed the door.

"You told them you're my wife," I said.

Shawna wiped her face with the back of her hand.

"They weren't going to let me in."

"You could've said sister."

"I wanted them to let me in, nigga. I didn't want to make it weird when I kissed you."

I laughed. That hurt too. Everything I wanted to do with her cost me something in my side.

"Say it again, nigga," I told her.

"What?"

"What you told the doctor."

She sat on the edge of the bed.

"Nigga, you trying to die married?"

"Trying to live that way."

Her hand stopped on my cheek.

For a second she looked scared of me. Not the gun, not the blood. Your nigga lying there asking for a whole fucking life when we'd barely made it through a night.

Then she put her head beside mine.

"Let me get you home first."

***

"These for her," he said.

"Put them down, nigga," I said.

He did.

"Niggas downstairs waiting."

"Whose niggas?"

"Were T's."

"And now?"

He looked at Shawna before answering.

"That's what they wanna know."

She stood up.

"I'm not leaving."

"Didn't ask you to," Tank said.

"You looked."

"I got eyes."

I held out my hand. Shawna took it, but she kept looking at him.

"Where's T, nigga?" I asked.

"Funeral people got him."

"His other niggas?"

"One alive. One ain't."

Shawna lowered her eyes. She knew which one she had shot. I squeezed her fingers. Nigga, if she needed me to lie awake with her about it, I would. If she needed me to say she'd done right, I would say that too.

"The money?" she asked.

Tank stared at the end of my bed.

"Pool was empty when I got back."

"Three million doesn't walk," she said.

"Plenty people got legs."

She let go of my hand.

"Look at me when you say it."

Tank looked at her.

"Money wasn't there."

I believed him then, my nigga. I had plenty of stupid left in me.

"Leave it," I said. "Nigga, she threw it away to save me. It saved me. That's money spent."

"I woke up."

He looked at the machines beside me, then back at my face.

"Yeah," he said. "You did."

"I drove your car."

"That ain't what I asked."

Shawna stood beside the window. The sunlight caught the watch on her wrist, and Tank looked at it long enough that I noticed.

"I ain't putting another man in the desert," he said.

Then he went to get the men downstairs.

***

Dr. Vega found nine men in my room, two in the hall, and a duffel bag on the visitor's chair.

"Absolutely not," she said.

Everybody turned. These niggas had gold teeth, prison scars, guns under their jackets, and the kind of silence they wore to make other people nervous. Vega pointed at the hallway.

"Two visitors. The rest of you leave."

Nobody moved.

"You heard her," I said.

They moved.

"Money coming in Friday," he said. "People need paying today."

"What's in the bag?"

"Yesterday."

"Unless you got some fucking magic beans, nigga."

He nodded and made a mark on the paper.

"Red wants a meeting."

"Nigga, Red can call."

"He wants it face to face."

"Nigga, look at mine. I'm busy."

"You heard him, nigga."

"You want me counting it?"

"I want you watching the door, nigga."

"Every fucking nigga."

That was the first night he guarded me.

Shawna's friend arrived carrying three bags, a red velvet coat, and enough rage to get past every man in the hallway.

"Where the fuck is she?"

"Rosa," Shawna said.

They hit each other halfway across the room and held on. "Later," she said.

"Whenever," Rosa told her.

Then Shawna fell asleep against me and whispered Dante's name.

Nigga, I heard it.

I lay still until morning.

Five days passed before Vega let your nigga leave, and she made it clear that leaving did not mean I was healed. I could walk slowly. I could sit. I could get tired crossing a room and pretend I had stopped to look at something expensive.

"No heroics," she said.

Back. Like I'd had a kingdom before I had a woman. Like these niggas hadn't learned my name while I was bleeding through somebody else's sheets.

I looked down at her.

"Home, baby. We just gotta get one."

## 3. The Palace of the Woman I Just Met

Nigga, I bought Shawna a house before I bought myself another shirt.

The real estate woman met us at Lake Las Vegas wearing heels that kept sinking into the grass. She looked at Tank, then the other cars, then the bandage showing through my open collar. Whatever she thought, nigga, the house was still for sale.

"Five bedrooms," she said. "Private dock. Heated pool. The piano can be negotiated separately."

"Leave it," I said.

Shawna had stopped in the doorway.

Beyond the living room, glass doors opened onto blue water. There were white curtains, high ceilings, a staircase that curved around a chandelier big enough to bury a man under. A black piano sat by the window. It looked like somebody had built a room around waiting for her.

"Aiden."

I watched her walk toward it.

"You like it?"

"Baby, you can't just—"

"Do you like it?"

She touched the piano with two fingers.

"Yeah."

"Then don't tell me what I can't do, nigga."

She turned around smiling, and nigga, I would've bought the water if she'd asked who owned it.

"We're doing this backward," she said.

"Nigga, then turn around."

"You know what I mean, nigga."

"Nigga, I know you said water. That's water."

"Dante said water."

I looked through the window.

I could see Tank standing at the end of the dock with his hands in his pockets. He looked like the nigga was guarding the whole lake from trespassing.

"You said it to me," I told her.

She touched my knee.

"I know."

"I live where she lives."

Shawna turned her face away. When she turned back, she kissed me so hard I had to hold the edge of the piano bench.

Nigga, that was my signature.

***

Rosa came over that evening and stood in the foyer without taking off her sunglasses. My nigga, she looked at that chandelier like it owed her money.

"Nigga, this bitch gets shot at one time and comes home with a staircase."

"You sure about all this?" he asked.

"House?"

"Woman."

"Watch it, nigga."

"Say it, nigga."

"He's dead."

"Nigga, that's what I'm telling you."

"No."

"Some nigga gotta tell you something."

***

The club opened for me the following night.

I wore a white suit because I wanted every nigga there to know I wasn't expecting to bleed. Shawna wore blue silk with her whole back out and earrings so long they touched her shoulders. Rosa had done her hair. I had bought the earrings. Dante's watch was still on her wrist.

Nigga, I stood under it long enough to make the cars behind us honk.

Shawna hooked her arm through mine.

"They waiting on you."

"Let them."

Inside, the whole west side had dressed like it was getting married to money. Women in diamonds. Men in velvet. Champagne sweating in silver tubs. Cocaine on mirrored tables in the private rooms. Hookers I knew from bad months wearing shoes that cost more than those months. Niggas who owed everybody suddenly buying bottles with both hands.

One of the dancers came down from a platform and kissed my cheek.

"You made it, baby."

"We made it, nigga," I told her.

I put a stack of cash in her hand. She held it against her chest and went back up laughing.

Shawna saw, my nigga. She moved closer.

At the back table, a pretty man in a silver shirt stood up when he saw me. I'd spent a reckless weekend with him the year before, broke and drunk and swearing on his balcony that one day I'd own the view.

"Look at you, nigga," he said.

"You too."

He kissed the corner of my mouth before I could turn.

Shawna's arm went stiff through mine.

"Congratulations," he said. "Always knew you had something."

"Nigga, he still has it," Shawna said.

He looked at her and smiled.

"Keep it, girl. Cost me a weekend's sleep."

We moved on. Shawna waited until we reached our table.

"How many niggas in here have you fucked?"

I looked across the room.

"Don't look around, nigga. Answer me."

"You want me counting niggas I only kissed?"

She hit my good shoulder with her purse.

"Nigga, I want you sitting your ass down."

I sat. Nigga, I had survived T, but I wasn't trying to survive her in those earrings.

My nigga, she sat sideways on my lap until my face told on me, then moved into the chair beside mine. Her hand stayed on my thigh. Every time some nigga approached the table, she pulled me closer by the collar before letting go.

I loved that shit. I had women flirting with me, men remembering me, the whole room learning which side of her I belonged on. Nigga, let them look. I'd spent my whole life feeling like people could leave me wherever they found me.

Then the music stopped, my nigga.

Rosa stood onstage in the red velvet coat. Under it she wore gold from her throat to the floor.

"Somebody owes me a song," she said.

Shawna shook her head.

"No."

"I ain't said your name yet, bitch."

"Rosa."

The room started clapping. Rosa held out her hand.

Shawna looked at me.

"You set this up?"

"No, baby."

I meant it. My nigga, I would've bought her an orchestra and scared her right out the back door. Rosa just stood there with a microphone and waited for her friend.

Shawna got up.

The first few steps looked harder than the walk I'd made out of the hospital. She kept one hand against her stomach. When she reached the stage, Rosa kissed her cheek and gave her the microphone.

"You know the one," she said.

Shawna closed her eyes.

The piano started.

Nigga, I had heard people say a voice could break your heart. I thought it meant somebody sang pretty while you remembered somebody ugly. Then Shawna opened her mouth and I stopped knowing where I was.

She sang about leaving a porch light burning. About a car going past the house. About getting dressed for somebody who wasn't coming home. Her voice shook on the first line. By the second verse, men stopped pouring drinks. A woman by the bar put her face in her hands.

I watched that watch slide down Shawna's wrist when she lifted the microphone.

"Dante's song," somebody behind me whispered.

Nigga, I turned so fast my side lit up.

The nigga was staring at the stage. He hadn't said it to hurt me. That almost made it worse.

All these niggas were listening to my woman sing for her husband while my name glowed outside.

I drank what was in front of me. Then I remembered what Vega had said and put the glass down, furious with myself for needing a fucking doctor in my head while another man had a song.

I couldn't tell which of us she saw.

When she finished, the whole club got up. Niggas banged on tables. Rosa cried openly. I stood too, slow and angry, clapping until my hands hurt.

Shawna came down smiling like a girl who had found something under the bed she thought she'd lost forever.

"Did you like it?"

I kissed her before I answered.

Long. In front of everybody.

"Beautiful," I said.

She touched my face.

"Why you looking at me like that?"

"Like what?"

Her smile faded.

"Never mind."

Later, a man from one of the cash tables grabbed Rosa by the coat when she told him she was done working.

"I paid," he said.

Rosa pulled away.

"You paid for last time, nigga."

He grabbed her again.

Nigga, I was standing before Tank got to me.

"Aiden," Shawna said.

The nigga looked over his shoulder, still holding Rosa. That was his mistake. Not knowing me could've been fixed. Holding on after he saw somebody coming was the part that put him on the floor.

I hit him once. Hard enough that I felt something pull under my bandages. Tank caught my elbow before I could hit him again.

"Take your fucking hands off her," I said.

"You hear me, nigga?"

He nodded from the carpet.

Rosa pulled her coat straight. Her hands shook. She hated that we could see them.

"Get him out," I told Tank.

"You sit down, nigga."

"Get that nigga out first."

The room had gone still around us. All these niggas had wanted a new boss to buy champagne. Now they had one bleeding into a white suit because somebody hadn't let go of Shawna's friend.

Tank handed the man to two others and walked me into the back office. Shawna followed. So did Rosa.

"You dumb motherfucker," Shawna said.

She unbuttoned my jacket. A little red had come through the shirt.

"I'm okay."

"Nigga, you don't know another fucking sentence?"

Rosa stood by the door with both hands inside her coat.

"I didn't need—"

"I know," I told her. "I wanted."

She pressed her lips together. Then she came over, bent down, and kissed my head again.

"Stupid nigga," she whispered.

But she stayed beside me until Shawna got a clean shirt.

***

At home, the lake was black under the windows.

Shawna took off her earrings and laid them on the bathroom counter. I watched her unclasp the watch, my nigga.

"Let me buy you one that fits," I said.

She stopped.

"A watch?"

"Yeah."

"I have one."

"It hangs off you."

"I like it."

"I can see that."

She turned around. The silence got ugly fast.

"Say it, Aiden."

I stood in the doorway, tired enough that my legs shook.

"That song about him?"

"Yes."

She hadn't even tried to soften it. Nigga, I would've taken one little lie right then. Just something I could lay down beside.

"You look at me when you sing to him?"

"I looked at you because you were there."

"I'm always there. You wearing his watch, my earrings, got his chain on my fucking neck—"

"You want it off?"

She reached toward the cross.

I caught her wrist.

We stared at each other.

Then I let go.

"Don't," I said.

She rubbed where my fingers had been. I hadn't hurt her. I could still see that I'd done something I didn't want between us.

"Nigga, what do you want from me?" she asked.

I looked down at the marble. I'd bought the whole floor and couldn't find a place to stand.

"I want one thing that's only mine."

Her face changed.

She came close and put her palm over my heartbeat, clear of the wound.

"This is you," she said. "I know who this is."

"Then why'd you say his name in the hospital?"

She closed her eyes.

I wished I'd kept it. Held it in until it killed me by itself. But it was out now, standing between us in our new fucking bathroom.

Nigga, I didn't know what to do with that.

She took my hand and led me to bed. We didn't settle it. She didn't put the watch away. I didn't take off the cross. She kissed me until I stopped asking questions, and when we lay down, she kept her head on my good side.

I woke once before dawn.

She was still there.

For that morning, that was enough.

## 4. Flowers for the Man I Killed

Nigga, Big T's coffin was blue.

Not dark blue. Not respectful blue. That deep candy blue you put on a car when you want other niggas to pull up beside you and hate their lives. Somebody had ordered gold handles. His picture stood at the front of the chapel, six feet tall, smiling over a room full of people who'd been scared to smile wrong around him.

Your nigga paid for all of it.

The flowers. The cars. The musicians. The lunch afterward that half the guests would be too nervous to eat. T had called me son, then put a bullet in me. I couldn't make those things fit, so I bought him the biggest funeral that place could hold.

Tank came downstairs at the house wearing black.

"Nigga, you ain't gotta go," he said.

"Nigga, I killed him."

"That's why you ain't gotta go, nigga."

I adjusted my cuff in the mirror. My hands weren't steady yet. The suit hid the bandages. The medicine made everything feel a half second farther away.

"He had nobody else?" I asked.

"Plenty people."

"People that loved him."

Tank looked toward the window.

I left the cuff alone.

"You did."

He kept looking out.

"Nigga, you can say it."

"He gave me work when I came out."

"Nigga gave me a car."

"Ain't the same, nigga."

"I know."

For a minute we just stood there, the two men who'd left T by a swimming pool, trying to get dressed for what came after.

Shawna came downstairs in white.

Tank turned.

"Nigga, oh, hell no."

She stopped on the last stair.

White dress. White coat. White gloves in one hand. Her dark hair pinned up. Dante's watch shining like she'd polished it with her rage.

"What?" she said.

"Nigga's funeral, Shawna."

"I know whose funeral it is."

"Nigga, you can't wear that."

She looked at me.

I could've asked her to change. Could've told her this was already hard enough without her walking in looking like she'd come to get married on his grave.

But nigga, she looked beautiful.

"Get the car, nigga," I told Tank.

***

My nigga, nobody knew what to do when we walked in.

Some niggas stood. Some stayed seated so hard it looked like work. A woman near the front started crying louder when she saw me. I didn't know if that was grief or hate. I kept going.

Shawna's hand was in mine.

Tank walked behind us.

Every nigga in that chapel knew the story had changed. T had sent me to collect a woman and some money. A week later he was in a blue box, I had his men at my back, and the woman was holding my hand in front of his face.

I didn't feel proud until somebody whispered that I shouldn't have come.

Then I felt proud enough to scare myself.

"Say it loud, nigga," I said without turning.

Nobody did.

We sat in the second row. Shawna kept her knees together and looked straight ahead. I could feel her pulse through her fingers.

The preacher talked about a generous man.

That part was true. T had been generous. He'd put money in people's hands, found rooms for niggas who had nowhere to sleep, paid somebody's mother's bill without letting anybody say thank you twice. I knew men who'd lived on that kindness for years.

The preacher talked about a man who loved his family.

Shawna let go of my hand.

I looked at her.

Her eyes had gone flat. She was staring at the picture, and her white glove was balled so tight in her fist I could see a seam giving way.

I put my hand on her knee. She didn't move.

Then Tank got up.

He hadn't told me he planned to speak. He walked to the front carrying a folded paper, opened it, looked at it, and put it back in his pocket.

"T knew my mother," he said.

His voice sounded too big for the microphone.

"Knew she worried about me. Every time I came by, nigga made me call her first. Didn't matter what we was doing. Call your mother, Tank. Tell her you ate."

A woman near the aisle laughed through her crying.

Tank looked at the coffin.

"She died while I was inside. T paid for everything. Told me don't owe him for that."

He rubbed one hand down the front of his suit.

"I owed him for it anyway."

I could hear Shawna breathing beside me.

"He did some shit," Tank said.

The preacher shifted.

"Some shit I can't stand up here and fix for him. I ain't gonna try. But he picked me up when nobody was answering. And I remember that."

He stepped away from the microphone.

On the way back, he looked at me once.

I nodded.

Nigga, it hurt. I needed to let it.

***

Red had sent flowers taller than the coffin.

White roses, blue ribbon, his name on a card big enough to read from the aisle. He hadn't come himself. Every time the door opened, your nigga looked. I hated that he could make me wait for him at a dead man's funeral.

When people went up to the coffin, Shawna stayed seated.

"You don't have to," I said.

"I know."

Then she stood.

We walked up together.

T lay there wearing a dark suit and the ring that had cut my mouth. Somebody had made him look peaceful. I remembered him saying he'd made me. I remembered being happy when he said son.

Nigga, a dead man shouldn't be able to embarrass you. But I stood over him ashamed of how much I'd wanted him to mean it.

Shawna put a folded photograph on his chest.

I recognized the chapel in the background. Her wedding. Dante smiling with his arms around her. This was another print, with T still visible at the edge, one hand on his brother's shoulder.

"You can take your fucking picture with you," she whispered.

I thought she was done.

Then she bent close.

"He loved you, nigga. You knew he did."

Her voice broke on loved.

I looked away before she saw me watching.

Behind us, the preacher cleared his throat. I wanted to turn around and tell that nigga to swallow it. Let her have a minute. Let her tell one dead man what the other couldn't hear.

Shawna straightened.

I touched T's cuff once. I don't know why. Maybe because I couldn't touch his hand. Maybe because he'd never get to pull me close again, and some stupid little part of me wanted that settled properly.

"You should've let us go," I said.

Then I took my woman's hand and walked out.

***

Outside, Rosa waited beside the cars in black sunglasses and a coat the wind kept trying to take.

She'd come for Shawna. She said that three times to everybody who asked.

"I ain't crying for that nigga," she told me.

"Nigga, nobody asked you to."

"I know. I just want the dead to be clear."

That was the first thing.

Then a man beside the hearse dropped the flowers he was holding.

Glass blew out of the car behind Rosa.

Everybody moved at once.

"Down!" Tank roared.

Nigga, there wasn't time to be brave. I grabbed Shawna and the three of us went against the side of the car. Pain tore through me when I bent. Rosa was swearing. Shawna's nails dug into my arm. Gunfire cracked across the parking lot and somebody screamed a name over and over.

Then he came down hard beside me.

"Stay down, nigga."

"Where them niggas at?"

"Stay fucking down."

A bullet struck somewhere above us. Rosa flinched into Shawna. I reached for my gun, but Tank shoved my shoulder back.

"You stand up, you die, nigga."

"Move your hand, nigga."

"Then die mad, nigga."

He wasn't moving. The big motherfucker covered half of us just by refusing to let me act like a king in a parking lot full of bullets.

Across the drive, one of the men who'd carried T's coffin lay against the curb. His shoe had come off. I knew his face from the hospital. He'd stood when I came into the club. I didn't know his name.

That hit me while the shots were still going.

Nigga, he knew mine.

Shawna put her hand over my mouth. I hadn't realized I was saying something.

"Look at me," she said.

I did.

"Stay here."

"I got you, nigga," I told her.

"I said stay, nigga."

So I stayed.

Tank looked over the car, then pulled the back door open.

"Inside."

Rosa got in first. Shawna held on to me while I tried to stand. My legs didn't want it. Tank took me under the arm without asking and put me in beside her.

Nigga, I'd never hated being saved so much.

From the window I saw T's blue coffin waiting on its wheels between the chapel and the hearse. Flowers lay all over the ground. The funeral men were crouched behind the open door, afraid to finish carrying him.

Tank went back, helped lift the coffin, and made sure T got into the car.

Then he came for us.

"You don't know it was him."

"Who the fuck else, Shawna?"

"People loved T."

"Nigga, I paid for the funeral."

"You defending Red now?"

"Say that again."

"You think I forgot what those men did?"

"You think because I got a house and some fucking earrings I forgot where they left him?"

"No."

"Then don't talk to me like I'm sleeping through this."

She picked it back up. Her fingers were shaking at the clasp. I reached for her hand, and she let me help her fasten it.

"I'm scared," I said.

She looked at me.

Nigga, I'd rather have taken another punch than said that. But I'd seen her under that car, and I couldn't get the glass out of my head.

"I'm scared they get you while I'm laying here."

I held on to her.

## 5. Forty Million Reasons to Die

*Dante didn't die for three million.*

“You look tired,” Tank said.

“You look unemployed if you keep talking.”

“Then listen to her.”

"And the car."

"What car?"

"I want one."

"Nigga, you can have a hundred fucking cars."

He shut his eyes again.

"Black ones."

I held on until the doctor came.

I kept my hand around his. The last nigga who called me family had put a bullet in me before the night was over. Tank had taken one instead. Every time a voice came through the door, I looked up expecting somebody to tell me what else I could pay to keep him breathing.

I wasn't letting him wake up alone.

***

Marcellus came into the casino after the shooting stopped.

He walked through the broken glass slowly. His shoes were old and polished. He looked at the walls, the marble, the great staircase Red had built with other people's lives.

Then he looked at me.

I put the ring in his hand.

Blue Mercy flashed under the work lights.

For a long time he didn't speak.

His thumb moved across the setting. Red had put her stone in his own ring. Marcellus turned it in his hand, held it beneath the light, and closed his eyes.

Then he took Celeste's letter out of his coat. He laid the ring against the paper and bent his head over both of them.

I let him take all the time he needed.

"What are you going to do?" I asked.

He closed his hand around the ring.

"Take my wife home."

I nodded.

Forty million dollars, my nigga. I put forty million dollars in an old man's hand and watched him walk away because the woman I loved had just killed a man while saying another man's name, and for the first time that night I understood why some things couldn't be bought back.

Everything else in that casino was mine.

I made sure every nigga understood that too.

## 15. Ten Toes Down Forever

They put Tank in a private room with a view of the mountains.

I paid for the room next door for Rosa because she wouldn't leave him. She said she was there for Shawna. Tank said Rosa had called him handsome while she was keeping him alive, and he wanted witnesses.

She told him he was full of shit.

He smiled at her anyway. Nigga was hooked. I could've told him that from the doorway.

My nigga, I had seen that man face down in the dirt, seen him take punches that would've broken somebody else's neck, seen him stand beside T like nothing in the world could ever make him move. I hadn't ever seen him nervous before a woman walked into a room.

I bought him a black Cadillac.

Then I bought him another one because he called me from the hospital and said the first picture didn't do the color justice.

"Nigga, you buying me a car or showing me a car?" he asked.

"Look out your fucking window."

I heard Rosa open the blinds. Heard that big nigga start laughing. He had to stop because it hurt him. Then he started again.

Nigga had almost died for me. I would've painted the whole fucking city black if he'd asked.

I went back to the house with Shawna three days after Red died.

The lake was quiet. There were flowers at the gate, flowers on the stairs, flowers in the bedroom. Niggas I'd never met sent bottles and watches. Women sent silk, perfume, diamonds. Somebody sent a white horse with a blue ribbon tied into its mane.

I had the horse taken around to the back where she could see it from the balcony.

Shawna stood at the bedroom window and looked down at it.

"You buy that?"

"Somebody sent it."

"Why?"

"Because they know who you are."

She watched it walking beside the water.

"I don't know how to ride."

"Then we'll learn."

She turned toward me.

For a second, I thought she was going to cry again.

Then she laughed.

I had missed that sound so bad, nigga. I had been hearing gunfire in my sleep, hearing T say son, hearing Red tell me she'd have got in the car with anybody. I had started thinking that was all the world had left to say to me.

Then she laughed and came across the room and kissed me.

I picked her up. She wrapped her arms around my neck, and I carried her away from the window while the sun went down behind the mountains. My side hurt. I didn't put her down. Nigga, I'd carried worse for men who loved me less.

Later, we lay awake listening to the water.

Her watch rested on the nightstand.

I looked at it for a while.

She saw me looking.

"You want me to take it off?"

I didn't answer right away.

I wanted everything, my nigga. Her mornings. Her nights. The way she turned her head when somebody said my name. I wanted to be the man she thought of when a song came on. I wanted to reach back three years and be the one carrying that cake into the club. I wanted to put myself in every photograph that had ever made her happy.

I couldn't, nigga.

And all the money in Vegas couldn't help me with that. I'd bought half a fucking city, my nigga, and I couldn't buy one night three years ago.

"No," I said.

She rested her head on my chest.

"Sometimes I miss him when I'm happy."

I looked at the ceiling.

"With me?"

"Yeah."

That hurt. I'm not going to lie to you, nigga. That shit went straight through me.

She lifted her head.

"Because I wish he knew I wasn't hurting all the time anymore."

I looked at her then.

She touched the cross against my chest.

"I wish he knew somebody stayed."

I took her hand and held it there.

Neither of us said anything for a long time.

Then I asked her to marry me. Right there, my nigga. No shirt, no ring, her dead husband's watch where both of us could see it.

She sat up.

"Aiden."

"I mean it."

"You just bought me a house."

"I'll buy you another one."

"That's not what I'm saying."

"Then tell me what you're saying."

She looked at me with tears in her eyes.

"You can't fix every bad thing that ever happened to me."

"I know."

"You gonna be mad when you find that out for real?"

I sat up beside her.

"I'm gonna be mad about a lot of shit, baby. I'm a fucked-up man. You know that."

She nodded.

"I'm asking you to marry me, baby. Bring the watch. Bring the pictures. Bring every fucking thing you can't leave behind. Just come with me."

She put her hand over my mouth.

Then she started laughing and crying at the same time.

"Yes," she said.

I kissed her palm.

"Say it again."

"Yes, nigga. I'll marry you."

I had fifty niggas outside that house ready to die for me and more money than I could count in one room, and I swear to you none of it had ever made me feel like that one word.

***

We got married beside the lake.

Three weeks after I walked into the Saint, my nigga. The flowers cost more than every rent payment I'd ever made put together. Shawna came toward me in a white dress while a woman sang the song she used to sing in her mother's kitchen.

Blue roses and the gardenias I had promised her covered the aisle.

There were diamonds at her throat. Dante's watch on her wrist.

I wore the cross.

Tank stood beside me in a black suit with his arm still healing under the jacket. Rosa sat in the front row wearing a blue dress and trying not to look at him. He looked at her enough for both of them.

Every table had a bottle of Hennessy on it. Every nigga at the gate had a gun. Behind the flowers, my people watched the road while men from other cities came to shake my hand and find out whether the stories were true.

Nigga, they were true enough that none of them tried me.

I paid the singer more than she asked. Then I paid her band. Then I bought the place where she'd been playing because Shawna said the owner had treated her like shit once, years ago, when she was trying to get an audition.

I didn't bring him to the wedding.

I made that nigga listen to it from the parking lot.

Shawna reached me and put her hands in mine.

For a moment I forgot everybody else was there.

The preacher asked me if I would love her in sickness and in health.

I looked at the scar above her eyebrow. At the mark still fading on her mouth. At the wrist she had held against my bleeding side when all I had to give her was a promise about a house near the water.

"I will."

He asked her the same.

"I will."

That's how it happened, my nigga. Her hand was shaking in mine. Tank kept wiping his face with the sleeve on his good arm. Rosa was crying into a napkin she'd stolen off his table. My girl said yes in front of the whole fucking city.

I kissed my wife while the band played and my people shouted my name.

Then we went inside and got so drunk the sun came up before anybody remembered there had been a schedule.

There was cocaine in the upstairs bathroom, cash changing hands on the terrace, hookers dancing with men who had brought other women to the wedding. Somebody drove a convertible onto the lawn. Tank fell asleep with his good arm around Rosa, and she threatened to kill the first motherfucker who took a picture.

I bought every picture the next morning.

I kept one. Nigga looks at it now and swears he was awake. Rosa has the same picture by her bed.

***

People tell this story like everything ended when Red hit the ground.

It didn't, nigga.

The calls kept coming. The money kept coming. The west side still needed a boss on Monday. A man in another city still thought Red dying meant he could take what Red used to sell. Somebody still owed somebody, and somebody's brother still wanted blood.

I didn't become a good man because I put a ring on Shawna's finger.

I was still the nigga who walked into a hotel with blood on his collar and told another man's people the girl was coming with him. I still had a temper. Still had a gun. Still woke up some nights with my hand on the place T had shot me.

And I still wanted everything.

But now I knew who I was bringing it home to, my nigga.

One night, months later, I came back from handling business and found Shawna in the room we'd made for her music.

She had all the lights off except the little lamp beside the piano. There were papers on the floor. A half-finished drink on the windowsill. She was singing something I hadn't heard before.

I stood in the doorway and listened.

When she finished, she turned around.

"How long you been there?"

"Long enough."

"You like it?"

"Yeah."

"You always say that."

"I always mean it."

She held out her hand.

I went to her.

Outside, there were niggas guarding the gates and a city waiting for me to make the next decision. In that room, she pulled me down beside her and put my hand against her heart.

"Stay a minute," she said.

I stayed all night, my nigga.

***

Aight, my nigga. Now you know.

You know about the money in the pool. You know about the blood in the car. You know why I wear a dead man's cross and why my wife still wears his watch. You know which nigga called me son and tried to kill me, and which nigga took a bullet before he'd let me stand alone.

Don't let anybody tell you I came out of that desert clean, nigga. I came out alive. I came out with Shawna's hand in mine and Tank bleeding in the back of an ambulance, and I made the whole fucking city learn our names.

My nigga, I gave away the most expensive diamond I ever held. Then I took everything the man wearing it thought he was going to leave behind. That's what happened. That's my money in the walls of that house. That's my name on the cars. That's my wife singing with the window open when the lake goes black at night.

And when she wakes up crying, nigga, I'm there. When Tank calls me at three in the morning, nigga, I answer. When some motherfucker thinks the years made me soft, nigga, I let him get close enough to see the scar.

You can call that love. You can call that crazy. Call it whatever the fuck you want, my nigga. I was crazy before she loved me. I had blood on me before she touched me. She didn't make me dangerous. She gave all that danger somewhere to come home.

I remember Dante, nigga. I never met him, but I remember him every time that gold cross hits my chest. I remember T, nigga. I wish I could remember him without the gun. I remember Red, nigga. I remember what he looked like when he found out there was something in that room he couldn't buy.

And I remember her in the Mercedes, my nigga. One hand on the wheel. One hand trying to keep my life inside me. She could've left me. She could've taken what she could carry and gone. But she stayed, nigga. My girl stayed.

So I stayed too, nigga.

Ten toes down, nigga. In the club. In the desert. In that hospital hallway waiting for Tank to open his eyes. Ten toes down, nigga, when the money came. Ten toes down, nigga, when the money wasn't worth a fucking thing.

And if another man comes through that gate thinking he's going to make her cry like that again, my nigga, he better bring every goddamn prayer he knows.

Because I'm home now, nigga.

Aiden.

Ten toes down. Forever, my nigga.
