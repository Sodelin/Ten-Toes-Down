# Editorial record: Blood Money and Blue Diamonds

Edition date: 7 September 2026. Published PDF: 200 pages. Full Markdown file: 35,140 whitespace-delimited words, including front matter and headings. Story text: 35,025 whitespace-delimited words, including the fifteen chapter headings.

## Creative direction

Nolan requested a full-length expansion of *Ten Toes Down* that follows the first-person crime and romance direction of *First Night in Vegas*. The brief calls for an outrageous, profane parody of a parody performed with conviction: the characters take their desires, rivalries, boasts, and betrayals seriously. Love and loyalty drive the action. The novel expands the cast, consequences, and nested stories while keeping Aiden's immediate voice at the center.

This is a separate edition. *The Final Final Cut* and the *First Night in Vegas* experiment remain available in their original repository locations. Publication records a new draft for reading; it does not imply that Nolan or other readers have approved its artistic quality.

## 11. Process reflection

The revision begins from the user's stated response to the previous versions and the supplied source material. Separate voice and continuity reviews inform the assembled manuscript. The final continuity pass corrected breakfast timing, premature knowledge of the diamond, a borrowed car image, gun and letter/ring custody, the location of the cash, the basis for Tank's remembered observations, and Rosa and Vega's arrival. It also checked that wounds and deaths remained consequential. These are editorial checks rather than an artistic score.

The final PDF was checked for 200 nonempty pages, sixteen bookmarks (contents and fifteen chapters), and the complete closing vow. The cover, contents, opening, and ending received visual inspection. These checks establish basic document completeness and readability; they do not imply that every page was visually inspected.

## 12. Robustness reflection

The principal artistic uncertainty is whether the committed excess sustains a shared reading experience across a much longer story. Repetition may create a recognizable voice for one reader and become tiring for another. Additional length also raises the risk that explanations of the nested histories interrupt the immediate relationships. These are questions for readers, not outcomes that file checks or internal editorial review can establish.
