# BOOK V: THE CROWN OF MIDNIGHT

## 1. The City That Stayed Up for Me

Midnight City had built a throne at the airport.

I thought that was considerate as hell until a nigga in a silver cape told me it wasn't mine.

"It's for the victor of the tournament."

"Nigga, then why you making me walk past it?"

"You have not won."

"You have not seen me lose. We both working with expectations. Move the chair."

A guard behind him lowered his eyes.

"That nigga wants the throne brought over," another whispered.

"That nigga just landed."

"Nigga, I been sitting all flight. I know what kind of chair I deserve."

The first guard nodded before he remembered whose rifle he was carrying.

He had a tall hat, a little mustache, and twenty men behind him holding gold rifles. Every rifle turned toward me at the same time. Shawna stepped out of the police Lamborghini, stretched after the flight, and took one look at the situation.

"Baby, we just landed."

"I know. These niggas already hiding my furniture."

The silver cape made a noise in his throat. Tank got out of the next car carrying a garment bag in one hand and the expression he usually wore before putting somebody through a closed door. Rosa was still in the car doing her lipstick. She rolled down the window to see who was about to interrupt her vacation.

"Is that your king?" she asked the silver cape.

"I am Lord Octavian Rusk, master of ceremonies."

"Then do a ceremony where you get out of the way."

Behind us, the whole airport had stopped pretending it was busy. Pilots watched from the stairs of their jets. Baggage men stood on their carts. A woman in a white flight uniform took her sunglasses off slowly, like her eyes deserved this without anything between them and me.

I had come to Midnight City because Emperor Zero thought the world was something he could buy, and General Solomon Saint had died proving that a nigga can have a whole army and still pick the wrong island. We had found enough money under Isla Gloria to make rich men look poor. We had also found enough of Zero's secrets to make him want all of us dead.

He had sent an invitation anyway.

Black card. Silver letters. My name bigger than the address.

COME AND CLAIM WHAT YOU THINK YOU DESERVE.

Shawna had read it twice, then asked if there would be dancing.

That was why I loved my wife. A man could threaten the future of everybody we knew and this woman would still make sure I packed my good shoes.

The ship from Isla Gloria had taken us as far as the mainland port. We changed to planes there, cars loaded into cargo holds, because a nigga was not arriving at a tournament of criminal kings in a rented van.

Isolde took a different flight from the same airfield. Vegas. She wanted the room above Marcellus's workshop ready for dancing when he came home. She kissed him at the gate, straightened his collar, and told him to call before he started missing her dramatically.

The old nigga called from the next gate.

“Already?” I asked.

“We lost thirty-five years to poor timing. I'm improving.”

He held the phone out. Isolde told me to bring him back with all his fingers because she needed somebody to fasten her necklaces.

“Nigga, he heard you.”

“I want you to hear me too.”

I promised. Marcellus took his phone back and walked toward our plane smiling at it like somebody had finally taught a jewel to speak.

One of the airport workers finally came close enough to stop pretending he was moving luggage. Young nigga, twenty-seven maybe, blue uniform, white gloves, a whole expression of recognition trying to get out through his mouth.

"That's the nigga," he whispered to another worker.

"Which nigga?"

"The island nigga. The champion. The cop."

"Those the same nigga?"

I turned.

"Nigga, I heard all three descriptions."

The first worker almost dropped his clipboard.

"Captain. I watched the championship. My father said no man could do all that."

"Your father doing better now?"

He laughed so suddenly the second worker started laughing too.

"He owes me money."

"Nigga, collect it with interest. I worked hard on that argument."

Shawna leaned against my shoulder, smiling. The worker looked at her and forgot the clipboard completely. It hit the ground. His friend caught it off the bounce and bowed like that had been a planned part of welcoming my wife.

"Mrs. Aiden," he said.

"Shawna," she told him.

"Yes. Of course. Shawna. We watched you sing. My mother made everybody be quiet. Even my father. Especially my father."

"Your mother sounds talented," my wife said.

Nigga, both workers looked blessed personally.

Rusk tried to interrupt. Neither one heard him the first time. A king could stand on a whole throne beside these niggas and still lose their attention to my wife being nice for twelve seconds.

The first worker took off his airport badge.

"Would you sign the back?"

"You sure they allow that?" I asked.

"My supervisor asked me to get his signed too."

The supervisor was standing behind a baggage cart pretending he hadn't sent the nigga over. I pointed at him. He gave up the performance and came running with his badge in his hand.

Tank watched the little line form.

"Nigga, you ain't even reached customs."

"Customs reached me."

"These niggas got jobs."

"They improving their workplaces."

Rosa took a pen out of her bag and handed it to Shawna.

"Sign the pretty side. Niggas gonna frame these."

We signed the badges. The supervisor asked whether he could name the terminal lounge after me. Rusk said that required royal authorization. The supervisor looked from Rusk to the throne to me and appeared to solve the question in a way the master of ceremonies didn't enjoy.

"We can put up a temporary sign," he said.

"Nigga, that's initiative," I told him.

By the time Vesper reached us, a porter was carrying a whiteboard across the terminal. Somebody had written CAPTAIN AIDEN'S ARRIVAL LOUNGE in blue marker. Underneath, in smaller letters, the younger worker had added AND SHAWNA'S.

My wife saw it.

"He gets a tip."

"Nigga gets a promotion if the king situation works out."

Tank shook his head.

"We been here eleven minutes."

"And you wasting them counting, my nigga. Look alive. The country learning."

"Lord whatever," I said. "Nigga, I'm tired. My wife looks beautiful. My brother got a suit in that bag that cost more than your first apartment. Look at that nigga. Does he look like he packed patience?"

Tank lifted the garment bag.

"This my good suit. Don't make a nigga meet you in it angry."

"So show us our rooms," I told Rusk, "or bring that throne closer. Nigga can keep talking while I enjoy my future furniture."

Rusk's face tightened. A few of the riflemen looked at each other. That little look meant they had already realized the same thing I had: their boss was standing too close to my hood.

Then somebody started clapping.

Slow. Three times.

A woman came down the airport steps in a dark green coat with nothing timid about it. Black hair pinned high. A gold snake around one wrist. Two guards carrying her luggage like they were transporting the last clean water on earth.

She was thirty-seven, I found out later, and she had the kind of face that could make a man apologize for a crime she had committed.

"Aiden," she said.

"That's usually who it is."

"Queen Vesper Vane."

"Queen of what?"

"You ask that before you kiss my hand?"

"Nigga, I'm married before I ask either."

Shawna laughed. Vesper looked at her, then at the wedding ring, then back at me with a smile that got sharper instead of smaller.

"How unfortunate for the city."

"The city's gonna survive," Shawna said. "I'm less sure about your master of ceremonies."

Vesper turned one finger and the gold rifles lowered.

"Lord Rusk mistakes anticipation for authority. Your rooms are ready. The opening procession is in forty minutes."

"We just flew in."

"The kings wait for nobody."

A jet engine started somewhere behind me. My police badge shone in the sun. I was looking at a queen, a throne, a thousand windows full of nosy rich people, and a city that thought forty minutes was enough time to put my wife in a dress.

"They finna learn," I said.

At the hotel entrance, the man taking our luggage dropped my smaller bag when he recognized my face.

The bag hit the marble and opened. A shirt came out. Then a shoe. Then the little framed photograph Shawna carried from our wedding.

The man went down on both knees.

“Captain. I'm sorry. I'm so—”

“Nigga, pick up my wife before you apologize to my shoes.”

He lifted the photograph carefully. The glass hadn't broken. Shawna took it, checked the frame, and smiled at him.

“She's fine.”

The man looked from the picture to her face.

“That's really you.”

“Still is,” she said.

Behind him, a second porter whispered, “Nigga, I told you.”

“You said the car man.”

“That is the car man.”

“You didn't say the wife was real too.”

Nigga, I heard every word. I stood there enjoying the exact moment two grown men realized greatness traveled with luggage and occasionally asked somebody to find the other shoe.

I took my bag off the floor myself.

The first porter tried to stop me.

“Sir, I can carry—”

“Then carry hers. Mine knows who it belongs to.”

Shawna gave him the photograph to hold while she straightened the bag. He held it with both hands, terrified of disappointing two versions of the same beautiful woman at once.

Mercedes leaned toward Porsche.

“Nigga looks like he got handed a religion.”

“Baby, look at the man standing next to it.”

I put an arm around my wife and let the porters lead us inside. Behind us, the one with the photograph kept whispering, “I told you,” and the other kept answering, “Nigga, you did not tell me enough.”

Our hotel had once been a palace. You could tell because getting to the bathroom required crossing land somebody had probably died over. Marble stairs, red carpets, chandeliers thick as trees. Two young men tried to take Shawna's bags and accidentally competed for the same handle until she assigned them separate suitcases.

Our suite overlooked the harbor. Modern yachts sat under stone walls older than everybody's grudges. Ferraris crawled along the waterfront between flower stalls and armed men drinking coffee. Above the city, on a black hill, Zero's palace stood with every window shut against the afternoon.

Shawna walked out onto the balcony and let the sea wind move her hair.

"That's pretty."

I looked at her instead.

"Yeah."

"You didn't even look."

"I looked where I wanted."

She turned with that smile I had crossed whole cities to keep, and I caught her around the waist before she could say something that would have had me canceling the tournament. For a minute there were no kings. No invitations. No bullet scars. Just my wife with her hands at my collar, straightening something that hadn't been crooked until she decided it needed attention.

"You gonna behave tonight?"

"You looking at the most beautiful behavior a nigga ever brought overseas."

"Aiden."

"Nigga, you said my name like I already did it."

"You looked at the throne like you were measuring the balcony."

"That's planning. A nigga got a wife. He thinks about where she's gonna sit."

"Beside you when you behave."

"Then I'm gonna misbehave beautifully enough you stay close."

She kissed me once, hard enough to make that answer official.

A knock came at the door. Tank carried in a black case with the hotel crest on it.

"Somebody sent you a welcome present."

I opened it. Inside was a pistol plated in white gold, a key to a red Ferrari, and a note.

FOR THE MAN WHO NEEDS HELP ARRIVING ON TIME.

"Who sent it?"

"Vesper."

Shawna read the note over my shoulder.

"She better send me something."

Another knock. Rosa came through holding a long green box.

"That queen sent you emerald earrings," she told Shawna. "I was gonna steal them, but Tank looked disappointed before I even did it."

"I was looking at your shoes."

"You were looking with judgment."

"Baby, one heel is a weapon."

"Both of them are weapons. Keep up."

Shawna opened the earrings, admired them, and set them beside the mirror without putting them on. She chose a blue pair I had given her instead. Nigga, my whole chest went stupid. People kept trying to give me cities, but my wife choosing my earrings could still knock the sense out of me faster than any man alive.

By the time we came downstairs, the procession had started without us.

There were drums outside. Horns. Men on black horses. Women in gowns riding in open cars. Every balcony on the avenue had a banner hanging from it. We were supposed to join at the front, behind the ceremonial carriage carrying an empty cushion where the Crown of Midnight would eventually sit.

The Ferrari was waiting at the curb.

So was a young hotel manager who looked ready to start a new life somewhere else.

"Captain Aiden, the avenue has been closed."

"Open it."

"I cannot. The procession route is sealed."

I looked at the route. I looked at the Ferrari. I looked at the elevated tram line running down the middle of the street.

Tank saw me looking.

"Nigga."

"What?"

"Nigga, that's a train track."

"That's a road with confidence."

"For a train."

"What this nigga have that I don't?"

"Tracks. A schedule. A whole different vehicle."

"And I got my wife looking at me. Nigga better keep his schedule."

Shawna was already getting into the passenger seat.

"Don't scratch my side."

Rosa grabbed Tank's sleeve before he could climb in behind us.

"We taking the normal road. I want to arrive with my hairstyle still believing in God."

I drove the Ferrari through the hotel courtyard, past a fountain, up a service ramp, and onto the low platform where the trams came through. A bell rang. People screamed. A tram driver leaned out and saw me coming toward him in a car that cost enough to buy his entire argument.

I took the outside rail.

The Ferrari tilted.

Shawna held her hair with one hand and my thigh with the other.

"Aiden."

"I got it."

"I know. I'm saying your name because I like it."

Nigga, I almost missed the turn.

We dropped from the platform into the procession between two marching bands. One band stopped. The other started playing louder like they were trying to survive through talent. My front tires landed inches from a drummer's shoes. He looked at me, looked at the Ferrari, and kept the beat.

I threw him a stack of money.

"That's professionalism, my nigga."

The crowd recognized the badge first. Then my face. Then the red car where no red car was supposed to be.

"Aiden!"

Somebody yelled it from a balcony. Another voice caught it. By the time we reached the empty royal carriage, the whole avenue was saying my name.

Shawna stood just enough in her seat to wave. Her blue dress caught the sun. Cameras turned away from kings who had spent years preparing to be looked at and pointed at my wife arriving late with me.

A man in a purple limousine slammed his fist against his window.

"You have broken the order!"

"Nigga, I improved it!"

That was when the first shot hit the ceremonial carriage.

The empty cushion jumped. A horse reared. The music tore apart into separate frightened noises.

I saw three black vans pull out from a side street. Men in white masks stood through their roof openings. They weren't firing into the crowd. Their guns tracked the cars carrying the visiting kings. One burst shattered the purple limousine's windshield. Another took the mirror off my Ferrari.

Shawna dropped into her seat.

"Now you can scratch my side."

I hit the gas.

The Ferrari crossed in front of the carriage as the next burst came. Glass broke above us. I pulled the white-gold pistol from its case and fired toward the nearest masked man while Shawna grabbed a fallen parade banner and threw it across the windshield of the van coming alongside.

The driver saw nothing but the city's royal crest.

He hit a flower cart so hard roses filled the air like the street had exploded romantically.

"Baby, you wrapped that nigga's whole vehicle!"

"You like my throw?"

"Nigga, I would marry that throw if I hadn't married the woman!"

"Then stop looking so pleased with that gun. Who gave you that?"

"Queen! Nigga, we can argue after the florist stops flying!"

The second van came at the carriage. Its driver wanted the road open and didn't care how many horses had to disappear to make that happen. I cut between them and caught his bumper with the Ferrari's back corner. The whole car jolted. My shoulder slammed the seat. Blue heat sparked inside my arms, that thunder I could call for a little while if I was willing to pay for it afterward.

I didn't spend it yet.

A little power was good. Knowing when a nigga needed to keep some was better.

I drove up onto the broad stone steps of the opera house. People scattered behind columns. The van followed until its front end struck a statue base and stopped hard enough to throw the masked gunman against his own roof.

The third van turned toward the harbor.

I went after it.

"Seat belt," I said.

Shawna pulled it tight.

"You say that every time we're already flying."

"Because you listen beautifully under pressure."

We passed fish stalls, a wedding party, and a priest who lifted his hands at exactly the right moment for our car to miss his sleeves. The van cut onto a loading pier. A cargo crane stood over the water with a steel container hanging from it. Beyond the pier, Vesper's black speedboat waited with its engines running.

The masked men were not trying to escape the city.

They were trying to reach her boat.

I saw Vesper on the upper dock. She saw me see her.

Neither one of us smiled.

The van braked beneath the crane. Three men jumped out. One carried a narrow silver case that must have come from the carriage during the confusion. The cushion had been empty. Something beneath it hadn't been.

I hit the blue thunder.

For three seconds the whole world became sharp.

My hands, the wheel, the engine, the air in my teeth. The Ferrari leaped forward with blue fire running across the hood. Shawna shouted my name and it sounded like a song somebody had turned all the way up.

We went off the end of the loading ramp.

The car struck the hanging container with its front wheels, rode the metal side at an angle no mechanic on earth would have signed his name under, and dropped onto the dock between the men and the boat.

Every window on the pier shook when we landed.

My arms went dead-heavy. That was the price. I let the thunder go before it took more than I had to spend.

One masked man raised his weapon.

Shawna opened her door into him.

The second slipped on the roses still caught beneath our tire. I put him down before he could stand. The third held the silver case over the water.

"Stop or I drop it!"

I got out slowly, my legs arguing about the landing.

"Nigga, you keep threatening me with stuff I don't know the price of."

"It belongs to the emperor!"

"That explains why you're scared."

He threw it.

I caught the case against my chest with one hand and caught him by his coat with the other. For a second we were both leaning out over the harbor. He stared at the water. I stared at him.

"Nigga, can you swim?"

"No!"

"You stole a case, ran to the harbor, and dressed for a fucking ballroom? Nigga, which part did you plan?"

"Please!"

"I'm holding you, my nigga. That's why you still get to ask questions above water. Stop kicking my car."

I dragged him back and dropped him on the dock. Harbor police were finally arriving, breathless, uniforms immaculate enough to prove they'd missed most of the trouble.

Their chief took one look at my badge.

"Captain Aiden?"

"Arrest these niggas. And somebody get my wife a different car. This one got opinions now."

The chief saluted.

Nigga, he saluted so hard his hat nearly left him.

Vesper came down the steps without hurrying. Her guards stayed above. She looked at the destroyed Ferrari, the captured attackers, my wife standing on the dock with one emerald box still in her handbag, and the silver case in my hands.

"You are very difficult to welcome."

"You sent the car."

"I expected you to use the street."

"You should have said that."

I opened the case. Inside lay a strip of black velvet and a heavy gold seal marked with a crown. No jewels. No magic. Just an old piece of authority a whole lot of men had nearly died trying to move from one place to another.

Vesper's eyes changed when she saw it.

"The signet of invitation," she said. "It admits one claimant to the private rounds."

"I thought I was already invited."

"You were invited to watch."

Shawna came beside me and closed the case.

"That's embarrassing for whoever sent the invitation."

By evening the city had put my face on the side of a building.

I hadn't asked. Nigga, I had been taking a shower. I came out in a towel and there I was outside our balcony, forty feet tall, stepping out of the wrecked Ferrari with the silver case in my hand. Underneath it, somebody had written THE KING FROM THE WEST.

Tank stood at the balcony rail with a drink.

"Niggas got your good side."

"I ain't got a bad one."

"Your left shoulder looks tired."

"Nigga, that's history."

"History needs ice."

"Then put ice on the real nigga. That nigga outside forty feet tall. We can't afford his treatment."

Tank looked from me to the building and started laughing into his drink.

Shawna sat at the vanity putting on my blue earrings. Her dress for dinner was black, fitted, and personally disrespectful to every other plan I had made that night.

I went behind her and put my hands on her shoulders.

"We can stay in."

"You just stole a king's invitation."

"I can steal dinner too."

She looked at me in the mirror.

"I want to see what they do when you walk in."

So did I.

Downstairs, Lord Rusk waited by the throne from the airport. It had somehow reached the hotel before our luggage. The silver cape was gone. His new coat was black, and there was a bandage across one eyebrow where the parade had given him a different opinion about the afternoon.

"Captain," he said carefully. "The council has acknowledged your claim."

"Good."

"There are rules."

I sat down on the throne to adjust my cuff.

Every conversation in the lobby stopped.

Shawna stood beside me, her hand resting on my shoulder like she had been born knowing where a crown would eventually fit.

"Aight," I told Rusk. "Start with the interesting ones."

## 2. I Arrested the Man Who Paid for Dinner

The first rule was that no king could arrest another king during dinner.

"Nigga, why that first?"

Lord Rusk looked at my badge.

"Experience."

"Whose?"

"The household's."

"So some nigga already needed arresting at dinner."

"Captain—"

"Nigga, you warned the right guest. Keep reading."

The second rule was that every claimant had to bring a gift worth a million dollars. The third was that the host could reject any gift he considered beneath his station. The fourth concerned seating, inheritance, and a dead prince's second wife, and I stopped listening because Shawna had started rubbing the back of my neck.

"What counts as a gift?" I asked.

"Gold. Jewels. Land. A pledge of valuable service."

"I brought myself."

Rusk waited.

I waited back.

"Captain, surely you understand—"

"Nigga, that's at least two gifts. I'm handsome and I'm here."

Shawna bent down beside my ear.

"You forgot punctual."

"Baby, they started early."

Rusk escorted us to a car before I could improve the rules any further. Tank and Rosa followed in a black Bentley the hotel had provided after seeing what had happened to the Ferrari. Mercedes and Porsche came down together, one in silver and the other in red, arguing about which of them a visiting prince had been looking at across the lobby.

"He was looking at me," Mercedes said.

"He was looking at the reflection of me in your bracelet," Porsche told her.

The prince hurried over holding two bouquets.

"Ladies, perhaps both—"

"Nigga, pick a struggle," I said, and kept walking.

Dinner was at the House of Nine Doors, a white mansion built over the water. Eight of its doors were enormous. The ninth was little and blue and stood halfway up an outside wall with no stairs beneath it.

"What's that one for?" Tank asked.

"Disgraced guests," Rusk said.

"Nigga, if somebody tries to put me through a wall door, I'm taking the whole wall."

The dining room ran the length of the house. A hundred candles burned along a table set for twenty-four. Every plate had somebody's crest on it. Mine showed a wolf standing on a car, which I decided was close enough.

Vesper sat beside an empty chair at the center. Our host stood when we entered.

Baron Silas Bell was a broad man in his forties with a polished head and a beard cut so sharply it looked like it had its own knife. His suit was cream. His rings were black. He wore a little silver crown tilted to one side, like royalty had caught him off guard.

"The captain," he said. "We heard you had a dramatic journey."

"Your city put train tracks in my way."

"Those have been there seventy years."

"Nigga, they should have moved them when they heard I was coming."

A man at the far end laughed into his wine. Bell didn't.

I kissed Shawna's hand before pulling out her chair. Three other men immediately stood to help their wives sit down. One wasn't fast enough and his wife gave him a look that ruined the rest of his meal before it arrived.

Vesper watched the chain of events with open amusement.

"You have introduced a new custom."

"You niggas wasn't helping your women sit?"

"Some of us were waiting for our women to help us," said an old king with a silver beard.

His wife patted his cheek.

"And some of us are still waiting."

Wine came. Fish came. A whole roasted bird came under a dome so big two waiters had to lift it together. Tank saw it and sat straighter, finally convinced the trip had a purpose.

Before Bell could begin the formal business, a king with a ruby over one eye leaned across the table and pointed at my badge.

"Is that genuine?"

I looked down at it.

"Nigga, it better be. They made me promise things."

"You are a criminal."

"And employed. Don't limit a nigga because your imagination got narrow shoulders."

His wife laughed into her wine. He looked at her. She looked right back, still laughing, like marriage didn't require her to pretend he hadn't walked into the joke carrying his own chair.

"I mean," the king said carefully, "what do you do with the authority?"

"Usually arrive where somebody thought I wouldn't."

"And then?"

"Depends how handsome the situation is when I get there."

Tank leaned over his plate.

"Nigga, don't ask him to explain the whole career. Your food gonna retire."

The ruby-eyed king ignored him.

"I have commanded men for thirty years."

"Nigga, congratulations. They still listen?"

His wife put her glass down before she lost it entirely.

He turned a shade darker.

"My household is loyal."

"I ain't doubt your household. You the nigga doing a speech about it."

Across the table, Porsche whispered to Mercedes, "That nigga asked one question and lost three anniversaries."

"His wife smiling like she just got a present she picked herself."

Shawna laid her hand on my forearm.

"Baby. Let him eat."

I kissed her fingers.

"See? That's command. Everybody comfortable."

The old silver-bearded king at the far end lifted his glass toward us.

"I would like to propose a toast."

Bell's face tightened. Apparently he owned the toasts along with the furniture.

The old man continued anyway.

"To the captain, who has introduced three useful customs to this city in less than a day: arriving when he pleases, telling the truth about our foolishness, and looking at his wife as though he has won something more valuable than an argument."

His own wife smiled at him.

Nigga, the old man had scored one for himself on my play. I respected the movement.

Glasses rose around the table. Even the ruby-eyed king lifted his, though he held it low enough that a man could pretend he was only checking the color.

I stood.

"To everybody smart enough to enjoy me while I'm here."

Tank raised his drink higher.

"That's our nigga."

Rosa touched her glass to his.

"That's an expensive nigga."

Shawna looked up at me.

"That's my husband."

Nigga, I sat down so happily the chair probably thought it had won the toast.

Bell tapped his glass.

"Before we eat, the gifts."

A procession started at the door. Emerald swords. A model yacht with keys beneath it. A painting of Bell wearing the exact expression he was wearing while looking at the painting.

Mercedes leaned toward Porsche.

"If I ever buy a man a picture of himself, call somebody."

"I'll buy you a better man."

My turn arrived. Rusk looked nervous enough for both of us.

I stood, buttoned my jacket, and set the silver invitation case on the table.

Bell's smile vanished.

Vesper's stayed still.

"This was stolen this afternoon," I said. "Some niggas tried to make off with it while everybody was busy running. I brought it back."

"A recovered object cannot be offered as your own gift," Bell said.

"Good thing the gift is this nigga not asking why your crest was inside their van."

Bell looked at the case.

Then at me.

"You consider that a gift?"

"Nigga, I gift-wrapped the question by waiting till everybody had wine. Shawna told me to be social."

"He was excited to meet you," my wife said.

The nigga looked considerably less excited to have been met.

The room went so quiet I heard Tank put his fork down.

Shawna looked at Bell like she had been expecting him to disappoint her and was annoyed by how quickly he had managed it.

Bell spread his hands.

"Many vehicles in this city bear my crest."

"They all carry masked niggas shooting at the parade?"

"I rent to a variety of clients."

"That's a hell of a variety."

Vesper lifted her wine but didn't drink.

Bell turned his head toward a side door. A servant stepped out. Two guards took his place. Their jackets sat wrong over what they were carrying.

I knew that shape. Nigga, I had owned that shape in several colors.

"You are a guest in my home," Bell said.

"Nigga, I'm also a cop in your dining room."

"You accepted my hospitality."

"The guest accepted a bird. The cop accepted evidence. You should've asked which one was hungry."

Tank looked at his plate, then at Bell.

"And this nigga ain't even been served yet. You got problems arriving from every chair."

Rusk cleared his throat.

"The first rule—"

"I remember. No arrests during dinner."

I picked up my plate, ate one bite, and put the napkin down.

"I'm finished."

Tank stood so fast his chair slid into the man behind him. Rosa caught her wine before it tipped. Porsche took a candle off the table. Mercedes lifted the long silver serving tray like she had been waiting all night for somebody to hand her a shield.

Bell's first guard drew.

I hit the table with both hands and tipped its heavy center section up.

The gunfire struck the underside. Plates shattered. Roast bird flew over Tank's shoulder. Every king in the room discovered a reason to be under his chair except the old man with the silver beard, who stayed seated long enough to finish his wine.

Shawna dropped behind the raised table beside me.

"You could have let me finish."

"I'll buy you another bird."

"I wanted the sauce."

"Baby, I'm arresting the sauce too."

Tank went around the end of the table and put a guard into the wine cabinet. Rosa swept broken glass away from the old queen's shoes and pulled her down behind a pillar. Mercedes caught a falling lamp on her tray, cursed its ancestors, and launched the whole arrangement at the second guard.

The curtains caught fire.

"That wasn't the plan," she said.

"It was a beautiful plan till the last second," Porsche told her, and threw a pitcher over the flames.

Bell ran through a private door.

I followed with Shawna behind me, both of us moving through the smoke and shouting while the kings crawled out of the way. A side corridor opened into a gallery full of cars. Inside the house. Nigga, the baron had parked a whole collection on Persian rugs and still expected me to respect his dinner rules.

Bell jumped into a silver coupe. Its engine screamed. Glass doors at the far end began opening.

I got into a black Ferrari beside it.

Shawna slid in with me.

"You choosing cars by color now?"

"I'm choosing the one with keys."

Bell tore through the doors onto a balcony wide enough to host another dinner. The sea flashed beneath the railing. He headed for a bridge joining the house to a neighboring tower.

I followed.

The Ferrari's tires slid on marble. My left arm still ached from the blue thunder at the pier, and I kept it honest. No power this time. Just me, the engine, and my wife's hand braced against the dash while she laughed like she had personally ordered this evening.

Bell knocked over a line of stone flowerpots behind him. One broke beneath our front tire. Another bounced against the windshield and exploded into dirt.

"He throwing gardens at us!" Shawna shouted.

"Nigga's out of ideas!"

We crossed the bridge. A servant pushing a cake cart flattened himself against the wall. I swerved, clipped the edge of the cart, and launched a six-tier cake through the open doorway of a bedroom.

Somebody inside screamed.

Then a woman shouted, "Silas, you said your wife wasn't coming!"

Shawna looked at me.

"Arrest him twice."

The bridge ended inside the tower's second-floor ballroom. Bell drove straight through a circle of musicians. They abandoned their chairs just before his car struck the little conductor's stand. My Ferrari followed beneath a chandelier that had not expected traffic.

The exit ahead was narrow.

Bell's coupe fit.

My Ferrari didn't look like it would.

I turned the wheel, rode one side up a fallen orchestra platform, and went through tilted onto two wheels. A violin bow caught in the passenger mirror. Shawna took it off carefully and laid it in her lap.

"Souvenir."

We landed on a terrace overlooking the harbor road. Bell had nowhere left to go. His car spun near the steps. He got out holding a gun and a little black remote.

"Stay there!"

I stopped.

Not because of his voice. Because four waiters and a woman in a chef's coat stood trapped between the cars. He had picked a place where somebody else would pay if the evening went wrong.

"Move them," I said.

"You don't command me in my house."

"Nigga, I been doing it for twenty minutes."

He showed me the remote.

"The garage beneath us is full of fuel."

Shawna opened her door slowly.

Bell pointed the gun at her.

My whole body went cold.

"Look at me," I said.

He did. Everybody did. There are moments when a man's voice stops belonging to his throat and comes from somewhere lower, somewhere that has buried too much to bargain politely.

"You put that on her again and you won't live long enough to hear what you pressed."

Bell's hand shook.

The woman in the chef's coat stepped sideways. Shawna noticed. So did I.

She was standing beside a large silver trolley, its top covered in empty serving dishes. I couldn't have told anybody afterward how we agreed. My wife looked at the trolley. I looked at Bell. That was enough.

"What do you want?" I asked him.

"The case. And your badge."

"Nigga, you want my jacket too? It fits better on somebody loved."

He took one angry step toward me.

Shawna kicked the trolley into his knees.

I was out of the car before he hit the ground. His gun went across the stone. I caught his wrist, drove the remote out of his hand, and pinned him against his own silver hood. The waiters ran. The chef picked up the remote, looked at it, and made the most disgusted face of the night.

"This opens the pool cover."

Silas Bell shut his eyes.

Nigga, I laughed so hard I nearly let him go.

Nearly.

"Nigga, you threatened my wife with a swimming pool?"

"You cannot understand the pressure I am under."

The chef held up the remote.

"There are four buttons. Heating. Cover. Lights. Fountain."

"Nigga chose fountain and spoke with his whole chest," Shawna said.

I had to brace myself against the hood. Bell tried to turn.

"Stay there, my nigga. I'm laughing. I ain't resigning."

"This is humiliating."

"Nigga, you brought that from the factory. I'm only delivering the handcuffs."

Harbor Chief Lucien Ardent arrived with twelve officers and the confidence of a man who had heard I was already there. He was forty-nine, immaculate, and wearing a medal he had not possessed at the pier. Apparently the city gave them out quickly when I was nearby.

I handed Bell over.

"Attempted murder. Stolen parade furniture. Whatever you call all that dinner shit."

Ardent stared past me at the Ferrari sitting crooked beside the shattered ballroom doors.

"You brought that through the tower?"

"Most of it."

One of his officers leaned toward another.

"That nigga drove upstairs."

"Inside?"

"Nigga, look at the violin. It's in the car."

The second officer looked through the Ferrari's open window and saw Shawna's souvenir laid carefully across the seat.

"That nigga went through the orchestra."

"Nigga still got his wife with him."

"She helped," I told them. "Don't have my wife stand here while y'all give all her greatness to the driver."

Both men straightened. One saluted Shawna. She nodded like he had finally read the proper name on the invitation.

"Captain," Ardent said, "our men would be honored to drive under your command tonight."

"Then drive my people somewhere with food."

By the time we reached the dining room again, Tank had found the kitchen.

He stood beside a new platter with gravy on his cuff. Rosa was sitting on the edge of the table, feeding him a bite while the old queen watched approvingly. Mercedes and Porsche had taken charge of a nervous prince and were making him apologize to the servers one at a time for something he'd said during the gunfire.

Vesper stood near the burned curtains.

Her dress was untouched.

That annoyed me more than a little.

"You could've helped," I told her.

"I moved three guests through the west door."

"You also watched your baron try to kill us."

"He is not my baron."

"He had your invitation seal."

"So do you. Shall I call you mine?"

Shawna came beside me and took my arm.

"Try it and see if he answers."

For a second Vesper's expression softened. It surprised me. Not jealousy exactly. More like she had looked through a window and found a room she used to know.

Then it was gone.

"Silas entered the tournament to clear a debt," she said. "He thought stealing the private seal would keep a stronger claimant out. He chose poorly."

"Who was supposed to get it?"

"A man who has not lost a fight in eleven years."

Tank stopped chewing.

"He overdue."

Vesper looked at him, then at me.

"Tomorrow night. The cathedral beneath the city. Bring your badge if it comforts you. The men there won't care."

"Nigga, they always care by the time I leave."

Outside, the police had closed the waterfront for us. A crowd filled the far side of the barriers. Somebody had already printed the picture of me holding Bell against his car. Shawna took one, studied it, and nodded.

"That's a good picture."

"You want your nigga to sign it?"

"I want you to explain why your hand looks that good in it."

"That's the hand that holds you. Nigga keeps his best work in circulation."

"Baby, you handcuffed a man with it."

"And came home to you. Look at this nigga completing assignments."

Rosa laughed. Tank told us to get in the car before he had to hear anything else married people thought was private just because they said it softly.

We went to a little place beside the harbor with blue chairs and grilled fish and an owner who tried to give us the whole restaurant when he saw the badge.

I bought dinner instead.

Nigga, I had arrested enough property for one night.

Shawna sat between my knees on the low seawall afterward, holding a paper cup of wine while the city shone behind us. The rest of our people argued over dessert at a table a few feet away. Police cars waited at both ends of the street. Somewhere a musician was singing about a man who had arrived from the west and broken a king's dinner in half.

"That song about me?" I asked.

"Already," Shawna said.

"He left out the Ferrari."

"Give him time."

I kissed the side of her neck. She leaned back against me, warm and alive and laughing at something Tank had said.

Then she put her hand over mine.

"I saw how you looked when he aimed at me."

"Don't want you seeing it again."

"I wasn't scared of you."

I held her closer.

"I know."

"And I wasn't waiting for you to do everything."

"Baby, you put a whole dinner cart through that nigga's legs. Nobody confused you with furniture."

She laughed so hard her wine nearly went into the harbor.

The musician by the harbor stopped singing when he saw me listening.

Nigga, that was rude. He'd just reached a part where I sounded extremely good-looking.

“Keep going,” I told him.

“Captain, I didn't mean—”

“You meant the nigga in the song saved everybody. Keep meaning it.”

He laughed and found the chord again. Young man, twenty-nine, hands strong from work that didn't always come with an audience. He'd put my name into a melody that climbed too high in the second line and made him fight his own throat to come home.

I held up a hand.

“Try that part lower.”

He tried it. The phrase opened.

Shawna lifted her head.

“There,” she said.

The musician looked at his guitar as if the instrument had been keeping something from him all afternoon.

“Nigga,” he whispered.

“Exactly,” I told him. “Now sing your whole song.”

He did. Tank started keeping time on the table. Rosa took the harmony without being asked. Mercedes clapped once, then again, until the people outside the restaurant joined her.

By the last chorus, half the harbor was singing about a nigga sitting on a seawall with his wife between his knees.

I sang with them.

Shawna turned around laughing.

“You harmonizing with your own legend?”

“Nigga, I know the material.”

The musician finished red-faced and happy. I gave him enough money to stop worrying about his next several evenings, and he asked whether I wanted my name on the song.

“It already in there seven times,” I told him. “Keep yours on it too.”

He nodded so hard his guitar moved.

Then he started the song again from the beginning, because apparently some niggas understood a good night when it asked for an encore.

The cathedral invitation arrived before dessert.

A little black card, carried by a man with one cauliflower ear and two enormous friends. On the front was a painted fist. On the back, in silver ink, somebody had written:

THE CHAMPION ACCEPTS YOUR BADGE AS COLLATERAL.

I took out my pen.

Underneath, I wrote:

NIGGA, HE CAN HOLD MY COAT.

I handed it back.

Shawna read it over my wrist and kissed me before the messenger could leave.

By then the man had seen enough greatness for one shift. He bowed, actually bowed, and walked backward all the way to the street.

## 3. Eleven Years Undefeated Until I Took My Shirt Off

The cathedral was underground because the man who built it had been banned from building anything else above ground.

That was what the driver told us.

I believed him as soon as I saw the elevator.

It held six cars, had a chandelier, and played organ music while lowering us through the bones of Midnight City. Shawna sat beside me in a white dress with a slit that could have started its own criminal organization. Tank leaned against the back of the elevator with his arms folded while Rosa checked whether the chandelier had real diamonds in it.

"Leave it," he told her.

"I'm looking."

"You looking with your bag open."

Mercedes and Porsche had come in boxing jackets over their dresses. Mercedes's said TEAM AIDEN. Porsche's said HE ALREADY WON. They had commissioned them that morning from a tailor who apparently understood the speed of history.

When the elevator stopped, the noise hit us first.

Men shouting. Money changing hands. Music loud enough to move somebody's religion around. The cathedral's old columns rose around a boxing ring built where an altar might once have stood. Above it hung six giant screens. Below them, rows of velvet seats held rich people who wanted to see poor decisions happen close enough to feel expensive.

At the far end of the ring stood the champion.

Malik "Graveyard" Gault was thirty-three and looked like a building that had learned resentment. He wore black gloves, black trunks, and a chain thick enough to tow a boat. Somebody had tattooed a crown across his chest. Underneath it were eleven little marks.

Years undefeated.

Nigga, he was about to need a new tattoo arrangement.

He watched me cross the floor.

"That the nigga?"

His voice came through the speakers because somebody had put a microphone too close to a man who should've been left to breathe quietly.

"That the college nigga?"

I stopped beneath the ring lights.

"Valedictorian. Nigga, say it with the whole education."

The crowd laughed. Gault didn't.

"And the cop nigga?"

I touched the badge on my jacket.

"Captain. You checking the nigga's credentials before you lose?"

"And this nigga sings?"

Shawna answered before I could.

"When he feels like it. Usually when the nigga hears a song he can improve."

Gault leaned on the ropes.

"What this nigga when he bleeds?"

"Still better dressed than you," Rosa called. "You asked about four niggas and they all him. Which one you ready for?"

Tank turned his head away so Gault wouldn't see him laughing. It didn't work. Nigga, Tank's shoulders were shaking like a truck at a red light.

Vesper sat in a raised box beside an empty chair draped in black cloth. Zero's chair. He wasn't there, but he had arranged for his absence to take up more room than everybody else's body.

Rusk stood beneath the box with a scroll.

"The captain enters three trials. Strength, courage, dominion."

"Y'all named three fights like they got degrees."

"The first is fought against the chosen hand of the cathedral. The second against the reigning champion. The third is determined by the queen."

"And if I win?"

Vesper leaned forward.

"A seat among the criminal kings."

"I sat in the throne yesterday."

"Yes. We noticed."

A thin older man approached carrying my gloves. He had silver hair cut close, a straight back, and a scar that crossed one cheek without disturbing the calm around his eyes. His suit was dark blue. Unlike everybody else in the room, he wasn't trying to look dangerous.

That made me watch him more carefully.

"Bastien Moreau," he said. "I will keep the rounds."

"You a referee?"

"Tonight."

"What were you yesterday?"

"Tired."

I liked the nigga immediately.

He checked my gloves while Shawna helped me take off my jacket. A few women near the ring started calling out before the shirt even cleared my shoulders.

"Wait till he breathes," Porsche told them. "You haven't seen the shoulders move yet."

Shawna looked around the crowd with a little smile, then put her palm on my chest where everybody could see whose hand belonged there.

The shouting doubled.

I bent and kissed her.

"How fast you want this done?"

"Fast enough that my dinner's still hot. Slow enough they learn your name."

"Baby, that's a narrow window."

"You're gifted."

My first opponent wore a red mask and had two attendants carrying a hammer behind him. I looked at the hammer. I looked at Bastien.

"That's allowed?"

"It is ceremonial."

"Good, because my ceremony didn't bring one."

The red mask came over the ropes, bounced on his feet, and hit his own chest three times. He was fast. I saw that before he moved toward me. Some fighters advertise power because they want you looking somewhere other than their feet. This one hardly seemed to touch the floor.

The bell rang.

He threw three punches before I had finished deciding where his face probably was under the mask. One caught my shoulder. One brushed my ear. The third met my glove hard enough to wake my arm all the way up.

The crowd roared.

"Aight," I said. "Nigga got somewhere to be."

He came again. I moved this time. His fist passed my face, his momentum carried him across me, and I caught him with a short punch that made the red mask twist sideways.

He stopped to fix it.

I stopped too.

"You good?"

He blinked through the wrong eyehole.

"Wait."

I waited.

The whole cathedral booed.

"Shut up," I told them. "The nigga can't see. You paid for a fight or a traffic accident?"

He straightened the mask and nodded. We started again. He was still quick, but now every time he moved the mask slid a little, and the fight turned into him trying to hit me before his costume betrayed him.

I caught him with a clean shot to the body. He folded. I stepped back and let Bastien count.

At eight, the man lifted one glove.

"I am finished."

"What's your name?" I asked.

"Adrian."

"Adrian, my nigga, next time just come as Adrian."

I helped him stand. The crowd went from booing to cheering so fast I wondered how any of these people stayed married.

Adrian stopped beside the ropes before they took him away.

He had the red mask in his hands now. His own face looked ordinary beneath it: a grown man with a swelling lip, embarrassed eyes, and enough courage left to come back toward the nigga who had just put him down.

“Could you sign it?” he asked.

I looked at the mask.

“Nigga, you keeping the thing?”

“My brother told me not to fight you.”

“Smart brother.”

“I said nobody was that good.”

I took the pen a man held out from ringside.

“Nigga, what your brother's name?”

“Luis.”

I wrote on the inside of the mask: LUIS WAS RIGHT.

Then I signed it.

The women behind our corner started laughing. Tank leaned over to read it, and the big nigga nearly dropped the water bottle in his hand.

Adrian looked at the words. Looked up at me.

Then he laughed too.

“He's never letting me forget this.”

“Nigga, that's why I made it permanent.”

I put one hand on his shoulder.

“You were fast. Get rid of the mask. Let people see the man doing the work.”

His smile changed. The joke stayed, but something steadier stood up beneath it.

“Thank you.”

He went through the ropes holding the mask against his chest. The crowd reached toward him now, asking to see what I'd written, calling his name alongside mine.

Porsche watched him go.

“This nigga came in to lose a fight and left a collector's item.”

Mercedes nodded.

“Baby, being near Aiden is a financial event.”

I looked at Shawna.

“My wife gets the best returns.”

She put one finger over my mouth before I could explain those in public, then steered me back to our corner and handed me the water.

"One."

"You keeping count?"

"I'm keeping dinner warm in my imagination."

Before Gault entered, a man in a silver jacket climbed up beside our corner carrying a little board covered in betting prices.

"Captain, the odds have changed."

"Nigga, I ain't changed. Catch the board up."

He swallowed.

"You are still the underdog."

Tank leaned over him.

"That nigga what?"

"The underdog. In the market."

"Nigga, look up. Does that look underneath anything?"

The man stared at my shoulders, then at the people behind him calling my name, then at Shawna wiping my face as if the whole room had been built to make sure she could reach me without standing on tiptoe.

"The champion has eleven years—"

"Nigga, we heard the years," I said. "We about to discuss the minutes."

Mercedes came over with Porsche and a stack of betting slips thick enough to make the man reconsider which direction he wanted to leave.

"We backed Aiden," Mercedes said.

"Obviously," Porsche added.

The silver jacket looked alarmed.

"How much?"

Porsche told him.

Nigga, his face lost money just hearing it.

"That is a significant exposure."

"Then cover your ass with a better opinion," she said.

He hurried away.

Shawna looked at the slips.

"You two bet that much?"

"On him?" Mercedes pointed at me like the answer should've been visible from space. "Baby, this nigga lands cars where helicopters get nervous. I know a sound investment when it takes its shirt off."

Porsche handed Shawna one slip.

"That's yours."

My wife looked at it, then at me.

"Now you really need to win."

"Nigga, I was planning to lose economically till you said that."

She laughed and kissed my shoulder.

Tank leaned close while the women returned to their seats.

"Listen. This nigga ain't one of those pretty fighters who gets surprised by a punch. He good."

"I know."

"I seen him move."

"So have I."

"Then don't be so busy being the greatest nigga in the building that you forget this nigga brought both hands."

I looked at my brother.

He wasn't doubting me. A nigga who loves you will sometimes be the only person in the room willing to remind you that your blood still works like everybody else's.

I touched his fist with mine.

"I got you."

"You better have you. I already got me."

"Nigga, that's a beautiful sentence from a man who lost his room key twice today."

"The hotel got too many doors."

We laughed together, and the laugh loosened something under my ribs the crowd couldn't reach.

Then Gault entered without music.

The cathedral supplied its own.

Eleven years made a heavy noise. Men stood when he passed. Women touched the edge of his robe. His attendants put the chain around a wooden stand shaped like a kneeling man.

I looked at the stand.

"Nigga, your necklace got staff?"

He hit me before the crowd finished laughing.

The punch landed on my gloves and still sent me backward. My heels touched the ropes. Something sharp went through my ribs, a little hello from all the trouble my body had survived before this city thought it was special.

Gault came after me.

He was not slow. That was the dangerous part. Big men sometimes give you enough time to admire how much they're bringing toward you. This nigga brought it right now.

I ducked one punch and took another against my side. The cathedral tilted. I heard Tank shout, but the words were lost beneath the blood rushing through my ears.

Gault leaned close.

"Stay down when I put you there."

"Nigga, you talking about next week? Do something."

He did.

He caught me hard enough that one knee touched the canvas.

The crowd exploded.

Shawna didn't move. Her eyes stayed on mine. There was no panic in them, and somehow that hurt more than panic would've. She believed I knew what I was doing. I had to become the man she was looking at before the next bell.

I stood.

Gault smiled for the first time.

"Good."

I wiped my mouth with the back of my glove.

"Nigga, you enjoy that?"

"Very much."

"Remember it pretty. Tell your niggas you had me kneeling."

"I'll tell them I had you down."

"Then explain how this nigga got back up while you were practicing the story."

I changed the fight.

Stopped giving him the straight line. Made him turn. Let his shoulders tell me what his gloves were lying about. When he swung at my head, I wasn't there. When he turned to find me, I put a fist where his breath had been.

The first good shot made his smile disappear.

The second made the crowd quiet.

The third made Tank climb halfway onto the ring apron before Rosa pulled him down by the back of his jacket.

"Nigga, let the man work!"

"That's my nigga! I'm supporting!"

"Then support him from the floor. Nigga, you shaking his whole workplace!"

Gault drove me into the corner. I could feel blue thunder waiting under my skin, eager and expensive. I left it there. Nigga, I wasn't about to need lightning to convince this man my name was Aiden.

He swung wide.

I stepped inside, hit him twice, and moved before the answer came. His glove struck the corner post. The sound made everybody flinch except him.

Then he came at me angry.

That was when he became easier.

Not easy. A storm ain't easy because you can see it. But anger told me where his next foot would land before he knew himself. I gave him room he couldn't use and took back room he thought he owned.

By the next bell, he was breathing through his mouth.

Bastien came between us.

"Corners."

Gault didn't turn. He stared at me, chest rising hard.

"You really don't have to do this," he said quietly.

"Nigga, I'm in the ring. We past invitations."

"If you win, he doubles what I owe."

For the first time, I looked beyond the fighter and saw the chain stand, the attendants, the empty black chair.

Eleven years undefeated.

Eleven years belonging to somebody.

I went to my corner.

Shawna put the towel against my face.

"What?"

"Zero owns his debt."

"Can you buy it?"

"Probably."

"Then win first. Men like that hear money better when you're standing."

She kissed the clean side of my mouth.

Nigga, how was I supposed to lose after that?

The last round started with Gault trying to take my whole head home.

I let him come. Took what I had to take. Gave more back. The ring shook beneath us. The crowd stood. Somebody threw a glass and Bastien caught it against his jacket without taking his eyes off the fight.

Gault hit the ropes. I hit him once more, clean and final, and the big man sat down like a building had decided it needed a minute.

He tried to stand at five.

Tried again at seven.

At nine, I held out my hand.

He looked at it.

The bell rang.

I kept the hand there.

He took it.

Nigga, the sound that room made could have raised the cathedral to street level.

Shawna came through the ropes and nearly took me off my feet herself. Tank shouted my name like he was mad at anybody who hadn't heard it. Mercedes kissed Porsche on the cheek, then told her not to get ideas because she'd just been overwhelmed by athletic excellence.

Vesper rose in her box.

"The second trial is complete."

I held Gault's wrist up with mine.

"The nigga's debt is complete too."

She went still.

"That is not a prize you were offered."

"Then offer it now."

A murmur passed through the kings. They didn't mind money changing hands. They minded somebody deciding which hands mattered.

Vesper looked toward the empty chair.

"The emperor will expect compensation."

"Tell him send an invoice with a picture of his face on it so I know who to disappoint."

Gault let out one shocked laugh.

I looked at him.

"Nigga, you free enough to laugh yet?"

"I think so."

"Then make it a good one. That's your laugh now."

He tried again. Louder. The nigga looked surprised by how far his own voice could travel without waiting for somebody to allow it.

"That nigga laughing," Tank shouted.

"I hear him," Rosa said.

"Nah. Nigga laughing like he got tomorrow."

Gault heard that too. He looked at my brother and nodded, once, hard.

Vesper descended from her box and approached the ring. Bastien held the ropes for her. She didn't climb in. She stood outside with one hand resting on the top rope, looking at me like the evening had stopped following the order she'd memorized.

"The final trial," she said, "is dominion. You may claim the cathedral's purse. Ten million. Or you may purchase the champion's freedom."

The room quieted again.

Gault's hand fell from mine.

"Take the purse," he said.

"Nigga, be quiet."

"I mean it."

"I know. That's why I told you to be quiet."

I looked at Vesper.

"Buy his freedom."

She studied me for a long second.

"Done."

The cheering started, but I lifted my glove.

"And I'm charging the cathedral eleven million for the fight."

Shawna bent forward laughing.

Rusk actually grabbed the rail.

"You cannot simply—"

"Nigga, I just sold out your dead church, beat your champion, saved your parade yesterday, and brought my own beautiful wife. You think all that come free?"

Vesper put her hand over her mouth.

It didn't hide the smile.

The old silver-bearded king from dinner stood in the front row.

"I'll pay it."

Everybody turned.

His wife pulled him back down by his sleeve.

"We'll pay it," she corrected.

That was how I won three trials, freed a champion, and left a million ahead of where the queen had meant to put me.

Gault met us outside the changing room afterward. Without the chain, he looked younger. Not smaller. Just less arranged for somebody else's benefit.

"If you need me," he said, "send word."

"I need you to go eat something you chose yourself."

He nodded, then looked at Shawna.

"Your husband is—"

"I know," she said.

Gault smiled.

"No. Nigga, I don't think anybody knows all of it yet. I came in here thinking I knew the man I'd have to fight."

"And?" she asked.

"The nigga I needed to meet showed up afterward. Same face. Same hands. Different damn use for them."

She looked at me, proud enough to make my battered ribs sit up straighter.

Nigga, I liked him better every minute.

Bastien waited by the elevator. While everybody else celebrated, he slipped a folded card into my hand.

No crown on it. No fancy threat.

Just an address and a time.

"There is a woman who ought to have seen that fight," he said.

"Who?"

He looked toward Vesper, standing alone beneath the cathedral's last chandelier.

"Someone she was before this city taught her what love cost."

Before I could ask another question, the elevator doors opened. Shawna took my arm. The whole room applauded as we went up.

I looked at the card again once the city lights returned.

A garden. Tomorrow afternoon.

Underneath the address, in a different hand, somebody had written:

COME WITHOUT A CROWN.

"You going?" Shawna asked.

I folded it and put it beside my badge.

"Depends if they got dinner."

"Baby."

"Nigga freed a champion. Let him investigate a menu."

"You already decided."

I smiled at her.

"Yeah. We going. But your nigga taking snacks. I can't keep changing these people's lives on ceremonial portions."

## 4. My Wife Wore Blue to a Black-Tie War

The garden had six fountains and no water.

"That's depressing," Shawna said.

"Rich niggas always find a way to own something thirsty."

"Nigga, I didn't ask if simple had a room. I asked where your people sleeping tonight."

Vesper looked at me, then past me toward the palace.

"He will count them."

"Then let the nigga learn subtraction while we find them beds."

Shawna took the opera key out of my hand and put it into Vesper's.

"A whole building just became mine. Use the dressing rooms. Use the stage. Use every stupid little balcony. Get them there."

Vesper stared at the key.

"You have not even seen it."

"Then make sure somebody waters the flowers before I do."

Nigga, my wife handed a queen a way out and still made it sound like she was arranging opening night.

Vesper closed her fingers around the key.

"Thank you."

I looked back at the shining palace.

Zero stood in an upper window, a dark shape surrounded by reflections of himself.

I kissed Shawna on the bridge where he could see.

Not for him.

Because the nigga had given me an excellent reason to remember who kept winning.

## 8. The King Behind the Wall

By noon the opera house belonged to Shawna, contained forty-seven people Vesper wanted alive, and had sold out a concert my wife hadn't agreed to give yet.

"Nigga, how you sell tickets before the singer says yes?" I asked the manager.

The manager, a round man named Paolo, held his hat against his chest.

"We heard she was coming."

"Nigga, she came to look at curtains."

"The city would pay to see that also."

I looked at Shawna, then back at him.

"This nigga got taste and no manners. Baby, come decide what part we keeping."

Paolo nodded so eagerly I almost liked him before my wife had approved the performance.

Shawna looked up from the stage.

"What time?"

"Eight."

"Get me a piano."

Nigga, that was the entire negotiation.

Rosa stood beside her in rehearsal clothes, looking out over the velvet seats.

"I want the second song."

"You can have three."

"I knew this marriage would eventually benefit me."

Tank sat in the front row with his feet spread so far nobody else could use it. He watched Rosa cross the stage like the theater had been built to solve his particular eyesight.

"That nigga in love," I told Shawna.

"That nigga has been in love. He just finally stopped looking surprised."

Vesper's people occupied the dressing rooms upstairs. Assistants. Drivers. Two singers. An old cook who had brought her own knives because she didn't trust palace kitchens she couldn't insult personally. No prisoners in chains. People who had served one house so long that leaving it felt like stepping out of their own names.

Vesper moved among them, checking rooms, carrying bags, doing little practical things with a seriousness nobody had ever applauded her for.

I let her work.

Paolo caught me before I reached the door.

“Captain. One small difficulty.”

“Nigga, don't introduce a large problem wearing a little hat. What is it?”

He pointed up.

The stage curtain had caught halfway open. A man on the high walkway was pulling at a rope with both hands while another shouted that he was making it worse. Below them, Shawna stood with her arms folded, waiting for the theater to stop taking rehearsal personally.

“Has it always done that?” I asked.

“Only for important performances.”

“Nigga, it better retire that habit today.”

I went up the side stairs. At the top, the two stagehands stared at my badge, then at my face.

“Captain, you don't have to—”

One leaned toward the other.

“That nigga fixes curtains too?”

“Nigga, that man's wife downstairs.”

“This nigga can hear you,” I told them. “And that part about my wife is why the curtain losing today. Show me what's caught.”

The first stagehand pointed with both hands. The other took the loose rope and got ready to help instead of spending the whole rehearsal admiring the volunteer.

The curtain's heavy edge had twisted around its guide. I got both hands on it, felt the stubborn weight, and pulled until the trapped fabric came loose with a long velvet sigh.

The curtain opened.

Below us, the whole stage filled with light.

Shawna looked up at me from the piano.

“You showing off up there?”

“Nigga fixing your sky.”

She laughed and played the first chord. Her voice came up through the theater, clear and warm, and the two stagehands forgot to look impressed with me because my wife had begun doing her part.

I let them.

When I came downstairs, Paolo shook my hand as if we'd concluded a matter of enormous artistic importance.

“Perfect,” he said.

I looked at Shawna singing beneath the open curtain.

“Nigga, I know.”

Bastien met me at the stage door with Marcellus. Both wore dark suits. Both looked like they had decided to attend a funeral and were willing to arrange one if necessary.

"Nigga, y'all dress like the portrait owes you money."

"In a sense," Marcellus said.

Shawna came down the steps and straightened my collar.

"Bring back the book."

"That's all?"

"And yourself. The book can't come to my show alone."

I kissed her.

"Front row."

"Better be."

The House of Nine Doors looked worse in daylight.

Nigga, I had done quality work. One balcony still had tire marks across the marble. The gallery doors were boarded. Somebody had placed flowers near the broken orchestra platform, and I couldn't tell whether they were mourning the musicians' equipment or the baron's dignity.

Police guarded the front entrance.

Black Mantle soldiers guarded the back.

Zero's men. Different uniforms, same rich-man posture: shoulders wide because somebody else had promised to pay for the consequences.

Ardent stood between both groups looking like his afternoon had become legally confusing.

He brightened when he saw me.

"Captain."

"Nigga, who invited the funeral jackets?"

"The emperor claims an interest in the property."

"I claimed it first. With evidence. And a Ferrari."

A Black Mantle officer approached. Tall. Blond. A little scar beside his mouth. He introduced himself as Colonel Severin Drake, forty-six, commander of Zero's household forces, like any of that was supposed to make me stop standing where I was.

"The house is closed."

I showed him my badge.

"Open."

"Your foreign rank has no authority here."

Ardent straightened.

"The captain acts with my full cooperation."

Drake looked at him.

"You should be more careful with that."

"Nigga, you should be more careful with your tone," I said. "This man been nothing but handsome and helpful."

Ardent's younger officers tried not to smile.

Drake stepped closer.

"You enjoy making people love you."

"Nigga, you make that sound difficult. Look at this man." I nodded toward Ardent. "Useful. Respectful. Helped my wife get home. Nigga, I liked him in twelve seconds. You been here longer and somehow getting worse."

One of Ardent's officers coughed into his fist.

Drake looked at him.

"That nigga ain't your problem," I said. "You threatening the wrong part of the conversation."

Bastien walked past us toward the front door.

Drake moved to stop him.

I caught Drake's wrist.

Not hard enough to injure him. Hard enough for his face to understand the rest of the sentence.

"Let the old nigga walk."

For a moment all the guns around us seemed to remember what they were for.

Then Drake stepped back.

"You have fifteen minutes."

"Nigga, I was gonna take twenty. You just made it thirty."

Inside, the house smelled of smoke, polish, and rich people pretending damage wasn't permanent if they could afford repairs. Bastien led us through a long corridor into a portrait room I hadn't reached during the car chase.

King Adrien Valcour hung above a fireplace in a gold frame.

He wore the Crown of Midnight.

For the first time, I got a good look at the thing we had crossed a sea to take from Zero.

Black diamonds. White gold. Seven rising points. A blue stone at the front dark enough to look like midnight had learned how to shine.

"That's a crown," I said.

Marcellus smiled.

"Yes."

"Nigga, I was expecting a crown, but that's a crown. Why nobody tell me the nigga had that much hat?"

"We've discussed it several times."

"Words, my nigga. You used words. Should've held your hands this far apart and looked worried about my neck."

Bastien stood beneath the portrait.

"His nose was wrong."

Eliane's letter came back to me.

The old royal face.

The face whose nose was wrong.

Marcellus studied the frame. Bastien looked at the fireplace. I looked at both men looking until I decided we were giving the wall too much time to feel important.

"Can I take it down?"

"Carefully," Marcellus said.

Nigga, I took it down carefully enough that only one corner of the wall objected.

Behind the portrait was a shallow bronze door.

No glitter. No dramatic ticking. Just a family crest worn smooth where somebody's hand had once touched it often.

Bastien took a key from inside his coat.

I looked at him.

"You had that the whole time?"

"I knew where it belonged. I was afraid to leave her alone with what opening it would bring."

"Nigga, she got company now."

The key turned.

Inside sat a small black book, a red ribbon, and a child's drawing old enough that the paper had browned. A little boy had drawn a ship and a man with a crown too large for his head.

Nico's name was written at the bottom.

Bastien touched the edge of the paper with one finger.

Marcellus lifted the book.

Names. Payments. Promises. Not magic. Worse, in a way: men writing down what they would do to other men if enough money reached the right table.

Eliane's stitched marks matched the page corners.

Marcellus found Rafael Sorel's name three times before I heard footsteps in the corridor.

"Time," I said.

Drake stood in the doorway with six men.

"You have recovered imperial property."

"Nigga, you timed that beautifully."

"Give it to me."

Bastien closed the bronze door. Marcellus slipped the book inside his jacket.

I picked up the portrait.

"You want your old king back?"

Drake raised his gun.

I threw King Adrien Valcour at him.

Nigga, the king finally did something useful after sixteen years on the wall.

The frame struck Drake across the shoulder. His shot went into the ceiling. I drove forward before his men could get a clear line, hit the nearest one hard enough to put him into a cabinet, and heard Bastien's pistol answer from beside the fireplace.

Marcellus ducked behind a table. Ardent's officers shouted from the front hall. The house filled with the same noise it had made two nights earlier, and I started to understand why rich people kept extra mansions.

Drake pulled free of the broken frame and came at me with a knife.

He was good.

Not Gault strong. Not red-mask fast. Precise. Every movement meant something ugly and arrived without introduction.

He cut my sleeve. I hit his jaw. He drove me against the fireplace. I caught his wrist before the knife found anything I couldn't replace with a tailor.

"You think they love you?" he hissed.

"Nigga, you asking while we wrestle? Pick a mood."

I slammed him into the mantel.

The knife fell.

Bastien fired over my shoulder at a man rushing the doorway. I shoved Drake into the broken portrait and went after Marcellus.

The front hall was blocked.

The gallery was burning again.

Nigga, that house had an unfortunate relationship with my visits.

"Back way," Bastien said.

"That's where his men are."

"Not all the way back. The ninth door."

I stared at him.

"The wall door?"

He nodded.

Tank's earlier threat about taking the whole wall came to mind, and I decided my brother deserved a chance to be right at a distance.

We ran upstairs.

The little blue door opened onto nothing.

Nothing, in this case, meant twenty feet of air above the harbor, a narrow stone ledge, and a maintenance boat tied below.

Marcellus looked down.

"I am sixty-two."

"Nigga, you wearing it beautifully. Come here."

I helped him through onto the ledge. Bastien followed. Gunfire struck the door behind us. I kicked it shut, looked at the boat, and called down to its startled driver.

"Move left!"

"What?"

"Nigga, your other left!"

He moved.

We jumped.

The boat rocked so hard the harbor tried to get in with us. Marcellus landed against a coil of rope. Bastien hit the bench and rolled. I came down last, caught the boat's canopy, and tore half of it loose before my feet found the deck.

The driver stared.

"Captain Aiden?"

"Drive, my nigga. Autographs when the roof stops shooting."

The boat shot away from the house as bullets struck the water behind us.

Ardent's patrol launch cut across the harbor to meet us. His officers opened the way. Above, on the balcony I'd driven through before, Drake stood with blood at his mouth and the king's torn portrait tangled around one boot.

I waved.

Nigga, manners matter when you leave somebody's house.

We reached the opera house at seven forty-three.

Shawna was furious.

Not because I'd been late.

Because I'd been early enough for her to see the sleeve.

"You bleeding?"

"Mostly architecture."

"Aiden."

"Nigga fought a portrait. Portrait lost. Little cut. Nothing interesting."

"Then let your wife be interested before you start explaining the nigga in the frame."

I held out my arm. Marcellus quietly held up the book behind me.

"At least one of you came back with all his corners," she said.

"Nigga, I came back with the useful ones."

She checked it herself, cleaned it, and put a fresh shirt in my hands.

"You have twelve minutes."

"To explain?"

"To sit down and look like my husband."

"Nigga, that's my strongest subject."

"Then get an A and keep your feet out of the aisle."

I did it in nine.

Nigga, greatness respects showtime.

The opera house filled until people stood along the walls. Vesper's household sat together in one balcony. Ardent and his officers occupied another, their uniforms bright beneath the chandeliers. Gault arrived in a dark suit with no chain. When he saw me, he raised a hand and the whole row around him stood to make room.

Tank sat beside me with Rosa's engagement ring box in his pocket, though she'd already said yes on the island. He liked carrying it. Said it reminded him he had managed one proposal without anybody shooting.

The lights went down.

Shawna walked onto the stage.

No crown. No gun. No car.

Just my wife, a piano, and a room that had no idea how quiet she could make it.

She sang the first song without looking for me.

That was what I loved about it. She wasn't borrowing courage from my face. She had brought her own.

By the second verse, a woman behind me was crying. By the final note, even the waiters had stopped moving. The applause came a second late because everybody needed a moment to remember they had hands.

Nigga, I stood first.

I stood so fast Tank stood with me on instinct, and the rest of the room followed like my wife's voice had pulled the city onto its feet.

She found me then.

Smiled.

Everything I'd stolen, won, survived, or driven through felt small enough to put in a pocket beside that look.

Rosa took the stage for the second song. Tank forgot to sit down. She pointed at him once during the chorus, and the nigga actually touched his own chest like he needed confirmation she meant him.

Mercedes leaned over from the next row.

"That's your woman, nigga. Stop looking adopted by surprise."

He laughed, embarrassed and proud, and I put an arm around his shoulders while the whole theater cheered her name.

"That's your nigga singing," I told him.

"I know."

"Then let the woman see you know."

Tank raised both hands over his head and shouted her name so loudly Rosa almost lost the next line laughing.

"There that nigga go," Mercedes said.

"Whole damn balcony heard his relationship," Porsche answered.

Nigga, so did Rosa. She found her note again and sent it straight back to him.

Upstairs afterward, Marcellus laid the recovered ledger beside Eliane's coat.

Vesper read in silence.

Payments to the harbor commander.

Orders transferring the old king's properties before the fire.

Names of men who had sworn loyalty at a funeral for people they'd helped kill.

Then she reached the last page and stopped.

"What?" I asked.

She turned the book toward me.

A newer sheet had been tucked inside the old binding. Bell must have added it after acquiring the house. A record of the coming tournament's final wager.

The Crown was to be awarded publicly.

Then the winner would be invited aboard Zero's armored yacht for a private coronation.

No winner was meant to return.

Tank stared at the page.

"Nigga, they got a whole funeral scheduled after your trophy."

I looked at Shawna standing in the doorway, still wearing the dress the city had just applauded.

Then I looked at Vesper.

"Aight," I said. "We gonna need a bigger after-party."

## 9. I Bought the Auctioneer's Car While He Was Still in It

The kings held their auction in an old train station because apparently every building in Midnight City had retired from one job and started committing a different crime.

Nigga, I respected ambition.

The platforms were covered in red carpet. Cars stood where trains used to stop. A jazz band played on a locomotive with gold wheels. Above everything hung the Crown of Midnight inside a glass case, turning slowly under a light bright enough to make half the men beneath it reconsider their marriages.

I looked once.

Shawna noticed.

"You want it?"

"On you first."

"It's a king's crown."

"Nigga, it's jewelry until somebody starts arguing."

She smiled and hooked her arm through mine.

We were here because Zero required every finalist to pledge something at the auction. Not buy. Pledge. A house, a fleet, a loyalty. Something the council could seize if a claimant withdrew.

I had no intention of withdrawing.

I also had no intention of letting a nigga touch my island because he liked the sound of paperwork.

Rusk approached carrying a silver tray.

"Your pledge, Captain."

I put a car key on it.

"The Ferrari Vesper gave me."

He looked down.

"That vehicle was destroyed."

"Nigga, parts of it still excellent."

"A pledge must have enduring value."

Shawna put a photograph beside the key. Me landing the car on the harbor dock.

"Its history," she said.

Rusk studied the picture.

Around us, other claimants leaned closer. Somebody asked whether the broken Ferrari would be displayed. Somebody else offered two million before the auction had even begun.

Nigga, I had turned a repair bill into a collectible.

"Three million," said a man in a velvet coat.

"It isn't for sale," Rusk told him.

"Everything is for sale."

I took the key back.

"Four million and a running car."

The man looked at me.

Then at the photograph.

Then he handed me the keys to a green Ferrari parked behind him.

"Done."

Tank put both hands on the back of his head.

"Nigga sold a wreck and got a better Ferrari."

"A nigga invests," I told him.

Mercedes turned to Porsche.

"We need to crash something famous."

"You already dated a musician. Let the rest of us learn."

The man in velvet was Lord Corvin Ash, a collector of cars, swords, paintings, and women who eventually escaped with at least one of the first three. His current companion, a twenty-nine-year-old sculptor named Dalia, shook Shawna's hand and asked whether the opera house had room for a marble bust of a woman looking disappointed.

"Of him?" Shawna asked.

Dalia smiled.

"Beside him."

Corvin pretended not to hear.

The auction began with a yacht, continued through three islands none of which were mine, and reached a red sports car with a silver roof. Its owner, Prince Laurent, stood beside it looking miserable.

Mercedes went still.

"That's the nigga from the hotel."

"The bouquets?" I asked.

"The bouquets, the apologies, the terrible choices. Yes."

Laurent saw us and looked away.

That interested me more than the car.

The auctioneer was a fast-talking man named Enzo Quill. He wore a green suit, white gloves, and an expression that had never met a price it considered final.

"The prince offers his family's last racing car," he announced. "A magnificent pledge of confidence."

Porsche leaned toward Laurent as we approached.

"Your last car?"

"It is a complicated family matter."

"Nigga, that's how men introduce simple lies," Mercedes said. "What happened?"

He glanced at Enzo.

"My father lost a wager. Enzo purchased the debt. If I don't place in the tournament, he takes the house."

"And if you do?"

"He takes the prize."

I looked at the auctioneer.

"That nigga good at math."

Laurent's jaw tightened.

"I am not asking for help."

"Nigga, you think I waited for your permission to notice you miserable?"

"A prince has obligations."

"So does a nigga with friends. We both standing here. Which one got the better offer?"

Laurent looked at Mercedes. She folded her arms.

"Don't start borrowing dignity to pay that nigga interest," she told him. "Tell Aiden the truth."

Enzo came over smiling.

"Captain. Shall I put you down for a bid?"

"Depends. How much for the prince?"

His smile sharpened.

"His obligations are not among today's lots."

"Nigga, everything was for sale five minutes ago. This city changes customs fast."

People gathered around us. Enzo knew an audience was money. He also knew Corvin had just paid me four million for a wreck with my photograph beside it.

He named a number.

A stupid number.

Tank laughed in his face.

"Nigga, for that we can get a whole other prince and raise his self-esteem from scratch."

Laurent looked offended, then couldn't keep the smile off his face.

I pointed at the old station clock.

"One race. You and me. Around the harbor road and back through the north gate. I win, you release the debt. You win, you get the four million from my Ferrari."

Enzo's eyes flicked toward Zero, watching from the upper platform.

Then he nodded.

"And your new car."

"Nigga, you haven't even seen me drive the old one."

The station changed in five minutes. Music stopped. Doors opened. Men who had been pretending to appreciate antiques suddenly remembered they loved engines. Ardent's police cleared the route because he claimed it would prevent disorder, which was a beautiful way of describing what he was about to encourage.

Shawna kissed me through the window of the green Ferrari.

"No hood riding."

"I'm inside this time."

"Stay ambitious about that."

Laurent climbed into the passenger seat before I could ask.

"I know the road."

"Nigga, you know how to hold on?"

"I intend to learn."

Mercedes leaned into his window.

"When this nigga turns, you breathe. When this nigga flies, you shut your mouth so your prayer stays inside."

Laurent stared at her.

"Flies?"

"Nigga, you entered the passenger seat of a solution. Stop interviewing the weather."

Enzo drove a black racing coupe with his name painted on the side. Nigga had branded the evidence of his own bad judgment.

The flag fell.

We left the station side by side, engines tearing the afternoon open. Enzo took the first turn tight, forcing me wide toward a row of empty stalls. I clipped a basket of oranges. Fruit bounced behind us. Laurent ducked one that came through the window and caught another against his chest.

"Keep it," I said. "Nigga needs a race souvenir."

"I need my heart back where it was!"

"That nigga can sit with the orange. Where we going?"

"Left at the fountain!"

I went left.

Enzo went right.

Laurent stared.

"That's the longer road."

"Nigga got a shorter conscience. Watch."

A delivery truck rolled into our route.

I saw it early enough to choose between crashing and doing something the Ferrari's designers had probably discussed while drunk and then crossed out.

I chose the second thing.

We hit a low loading ramp, jumped the corner of the truck bed, and landed on the road beyond with a noise like a rich man's savings account cracking in half.

Laurent made a prayer out of several languages.

"Nigga, pick one god and give him directions!"

Enzo reappeared ahead of us at the next intersection. Smiling. He thought the truck had solved his problem.

I pulled alongside.

His smile left first.

Then his mirror.

The cars touched. Metal scraped. We raced beneath the old city gate with inches between our doors and the stone. Above us, people leaned from windows shouting my name. One woman threw a scarf. It wrapped around Enzo's aerial like his car had decided to surrender fashionably.

The final stretch ran through the station's covered loading yard.

Enzo cut in front of me and braked.

Laurent shouted.

I turned into the empty lane beside him, rode the Ferrari along the raised platform edge, and came down ahead just before the finish line.

The station erupted.

I stopped under the Crown's glass case.

Enzo kept going into a stack of velvet display chairs.

Nigga, he had finally bought himself a seat.

Laurent climbed out shaking. Mercedes and Porsche reached him first. One checked his face. The other took the orange from his hand and peeled it while he tried to explain what had happened to his understanding of speed.

Enzo staggered from his car.

"The race was irregular!"

I leaned against the Ferrari.

"Nigga, your truck missed. That's irregular for you."

"You left the road!"

"Your nigga put a truck on it. I went where the nigga wasn't. That's racing with good judgment."

Laurent lifted his orange.

"He caught this too," he announced, then looked down. "No. I caught it. He made me keep it."

"See?" I said. "Nigga already remembering his own achievements. Sign his paper."

The crowd laughed.

Zero did not intervene.

That was how I knew the auctioneer wasn't worth enough to save.

Enzo signed the release on the hood of his own wreck. Laurent took it with both hands. For a moment the prince looked younger than twenty-seven, stripped of all the little performances he'd been using to keep nobody from noticing he was frightened.

"I don't know how to repay you," he said.

"Start by choosing a bouquet for one woman at a time."

"That is all?"

"Nigga, you tried it yet? Let's not call the assignment easy before you pass."

Mercedes took the release from him long enough to check the signature, then put it back against his chest.

"That's yours, baby. This nigga gave you back a future. Quit holding it like somebody else's receipt."

Mercedes and Porsche laughed.

Enzo tried to leave in a second car.

I stopped him.

"Where you going with that?"

"It is mine."

"You owe me a running car."

"That was not part of our wager."

"Nigga, I bought it while you was crashing. Ask the auctioneer."

His mouth opened.

Behind him, the entire station began chanting my name.

He handed over the keys.

Sometimes a man signs because of a contract. Sometimes he signs because a thousand people have just realized what kind of nigga he is, and the door has become more valuable than the car.

Corvin stopped me before I reached the new car.

“Captain. A small favor.”

“Nigga, I just sold you a wreck. You want it damaged in a particular direction?”

He held out a silver pen.

“A signature. On the photograph. For the collection.”

Dalia stood beside him with her arms folded, enjoying every second of a man who usually bought other people's attention trying to ask for mine without showing how badly he wanted it.

I took the pen.

“Where?”

“Anywhere you like.”

“Nigga, that's how the Ferrari ended up there in the first place.”

Porsche laughed. Mercedes took the photograph from him and held it flat while I signed.

Then she looked at the picture properly.

“Aiden, baby. Your face.”

“What about it?”

“You look offended by the landing.”

“Landing came up fast. Nigga prefers an introduction.”

Corvin leaned closer, delighted.

“May I quote that beside the display?”

I looked at him.

Dalia started laughing before I answered.

“Nigga, put the whole conversation up if the wall's big enough.”

He took the photograph back with both hands. I had seen men carry diamonds with less care. Beside him, Laurent looked from the picture to the debt release in his own hands and began smiling again.

“What?” Mercedes asked him.

The prince shook his head.

“Nothing. I came here terrified of losing my family's car.”

“And?”

He looked at me.

“This man sold one that no longer worked and bought me back with another.”

“Nigga,” Porsche said, “you finally watching the right part of the evening.”

Laurent nodded. Folded the release carefully. Put it inside his jacket over his heart.

I watched him do it and let myself enjoy something I couldn't have parked in any garage.

That night, House Laurent pledged itself to my claim.

The prince arrived at the opera house with one bouquet.

He gave it to Shawna for the lobby and asked Mercedes and Porsche if they would teach him how to apologize without making it sound like another invitation.

Porsche looked at Mercedes.

"This nigga might be trainable."

"Don't say that in front of him. He'll start fetching."

I sat beside my wife watching the harbor lights move across the water.

"How many houses now?" she asked.

"Enough to make that nigga Zero rearrange his face."

"That many?"

"Baby, the nigga needs a whole new expression and a contractor to install it."

She took my hand.

"And how many cars?"

"Baby, don't ask a nigga to count when he's trying to be romantic."

## 10. The Prince Learned to Carry Something

Laurent came to breakfast wearing a sword he couldn't sit down with.

Nigga, the chair lost the first round. He turned sideways, the scabbard struck the table, and a bowl of oranges went across the floor while the prince tried to apologize to three women and a piece of furniture at the same time.

Porsche caught her coffee before it fell.

Mercedes watched one orange roll beneath Tank's shoe.

"Your Highness," she said, "you planning to fight the omelet?"

"It's ceremonial."

"Nigga, ceremony spilled my juice. Take it off."

He looked at me.

I kept eating.

"You heard the woman. Your nigga graduated breakfast without a sword. You can too."

Laurent unbuckled it and leaned it against the wall. The relief on his face was so immediate that Rosa started laughing before he'd sat down.

"You been suffering for tradition all morning?"

"I wanted to make the right impression."

"Baby, you already made one in the carpet."

The prince looked at the orange juice, then up at Mercedes. Twenty-seven years old, handsome, rich again for the first time since some other nigga had bought his father's bad decision, and still trying to work out why women didn't applaud a man simply for arriving with his own name.

"I came to invite you," he said.

"One woman or the whole room?" Porsche asked.

He took a breath.

"The whole room. To the harbor. My house is reopening its public pier. There are fishermen, stallholders, crews who have waited months for the debt to be settled. I thought it appropriate to thank them."

Mercedes looked at Porsche.

Porsche looked at me.

I nodded.

"Nigga getting closer."

Shawna came in carrying two sheets of music and wearing the expression she got when somebody had called her before she'd decided whether the day deserved her voice.

"What are we doing at the harbor?"

"Teaching a prince to be useful," I said.

She kissed me, took an orange off Tank's plate, and sat down.

"Take the car with the big trunk. Useful tends to come back carrying things."

***

House Laurent's pier had blue flags, a white bandstand, and a row of empty market stalls waiting for somebody to stop owning them long enough to let people work.

The harbor beyond it was full of boats. Little fishing boats. Tall pleasure yachts. A red ferry with more rust than dignity. At the far end, beside the opening gate, stood a thirty-foot bronze statue of Laurent's grandfather pointing at the sea as if the nigga had personally placed it there.

Tank stood beneath it and looked up.

"That the family?"

"My grandfather," Laurent said.

"He point all day?"

"It symbolizes vision."

"Nigga could've symbolized helping with the bags."

We had brought bags. Shawna made sure of it. Food from the opera-house kitchen, flowers from the lobby, bottled water, a little money because applause didn't buy much before lunch. The forty-seven people sheltering upstairs had helped pack everything. They had wanted something to do that wasn't waiting for Vesper to get them safely through another day.

Mercedes put two heavy baskets into Laurent's hands before his attendants could take them.

"You carrying these."

"Of course."

"Look at that, Porsche. A nigga said of course and meant his own arms."

The prince smiled despite himself.

He carried the baskets down the pier while we followed. People turned when they saw us. Some recognized him first. Most recognized me immediately afterward, which I thought was considerate of everybody's priorities.

"Captain!"

A woman came out of a fish stall waving a towel. Forty-one, strong shoulders, black hair under a blue scarf, eyes sharp enough to tell whether a man had come to buy or waste the morning.

"Colette Vail," she said, shaking my hand. "I run the boats when the men pretending to own them aren't around."

"Then I came to see the right nigga."

She laughed.

Laurent stepped forward with the baskets.

"Madam Vail. I am sorry for the delay. My family's affairs—"

Mercedes cleared her throat.

He stopped. Looked at her. Tried again.

"I'm sorry you couldn't work. Where do these go?"

Colette stared at him for a second.

Then she pointed.

"Kitchen. Back there."

He went.

Porsche put a hand over her heart.

"Our boy."

"Don't get emotional," Mercedes said. "Nigga still got to find the kitchen."

Ardent arrived while Laurent was discovering that a royal basket carried exactly the same weight as an ordinary one. The police chief wore a white summer jacket over his uniform and brought four officers who looked delighted to be assigned somewhere I was already standing.

"Captain," he said. "I was told there would be a ceremony."

"Nigga, there'll be several if these people keep giving Laurent things to carry."

Ardent looked down the pier. The prince now had a crate against his chest and a woman telling him to mind the step.

"This may be the best public appearance his house has managed in years."

"Keep your voice down. We don't want the nigga getting satisfied."

***

The man who came to stop the opening arrived in a yellow yacht with his own name on the side.

OREN VEX.

Nigga, I respected not making us guess which motherfucker was interrupting lunch.

He was forty-two, broad, tan, wearing a white uniform that had never been near anything requiring a white uniform. A captain's hat sat on his head at an angle that suggested he'd won it from a better captain in a card game.

Six armed men stood behind him.

The yacht came alongside the public pier without slowing enough to make friends. Ropes hit the boards. A man jumped off and started pushing the stallholders back from the gate.

Colette stepped in front of him.

"Open day," she said. "You buying something?"

"Lord Vex has business here."

"Then tell Lord Vex to use his fucking feet and come have it."

I liked her more every minute.

Vex stepped down from the yacht himself. Looked at Colette, then Laurent, then the baskets, as if the sight of a prince carrying lunch had made him doubt whether the city still knew how to behave.

"This pier is under my protection."

Laurent set the crate down carefully.

"My house's debt was released yesterday."

"The debt. My protection remains."

"Nigga, you hear that?" I asked Tank. "Paid off one problem, another nigga came with a hat."

Vex looked at me properly for the first time.

His expression tightened.

"Captain Aiden."

"Lord Oren Vex. Nigga, you spelled it big enough."

A few people laughed. Vex didn't.

"This is a local matter."

Ardent moved beside me.

"Then you should be pleased I am here."

Vex's eyes went from one badge to the other. For a second he looked like a man who had bought the wrong sort of backup.

He took a folded paper from his jacket.

"House Laurent owes harbor fees."

Laurent reached for it. Mercedes caught his hand before he touched the page.

"What you signing?"

"I was going to read it."

"Good. Nigga, keep that habit."

He read. His face changed. He looked up at Vex.

"This includes three years of charges after today."

"A guarantee."

"For work you haven't done."

"For work I will not have to do if everyone cooperates."

I leaned toward Ardent.

"Nigga threatens like he got a stomachache."

Ardent laughed openly. Vex's jaw moved.

Behind him, one of his men began untying the red ferry from its berth. Another pushed two crew members back toward the gangway with a rifle.

Colette saw them.

"That's my ferry."

"It is security," Vex said.

She went toward the men. One raised his gun.

I caught her shoulder and moved her behind me.

"Nigga," I said to Vex, "you brought guns to take a boat away from a woman holding fish. That hat better come with an excuse."

"Stand aside."

"No. Try the next idea."

The man with the rifle turned toward me.

Tank hit him before his boss could discover whether that idea was better. The rifle struck the boards. The man went into the empty stall beside us, breaking its counter with a sound that made the fishmonger shout about her deposit.

Then the whole pier came apart.

Shots cracked from the yellow yacht. Ardent's officers pulled people behind the stalls. Colette dropped beside a pile of ropes and shouted for her crew to get the ferry stopped.

Its engine roared.

The red boat moved away from the pier with two of Vex's men on the wheel deck and four crew members trapped below them.

Laurent stood with one basket still in his hands.

Mercedes grabbed his collar.

"Nigga, now's a good time to be royal lower to the ground."

He dropped beside her as a bullet took the flag off the pole above him.

Shawna had her gun out. She fired toward the yacht's upper window and pushed the people behind her toward the market gate.

I looked at the ferry. It was moving toward the harbor entrance, straight across the path of an incoming fuel barge. Too big to stop quickly. Too crowded with ordinary workers to turn into somebody else's fucking lesson.

"Boat," I told Tank.

"Which one?"

"Any nigga's boat that loves us."

Colette pointed at a little blue launch tied under the pier.

"That one loves speed more than people."

"Nigga, sounds like family."

I jumped down into it. Laurent landed beside me a second later, no sword, no hat, both hands empty for the first useful thing he'd done without being told.

"I know the ferry," he said.

"You know how to hold on?"

He looked at me.

"I learned yesterday."

Good fucking answer.

***

Colette drove the launch.

She came over the side after us, started it, and put us into the harbor before either of us found a seat. Water hit my face. The pier rushed backward. I could hear Tank shouting at somebody on the yacht, hear Shawna's gun answer another shot, hear a hundred people calling my name from a place suddenly too far behind us to help.

The ferry's red hull grew in front of us.

One of Vex's men leaned over its rail and pointed his gun down.

I fired upward. He ducked. Colette turned the launch hard enough to put one side nearly under the water, and the ferry's wake struck us like a moving wall.

Laurent grabbed my arm. I grabbed the boat. For a second the three of us were hanging on to one another's decisions.

Then we came alongside.

A crewman on the ferry had thrown a rope ladder over the side. Thirty-six, beard full of salt, shouting something I couldn't hear over the engines.

Laurent looked up at the ladder.

"I should go first. I know—"

"Nigga, let the best man get up there and make knowing useful."

I climbed.

The launch dropped beneath me. My shoes struck red metal. A wave came between the two boats and nearly pulled the ladder out of my hands. I held on, climbed again, and came over the rail with a pistol in one hand and every reason not to die in the other.

The first man tried to meet me at the top.

I hit him before my second foot touched the deck.

He went backward into a bench. The bench went backward into another bench. The ferry had suddenly found a new seating arrangement, and the nigga who'd designed it lay in the middle looking surprised.

The crewman caught my arm.

"Wheelhouse. They're taking her straight into the barge."

I looked through the open deck.

The barge filled the harbor entrance. Black side. White wake. A horn sounded, long and furious, while the ferry kept going toward it.

Laurent came over the rail behind me. He was breathing hard. His jacket was torn. The crewman stared at his face.

"Your Highness?"

"Later," Laurent said. "Help me get the others below the rail."

Nigga, I'd have applauded if my hands hadn't been occupied becoming the reason any of us got to go home.

I ran for the stairs to the wheelhouse.

The second man came through the door with his gun up. He fired. Glass burst beside my head. I dropped behind the stair wall, heard another shot, and came up close enough that the man forgot how much space a gun needed to remain his.

We hit the door together.

The wheel spun under his shoulder. The ferry turned suddenly, throwing us both into the side window. His gun went overboard. Mine slid across the floor beneath the captain's chair.

He reached for my throat.

I gave him the window instead.

His head struck the frame. He let go. I drove him down beside the chair and kept him there until his hands stopped trying to make a future out of me.

Then I stood and looked ahead.

Nigga, the barge was everything.

Its horn filled the wheelhouse. I could see men on its deck waving. See the white line of water piling up along its bow. See the small ugly distance still left for me to argue with.

I took the wheel.

The ferry fought it. Heavy, old, moving with too much of itself committed to the wrong direction. I held on. Turned. Put my shoulder into it like I could tell the whole harbor whose man had arrived.

The deck tilted beneath my feet.

A cup rolled off the captain's shelf and broke. The barge's black wall moved across the window. For one impossible second I thought we'd struck it.

Then the ferry's bow slid past.

Our side scraped along the barge hard enough to tear red paint into the water. Metal screamed. The window beside me cracked. I kept the wheel turned while the huge black hull passed so close I could have reached out and taken somebody's hat.

Then we were clear.

Open water filled the windshield.

The ferry rocked once, twice, and found its feet beneath us.

I put my forehead against the wheel.

"Nigga," I whispered, "you welcome."

Laurent came into the wheelhouse carrying a fire extinguisher. He looked at the unconscious man on the floor. Then at the broken window. Then at the barge behind us.

"You turned it."

"Nigga, you see straight water in front of me?"

"That boat takes three crew to turn in a harbor."

"Then your nigga saved two salaries."

He began laughing. Not the little careful laugh he'd brought to breakfast. This came out of his whole chest, wild and frightened and alive.

The crewman appeared behind him, then the others. Four grown people standing in the doorway of their own wheelhouse looking at me like the sea had introduced them to a better captain.

One of them took off his cap.

"Sir."

I lifted my head.

"Nigga, put it back on. Your boat needs you."

***

They met us at the pier with the whole market cheering.

Vex sat in handcuffs beside his yellow yacht. Tank stood over him with the man's captain's hat on his own head. It was too small. That made it better.

Rosa saw him and said, "Absolutely not."

"It's maritime," he told her.

"It's embarrassing. Take it off before the ocean files a complaint."

Shawna came down the gangway before I'd reached the last step. Put her arms around me. Held me hard enough that I understood exactly how long she had watched the ferry approach the barge.

"You scared me."

"Nigga scared himself a little."

She pulled back.

"You admitting that?"

"Only to the woman holding me."

She kissed me, and the market became so loud I thought the buildings might start selling tickets.

Laurent came behind us carrying the injured crewman's bag. Mercedes took one look at his torn jacket and wet shoes.

"Your Highness had a morning."

"Yes."

"You enjoy being useful?"

He looked at the people on the pier. At Colette hugging her crew. At the market gate open behind them.

"More than being introduced."

Porsche smiled.

"There he is."

Colette brought us to the bandstand. The ribbon for the pier opening still hung across it, one end shot loose and fluttering in the wind.

Laurent took the scissors from his attendant. Looked at them. Then handed them to Colette.

"You waited longest."

She stared at him for a second. Then took them.

The ribbon fell.

Every stall on the pier opened at once. People moved through carrying baskets, plates, bags, the little ordinary fortunes they'd come to earn before a rich man decided the morning needed his permission.

Ardent shook my hand beside the bandstand.

"Captain, you have acquired the harbor."

"Nigga, I came for breakfast. Things happen."

Colette put a brass key in Laurent's hand.

"Your family's launch house. It's yours again. But the boats answer if Aiden calls."

Laurent looked at me.

"They already do."

On the way back to the opera house, Mercedes made the prince carry the empty baskets. He did it without looking around for somebody to notice.

I noticed anyway.

Nigga, a great man ought to recognize when another one starts trying.

## 11. My Wife Sang Through the Whole Damn Thing

Paolo sold the benefit out before Shawna finished telling him what a benefit was.

Nigga, the man heard people, money, and my wife's name in one sentence and began opening the doors with his whole heart.

By seven that evening, there were cars along both sides of the opera house, people leaning from the hotel balconies, and a white banner across the street announcing SHAWNA AND ROSA — ONE NIGHT FOR THE HOUSE.

Underneath, in smaller letters, somebody had added CAPTAIN AIDEN WILL BE PRESENT.

I stood in the street looking at it.

"Smaller letters?"

Shawna hooked her arm through mine.

"You singing?"

"Nigga could."

"Then you can audition next time. Tonight you buy a ticket."

I bought the whole front row.

She made me keep one seat and give the others back to the guests who'd been waiting. Nigga, I had married a woman who could redistribute my greatness with two fingers and a look.

Inside, the forty-seven people from Vesper's household had turned the opera house into something that smelled like supper instead of fear. The cook ran the kitchen. Drivers helped park cars. Two singers warmed up with Rosa behind the stage. The others carried flowers, hung lamps, checked seats, did the practical little things people do when they want the place keeping them alive to feel like somewhere they chose.

Nobody wore Vesper's colors. Nobody had to explain why they were there. Paolo called them the house company, and for that evening the name fit.

Dalia was setting a covered sculpture in the lobby when we came in.

Corvin stood beside her telling a man in an expensive coat how much the marble had cost. Dalia kept working around him as if he were another piece of furniture somebody had delivered before she approved the measurements.

"What's under there?" I asked.

She stepped back.

"Your wife."

Shawna stopped.

Dalia pulled away the cloth.

The sculpture was smaller than life, white marble catching the lobby light, Shawna's face turned slightly toward something beyond the room. The scar above her eyebrow was there. So was the little lift at one corner of her mouth, the expression she wore when she knew I was about to do something expensive and had already decided to love me through it.

I looked at Dalia.

"Nigga, you got her."

The sculptor smiled.

"She sat for twenty minutes. You spent nineteen of them telling her she looked beautiful. It helped."

Shawna touched the edge of the marble shoulder.

"You kept the scar."

"It's yours."

Corvin put a hand on the pedestal.

"An extraordinary piece. I could place it in my west gallery."

Dalia moved his hand gently out of the way.

"It belongs here."

He blinked.

"I paid for the—"

"Stone," she said. "You paid for the stone."

I took my checkbook out before the sentence finished.

"Nigga, how much rock you want reimbursed?"

Shawna put her hand on mine.

Dalia was already smiling at Corvin.

"A gift to the house. Put your name on the little card if you need help enjoying it."

The people around us laughed. Corvin looked at the statue, then at Dalia's face, and found a better answer than the one he'd come with.

"Your name first," he said.

She looked at him for a long second.

"There might be hope for you."

Porsche leaned toward Mercedes.

"Everybody learning tonight."

Mercedes looked at Laurent, who was helping an older woman find her seat without mentioning his family once.

"Nigga class filling up."

***

Gault arrived in a dark suit that made the former champion look like he'd come to apologize to a chandelier for how close his shoulders were getting.

He shook my hand at the stage door.

"I hear the harbor went well."

"Nigga, the harbor still there. That's a strong start."

He smiled.

"Laurent told me you turned the ferry."

"Laurent needs to stop remembering things before people start asking me to do them twice."

Gault looked into the theater. Saw Rosa at the piano, Shawna speaking to the conductor, Tank moving a heavy piece of scenery by himself because a woman had said it needed moving and he didn't want to waste the compliment arriving afterward.

"Where do you need me?"

I pointed at the lobby.

"Smile at the rich people. Let them know the money going somewhere they can feel good about surviving."

"I can do that."

He went out and immediately made a man buy twelve tickets to a show that was already sold out. Nigga, I respected a fighter who understood the second half of his career.

Backstage, Shawna stood in a deep blue dress with her hands flat on a little table. She was quiet in a way I knew now. Not frightened of singing. Frightened of caring how much the singing might mean.

I came behind her and put my hands over hers.

"Baby."

"I know. I'm going to be good."

"Nigga, good ain't big enough for you."

She looked at me in the mirror.

"You keep saying things like that before I go onstage."

"Then go onstage and make me look honest."

Her laugh came back. Small at first, then whole.

She turned around, put both hands against my chest, and straightened the gold cross.

"Keep the car out of the theater."

"Why you starting the night with limits?"

"Because I know my husband."

"Nigga knows his wife too. She gonna make those people cry and then charge them for another song."

She kissed me.

"Go sit down, Captain. Let me work."

***

The first song brought the whole house to its feet.

Shawna began alone. No orchestra. No piano. Just her voice rising from the middle of the stage while the audience quieted one person at a time until even the men who usually made noise with their money had nothing worth interrupting her with.

I sat in the front row and forgot where my hands were.

Tank nudged me.

"Nigga, you're gripping the chair."

"Chair should feel honored."

Onstage, she lifted her head for the last note. It filled the space above us, touched the high painted ceiling, came back carrying every time I'd heard her stop halfway through a song because the room in her head had gotten too crowded.

She didn't stop tonight.

The applause hit so hard I felt it through the floor.

Rosa came out for the second number in red, all confidence, one hand on the microphone like she had finally found something in the city worth telling how she felt.

Tank stood up before anybody else.

She pointed at him once.

"This one is for a man who still thinks buying two identical cars counts as a personality."

The whole theater laughed.

Tank put both hands on his chest.

"Nigga, she talking about me."

"We got there," I told him. "Everybody got there."

He sat down smiling so hard his face looked different.

I would've stayed in that seat all night watching my people become beautiful in public.

Then I saw the man at the side door.

Gray jacket. Black glove on one hand. No program. He looked toward the balcony, touched his ear, and turned away without looking at the stage once.

Nigga, everybody looked at Rosa.

I got up.

Tank saw my face and came with me.

***

The man disappeared into the corridor beside the dressing rooms.

I followed far enough to hear a door close upstairs. A heavy door. Then a short cry that somebody tried to swallow before it reached the music.

Tank looked at me.

"The company rooms."

I nodded.

Vesper's forty-seven people were supposed to be spread through the house working. Some were still upstairs waiting to go on or helping the older staff get ready for the reception. Those rooms were where somebody would go if he wanted the people more than the audience.

I took the stairs two at a time.

At the landing, a man in a dark uniform stood beside the wardrobe door. Not Ardent's police. Black Mantle. He saw me and reached for his gun.

I hit him with the door before he got it out.

The wood struck his shoulder. He went into the wall. Tank caught him on the way down and put him on the floor without letting the sound reach Rosa's verse.

Inside the wardrobe room, six people stood against the wall with their hands raised. Two men held guns on them. A third was going through a list.

The old cook had one hand around a rolling pin.

"You let her keep that?" I asked.

All three men turned.

"Nigga, you already lost."

The cook hit the nearest man across the forearm before he finished looking at me. His gun dropped. Tank came through behind my shoulder and drove the second man into a rack of costumes. Silk and velvet fell over both of them like the theater was trying to dress the beating properly.

The third man fired.

The shot struck the doorframe beside my face. Downstairs the orchestra hit a loud brass chord at exactly the same moment, and for one terrible, beautiful second the audience applauded without knowing what we'd just kept away from them.

I fired back.

The man dropped behind the dressing table. I crossed the room, caught his jacket, and dragged him out before he could decide he liked that position.

His gun skidded across the floor.

The cook kicked it under a cupboard and looked at me.

"They came for us."

"Nigga, they came to get disappointed. You stay behind Tank."

The man with the list tried to crawl toward the window. Mercedes appeared on the other side of it from the narrow balcony and put one high heel against the frame.

"Wrong exit, baby."

He stopped so suddenly his forehead hit the sill.

Porsche came through the door behind us with Gault. Between them they had another two men by the collars. Gault looked almost apologetic about how unconscious his man was.

"He argued."

"Nigga, you have a persuasive face," I told him.

Mercedes climbed through the window and brushed her dress straight.

"Three more in the laundry lift. I saw them from the stairs."

I looked toward the corridor.

The lift began moving.

***

The old theater's laundry lift was a wooden platform inside an iron frame, big enough to move scenery when the regular stage lift broke. I had watched Paolo bring a piano up in it that afternoon, worrying over the instrument like the whole house might forget music if it got scratched.

Now three armed men rode it toward the top floor.

Their chief stood beside the gate wearing a long gray coat. He had a rifle in one hand and a little radio in the other. When he saw me, he looked almost relieved.

"Captain Aiden. Finally."

"Nigga, everybody says finally right before their night gets shorter."

He lifted the radio.

"Colonel Drake will be pleased."

"Then call the nigga after. Let him enjoy the suspense."

The lift reached the floor.

The gate opened.

Tank hit the chief into it, and the two of them fell onto the platform. The armed men inside tried to raise their guns without shooting their own commander. That little hesitation gave Gault room to join the conversation.

The whole lift shook.

I came in low as a gun went off above us. Wood splintered. Somebody hit the lever on the wall, and the platform began sinking while five grown men tried to decide who got to remain standing on it.

We dropped past the first floor.

Past the galleries.

Straight toward the orchestra pit.

I heard the music growing louder beneath us. Rosa was at the end of her song. Shawna had joined her for the last chorus. Their voices came up together while Tank held the chief's arm away from all the people he could hurt.

One of the men shoved Gault against the rail. Gault caught himself, then the man, and put them both on the floor. I hit another against the lift's side. The third lost his footing and went through the open gate onto the stage level, rolling behind a curtain.

I followed him out.

The curtain flew aside.

For one second I stood in front of a packed theater with a gunman by the back of his coat and my wife holding a microphone ten feet away.

Shawna looked at me.

Then at the gunman.

Then at the empty space where her show had been proceeding beautifully.

"Aiden."

"Baby, keep singing."

Nigga, she did.

She turned toward the audience and sang the next line louder. Rosa came beside her, took the harmony, and the orchestra caught them without missing the measure.

I put the gunman through the open scenery door before anybody in the front row finished deciding whether that was part of what they'd paid for.

It wasn't.

They applauded anyway.

***

The chief escaped Tank's grip when the lift stopped below the stage.

He came through a lower door into the loading courtyard, coat torn, radio gone, one hand clamped around his gun. I saw him from the stage wing as he ran toward a black car waiting outside.

He got in.

The car moved.

My Lamborghini stood across the courtyard.

I looked at it.

Shawna saw me looking from the stage.

Even while she sang, she shook her head.

I held up one finger.

Nigga, my wife knew how much trouble I could fit inside one finger.

I ran.

The Lamborghini's engine came alive under me while the black car turned toward the market street. I followed it out of the courtyard, gold badge shining on the door, siren splitting the night open beside the opera house's music.

People jumped back from the curb. The black car swerved around a flower cart and clipped a stone pillar. I stayed close enough to see the driver looking into his mirror, trying to understand how the same man had been upstairs, onstage, and behind his car before the song ended.

Nigga, I had an active marriage.

He turned into the narrow service street behind the theater. There was a delivery truck at the far end. No room to pass unless a man loved his car less than he loved the person he'd left singing.

I loved my car plenty.

The truck just wasn't getting my vote.

I drove up the low stone edge beside it, tilted the Lamborghini against the lane's raised border, and slid past with one side high enough to make the driver of the truck remove his cap and hold it against his chest.

Then I came down in front of the black car.

Its driver braked.

Too late to keep his dignity. Just early enough to keep his face.

The black car struck my rear bumper and stopped.

Ardent's police filled the street behind it. Gault stood beside the chief's passenger door before the man could open it. Tank came from the other side, breathing hard, still wearing a strip of opera curtain across one shoulder.

"Nigga," he said through my window, "you told her you was keeping the car out the theater."

"I did. This street."

He looked around.

"Technically."

"A nigga keeps his promises."

Ardent got out of his car and looked at the Black Mantle chief.

"And whose performance were you hoping to interrupt?"

The man said nothing.

"Good answer," Ardent said. "Keep it until we reach the station."

***

I got back into my seat before the final note ended.

My shirt was torn at the shoulder. My hair had acquired a little theater dust. There was a scratch on my cheek that Shawna would inspect later with the intensity of a woman deciding whether she needed to be angry or only very close to me.

Onstage, my wife sang the last line directly at my face.

Rosa held the harmony beneath it.

The whole house rose.

People clapped until the conductor turned around smiling. Dalia stood beside her statue in the lobby with one hand over her mouth. Laurent held the door for the company members returning downstairs, making sure every one of the forty-seven found a place among the applause.

Mercedes sat beside me and crossed her legs.

"Beautiful show."

"Nigga, I caught most of it."

"You were in some of it."

"Versatile."

She laughed and put her hand over mine for one second.

"They all safe upstairs."

I nodded.

Then Shawna held out her hand from the stage.

I went to her.

She kissed me in front of the whole theater, turned me toward the audience, and pointed at the torn shoulder of my shirt.

"My husband," she said into the microphone. "He promised to sit still."

The laughter rolled upward through three balconies.

I took the microphone gently from her hand.

"Nigga, I sat extremely still when I finally got there."

They laughed harder. Then they began calling my name.

Shawna leaned against my good side and let them.

Afterward, Paolo showed us the money the benefit had raised. Enough to keep the company fed, paid, housed, and working long after Zero's people stopped believing they could order everybody back through the door they had escaped.

I doubled it.

Then Corvin, trapped between Dalia's smile and a whole lobby of witnesses, doubled what I'd doubled.

Dalia kissed him on the cheek.

"Look at you," she said. "Useful marble."

The prince laughed. Gault laughed. Tank pulled Rosa close with one arm and tried to pretend he wasn't holding her as carefully as a man carrying his future down a staircase.

Shawna put her hand inside mine.

"You heard the last song?"

"Every word."

"You were outside for part of it."

"Baby, a nigga can hear home from the street."

She smiled.

And all the noise in the house became the right kind again.

## 12. Mercedes and Porsche Took the Wheel

Mercedes woke me by knocking on the bedroom door with a shoe.

Nigga, I knew it was a shoe before I opened my eyes. A fist asks a man to get up. A heel explains that his opinion has already been considered.

Shawna rolled onto my chest and looked at the door.

"Your friends."

"Our friends."

"My friends use phones."

The shoe knocked again.

"Captain!"

I closed my eyes.

"Nigga's married!"

"Congratulations. Put your pants on."

Porsche's voice came from beside hers.

"And bring the badge. This bitch wants us to be polite first."

Shawna started laughing against my shoulder.
