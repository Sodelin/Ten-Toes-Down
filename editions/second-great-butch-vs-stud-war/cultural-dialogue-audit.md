## Concrete corrections

### A1 — V5 paragraph 76: ambiguous object and woman

Current: “I offered to have it inspected. She put one finger against my lips.”

The immediate preceding passage concerns Rae taking Nia's hand; the earlier floor reference is several paragraphs away. “It” can therefore sound like the hand, and “she” can sound like Nia rather than Shawna. That accidentally changes an affectionate married-couple interruption into a different interpersonal action.

**Proposed replacement:** “I offered to have the floor inspected. Shawna put one finger against my lips.”

### A2 — V6 paragraphs 32–34: the banner changes location twice

V5 paragraphs 83–85 lower the banner into the portrait's transport crate. V6 paragraph 32 then places it folded **beside** the crate, and paragraph 34 has Aiden take it **off the public door** again. No intervening rehanging is established inside the reviewed sequence.

**Proposed correction:** Keep the banner folded **inside** the crate in paragraph 32. In paragraph 34, replace the second removal with Aiden checking that the entrance now shows neutral room names and the help contact rather than caucus assignments. This preserves a concrete action without resetting the prop.

### A3 — V4 paragraph 26: the grouping error belongs to Owen, not Martin

Rae tells Martin, “You already did a little grouping exercise.” Owen did the explicit grouping in paragraphs 1–4. Martin has made a different mistake about the imagined general public.

**Applied replacement at root's request:** “You're already choosing who counts as the public. Tell us who gets left out.” The line now answers Martin's own claim about the imagined public. The conversation increases from 1,473 to 1,477 words; no game fields changed.

### A4 — V4 paragraph 11: Owen's compositional explanation does not match his arrangement

The narrator says Owen imagined sharp tailoring on one side and flowing costumes on the other. But he has placed Velvet's silver costume with Kit and Rae under “the masculine side.” The narration currently endorses an explanation that does not fit the visible grouping.

**Applied replacement at root's request:** “Owen lowered the camera and claimed he had meant to separate sharp tailoring from flowing costumes. Velvet looked down at the dress he had put on the tailored side. Even his excuse needed rearranging.”

This makes the inconsistency the character's exposed mistake rather than an unnoticed narration error.

## Repeated character work worth revising

### R1 — V5 paragraphs 1–23 replay Kit's earlier labor presumption

V2 paragraphs 7–13 already expose and remove unagreed repair/legal labor assignments. V3 teaches Kit to ask before deciding Rae's comfort, and V4 has her refuse the flattering comparison with Rae. V5 begins another near-identical sequence: Kit has sent a ladder before Rae answers, Rae must correct her, and Kit explains her own experience.

This can be plausible backsliding, but its accumulation across six conversations makes Kit a recurring pupil and Rae a recurring correction service. The new volume's strongest material is the shift from competence to desire and Rae/Nia's mutual vulnerability.

**Recommended bounded revision:** Recast V5's opening around Rae automatically volunteering after Kit asks an appropriately limited question, with Nia initially overinterpreting or intervening before Rae answers. Kit can have learned something; Rae and Nia can make the next mistakes. Preserve the later invitation, request for tenderness, and Nia's refusal to translate Rae.

A smaller fallback is to acknowledge the recurrence explicitly in dialogue and make Kit correct the arrangement before Rae must repeat the lesson. The first option better differentiates the volumes.

### R2 — V4 paragraphs 43–44 reset an already established distinction

Kit asks, “Does that mean we can't share anything?” after V1 has already shown her understanding that shared masculinity does not make stud a generic category.

**Possible replacement:** “I like what we recognise in each other. I don't want to make your word mine.”

Let Rae answer the more developed question. This preserves affinity and cultural specificity without sending Kit back to the start.

### R3 — V2 and V4 both stage a sponsor/photo respectability dispute

V2 paragraphs 14–24 and V4 paragraphs 14–36 both involve an official asking for a more welcoming/respectable image, Velvet challenging it, Kit and Rae being evaluated differently, and the room correcting the official. The outcomes differ usefully: V2 protects pay, V4 controls image and caption consent.

The V4 incident would benefit from an explicit short callback that the fee question is already settled and this is now about who controls representation. Alternatively, remove the second sponsor setup and let Owen's photo arrangement/caption create the entire conflict. Do not turn the earlier settled fee into a fresh threat.
