## Findings

The claims inspected fit their declared evidence. R03, R04, and R11 candidly identify indexed retrieval. R06 also has an indexed newsletter component, and W05 declares indexed retrieval for two organizational pages. Consequently, “three indexed entries” should not be read as “only three indexed source records in the complete appendix.” No declaration falsely claims complete collection, recording, trial-file, or full-book inspection.

The entries distinguish a person's testimony, an organization's account, a catalog description, a political argument, and editorial interpretation. Hampton's remembered use of terms is not presented as their universal origin. Sappho is not made the ancestor of every culture's same-sex desire. W02 retains separate 1999, 2003, and 2004 milestones; W03 identifies period terminology and catalog limits; W04 avoids equating Two-Spirit with either a universal Indigenous identity or lesbian identity; W05 preserves differences among its constituency. Theoretical claims are attributed to their authors rather than adopted as universal traits.

No historical entry duplicates another. Visibility, archival survival, meeting spaces, and coalition recur as themes, but the entries use distinct cases and evidence. BH05's focus is Combahee's relationships and practical arrangements; it should not be relabeled as a comprehensive summary of the collective's manifesto.

All fictional interludes occupy separate fields and use the established invented cast. No fabricated dialogue is assigned to a historical person. This finding depends on the output retaining explicit fictional-interlude labeling; the JSON separation alone is not a rendered-layout check.

URL comparison ignored capitalization and a final slash. There is no identical cited webpage reused across the history entries or between their 32 URLs and the eight earlier cultural-source URLs. Thus there is no same-URL cumulative use hidden by treating each entry as a fresh 200-word allowance. Each historical body is below that default allowance even when conservatively assigned in full to one of its sources; the earlier notes are also substantially below it. No extended source quotations appear in the reviewed prose.

Distinct URLs can still contain versions of the same underlying material. The clearest related case here is C7's Hampton exhibit description and BH02's later edited oral-history episode. Conservatively combining the entire BH02 historical body (133 words) with the entire C7 note (35 words) gives 168 words. Adding BH02's original caveat and question gives 195 words. No budget correction is required even under that conservative grouping. This does not assert that the two pages are the same work or that every word in that grouping is source-derived.

Other related earlier notes use different material: C3 cites the Combahee statement while BH05 uses Barbara Smith's retrospective interview about relationships and retreats; C4 uses Feinberg's selected author notes while R11 summarizes White's contemporary terminology discussion. Related people and themes are not, by themselves, evidence of duplicated source text. No additional same-work cumulative-use problem was identified in these four files.

The source-budget result is limited to the reviewed history entries and eight cultural notes. It does not claim a sentence-by-sentence attribution audit of every fictional volume, proof that no unlisted page republishes another source, or a legal rights opinion. Additional source-derived expansion should be counted cumulatively against the relevant source rather than assuming that a second URL or a new entry automatically resets its allowance.
