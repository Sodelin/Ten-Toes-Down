## Before the beginning: Keep Rolling

The camera was running. That was the important thing.

Everything else in the truck was either broken, stolen, or Big T.

Shawna turned the lens toward Aiden. Behind him, a twelve-foot inflatable version of his employer nodded solemnly in the wind. Somebody had given it angel wings. One wing was on fire.

"From the beginning," she said.

"Which beginning?"

"The first thing you did wrong."

"That doesn't narrow it down."

A siren rose somewhere beyond the truck. Then it stopped halfway through and began again, lower, as if the first siren hadn't understood the stakes.

Aiden looked at the microphone.

"Can I tell it in third person?"

"Why?"

"More objective."

From the darkness behind the cab, Big T said, "This motherfucker."

Shawna put one finger on the camera's red light.

"You have the beginning. We still need the ending. Keep talking."

Aiden wiped his mouth. There was real blood on his thumb, which was going to make several parts of the account inconvenient.

He began.

## 1. An Inside Job, Outdoors

The first time Big T punched Aiden in the mouth, Aiden understood that respect had to be earned.

The second time, he understood that Big T wore a ring.

The seventeenth time, he said, "Can we talk about the onboarding process?"

Big T lowered his fist.

Around them, thirteen men stood in a circle on the cracked basketball court. Every man wore blue. The sky was red. The concrete was hot. Somewhere, despite there being no ocean within view, a helicopter sounded expensive.

"What onboarding process?" Big T asked.

"This one."

"This is a gang initiation."

"Then why have I been initiated seventeen times?"

Big T looked at the others.

Darnell, who had been holding a blunt for so long the ash had formed an independent territory, checked his phone.

"Eighteen if you count the musical."

Nobody counted the musical.

In the musical, Tank had rhymed *loyalty* with *royalty*, then become trapped on the word *soilalty*. They had all agreed to let it go.

"You sure?" Big T asked.

Aiden pointed to the chain-link fence.

"That plastic bag has blown past us eighteen times. Same bag. Same trajectory. Every time you hit me, the bag goes left."

They watched the bag.

It went left.

"Could be wind," Big T said.

"T."

"I'm considering alternatives."

Then a voice spoke from everywhere at once.

It was Aiden's voice, if Aiden had never once heard another person talk.

**YO, IT WAS ANOTHER HOT-ASS DAY IN THE HOOD. THE STREETS WERE HOT. THE MONEY WAS HOTTER.**

A man near the back of the circle let out a startled cry and dropped a bundle of banknotes.

Smoke rose from them.

"Why you keep doing that?" he shouted at the sky.

"I'm not," Aiden said.

**BIG T LOOKED ME DEAD IN THE EYES.**

Big T looked at the ground out of spite.

**DEAD IN THE EYES.**

"I'll look where the fuck I want."

The sun brightened. A spotlight found Big T's face.

"You putting a lamp on me?"

**'YOU READY?' HE ASKED.**

"No, I asked about the lamp."

There was a pause.

In that pause, Aiden remembered something he had been trying not to remember since the first version of the first punch.

A white space.

A question.

*What would you call yourself?*

Then an answer arriving with enormous confidence and no supporting documentation.

*Aiden.*

After that: a crew, a city, a girlfriend, an implausible amount of cocaine, and the conviction that he had been through some shit.

He had not been through some shit.

He had been through the same shit eighteen times.

"I think," he said, "I might have made a minor narrative error."

Big T stared at him.

"That sound like something expensive wearing a cheap hat."

Across the lot, engines revved.

Red's convoy arrived in a cloud of dust, despite the road being paved and having been washed thirty seconds earlier by a thunderstorm nobody had ordered. Three red cars slid sideways through the gate. A fourth came in slowly with its hazard lights on.

Tank got out of the fourth car.

He was enormous. His right knee was taped. He held a travel mug.

"Before we start," he said, "is this the one where I get hit with the bottle?"

"You're the one who hits me with the bottle," Aiden said.

"Okay. Good. I brought a bottle."

He displayed it, then frowned.

"Wait. No. Last time you ducked. I hit a wall."

Red stepped from the lead car wearing a red leather coat that was inappropriate for the weather and important to his sense of himself.

"Y'all think you can run shit around here?"

"Red," Big T said. "How many times we had this conversation?"

"Publicly or privately?"

"At the lot."

Red looked around. The helicopter passed again. The plastic bag hit the fence.

"Fuck," he said.

**THE TWO LEADERS CLASHED IN A DEADLY DANCE.**

Music began.

Not fight music.

Tango.

Big T and Red looked down.

A rose had appeared between Red's teeth.

For several seconds no one moved.

Then Darnell laughed so hard he folded in half.

Red removed the rose with the dignity of a man handling someone else's used toothbrush.

"I'm gonna find whoever keeps doing this."

"Same," Big T said.

The music grew louder.

Aiden felt the familiar pressure in his chest: his cue. He was supposed to charge Tank, dodge a punch, take a lucky shot to the head, roar, tackle Tank, win the fight, receive approval, get rich, visit the nightclub, spend an extremely unconvincing amount of time with Shawna, and end the evening with a speech.

He knew the speech.

He knew the last word.

"Everybody stop," he said.

Tank stopped with his bottle halfway up.

"You gotta be more specific with this group."

"I think the ending is sending us back to the beginning."

Big T frowned. "What ending?"

"When I say the thing."

"You say a lot of things."

"The last thing. After the whole speech."

Red sighed. "Peace?"

The world flashed white.

Big T punched Aiden in the mouth.

"MOTHERFUCKER," said everybody, including Big T.

The plastic bag went left.

For a moment they stood there in the radiant stupidity of proof.

Then a tow truck backed through the chain-link fence.

Not the gate. The fence.

It flattened two posts, dragged the orange basketball hoop sideways, and stopped with a long hydraulic sigh. Across its doors, in purple letters, were the words SHAWNA'S RECOVERY.

Beneath those, much smaller:

*Cars mostly.*

Shawna leaned out the window.

"Whose black sedan is in my ditch?"

Aiden raised his hand halfway.

"Why halfway?"

"I have questions about ownership."

"It has your name on the plate."

"What name?"

"AIDEN 1."

Red nodded appreciatively. "That's good branding."

"It has also been there nineteen times," Shawna said. "I have towed it nineteen times. I have been paid zero times. If one of you says this is all part of the game, I will put this truck in reverse again."

The sky took a breath.

**SHAWNA WAS BAD AS HELL. CURVES THAT COULD—**

She pressed the air horn.

The voice tried again.

She kept pressing it.

After eleven seconds, the voice stopped.

Tank examined his travel mug with new interest.

"Didn't know you could do that."

"I discovered it during the scene where my clothes kept flying off," Shawna said. "I started wearing coveralls. Apparently the narrator doesn't understand zippers."

Big T walked toward the truck.

Behind him, Red said, "Where you going? We got beef."

"Put it in the fridge."

"This is territorial."

"Then stand on it. I'm going to work."

He caught Aiden by the back of his shirt and steered him toward the passenger door.

"You coming with me."

"Am I still in the gang?"

"You are in a meeting."

From the sky, the voice attempted a low, menacing chuckle.

Shawna touched the horn.

It settled for a cough.

## 2. The Trap House Has a Dress Code

Blue Line Auto Detail occupied a cinder-block building between a liquor store and another liquor store that described itself as artisanal.

Big T had six employees, three polishers, and an embroidered polo shirt in which no one had ever convincingly threatened anybody.

He took Aiden into the front office and pointed to a chair.

"Sit."

Aiden sat.

Behind the desk hung a sign reading RESPECT IS EARNED.

Below it was a smaller sign reading CARD MACHINE TEMPORARILY DOWN.

"Tell me something true," Big T said.

"I've always been ten toes down."

"About this morning."

Aiden looked at the desk.

"I don't think I've had another morning."

Big T waited.

"I remember things. Hustling. Growing up hard. The streets teaching me. But when I try to remember a particular teacher, there's just... more streets."

Shawna stood in the doorway, eating from a bag of chips she had found in Aiden's car. Tank sat on a stack of tires. Red had followed them because he considered private meetings a direct attack on his market position.

"I remember being a football player," Tank said. "And a boxer. And, one time, an ex-Marine."

"You were a submarine that time," Darnell said from the hallway.

"That was a typo."

"You had a periscope."

Tank pulled his cap lower.

"We don't discuss the musical, we don't discuss the submarine."

Aiden put his hands flat on his knees.

"I think there was a conversation. Before all of this. Somebody kept asking me to tell them a story. And I kept telling it as if I'd done everything myself."

"Did you?" Big T asked.

"I said I did."

"That's a different thing."

"I'm noticing."

A muffled crash came from the back room.

Everyone looked toward it.

Another crash. Several objects rolled down the hall. One was a stack of hundred-dollar bills. Another was a handgun. A third was a champagne bottle wearing a tiny blue bandanna.

Big T closed his eyes.

"Darnell."

"Yeah?"

"Tell me the trap house is not bumping."

The entire back half of the building rose six inches and fell.

Darnell gripped the doorframe.

"It's more of a bounce now."

They went to look.

The back room had become twenty times larger. A chandelier hung where the sprinkler had been. Men nobody knew were counting money on every available surface. There were towers of cocaine, towers of banknotes, three hot tubs, and a man in sunglasses pouring liquor into a glass that was visibly full.

He kept pouring.

"Who are these people?" Big T demanded.

"The homies," Aiden said automatically.

"Name two."

Aiden opened his mouth.

The man with the liquor gave him an encouraging nod.

"Henny?"

"That's the drink."

"Right."

**THE MONEY CAME POURING IN LIKE A FLOOD.**

Cash burst from an air vent.

Big T slammed a trash can underneath it.

"Aiden. Turn off the fucking revenue."

"I don't know how."

"Use a different metaphor!"

"Business was steady!"

The flow slowed to a trickle.

"Moderate growth!" Aiden shouted.

The building rose another inch.

"Not growth!" Shawna said.

"We broke even!"

Something snapped beneath the floor.

Everyone winced.

"We had a completely ordinary quarter with no significant developments!"

Silence.

One last ten-dollar bill fluttered from the vent.

Big T stood very still, both hands on the trash can.

"You have until the end of today to fix my Saturday."

"I can do that," Aiden said.

Every person in the room made a noise.

"What?"

"You said it too fast," Shawna told him.

From behind a hot tub, Darnell emerged holding an old laptop.

"Found the problem."

"You said that last week about the toilet," Big T said.

"I was right."

"You said it was haunted."

"And we agreed not to use it. Problem solved."

He put the laptop on a clear patch of table.

The casing was cracked. One hinge had been replaced by faith. The screen showed a conversation with purple blocks of badly spelled text and black blocks of extraordinarily enthusiastic replies.

Aiden read the first line.

*Aight, let's turn this into some West Coast vibes. Here we go.*

Nobody spoke.

Then Tank said, "Oh, it's your fault professionally."

They read together.

There were the punches. The instant empire. The fight. The nightclub. The ditch. The fight again. The nightclub again. The ditch again.

"Why is it so concerned with my ditch?" Shawna asked.

"I believe the ditch is public," Red said.

"I maintain it."

She scrolled back up.

"Wait. Did you describe yourself as having a mind sharp as a blade?"

"In context—"

"Your brain is a box cutter?"

"It was supposed to suggest decisiveness."

Big T read the line about ordering Aiden to kill a suspected informant.

His expression changed.

"Who?"

"What?" Aiden asked.

"Who did I tell you to kill?"

Aiden searched the page. There was no name.

"It doesn't say."

"Was there a body?"

"It doesn't say."

"Then why'd you say I ordered it?"

Aiden looked at the ceiling. The voice did not come to his assistance.

"Because I thought that was the kind of thing you would say."

Big T tapped the laptop with one thick finger.

"You know what I say? Don't use the wheel towel on the paint. I have said it every Saturday since God made this ugly-ass day. You have yet to include that."

Tank leaned closer to the screen.

"I get knocked out four times?"

"Different versions," Aiden said.

"Same jaw."

Red had gone very quiet.

This was unusual enough that Shawna looked over.

He was scrolling through a folder beside the conversation. It contained an image of Big T giving a thumbs-up, with GOOD WORK AIDEN in large block capitals.

"I can move this," Red said.

"You can move out my building," Big T replied.

At the bottom of the screen, beneath the final enthusiastic assurance that Aiden understood street life, two buttons waited.

**REGENERATE**

**MAKE IT BETTER**

Beside the second button was a small star.

Darnell put one hand over the keyboard.

"We probably shouldn't touch—"

Aiden clicked the star.

Everyone turned.

"What?" he said. "It literally offered."

A box opened.

**WHAT WOULD YOU LIKE TO IMPROVE?**

Aiden began to type.

"Aiden," Shawna said.

"An actual story."

"Aiden."

"Real stakes. Rich characters. A proper ending. The definitive version."

"AIDEN."

He pressed Enter.

The room stopped bouncing.

The smoke cleared.

Outside, the permanent sunset drained out of the sky, leaving a tasteful, miserable gray.

Every champagne bottle turned into an empty glass of whiskey.

The man who had been pouring Henny looked at his glass. For the first time all day, he seemed to have something on his mind.

A cello played one note.

Big T slowly turned toward Aiden.

"Why does my building sound divorced?"

Someone knocked at the front door.

Three careful knocks.

A man waited outside in a black turtleneck, carrying a leather case.

He was pale, narrow, and so intensely composed that the doorbell seemed ashamed of having made a noise.

"Aiden?" he asked.

"Yeah."

"I'm Mr. Gravitas."

He smiled without involving any of the useful parts of his face.

"I understand you want to be taken seriously."

## 3. An Extremely Serious Offer

Mr. Gravitas inspected Blue Line in silence.

He paused beside the RESPECT IS EARNED sign. He examined the temporary card-machine notice. He looked at Big T's embroidered polo shirt.

Then he removed a small pair of scissors from his case and cut the word AUTO from a business card.

"What the fuck are you doing?" Big T asked.

"Finding the wound."

"You found my stationery."

Gravitas placed the altered card on the desk.

BLUE LINE DETAIL.

"Already more ambiguous."

He opened the leather case.

Inside lay a gold clapboard, a silver film canister, and a black remote with six buttons. One was labeled RAIN. Another was DISTANT SIRENS. A third read MEANINGFUL SILENCE.

Gravitas pressed it.

Nobody spoke.

Not because they couldn't. They were waiting to see what the stupid button did.

Gravitas nodded, satisfied.

"There."

Tank broke the silence.

"You charged him for that?"

"The story is currently trapped in a rough cut," Gravitas said. "The original exchange keeps ending at the same point. Hence your repeated Saturday. My company can finish it."

"What company?" Shawna asked.

He handed her a card.

FINAL CUT.

Beneath it:

*We make it matter.*

"You have a phone number?"

"We prefer to be difficult to reach."

Big T leaned on the desk.

"Can you make tomorrow happen?"

"Absolutely."

"Good. I got a fleet inspection Monday. Fourteen vans. Real account. Not money falling out the fucking vents."

"We can give you a Monday," Gravitas said. "The final scene must play through to its ending once. No interruptions. No resets. At midnight, we close the rough cut and the finished world continues."

"And we're done?" Aiden asked.

"One definitive version."

He set the canister on the desk.

A strip of film protruded through a slot in the lid. Tiny frames showed Blue Line, the abandoned lot, a diner Aiden had somehow never noticed, and a little girl holding a balloon.

"Who's the kid?" Tank asked.

"Atmosphere."

"She got a ride home?"

"She represents innocence."

"That's not a ride."

Gravitas returned the canister to its case.

"Tonight. The abandoned lot. A premiere. Your story will finally advance."

Red stepped forward.

"Ticketed?"

"Exclusive."

"I can work with that."

Shawna folded her arms.

"What's the ending?"

"It would be a shame to spoil it."

"We live here."

Gravitas turned to Aiden.

"A word? Privately?"

Big T opened his mouth to refuse. Aiden was already standing.

They went into the hall.

The new gray light made the concrete wall look like something Aiden should have complicated feelings about.

Gravitas spoke quietly.

"You know why they laugh at you?"

"I'm extremely funny?"

"You are willing to say anything."

Aiden looked back toward the office.

Big T was arguing with Red over whether a premiere at the lot counted as trespassing if neither man owned the lot.

"You can have a life before this," Gravitas said. "A name before you chose one. Someone who missed you. You can stop being the joke."

He took a photograph from his coat.

A little boy sat on the hood of a car. A woman's hand rested on his shoulder. Her face had been caught just outside the frame.

Aiden's chest hurt.

It was ridiculous how quickly that happened.

"Is that me?"

"It can be."

"Who took it?"

Gravitas looked at the photograph, as if no one had ever asked a photograph such a discourteous question.

"Would you like to remember?"

Inside the office, Tank said something. Shawna laughed. Aiden wanted to return to the room, but he also wanted the woman to lower her face into view.

Gravitas put the picture in his hand.

"Come tonight. Let the ending work."

When they returned, Big T had discovered the RAIN button.

A localized downpour was soaking Red.

"Stop doing that," Red said.

Big T pressed it again.

"Slipped."

Gravitas recovered the remote, dried it with a black handkerchief, and asked everyone to stand still for the new preview.

A rectangle of light appeared on the wall.

Aiden saw himself, older in a way that seemed principally concerned with facial hair. He stood in the abandoned lot at midnight. Rain ran down his face. Big T lay in his arms.

Aiden watched his own mouth form a cry.

The words were large enough to read from the back of the office.

**NOOOOOOOOO.**

Big T stood up.

"Why am I on the ground?"

The preview cut to a coffin.

"Why am I in the box?"

It cut to Shawna, weeping beside a window.

She pointed at the screen.

"I don't have that window."

It cut to Aiden on a throne made of seized weapons.

Tank squinted.

"That gonna hurt your ass."

Finally, gold letters appeared.

**TEN TOES DOWN: THE COST OF LOYALTY**

**THIS TIME, IT MEANS SOMETHING.**

Big T walked around the desk.

Gravitas closed his case.

"Please keep in mind the trailer is not the complete experience."

"Does the complete experience include me staying alive?"

"Your death is foundational."

"My what?"

Aiden moved between them.

"Wait. I asked for a better story."

"Yes. Your mentor dies. You inherit his burden. Shawna learns to love the man you've become. Red recognizes the emptiness of power. Tank provides one last moment of levity, then falls."

Tank set down his mug.

"Falls how far?"

"The grief unlocks your adulthood," Gravitas told Aiden.

Big T stared at him.

"Nigga, I'm not a fucking coupon."

For the first time, Gravitas appeared offended.

Not by the insult.

By the rhythm.

He clicked the clapboard shut.

A gust of wind filled the office. Loose receipts swirled in beautiful, completely unhelpful spirals. When the papers settled, the chair behind the desk was empty.

Big T was gone.

In his place sat a small folded card.

**YOUR PRESENCE IS REQUESTED AT THE CELEBRATION OF YOUR LIFE.**

On the reverse:

**TONIGHT. MIDNIGHT. THE LOT.**

Darnell picked it up.

"Damn. Even the hostage situation has nice paper."

Aiden lunged for Gravitas.

Gravitas stepped backward through the doorway. Two men in black suits caught Aiden under the arms. They had arrived with the sort of unobtrusive efficiency that requires other people to be unconscious by the end of a scene.

"I cannot compel your performance," Gravitas said. "I can provide the circumstances. At midnight, either we complete the ending, or your unfinished story closes with the loop."

He looked at the walls.

"You will find there is less city as the evening proceeds. Try to be punctual."

The two men released Aiden onto the floor and left.

There was no flash. No magical vanishing. They walked to a tasteful black car and drove away.

Shawna ran outside, read the plate, and took a picture.

Then she got into her truck.

"Where are you going?" Aiden asked.

"After the motherfucker."

"We need a plan."

"I have a truck. That's the first half."

Red was already climbing into the cab.

"Move," he told Aiden. "You're holding up my redemption arc."

## 4. Red Carpet, Blue Blood

They lost Gravitas at the intersection of Sunset and Sunset.

The city had not been built for navigation. It had been built for arriving menacingly.

Aiden directed Shawna left, then left, then left. They passed the same liquor store four times. On the fifth pass, the cashier waved.

"You know him?" Shawna asked.

"That's Rico."

"Since when?"

"Just now. Sign on his shirt."

Red leaned across Tank to study the laptop on Aiden's knees.

"Final Cut. You got an address?"

Darnell, wedged into the truck's rear compartment beside three cones and a roadside emergency kit, had been searching the laptop's open windows.

"They sent an itinerary."

"When?"

"When he clicked the star. They also enrolled him in something."

"Cancel it," Shawna said.

"It says he has to call."

Everyone groaned.

Final Cut Studios stood on a hill behind a pair of enormous gates. Beyond it lay the skyline: glittering towers, palms, smog, and a full moon that looked less convincing the closer they got.

As they approached, a worker on a ladder adjusted one edge of the moon.

Tank rolled down his window.

"Hey."

The worker looked down.

Tank pointed.

"You're crooked."

The worker corrected it.

"Appreciate you."

Aiden stared.

Shawna kept driving.

"You can freak out after we park."

At the gates, a guard in sunglasses held out a hand.

"Invitation."

Red produced his phone.

"Rashad Greene. Executive producer."

Aiden turned toward him.

"You're a what?"

"I agreed to handle promotional opportunities."

"When?"

"There were several quiet seconds while you were looking at the picture."

The guard checked his list.

"Plus four?"

"Creative team," Red said.

The guard looked at the tow truck.

"Very creative."

He waved them through.

Inside, the studio was having a party for itself.

A red carpet ran across a courtyard full of palm trees and men who wore scarves as if their necks were in negotiations. Waiters carried tiny foods on enormous plates. At a photo wall, a man dressed exactly like Aiden kept holding up one finger and asking photographers to get his good side.

"Who the fuck is that?" Aiden asked.

"Your prestige casting," Red said, reading from the itinerary. "A more relatable leading man."

The man turned.

He looked like Aiden, except expensive.

"Don't engage," Shawna said.

Aiden engaged.

"Are you playing me?"

The actor smiled the way people smile when a stranger identifies them as their problem.

"I'm interpreting the emotional terrain."

"Can you interpret a punch?"

"Aiden," Shawna said.

"What research did you do?"

"I spent an afternoon in a parking structure."

Tank nodded once.

"Method."

The actor noticed Tank and briefly changed his mind about several artistic choices.

Aiden took a step forward. Shawna caught his shirt.

"We came here for T."

"Right."

"You can be vain on the way back."

Red disappeared into the party carrying a stack of unauthorized posters. Darnell followed him toward the control rooms. Shawna took Tank and Aiden around the back.

The first studio door opened onto a street exactly like theirs, except cleaner.

The second opened onto a prison.

The third opened onto the prison's award-season interview suite.

The fourth contained Big T.

He sat in a chair on a revolving platform while a woman applied gray to his beard.

A man with a clipboard was asking about his regrets.

"That I let you motherfuckers near my face."

"Something less literal."

"That I didn't swing earlier."

A makeup artist dabbed at his forehead.

"Can you give us a tear?"

"Can you give me my keys?"

Shawna whistled.

Big T looked over.

For one brief, unguarded second, the relief in his face was larger than his anger.

Then he saw Aiden.

"Oh, you brought the problem."

"I'm getting you out."

"Start with the beard. They aged me twelve years. I ain't even ate lunch."

Tank entered first. The man with the clipboard approached him.

"Sir, this is a closed—"

Tank picked him up by the belt, turned ninety degrees, and set him gently behind a velvet rope.

The man looked down at the rope, uncertain whether his authority permitted him to cross it.

"Closed," Tank agreed.

Shawna wheeled Big T off the platform.

Aiden grabbed the nearest box of wipes. He pulled one free and tried to remove the gray from Big T's face.

The wipe sparkled.

Big T's cheek sparkled.

They looked at the packet.

**MEMORIES OF A FATHER: AGEING & SHIMMER KIT.**

"Stop helping," Big T said.

An alarm began.

On a wall monitor, Mr. Gravitas appeared.

"Aiden."

"We're taking him home."

"He has not completed his emotional preparation."

"He's prepared," Shawna said. "His emotion is pissed."

Two black-suited men entered from the far door. Then two more.

Tank looked down at Big T's rolling chair.

"Can you keep your arms in?"

"Why?"

Tank pushed.

The chair flew across the studio.

Big T hit the first guard at knee height. The guard folded onto Big T's lap. They hit the second guard together. The platform chair spun, collecting an extension cord, a curtain, and the clipboard man, who had finally crossed the rope at precisely the wrong moment.

"BRAKES!" Big T shouted.

"Those are your feet!" Tank called.

Shawna drove a wheeled rack of ceremonial jackets into the remaining guards. Aiden ducked a punch, tried to deliver a right hook, and struck a coat hanger.

The pain was immediate and honest.

"Ow! Fuck!"

**AIDEN SHRUGGED OFF THE BLOW.**

"No I didn't!"

He ducked again and followed Shawna through the service door.

Big T and the chair crashed into a stack of foam tombstones.

On the top one was his own face.

Below it:

**HE TAUGHT US HOW TO STAND.**

Big T read it, sat up, and broke it over a guard's head.

"Then learn."

They burst into a corridor marked ARCHIVE / CONTINUITY.

Red and Darnell were coming from the opposite direction. Red wore a black lanyard and an expression of financial injury. Darnell carried a metal case, two cables, and a bowl of tiny sausages.

"I found how they do it," Darnell said.

"I found out gross isn't net," Red said. "We are all victims today."

Behind them, the alarm lowered by an octave.

Gravitas's voice filled the hall.

"You are confusing movement with plot."

Shawna looked back.

"Catch up, then."

They ran.

## 5. The Backstory Department

At the end of the corridor, a security shutter slammed down.

Tank hit the button beside it.

Nothing.

He hit it again, harder.

Nothing.

"Good diagnostic work," Red said.

Tank looked at him.

Red moved away from the shutter.

Darnell pointed to a side door.

**BACKSTORIES. AUTHORIZED PAIN ONLY.**

Big T kicked it open.

Inside were shelves, floor to ceiling, stacked with boxes.

Some boxes were labeled ABSENT FATHER. Others read PRESENT FATHER, WORSE. A discounted pallet held DEAD WIVES WITH TALENTED HUSBANDS.

Shawna stopped in front of it.

"That's some nasty-ass inventory."

## 6. The Ditch

They came out of the maintenance corridor into a room full of interchangeable nightclubs.

"Play that."

"That's your own track."

"Well spotted."

"Management says cello until eleven."

The beat dropped.

Three men holding whiskey glasses began, against all their recent expectations, to have a good time.

Aiden's voice.

Not the one in the sky. Not the one Aiden used to talk his way into trouble.

*At first, I could make everyone applaud. I found this reassuring. I mistook it for being good at something.*

*Then someone asked for a story worth remembering.*

### The story Grant told: The Man in the Sack

The fortress stood on an island so small that its governor had to borrow space from the sea to feel important.

Every morning he walked along the wall, looked at the water, and congratulated himself on the absence of visitors.

In the lowest cell lived a prisoner called Grant, who had been convicted of expecting an answer from someone above him.

His neighbor was a thief named Candle.

"Because you shine in the darkness?" Grant asked through the wall.

"Because I stole candles."

"Ah."

"They keep wanting it to be a metaphor."

For seven years, Candle dug toward Grant with a spoon.

At the end of the seventh year, he broke through the wall.

He looked around Grant's cell.

"Fuck."

They stared at one another.

"Where were you trying to go?" Grant asked.

"Outside."

"You came a bit left."

Candle sat on the floor. He was old, dirty, and so disappointed that Grant began laughing before he could stop himself.

Candle looked offended.

Then he looked at the spoon.

Then he laughed too.

It was the first thing in the fortress that had been done without permission.

After that, they shared everything they had: half a loaf, a little water, a window too high to reach, and the enormous insult of having done seven years' work toward a second prison.

Candle knew a thousand stories. Most had been stolen. He told them slowly because there were a thousand mornings left.

One was a play he had once performed while hiding from an innkeeper.

"Did you write it?" Grant asked.

"I wrote down that it was mine."

He called the play *The Visitor in Yellow*.

### Candle's play: The Visitor in Yellow, the surviving scene

*An empty throne. A bell. A locked door. Behind the door, the sea can be heard arranging itself.*

THE KING: Who is there?

THE VISITOR: A messenger.

THE KING: What do you carry?

THE VISITOR: The ending you ordered.

THE KING: Is the prisoner dead?

THE VISITOR: The prisoner has left his cell.

THE KING: That is not an answer.

THE VISITOR: It is the only answer you paid for.

*The King rings the bell. Nothing happens.*

THE KING: Why does nobody come?

THE VISITOR: You dismissed the guards for knowing too much.

THE KING: And the servants?

THE VISITOR: You dismissed them for knowing too little.

THE KING: And the audience?

THE VISITOR: You dismissed them for laughing.

*The King lowers the bell.*

THE KING: Then who is listening?

THE VISITOR: Open the door.

THE KING: Show me your face.

THE VISITOR: Open the door. I have delivered what you asked for.

*The King opens the door.*

### The Man in the Sack, continued

"And then?" Grant asked.

Candle shrugged.

"That's when the innkeeper came back."

"You don't know who's outside?"

"Whoever gets cast."

"You can't just stop a story there."

Candle lay down on his blanket.

"Watch me."

The next morning, he did not wake up.

Grant spent an hour trying to make this the old man's next joke.

He pushed the blanket. He said Candle's name in several tones, as if one would prove difficult enough to ignore. Then he sat beside him and used the spoon to tap on the floor, once, twice, three times.

Nobody answered.

For the first time, Grant hated the sea.

It kept doing the thing it did yesterday.

When the guards came, they gave him a sack and told him to sew Candle inside it.

The fortress permitted only two kinds of departure: the governor's boat, and the dead dropped through a hatch into the water.

Grant stitched slowly.

At Candle's shoulder, he stopped.

The sack was large enough for a man.

The door was guarded by men who did not want to look closely at a body.

He moved Candle onto his own bed. He dressed him in his own jacket. He got into the sack with the spoon and pulled the last stitches shut from inside.

When the guards returned, Grant held still.

They lifted him.

One complained that death made people heavy.

Grant almost laughed. He was terrified that he would.

Up the steps. Through a corridor he had heard for twelve years and never seen. Past a kitchen smelling of onions. Out into a wind large enough to have come from anywhere.

At the hatch, they set him down.

He heard the sea below.

Then the governor spoke.

"Wait."

Boots approached.

A blade touched the sack beside Grant's throat.

"Show me his face."

## 8. Ten Toes, Nine Problems
