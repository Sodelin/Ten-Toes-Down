# BOOK I: BLOOD MONEY AND BLUE DIAMONDS

## 1. The Funeral Had a Two-Drink Minimum

“Nigga, it’s my motherfucking funeral.”

The man taking tickets didn’t look up.

“Forty dollars.”

“I’m Aiden.”

He looked at me. Looked at the six-foot photograph beside the door. Looked back.

“You look better in the picture.”

“Nigga, I’m alive.”

“Then you can pay.”

Vegas. After the homecoming. I had been back long enough for these niggas to start charging admission to my problems.

Somebody had put angel wings on a photograph of me getting arrested. Cropped out the cuffs, left the officer’s hand on my shoulder. Nigga had a police escort into heaven.

I called Tank.

“Why there a cover charge at my funeral?”

“The shrimp was expensive.”

“Why do I have shrimp?”

“You died successful.”

I heard chewing.

“Nigga, are you eating?”

“Grieving.”

I paid the forty. The nigga stamped DECEASED on my wrist.

“That gets you one well drink.”

“One? Nigga, the sign says two-drink minimum.”

“Second one’s your responsibility.”

A nigga behind me shook his head.

“Hold up. Ain’t that the deceased?”

Ticket man looked at my stamp.

“Paid deceased.”

The nigga pulled out his phone.

“Can I get a picture?”

“Of me alive?”

“People gonna think I knew you personally.”

“Nigga, you meeting me personally.”

“Yeah, but the funeral picture hit different.”

I kept walking.

***

Shawna found me beside the chocolate fountain, turning my memorial program upside down.

“Why this say I’m survived by eleven children?”

“Those are sponsors.”

“Nigga, one of them’s Jeremiah.”

“Jeremiah’s Collision Repair.”

My wife looked beautiful in black. Slit up one leg, diamond at her throat, hat tilted like she had already forgiven herself.

“You enjoying widowhood?”

“The parking is excellent.”

I pulled her close. She put two fingers against my mouth.

“Eight minutes. Kitchen. Stay there until we get the case.”

“Nigga, I could take it right now.”

“And Silas’s men could start shooting into everybody who paid to eat shrimp.”

“Apparently excellent shrimp.”

“Aiden.”

I straightened up.

Mercedes stood beside the stage in a red suit, thumb pressed into her palm. She’d been doing that all week. Silas Gold had stolen the original recordings of her first album. The nigga had locked the drive in a silver case and invited buyers to bid. She could sing every song again. She couldn’t rebuild the demolished studio, recall every musician to the same night, or make her voice crack exactly where it had in the last chorus.

She had left that crack in. Said it sounded like her.

Silas lived behind bulletproof glass and sent other niggas out to be unpleasant. Tell him Aiden was dead, though, and suddenly the motherfucker wanted fresh air.

Shawna had promised a private auction after the memorial. My watch, my cars, the collection. Silas would bring the recordings so they could settle everything together.

Mercedes would inspect the case backstage. Porsche would switch it. I would stay in the fucking kitchen.

Mercedes caught me looking and came to fix my collar.

“Nigga, don’t do the thing.”

“Which thing?”

She looked around my funeral.

“The thing where this becomes about you.”

“My photograph is six feet tall.”

“Exactly. The nigga has enough publicity.”

I stopped smiling.

“You’ll get your music.”

“I know. I want to enjoy getting it.”

She squeezed my shoulder and went back to the stage.

Shawna kissed me.

“Kitchen.”

“Nigga, y’all got one word for me tonight.”

She turned me toward the door.

***

They had hired a full band. Horns, two keyboards, drummer wearing sunglasses he hadn’t removed for either prayer.

Tank took the microphone carrying a plate.

“We gathered here because my brother would want these niggas supporting the bar.”

Glasses went up.

“Aiden was generous. Nigga gave me the shirt off his back.”

I leaned toward the kitchen speaker.

“Police was looking for a man in a red shirt.”

The room broke open.

I pushed through the kitchen door. Shawna held up one finger without turning around.

Back I went.

“But my nigga came back,” Tank said.

The room quieted.

“Came to the station. Told them I hadn’t done shit. Paid everything. Waited six hours. Bought me breakfast.”

My palm rested against the door.

Tank sniffed.

“Then the nigga asked for his shirt.”

The drummer caught the laugh with a roll. I had to smile. Nigga knew when to hit.

Then somebody booed.

Silas Gold entered in a white fur coat. Four men followed him. Smallest nigga carried the silver case. Biggest had a neck wider than my thigh and an expression like somebody assembled him without the last page.

Silas raised a bottle.

“Miss the good part?”

Tank looked at the closed coffin.

“Depends how this speech goes.”

Silas laughed. His niggas joined a second late.

Shawna offered her hand. He bent to kiss it. She moved it just enough that his mouth met his own thumb.

“The recordings.”

“After my tribute.”

“Mercedes inspects them first. We agreed.”

“Darling, look at the crowd. Can’t buy timing.”

He took the microphone from Tank and balanced his bottle on my coffin.

Tank handed it back.

Silas put it down.

Tank handed it back.

The drummer gave them a little tick, tick, tick.

Silas stopped smiling.

“You got somewhere to be?”

“Nigga, eventually.”

Silas faced the crowd.

“Aiden and I had differences.”

From the kitchen I mouthed, *Nigga, you stole my boat.*

“But he was a man of appetite.”

He looked at Shawna.

“Appetite leaves things unfinished. People unprovided for. I would never presume—”

“Then don’t.”

A woman slapped her table.

“That nigga got instructions!”

Silas waited out the laughter with his teeth showing.

“Mercedes. Up here.”

She didn’t move.

The case opened. Silas put his bottle on the coffin and took out the hard drive.

“Perhaps our little lady could sing a tribute. And apologize for some things she’s said about me.”

Mercedes walked toward the steps.

“You promised an inspection.”

“Sing first.”

The biggest nigga blocked the backstage doorway. Porsche stood behind him with the duplicate case.

Silas offered Mercedes the microphone. Pulled it back.

“Ask nicely.”

I took off my jacket.

The cook looked up from his potatoes.

“Nigga, you want me to hold that?”

“Please.”

“You coming back?”

I looked through the round window.

“Apparently I do.”

***

I walked out carrying shrimp.

Shawna saw my hands, looked at the stage, and shifted her chair clear of the aisle.

Tank set down his plate.

A woman near the aisle looked from me to the coffin.

“Lord.”

The nigga beside her crossed himself.

“Don't do that,” she whispered. “He got shrimp.”

“Could still be spiritual.”

“Nigga, move your chair.”

They moved. I passed between them without losing a shrimp.

From the stage, the drummer lifted one stick and watched me approach.

I stopped beside the smallest nigga.

“Shrimp?”

He took one.

“Sauce?”

He nodded.

“Help yourself, my nigga.”

Silas heard me. Turned so fast the fur collar slapped his mouth.

He stared.

Forty dollars. Worth it.

“You—”

“Nigga, it’s my motherfucking funeral.”

He shoved the microphone into its stand and reached inside his coat.

I drove the tray under his wrist. The pistol fired through the ceiling, flew from his hand, and skidded under the piano. Plaster burst over the stage. The trumpet player ducked. Tank tackled two guests behind a table without spilling his drink.

I caught Silas by the coat.

“Nigga, everybody else brought flowers!”

He grabbed his bottle from the coffin and swung. Glass broke against my shoulder. Pain ran down my arm.

“Motherfucker.”

I hit him with the tray.

The drive flew from his other hand. Mercedes caught it against her chest.

“Got it!”

The biggest nigga lunged. Porsche hooked his ankle with the duplicate case. He stumbled down the steps into the chair Shawna had picked up.

The chair lost.

So did his teeth.

He sat carefully among the pieces.

“Stay,” my wife said.

Nigga stayed.

Mercedes tucked the drive inside her jacket. Behind me, another man drew a gun. I saw him in the mirrored wall and ducked as she threw the microphone stand over my head with both hands.

It hit the nigga across the chest and carried him into the drum kit.

The crash filled the room.

The drummer stood. Looked at his broken cymbal. Looked at the man under it.

“Nigga, that’s vintage.”

Then he hit him with the stool.

Silas wriggled free of his coat. Ran for the steps. Tank caught him around the waist, flung open the coffin with his free hand, and tipped him inside.

The coffin rocked. Lid banged shut.

Something squealed.

Tank froze.

“Shit.”

The lid flew up.

A little man in a gold tracksuit sat upright underneath Silas, wearing my spare sunglasses and looking ready to fight the entire funeral.

“NIGGA, I TOLD YOU NINE-FIFTEEN!”

I stared at Tank.

Tank stared at his shoes.

“You hired a corpse?”

“Resurrection package.”

“Nigga.”

“It included the dance.”

The little man pointed at Tank.

“And fog.”

Tank pointed at the dust hanging over the stage.

“Nigga, we got atmosphere.”

“That’s ceiling.”

“Nigga, work with the venue.”

The little man climbed over Silas’s stomach and straightened his tracksuit.

“I do the surprise. I do the dance. I leave. Ain’t no nigga mentioned wrestling.”

Silas’s fourth man backed toward the exit. Tank pointed at him without looking away from me.

“Sit your ass down. The nigga hasn’t danced.”

He sat.

The smallest man still held the empty case and a shrimp tail. He lowered the case very slowly.

“I’m with catering.”

From the kitchen: “No the fuck you are not.”

***

Seven minutes to collect the guns, tie up Silas’s men, and convince everybody the bar remained open.

Silas kept trying to sit up in the coffin. The little man eased him down with a sneaker against his forehead.

“Stop touching my face.”

“Stop being in my office, nigga.”

At the sound desk, Mercedes took the drive from her jacket and plugged it in. Files appeared. She chose one, listened through the headphones, and closed her eyes.

Porsche took her hand beneath the table.

I moved closer. Shawna caught my belt and pulled me back.

We waited.

Mercedes removed the headphones and wiped under one eye.

“All here.”

Tank breathed out.

“Then play one, my nigga.”

She looked around. Overturned flowers. Two men bleeding into memorial napkins. My giant face leaning crookedly against the wall.

Then she went to the stage.

The drummer was fitting a spare cymbal.

“You good?”

“You doing something slow?”

“No.”

He sat.

“Then yeah.”

She gave the sound man a track number. A hiss came through the speakers, then saxophone, warm and rough. The band found its rhythm.

Mercedes pointed at my portrait.

“Turn that nigga around. I need the room.”

I turned it myself.

She sang low at first, almost speaking. Bass came underneath. She planted a heel against the monitor and let the next line climb. By the chorus, people stood between fallen chairs finding somebody to dance with.

The little man shut the coffin and started dancing on it.

“Get off!” Silas shouted.

“Nine-fifteen, bitch!”

Tank raised both hands.

“That nigga earned his deposit!”

Porsche laughed with her face turned up. The cook came out with my jacket over his shoulder and a tray in his hands. A woman took the tray, put it on a table, and pulled him onto the floor.

“Nigga, I’m working.”

“Not anymore.”

Shawna slipped her fingers into mine.

“You’re bleeding.”

“Little bit.”

She touched my sleeve. I winced.

“We’ll get it looked at.”

“After the song.”

She stayed against my good side.

The last chorus came. Mercedes’s younger voice caught for half a breath through the speakers. Onstage, she sang alongside it, smiling. Porsche stopped laughing to listen.

Shawna leaned to my ear.

“Eleven sponsors is a lot of mouths to feed.”

I laughed and hurt my shoulder again.

The ticket man opened the door for another group.

“Sixty dollars.”

I pointed.

“Nigga, it was forty!”

He nodded toward Mercedes, the man dancing on the coffin, and me.

“Live show now.”

***

By the time the police took Silas away, the band had demanded overtime and Tank was negotiating for the leftover shrimp.

The young sound technician came over with two clean glasses. Twenty-three, little beard, still looking between Shawna and my DECEASED wrist stamp.

“Can I ask something?”

“Nigga, apparently you can charge for it.”

“How’d you two meet?”

My wife laughed before I opened my mouth.

“Let me tell it,” she said.

“Baby, I was there.”

“So was I. One of us remembers who saved whom.”

The technician pulled up a chair.

“Somebody get this nigga a plate,” Tank called.

“I'm on the clock.”

“So am I. I organized a death.”

Mercedes passed the young man a napkin. Porsche moved the bottle closer. He loosened the headphones around his neck and sat.

Tank dragged over another chair. Shawna sat against my good side and took one of the glasses.

I looked at the nigga who had asked.

“First night in Vegas. You want the whole thing?”

He nodded.

Shawna settled in.

“Start before you became reasonable.”

“Nigga, we still waiting on that chapter.”

Tank laughed into his shrimp.

I took a drink. Back then, before any of these niggas knew what we'd become, the bass was shaking a different wall. Big T had his hand on my shoulder, and twelve men waited beside the cars.

## 2. First Night in Vegas

The night I met Shawna, I had cocaine on my shirt and another man's blood inside my watch. Nigga, inside. I could wash the shirt. Every time I checked the hour, the watch reminded me I needed to apologize to somebody or finish the job.

I was twenty-four. My last car had burned outside my ex-boyfriend's apartment. He still thought that meant I wanted him back. Before sunrise I would steal three million dollars, leave with a woman every killer in Vegas wanted, and catch a bullet that would stay in me for the rest of my life.

First, twelve niggas wanted to beat my ass behind a nightclub.

Big T led me out while the bass was shaking the exit door. Blue rags, gold chains, cars parked crooked. I knew some of the faces. Niggas who borrowed ten dollars with both hands now stood there warming up to break something I needed.

T squeezed my shoulder.

"Once a nigga in, that nigga in. Understand?"

"Nigga, I understand."

"Summer ends, a body drops, suddenly a nigga got somewhere else to be."

"I got nowhere else to be. That's why I'm standing here instead of enjoying the fucking music."

Somebody laughed. T didn't.

"You think this funny?"

"Nigga, I think twelve is a committee. Which one of you takes the minutes?"

He hit me against a Cadillac.

That answered it. T took the minutes.

Then the other eleven arrived with amendments.

I caught a fist in the mouth and an elbow behind my ear. Somebody kicked my leg out. Concrete scraped my palms. I got up with blood in one eye and swung at whatever the good eye could find. Nigga, I wanted in. I wanted somewhere to put my name where a motherfucker wouldn't cross it out when I left the room.

"Stay down," somebody said.

"Nigga, y'all just told me to stay IN. Pick an instruction."

Tank came under my guard and hit me in the ribs. Twenty-eight, twice my size, a big nigga with a shirt that had already lost its argument with his shoulders. I grabbed that shirt and drove him into another car. The alarm went off.

"Not the paint!" somebody shouted.

"Nigga, you should've thought about the paint before you started throwing people at it!"

Tank tried to turn me. I kept hitting him.

"Get this nigga off me!"

Big T laughed. Nobody helped. Tank looked past my shoulder, betrayed by eleven niggas and an alarm manufacturer.

"T!"

"You said he was little."

"Nigga, I have new information!"

T pulled me away before Tank could kill me for making him say that. I stood with my fists up, shaking so hard I couldn't open them.

"Enough," T said. "Twelve niggas. Look at him. Still shopping."

"Nigga, I'm done," I said.

"You sure?"

"I'm waiting for my hands to hear it."

He took a gold chain from his pocket and lowered it over my head. A little cross settled against my chest. The niggas around us stopped grinning.

"My brother wore that," he said. "Real nigga."

I looked up.

"Where my nigga at, then? I should thank him."

"Dead."

The car alarm finally stopped. I could hear T breathing.

"Nigga, don't make me bury you too."

I closed my hand around the cross. A minute earlier he'd been punching me. Now I was standing there trying not to cry because this motherfucker had found a way to call me family.

"Not this nigga," I said.

He kissed my forehead and pointed toward the club.

"Come get your money. Niggas, make room for my son."

Tank held the door. I tried to help him with it.

"Nigga, walk through," he said. "I can still defeat a door."

I walked through.

Inside, everything was blue light and smoke. The back room was packed with niggas counting stacks of cash. Bricks of cocaine covered a glass table. Bottles of Hennessy stood between them. Three women in diamonds were drinking with a man who had two pistols tucked into the same belt. One of them lifted her glass when she saw my face.

"Damn, baby. You survive a war?"

"Yeah," I said. "And I won."

They cheered. Somebody pushed a bottle into my hand. Somebody else handed me a blunt already burning. I drank, smoked, wiped the blood off my lip, and sat down beside a pile of money bigger than anything I had ever seen in my life.

"This the nigga beat Tank?" one of the women asked.

"That's the nigga," T said.

She pushed a folded bill into my shirt pocket. A thousand dollars. I'd been back inside for ninety seconds and a woman was paying me to be handsome with a broken face. That's the kind of night it was.

Tank came in carrying a bag of ice against his jaw. He took the bottle out my hand, drank, and gave it back.

"Nigga, who taught you to fight dirty like that?"

"Same nigga taught you to fight unconscious."

The room went crazy. Tank stared at me long enough I thought we was going again. Then that big nigga started laughing too, blood on his teeth, and pulled a chair up beside mine. We drank from the same bottle. I would've trusted him to hold my heart while I fought with both hands.

T threw me the keys to a black Mercedes.

"That's yours."

I looked at the keys.

"Nigga, these keys real? You serious?"

"West side need a nigga. West side got you."

An hour earlier I hadn't even been in the crew. Now the west side was mine. That's how fast life moved when you were ready for it. Some men spent their whole lives waiting on a chance. I took mine with blood still coming out of my mouth.

T opened a photograph on his phone.

A woman stood beside a swimming pool at night. Black dress. Diamond earrings. Dark hair over one shoulder. She wasn't smiling. Behind her, the whole Strip was lit up, but I was looking at her.

"Shawna," T said. "Twenty-six. Works for Red."

"Nigga, working for Red doing what?"

"Whatever Red wants."

Tank watched me from across the table.

T kept talking.

"She's at the Saint tonight. Penthouse. She's bringing a case down at eleven. You take her and the money to my house in Henderson. That's all you gotta do."

"Why she leaving him?"

"Because she finally got smart."

I looked at the picture again.

"Nigga, does Red know about this little family pickup?"

"If he knew, we wouldn't be having this conversation."

T gripped the back of my neck and pulled me close enough that nobody else could hear.

"Don't fall in love with her."

I laughed.

"I'm serious. Look at me."

"Nigga, look at me. Do I look like the nigga falls in love?"

He let go of me.

I believed that shit when I said it. Nigga, remember that. I meant it with my entire chest.

***

The Saint had a gold fountain outside and bulletproof glass on the top floor. Red owned the penthouse, the nightclub underneath it, and enough people in the building that nobody asked questions when his men dragged somebody into a service elevator. Nigga had built himself a palace where the carpets could swallow a scream.

I parked the Mercedes right in front.

The valet looked at the blood drying on my collar.

"You need somebody to call a doctor?"

"Nigga, my car needs you first. Nothing happens to it."

I gave him a hundred and went inside.

Upstairs, the penthouse was going crazy. Music pounding. Cocaine all over the tables. Niggas gambling with money that was still wrapped from the bank. Hookers in dresses that cost more than the cars outside, laughing with men who never gave their real names. Everybody was rich. Everybody was armed. Every nigga thought the night belonged to him.

A man in a white fur coat was doing coke off a trophy. It was September in Vegas. I didn't know what the trophy was for, but that nigga had brought it with him and was guarding it like the whole room wanted his championship.

I knew a woman by the bar, Mercedes, thirty, who had once left me naked in a hotel room and taken my shoes because I'd called her by her sister's name. She looked down at my new keys.

"You finally got a Mercedes you can afford, baby?"

"Come see it."

"Nigga, you still owe me flowers."

I gave her five hundred out the money in my pocket. "Buy the whole fucking garden."

She kissed my cheek. "You bleeding on the good side of your face."

"Both sides good, baby."

She laughed, and I left her with it. I was shining. I had a dead man's gold against my chest, a living man's keys in my hand, and no idea how much that shit could cost.

I had been with men and women all over that city. Big men who put their guns down gentle when they came into my room. Women who left lipstick on the champagne glasses and somebody else's diamonds on my pillow. I thought I knew every kind of trouble a beautiful person could bring into a room.

Then I saw Shawna.

She stood by the window with a cigarette in one hand and a silver case beside her ankle. She wore the same black dress from the picture. There was a little scar above her eyebrow. Somebody was talking to her, but she was looking straight at me.

I walked over. My mouth hurt, my ribs hurt, and every other woman I'd ever wanted fell clean out of my fucking head.

"T sent me."

"I know."

"How?"

She looked at the cross on my chest.

Her cigarette stopped halfway to her mouth.

"Where'd you get that?"

"T."

"Tonight?"

"Yeah."

She reached out and turned the cross over.

On the back were three little letters I hadn't noticed.

D. B. M.

Her fingers tightened around it.

"You know what those mean?"

"No."

She let it fall against my shirt.

"Drive before you find out."

I took the case. She took my hand. We walked through the party together while men who had been smiling a second earlier started reaching for their phones.

At the elevator, one of Red's guys stepped in front of us.

"Nigga, where the fuck you taking her?"

"With this nigga right here. You need my picture?"

"Nigga, I'm asking her."

I hit him in the throat. He folded against the wall, and I shoved him down before he could get his hand inside his jacket. Shawna pressed the elevator button. The doors opened. Nigga was still looking at me like he couldn't believe I'd touched him. I stepped over his shoes.

Another man came around the corner.

She drew a little silver pistol from her purse and pointed it at his chest.

"You get one chance," she said.

He stopped.

The doors shut between us.

She didn't put the gun away until we reached the lobby.

I looked at her.

"Niggas chasing you out of every party, or I got the special one?"

"Only the ones I survive."

I was already in love with her. I just didn't know how bad it was going to get.

***

The first car came after us before I had learned where the fucking seat warmer was.

Black Charger. No lights. Two men in it and a third leaning across the back seat with something long in his hands. Shawna saw them in her mirror before I did.

"Aiden. Don't look."

Naturally I looked. Nigga, I had owned a Mercedes for eleven minutes. I was checking everything that got close to it.

The first shots came through the rear glass.

Shawna dropped against the silver case. I ducked and the steering wheel jerked under my hands. We clipped a fountain with the right fender, threw white water over three gambling executives, and hit the street with the horn stuck screaming.

"Your niggas?" I shouted.

"You think I'd ride with you if those were my niggas?"

"You met me tonight!"

"I'm trying to meet you tomorrow! Drive!"

I drove.

A yellow Ferrari came sideways out of the next street and joined the Charger. The man driving the Ferrari had an expression like he'd paid extra to kill somebody comfortably. He came level with my window and smiled while his passenger raised a pistol.

I looked at Shawna.

"Baby, move your hand."

"What?"

"The horn."

She lifted her wrist. The screaming stopped. For one beautiful second all I could hear was that Ferrari and my own heart getting ready to become somebody's problem.

I turned toward the yellow car.

The passenger's face changed first. His gun went up. The driver tried to go with it. Yellow metal struck a street pole, and the Ferrari spun across a valet entrance spraying sparks over a hundred thousand dollars' worth of men's shoes.

"Nigga!" Shawna said.

"That's one."

"That's a Ferrari!"

"That's one Ferrari. Keep up."

The Charger stayed on us. I could see the long gun moving behind its windshield. Then a red convertible shot out of a hotel drive between us, and Mercedes stood up in the passenger seat with her heels in one hand.

"I told you about my fucking flowers, Aiden!"

Porsche was driving. Twenty-nine, dark red hair, red dress, eyes fixed on the road as if she had personally laid it. I'd met her once at a party where a rich man had bought her a sports car and cried when she actually drove away in it. She had kept both the car and his apology.

"Sit down, Mercedes!" she shouted.

"He owes me!"

"Nigga, he owes the whole street right now!"

Mercedes dropped as the red car swept across the Charger's nose. The black car braked hard enough to lift at the back. Shawna watched the women go by with her mouth open.

"Your friends?"

"Good people."

"Your good people have terrible timing."

"Look behind us."

She looked. The Charger was trying to pass Porsche. Porsche wouldn't let it. Every time its nose appeared, that red convertible slid over with her, carrying two furious beautiful women who had decided nobody was dying until their night got better.

Shawna laughed once. It was the first happy sound I'd heard from her. It went through me harder than the gunshots.

Then the Charger hit their rear bumper.

Porsche's car swung. Mercedes grabbed the windshield frame. I saw her heels fly into the air, one silver shoe catching a streetlight before it disappeared into somebody's outdoor dinner.

I braked.

"Aiden!"

"Not leaving them."

The Charger filled my mirror. I could see the driver now, big white scar across his eyebrow, teeth showing as he tried to decide whether he wanted me or the women more.

"Nigga, choose me," I said.

I dropped behind the red car, and the Charger hit us instead.

Metal folded. My head struck the seat. Shawna's hand found my arm and held. The Mercedes kept rolling, its rear bumper screaming along the ground, and Porsche caught her car before it reached the wall. She lifted one finger without looking back. Not thank you. Something stronger.

The boulevard opened ahead. A transport truck crossed the intersection carrying a silver speedboat high above its cab. Beside it, the delivery ramp of a closed hotel pointed up toward a black ribbon of roof.

I looked at the ramp.

Shawna looked at me.

"No."

"You don't know what I'm thinking."

"Nigga, I know exactly what you're thinking."

"Then hold the case."

We hit the ramp so hard the sound disappeared.

My stomach went cold. The street fell below the windshield. For a second the whole fucking Strip lay under us, beautiful and useless, all those men spending their lives trying to buy a view while I flew through theirs with a woman on my arm and a three-million-dollar suitcase on her knees.

Shawna screamed my name.

I would've stayed in the air forever to hear it like that.

We cleared the speedboat by less than the height of its flag. The Mercedes landed on the far side with a crash that drove my teeth together. A tire exploded. The car leaned, caught, kept moving. Behind us the Charger tried the same turn too late and went sideways into the truck's empty rear trailer.

I heard the collision through our broken windows.

Shawna twisted around. A black wheel rolled through the intersection by itself.

The nigga wasn't following us anymore.

I slowed. My hands stayed steady on the wheel, though my whole chest was jumping. Porsche came alongside at the next light. Mercedes had one shoe left and looked ready to beat God with it.

"You jumped a fucking boat!" Porsche shouted.

"Had to get over it somehow."

"Aiden, nigga, you jumped the boat with a car!"

Behind her, a man on the sidewalk started clapping. Then the couple beside him. Then a whole row of people waiting for taxis, strangers putting down luggage to salute the greatest driver Vegas had ever fucking seen.

I lifted my hand.

Shawna caught it and put it back on the wheel.

"They can admire you moving."

Mercedes pointed at her. "Keep him. He's expensive, but nigga, he comes with a show."

Porsche blew me a kiss and turned away, her convertible carrying one silver heel and two women who'd just made themselves family. I took us toward the desert on a damaged tire and the kind of pride a man could drive forever on.

Shawna reached across and touched my cheek.

"You could've kept going when they hit those girls."

"Wouldn't have been me."

She watched me another second. Then she put her hand on my leg and left it there.

Nigga, I could feel that hand all the way down to the tire.

***

We got out past the last casinos before she told me to pull over.

"T's house is farther out," I said.

"I know where his fucking house is. Pull over."

I took the Mercedes off the road behind a shuttered wedding chapel. Half the sign was burned out. The parking lot was empty. Shawna opened the case on her lap.

Money filled it from one end to the other.

"Three million," she said.

I stared. Three million fucking dollars. That afternoon I'd been eating a gas-station sandwich in a borrowed car. Now this woman was opening a fortune in my Mercedes.

"T said—"

"T says a lot."

She took an envelope from beneath the cash. Inside was a photograph, bent across the middle.

Shawna stood in front of the same chapel, three years younger, wearing a white dress. A man stood beside her with his arms around her waist.

The cross on his neck was the one T had put on mine.

"Dante," she said. "His brother. My husband."

I looked from the photograph to her face. My nigga, I'd been carrying her dead husband against my chest and talking like the whole world started when I walked in.

She took the cigarette from behind her ear, tried to light it, and couldn't get the lighter to work.

I did it for her.

She watched the flame for a second before she leaned into it.

"I was twenty-three when I came out here. Thought I was going to sing. I had a suitcase, eight hundred dollars, and a number some girl gave me in Phoenix."

She looked out at the chapel.

"The number was Valentino's."

I knew the name. Everybody did. Valentino ran women through half the hotels on the Strip. He wore white suits and had a diamond in one of his teeth. Niggas laughed around him like he was funny. Women watched his hands.

"He bought me clothes. Got me a room. Told me he had people in music. By the time I knew what the fuck was happening, everything I had belonged to him."

She blew smoke against the windshield.

"Dante came into the club on my birthday. Everybody else wanted something. He asked what kind of cake I liked."

Her mouth moved like she was going to smile.

She didn't.

"He came back with it the next night. Carrot cake. Whole fucking cake. Put it on the table between the bottles. Valentino laughed at him. Dante knocked out that diamond tooth."

"My nigga," I said before I could stop myself.

"That's what I said." She looked at the photograph. "That's exactly what I fucking said."

She wiped one eye with her thumb, angry that she had to.

"We got married here six weeks later. T stood beside him. Red paid for the room. They all smiled in the pictures."

I looked at the gold cross in my hand.

"What happened?"

"Dante took money out of a deal. Three million. Told me we were going to leave. Said he had a brother, so he didn't have to be scared."

She turned the photograph over.

There was writing on the back.

*Damn, boy. Magnificent.*

"That's what the letters mean," she said. "Damn, boy. Magnificent. He looked in the chapel mirror after we got married and said it like God had just finished him. I laughed so hard I nearly dropped my flowers. Next morning I had it put on the cross."

"Nigga was right about you."

"He was talking about himself."

I looked down at the gold against my chest.

"Then the nigga finally got it on the right neck."

She laughed before she could stop herself. One bright, startled sound in the middle of all that hurt. She covered her mouth, still holding the photograph, and looked at me like I had just brought something she thought was gone into the car with us.

She held the photograph against her chest.

"They left him in the desert. Valentino brought me his watch. Told me Dante died begging for somebody to take care of me."

I wanted to kill a man I had never even met. Nigga was already dead, and I wanted another turn.

"What did you do?"

"Waited."

Her voice got quieter.

"For eight months, I waited. Valentino thought I came back because I had nowhere else to go. He thought I loved him by the end."

She looked straight at me.

"Last Tuesday, I shot him in his car. He was talking when I did it. He kept talking after the first shot, so I shot him again. Then I took Dante's watch out of his glove compartment."

She showed me the watch on her wrist.

It was too big for her.

"Red took me in. Said he'd protect me. Tonight I took the three million back."

"And T?"

"T says he's going to get me out of Vegas."

I thought about the way he'd told me not to love her. Nigga knew what she was carrying besides the case.

I thought about the cross.

"You believe him?" I asked.

"I believed his brother."

She put the photograph inside her dress and closed the case.

Then she leaned across the seat and kissed me.

I forgot about the money. I forgot whose car I was driving. All I knew was her hand against my face and the taste of smoke on her mouth. When she pulled back, she was still holding on to me. My nigga, I could feel her trying to decide if I was another room she couldn't leave.

"Don't take me to that house," she said.

"Then this nigga ain't taking you. Give me somewhere else."

Just like that. My first night in the family, and I was ready to go to war with every fucking one of them.

I took her to a motel outside town and paid for the whole top floor.

The clerk looked at the stack of money in my hand.

"All six rooms?"

"All six. Nobody comes up."

Upstairs, Shawna put the case on the bed and her gun on the nightstand. I locked the door. We could hear trucks on the highway and somebody arguing with a woman in the parking lot below.

She unbuttoned my shirt to look at what Tank had done to my ribs.

"Jesus, Aiden."

"I'm good."

"You're bleeding."

"I've been bleeding all night."

She got a wet towel from the bathroom and cleaned my face. Every time she touched the cut on my mouth, she said she was sorry. Every time, I told her not to be.

I took off the cross and put it in her hand.

She started crying then. Hard. Like she'd been holding it in since the day somebody handed her that watch.

I held her. I didn't know anything worth saying to a woman who had carried that much pain into a room, so I just held her until she stopped shaking. Nigga, I was ready to threaten God for putting her through it. There wasn't anybody else left in the room to threaten.

Then she asked me to stay.

"I'm here, baby."

"Tonight. I mean tonight."

"I'm right fucking here."

She pulled me down to kiss her. I could taste salt at the edge of her mouth. All that blood and money downstairs, all those niggas looking for us, and she wanted my hands on her. I let the shirt fall off my shoulders. She shut the lamp off herself.

We spent the night together in that room with the money on the other bed and our guns within reach. Afterward, she lay against my chest while the blue light from the motel sign moved across the ceiling.

"What do you want?" she asked.

"You."

"You don't know me."

"Then tell me more."

She told me about the songs she'd wanted to sing. About her mother in Phoenix, who thought she was working in a restaurant. About a house Dante wanted near the water, even though neither of them knew how to swim.

I told her we'd buy it.

I told her I'd take her anywhere she wanted.

I had known her less than three hours. I meant every fucking word. Give me a country and I would've put her name on it. Give me a year and I'd have fought every man in it to keep her.

Then my phone lit up.

T.

I let it ring.

A picture came through.

My Mercedes outside the motel.

Then a message.

*Come down, son.*

***

T stood beside the empty pool with Tank and two niggas I didn't know.

He wore the same shirt he'd been wearing when he brought me in. There was still a little of my blood on the cuff.

I came down the stairs with my hands where he could see them.

"Where's the money?" he asked.

"Upstairs."

"The girl?"

"Upstairs."

He nodded slowly.

"Nigga, one thing. I gave you one fucking thing to do."

"She told me about Dante."

Tank looked away.

T kept looking at me.

"Dante stole from his family."

"Nigga, he was your brother."

"Then the nigga should've known better."

The words hit harder than the first punch.

I had wanted there to be something else. A lie. A misunderstanding. Some way he could say he hadn't done it and I could let myself believe him.

He didn't even try.

"That woman put him in the ground," T said. "Now you standing here ready to follow him."

"You put him in the ground."

T stepped close.

"Nigga, who made you? Who put you in that car?"

I could smell his cologne. The same cologne I'd smelled when he put the chain around my neck.

"I gave you money. A car. A fucking name in this city. You think she would've looked at you without all that?"

I didn't answer.

"She's upstairs with my three million. That's what she's in love with."

Behind him, Tank shifted his weight.

T saw me notice.

"Last chance. Go get her."

Upstairs, a door opened.

Shawna stood on the balcony holding the silver case.

"You want your money, T?"

He looked up.

She threw the case over the railing.

It hit the bottom of the empty swimming pool and burst open. Cash flew across the cracked blue tiles.

"There," she said. "Now tell him what I love."

T pulled his gun.

I hit his arm as he fired.

The shot shattered a window above us. Tank grabbed me from behind. One of T's men came in swinging, and I caught him with my forehead. My vision went red. I twisted free and drove Tank backward against the pool fence.

Shawna fired from the balcony.

The man beside T went down.

T shoved his gun against my side.

I felt the shot before I heard it.

For a second, I couldn't breathe. I couldn't feel the ground. I had blood running under my shirt and T's face right in front of mine.

"Should've listened," he said.

I grabbed his wrist with both hands and held on.

I wasn't letting that motherfucker point the gun at her. Nigga could empty it into me first.

Tank hit me again. I barely felt it. I drove my knee into T, tore one hand free, and got the pistol out of my waistband. My nigga, I loved him. Right up until that second, I still wanted him to let go.

I fired.

T fell against the fence.

I fired again.

He went down with his hand still reaching for me.

All the music in my head stopped.

Tank stared at him.

Then he looked at my gun.

"Aiden—"

"Nigga, back—Tank, back the fuck up!"

He did.

Shawna came down the stairs barefoot, still holding the little silver pistol. She saw the blood on my shirt and ran to me.

"Baby. Oh, fuck. Baby, look at me."

I tried to tell her I was good.

Nothing came out.

She pressed both hands against my side and looked at Tank.

"Get the fucking car!"

Tank looked down at T one more time.

Then he went for the Mercedes.

That's how the west side became mine. With a dead father at my feet and another man's wife holding me together.

***

We left the money in the pool. All three million. Not a fucking dollar could've bought me another minute standing there.

Shawna drove with one hand on the wheel and the other holding a towel against me. The sky was getting light over the desert. Behind us, Vegas kept shining like nothing had happened.

"Stay with me," she kept saying.

"I'm here."

"Keep talking."

I told her about the house. About the water. About how we'd have a room just for her music. I told her we'd learn to swim. I said all that shit with blood in my mouth because she was crying again, and I couldn't stand it. Nigga, I was bleeding through a towel and promising waterfront property. She kept asking about the windows. I kept telling her they'd face the sunrise.

At the edge of town, she pulled over long enough to put the gold cross back around my neck.

"Don't," I said. "That's his."

"I know."

She closed my fingers around it.

Then she kissed me and got back behind the wheel.

I watched the sun come up beside her face. My phone wouldn't stop ringing. T's people. Red's people. Niggas I'd never met calling to ask who was running things now.

I answered the third call.

"Aiden," I said.

On the other end, somebody went quiet.

Then he said, "What you want us to do?"

I looked at Shawna.

She was watching the road, still wearing Dante's watch.

"Some nigga better find me a doctor," I said. "And tell everybody the girl is with me."

Ten toes down. All the way to the end.

## 3. A King with a Hole in Him

Nigga, the doctor asked Shawna who she was to me.

"His wife," she said.

Nigga, I tried to live harder when I heard that. I had been slipping in and out the whole ride, seeing my mother's kitchen and the motel swimming pool, hearing Big T call me son like he still had the right. But I heard wife clear as a church bell.

"Name?" the doctor asked.

"Shawna."

"His name."

"Aiden. His name is Aiden."

"Let go of him, Shawna."

She wouldn't. They were trying to move me onto another bed and she had both hands caught in my shirt. Her black dress had gone brown where my blood dried. One of her earrings was missing. She was barefoot in a hospital that smelled like flowers and cost more every minute than my old apartment cost every month.

"Baby," I said.

She looked down.

"Don't let them cut my chain."

She took it off me herself. Kissed the cross. Put it around her neck.

Then everything went dark.

***

When I woke up, a woman in a white coat was looking at me like she knew exactly how much bullshit I was capable of.

"Dr. Marisol Vega," she said. "You're alive."

"Shawna?"

"Sleeping."

"Where?"

She pointed.

Shawna was in a chair by the window, curled around my bloody shirt. Somebody had put hospital socks on her. She had my cross around her neck and Dante's watch hanging loose on her wrist.

"Don't wake her," I said.

"She'll wake herself. She has been doing it every eleven minutes."

I tried to sit up. Pain hit me so hard I forgot what I was trying to do.

"Fuck."

"Yes," Vega said. "That. You will be doing quite a lot of that if you continue."

I stayed down.

"Where's the bullet?"

"Still inside you. Removing it would add risks you do not need. We treated the damage. You need time."

I looked at the ceiling. Big T was dead and the nigga had still managed to move in with me.

"How much time?"

"More than you want."

"Niggas waiting downstairs, Doc. A nigga can't run a whole west side horizontal."

"They can keep waiting."

She checked what she needed to check. I couldn't lift my head without the room going sideways. There was nothing kingly about it. A woman I barely knew had to help me drink water. Nigga, I could own the whole city and still drown trying to take a fucking sip.

I dropped the cup, and Shawna woke.

She came out of the chair like somebody had called her name in a burning house.

"Aiden?"

"Right here."

She touched my face, my shoulders, the hair above my ear. She wouldn't look at the bandages.

"You scared me," she said.

"I'm sorry, baby."

"Don't be sorry. Don't do it."

She kissed my forehead. Then she slapped my arm. Then she kissed me again, crying so hard I felt the tears on my mouth.

Vega went out and closed the door.

"You told them you're my wife," I said.

Shawna wiped her face with the back of her hand.

"They weren't going to let me in."

"You could've said sister."

"I wanted them to let me in. I didn't want to make it weird when I kissed you."

I laughed. That hurt too. Everything I wanted to do with her cost me something in my side.

"Say it again," I told her.

"What?"

"What you told the doctor."

She sat on the edge of the bed.

"Nigga, you trying to die married?"

"Trying to live that way."

Her hand stopped on my cheek.

For a second she looked scared of me. Not the gun, not the blood. Your nigga lying there asking for a whole fucking life when we'd barely made it through a night.

Then she put her head beside mine.

"Let me get you home first."

***

Tank showed up that afternoon in a fresh suit with dirt on his shoes. My nigga had dressed like he was coming to finish the job.

He stood in the doorway holding flowers. He looked wrong holding anything that couldn't break a man's ribs.

"These for her," he said.

Shawna didn't take them.

"Nigga, put the flowers down before we both start feeling strange," I said.

He did.

The nigga had a bruise on his forehead shaped like the mistake he'd made grabbing me. I wanted to hit him again. I wanted to thank him for getting the car. I wanted him out of my room and I wanted somebody big between that door and Shawna.

"You alone?" I asked.

"Niggas downstairs waiting."

"Whose niggas?"

"Were T's."

"And now?"

He looked at Shawna before answering.

"That's what they wanna know."

She stood up.

"I'm not leaving."

"Didn't ask you to," Tank said.

"You looked."

"I got eyes."

I held out my hand. Shawna took it, but she kept looking at him.

"Where's T?" I asked.

"Funeral people got him."

"His other niggas?"

"One alive. One ain't."

Shawna lowered her eyes. She knew which one she had shot. I squeezed her fingers. Nigga, if she needed me to lie awake with her about it, I would. If she needed me to say she'd done right, I would say that too.

"The money?" she asked.

Tank stared at the end of my bed.

"Pool was empty when I got back."

"Three million doesn't walk," she said.

"Plenty people got legs."

She let go of my hand.

"Look at me when you say it."

Tank looked at her.

"I looked, Shawna. Niggas move fast when nobody watching. Money wasn't there."

I believed him then. I had plenty of stupid left in me.

"Leave it," I said. "Nigga, she threw it away to save me. It saved me. That's money spent."

"You always this rich?" Tank asked.

"I woke up."

He looked at the machines beside me, then back at my face.

"Yeah," he said. "You did."

He told me what waited downstairs. Men from the club. Men who supplied it. Men who thought a dead boss meant they could keep whatever they owed. Red had stopped answering calls from the west side. T's office was locked. By sundown, somebody was going to decide that meant there wasn't a west side anymore.

"T put you there," Tank said. "People heard him. But putting you there and keeping you there ain't the same shit."

"Nigga, you keeping me there?"

He worked his jaw.

"I drove your car."

"That ain't what I asked."

Shawna stood beside the window. The sunlight caught the watch on her wrist, and Tank looked at it long enough that I noticed.

"I ain't putting another man in the desert," he said.

Then he went to get the men downstairs.

***

Dr. Vega found nine men in my room, two in the hall, and a duffel bag on the visitor's chair.

"Absolutely not," she said.

Everybody turned. These niggas had gold teeth, prison scars, guns under their jackets, and the kind of silence they wore to make other people nervous. Vega pointed at the hallway.

"Two visitors. The rest of you leave."

Nobody moved.

"Niggas, she said move. You want the woman who kept me alive having to step over you?" I said.

They moved.

Tank stayed. So did an older man from T's club who laid a stack of papers on my bed and wouldn't look at the wound.

"Money coming in Friday," he said. "People need paying today."

"What's in the bag?"

"Yesterday."

Tank opened it. Cash all the way to the zipper. My old life had ended less than twenty-four hours earlier, and now niggas described a fortune by the day of the week.

"Niggas worked, niggas getting paid," I said.

"From this?"

"Unless you got some fucking magic beans."

He nodded and made a mark on the paper.

"Red wants a meeting."

"Nigga, Red can call."

"He wants it face to face."

"Nigga, look at mine. I'm busy."

The man swallowed. Tank closed the bag.

I could feel the room waiting for me to become somebody. T had known how to sit still and make men ashamed of talking. I was twenty-four with a leaking hole under my ribs and Shawna's lip gloss on my forehead. I didn't know how to become T. I just knew the nigga couldn't have his chair back.

"Nobody touches Shawna," I said. "Nobody says her name to Red. Anybody got a problem with that can leave the keys on the bed."

The older man looked at Tank.

Tank looked at me.

"You heard him."

The keys stayed in his pocket.

When he left, I sank back against the pillows. My shirt was wet under the bandages. All that talking had taken more out of me than the fight.

Tank moved the bag closer.

"You want me counting it?"

"Nigga, I want you watching that door like your brother in here."

"For who?"

"Every fucking nigga."

The nigga took the chair outside.

That was the first night he guarded me.

***

Shawna's friend arrived carrying three bags, a red velvet coat, and enough rage to get past every man in the hallway.

"Where the fuck is she?"

"Rosa," Shawna said.

They hit each other halfway across the room and held on. Rosa was thirty-four, with copper hair, a broken-heart tattoo under her ear, and a voice that sounded like she'd been awake through every bad night in Vegas. She sang at the Saint on weekends and took paying men upstairs when the singing didn't cover what life wanted.

She had known Shawna when the suitcase still had its tags.

"You don't answer your fucking phone?" she said against Shawna's hair.

"I broke it."

"You borrow a phone, bitch. You send a pigeon. You don't let me hear you're dead from some nigga in a rhinestone hat."

"I'm here."

"I see you. I'm mad anyway."

Rosa held her at arm's length and checked her face. Then she looked at me.

"This him?"

"Aiden."

"You shoot that nigga T?"

"Yeah."

"Good."

She came to the bed and kissed my forehead, right on top of Shawna's mark.

"Stay alive. She doesn't have another black dress in her."

Shawna started crying again. Rosa pulled her close before I could see her face.

I lay there wishing money could do one useful thing. A whole fucking bag beside my bed and I couldn't make either woman stop hurting.

Rosa had brought clothes, food nobody in that hospital could spell, and Shawna's old songbook. She'd kept it after Dante died. Shawna opened the book to the middle and shut it so fast the pages snapped.

"Later," she said.

"Whenever," Rosa told her.

That night Rosa sat at the window painting Shawna's nails. Tank ate in the hallway. Some nigga downstairs sent more money, and I told them to leave it there. For once I had everything I wanted inside the room.

Then Shawna fell asleep against me and whispered Dante's name.

Nigga, I heard it.

I lay still until morning.

***

On the third afternoon, somebody sent me a coffin.

Not an arrangement shaped like one. A real fucking coffin. Black lacquer. Silver handles. My name engraved across the lid like the nigga had paid by the letter and wanted his money's worth.

Two delivery men rolled it into the private entrance downstairs. The nurse who called up sounded offended more than scared.

"Mr. Aiden, there is an item here for you."

"Flowers?"

"It is larger than flowers."

"Piano?"

"It is a coffin."

Tank came out of his chair so fast his food hit the floor.

"Don't open it," I told her.

"I wasn't planning to try it on."

Shawna grabbed the phone from me and started telling the nurse to move people away. I was already reaching for my trousers.

"Nigga, no," Tank said.

"Get my shoes."

"You got hospital socks."

"Then get shoes worthy of the fucking socks."

Mercedes and Porsche had come to visit with fruit, champagne nobody was allowed to open, and a photograph of my Mercedes in the air. Somebody on the sidewalk had taken it. They had enlarged the picture until you could see Shawna screaming and me looking handsome under tremendous pressure.

"That's the coffin they're sending you?" Mercedes asked, looking down through the window. "Baby, even your enemies know you're expensive."

Porsche wasn't laughing. She was counting the men outside.

"There are four more by the cars. Those two aren't delivering shit."

I saw them then. Two white sedans across the private drive. Men trying too hard to look bored, looking at the entrance instead of the street.

The men by the coffin reached into their jackets.

Tank pulled me back from the window as the first shot broke the glass downstairs.

For a second the hospital filled with sounds it had never charged anybody for. A woman screamed. The entrance doors shattered. Somewhere below us an alarm began ringing, and through it I could hear a man's voice telling people to get on the floor.

"Shawna, with Rosa. Bathroom. Lock it."

"I'm not—"

"Nigga, be alive when I come back so you can cuss me out."

She looked at the gun in my hand, then my face. Whatever she found there made her take Rosa's hand. Mercedes went after them with a heavy glass vase in both fists. Porsche stayed by the hall door.

"Wheelchair," she said.

Tank looked at her.

"The man can't walk fast. He can roll."

Nigga, that was why I respected women who could drive.

Tank put me in the chair, and Porsche pushed. We came into the hall as a nurse was trying to move an older man toward the stairs. He had one leg in a cast, a hospital gown falling open at the back, and the face of a man who had lived sixty years without expecting his next problem to come in a coffin.

"Go with them," I told Porsche.

"Who pushes you?"

"Nigga, I got hands."

She caught the old man's bed before it struck the wall. Tank went ahead to the corner. I followed, one wheel at a time, my side hurting like somebody was twisting the bullet to find a better station.

At the far end of the hall a gunman came through the stairwell door.

He saw Tank first and stopped smiling. Then he saw me in the chair, and the smile came back.

"That's the king?"

"That's the nigga," Tank said.

The man raised his gun.

Tank hit the wall beside me. I leaned out of the chair and fired. The gunman fell back through the doorway, his weapon skidding across the polished floor. I could hear more feet coming up behind him.

Porsche had reached the corner with the nurse and the bed. The old man was holding his gown shut with both hands.

"Don't look down," I told him.

"Wasn't planning to, son."

"Good. Your nigga's got this."

The ceiling sprinkler above us burst. Water came down so suddenly I thought somebody had opened the sky. Tank grabbed the back of my chair and ran.

We shot through the wet corridor. Wheels rattling. Water in my face. Pain running up my ribs every time the chair hit a seam. Behind us two men came around the corner, and I turned far enough to see them trying to follow without slipping.

"Tank!"

"I see them!"

"Then quit sightseeing and push!"

He pushed harder. We hit the doors to the atrium, crashed through, and came out on a tiled balcony above the entrance. Below, the coffin stood in the middle of a fountain. One of the delivery men had taken cover behind it. The other was pointing his gun up at us.

I fired first.

Tank pulled me away from the railing as shots cracked into the tiles. My chair went over on its side. I caught the arm, rolled, and came up on one knee with all the breath knocked out of me.

The old man on the bed was behind us. So was the nurse. Porsche stood between them and the doors, holding a chair like she intended to make a career-ending point with it.

There wasn't anywhere else for them to go.

I looked down at the coffin.

"Nigga," I said, "who sent it?"

The man below looked up. He actually answered.

"Who you think?"

"I think your taste is fucking awful."

I kicked the overturned wheelchair through the rail's broken section.

It fell into the fountain with a crash of metal and water, hitting the coffin hard enough to send it sliding. The man behind it jumped clear. Tank fired from the balcony. The man spun behind a marble column and stayed there.

I stood up.

Not because the pain had stopped. Nigga, the pain got its own fucking feet and stood with me. But the people behind us needed a door, and I could see one across the atrium below.

Tank saw where I was looking.

"Don't you dare."

"Catch up."

I went over the rail.

The coffin took my landing in the middle of its lid. Black wood split beneath my shoes. The fountain water exploded around me. For one second I was standing on my own fucking name with a pistol in my hand and every person in that lobby staring up from the floor.

Then the nigga behind the column came at me.

He didn't fire. Maybe his gun was empty. Maybe he thought a wounded man standing inside a coffin would finally understand the invitation.

I hit him with the broken lid.

Wood cracked against his shoulder. He went backward into the fountain, and I went with him, water in my mouth and his hands trying to find my throat. My side screamed. I drove him off me and held the pistol close enough that he stopped reaching.

"You finished?"

He nodded.

"Nigga, use your words."

"Finished."

"Then stay fucking finished."

Tank reached me before I could get out by myself. He had come down the stairs like an avalanche in a suit. Porsche and the nurse brought the bed through behind him, and the old man on it raised one fist at me.

"That's my boy!"

I didn't know that man from anybody. He was proud of me anyway. Nigga, I was collecting fathers faster than the bad ones could die.

Outside, the white sedans tried to leave. One got as far as the gate before a police cruiser struck it broadside. The other stopped behind it with nowhere to go. A woman stepped out of the cruiser carrying a long gun and wearing dark glasses that didn't move when she shouted.

"Hands where I can fucking see them!"

Every hand in the white cars appeared immediately.

Chief Gloria Knox came through the broken entrance two minutes later. Fifty-three, silver at her temples, blue uniform sharp enough to cut a confession out of a man's throat. She looked at the fountain, the coffin, the shattered rail above it, and finally at me.

"You jumped from there?"

"Nigga was in my way."

She tilted her head.

"And that solved it?"

The gunman in the fountain groaned.

Knox smiled.

"Apparently."

Vega arrived behind her with Shawna and the others. The doctor took one look at my wet clothes and made a sound that hurt worse than the bullet.

"Aiden."

"Everybody's alive."

"You were in bed."

"Didn't have wheels."

## 4. The Palace of the Woman I Just Met

"You owe Leon a car."

The club opened for me the following night.

## 5. Flowers for the Man I Killed

Nigga, Big T's coffin was blue.

Not dark blue. Not respectful blue. That deep candy blue you put on a car when you want other niggas to pull up beside you and hate their lives. Somebody had ordered gold handles. Your nigga paid for all of it.

"Nigga, you ain't gotta go," he said.

"Nigga, I killed him."

"Plenty people."

"People that loved him."

"You did."

"Nigga, you can say it."

"He gave me work when I came out."

"Nigga gave me a car."

"Ain't the same."

"I know."

"Nigga, oh, hell no."

"Nigga, you can't wear that."

***

The preacher talked about a man who loved his family.

Shawna let go of my hand.

Her eyes had gone flat. She was staring at the picture, and her white glove was balled so tight in her fist I could see a seam giving way.

I put my hand on her knee. She didn't move.

"T knew my mother," he said.

His voice sounded too big for the microphone.

"Knew she worried about me. Every time I came by, nigga made me call her first. Didn't matter what we was doing. Call your mother, Tank. Tell her you ate."

A woman near the aisle laughed through her crying.

"She died while I was inside. T paid for everything. Told me don't owe him for that."

"I owed him for it anyway."

"He did some shit," Tank said.

Nigga, it hurt. I needed to let it.

Red had sent flowers taller than the coffin.

## 10. Forty Million and One Woman

That was his first mistake.

His second was parking in front of my Lamborghini.

"Baby, hold these."

She took both cups.

"You getting involved?"

"Nigga involved me when he parked."

"Keep walking, pretty boy."

"Police?"

"Captain."

"You don't look like police."

"Coffee!" I shouted back.

"It's my van!"

"Don't."

"You're Aiden."

"She's police too?"

“Okay.”

“You are?”

“Yes.”

“I have been ready for years.”

“Nigga, that's not what I asked.”

“Does she know how much that hurts?”

“She knows everything.”

“Does she?”

I looked toward the stairs.

“Nigga, she knows enough.”

“The stone cannot love you back,” he said.

“I got somebody for that.”

“Then remember which one you came for.”

“Niggas, the cars ready.”

“What nigga calling you?”

“Nobody.”

“Nigga, then why you got two phones?”

“In case somebody does.”

I would've laughed on any other night. That night I watched him put both in his pocket, and something in my stomach moved. I thought about asking again. Then I heard Shawna on the stairs.

My nigga, everything in me turned around.

She wore blue. Dark blue, cut close to her body, with a train dragging behind her like even the floor wanted to hold on. Diamonds touched her ears. Dante's watch was on her wrist. Her hair was up, and that little scar above her eyebrow stood out plainly. I loved the scar. I loved knowing the exact place on her face where the world had tried some shit and failed.

“Rosa's dress,” she said when she saw me looking. “She never wore it.”

“Nigga, I said I'd buy you anything.”

“I wanted this one.”

Rosa lifted the train off the last stair.

“And I want it back. Both of you hear me?”

“Yes,” Shawna said.

I should've heard how softly she said it.

Rosa came down behind her carrying a small envelope.

“I've got the watch paper,” she said. “The original. Nobody gets it off me.”

The order hidden in Dante's watch was inside. Red's words. Red's decision. All the grief he had handed Shawna while pretending he was helping her carry it.

Shawna had made a copy for tonight. Rosa would keep the original safe, along with the truth if none of us came back.

I didn't like thinking about that second part, so I stood up.

“Turn around for your nigga,” I told Shawna.

She did.

“Again.”

“Aiden.”

“Nigga, I'm trying to remember whether there's anybody left alive I need to thank.”

She slipped her little silver pistol into her purse, then came over and put both hands against my chest.

“If you tear this dress, I'm killing you before Red gets the chance.”

“Your nigga will buy the factory.”

“I'll kill you in the factory.”

I kissed her. I kissed her until Tank cleared his throat, then I kissed her again because that nigga did not determine the length of my happiness.

Red had made the gala white tie. Nigga thought if he dressed his sins in enough silk, God might mistake them for somebody else's.

I came out in my white suit with the cross against my chest, the captain's badge inside my jacket, and Shawna beside me. Cameras started flashing.

My nigga, that surprised me. I had expected guns.

“Keep walking,” she whispered.

I kept walking like I had paid for the fucking sidewalk.

“Every nigga that owes them anything owes me,” I said.

“And your relationship with Mr. Redmond?”

Redmond. Nigga had a government name tonight.

“Ask me at the end.”

Rosa stepped between the reporter and us before I could make it worse. Tank followed so closely I felt his jacket brush mine. For a second his hand touched my arm.

“Anything happen tonight,” he said, “you stay with me.”

“Nigga, that was already the idea.”

The nigga didn't answer.

Inside, a staircase curved around a statue of a naked woman holding a bowl of fire. Red had put real flames in the bowl. I respected that nigga for that one thing. If you were going to waste money, make the building itself nervous.

Women in jewels walked past women who had sold them drugs in the bathroom. Men greeted men they had paid to have killed and had not yet heard back about. A preacher I recognized from television stood beside a man whose entire fortune came from stolen cars. The preacher had the bigger watch.

There was a private lounge full of cocaine and champagne. Somebody called my name from inside. I looked over and saw a man I had spent a filthy weekend with before I ever met Shawna, sitting between two women I had met the same weekend.

He lifted his glass.

“Aiden! You disappear when you fall in love?”

“Nigga, I got promoted.”

The women laughed. Shawna looked at all three, then at me.

“All of them?”

“Your nigga had a long weekend.”

“You nasty motherfucker.”

“You love me.”

She squeezed my hand hard enough to hurt.

“Don't get confident.”

I got confident. I got so fucking confident that when Red came down the far staircase in a dark green jacket, I smiled at him like he had arrived at my party.

He was looking at Shawna.

## 11. Blue Mercy

The band started playing a song Shawna knew.

Nigga, I wanted to rip the song out of the speakers.

One.

## 14. What She Told Me After

Shawna told me what happened in that room later. She told it sitting beside me with her bare feet under my leg, stopping whenever she wanted water and starting again wherever it hurt. I asked her the same questions until she threatened to throw the glass at me. Some parts she could give me word for word. Some parts she wouldn't give anybody.

This is what she told me.

Red took her back through the Saint after we'd gone, with two men behind her and one hand around her arm. The party hadn't completely emptied. A few people were still holding drinks because nobody had personally told them the good time was dead.

She saw a woman by the elevators lift a phone.

“Get my good side,” Shawna said.

Red pulled her harder.

The woman lowered the phone.

“You always gotta do that?” he asked.

“Look good?”

“Make a fucking scene.”

“You dragged me out of one.”

He took her into a private room she knew. White chairs. White carpet. A huge painting of a horse coming out of the ocean. Red loved that painting. He'd once spent twenty minutes explaining the horse to her while she tried to work out whether she could leave without him noticing.

Tonight the carpet had somebody else's blood on it.

One of the men had brought her purse from the office. Red took it from him and put it on the table. Then he shut the door and told the men to wait outside.

Shawna said that was when she first got scared enough to feel cold.

Not at the gunfire. Not at his hand on her arm. At the little click of the door and the fact that he wanted no one else to hear him talk.

“Sit,” he said.

She stayed standing.

“Shawna.”

“Give me a cigarette.”

He went to the table and took one from a silver box. He lit it before he handed it to her, like he was bringing her flowers.

“I could've killed that nigga,” he said.

She took the cigarette.

“Nigga, you tried.”

“Nigga ran.”

“My nigga's coming back.”

Red laughed.

“They always come back for the money.”

“Then put me outside and see.”

He didn't answer that. He poured a drink, missed the glass a little, and wiped the spill with his sleeve. Shawna noticed because Red never cleaned anything. He would wait for somebody to come across a whole room to pick up a thing beside his shoe.

He was angry enough to forget who he thought he was.

“You think that nigga loves you?”

She smoked.

“I asked you something.”

“I heard you.”

“That nigga loves winning. You're what he stole. Soon as you're just a woman in his house, watch what happens.”

She looked at the painting instead of him. The horse had its mouth open. She'd always thought it looked surprised to be swimming.

“He begged, you know,” Red said.

She kept looking at the horse.

“Dante. That nigga begged us to look after you.”

“Us.”

Red stopped with the bottle in his hand.

Shawna turned toward him.

“You were standing there?”

“I didn't say that.”

“Then who told you?”

He put the bottle down too carefully.

“Everybody knew what happened.”

“I didn't.”

He looked toward the door. She watched him do it. Shawna said she would've given anything to have the old letter and the order in her hand right then, to lay them in his lap and make him touch what he'd done. All she had was the watch. She turned it around her wrist.

“What did he call me?” she asked.

“What?”

“When he begged. What name did he use?”

“Your fucking name.”

“Which one?”

“Shawna, I don't remember every—”

“Dante called me Trouble.”

He stared at her.

“Miss Trouble if he was happy. Mrs. Trouble when I was mad at him. He'd come in and say, Mrs. Trouble, I fucked up, and I'd know I had to sit down before that nigga finished talking.”

She laughed, and that made Red angrier than anything she'd said. She couldn't help it. She was remembering Dante coming into their little apartment with a whole bumper in his hands. He'd taken it off the car he'd borrowed for their honeymoon. He'd brought the bumper upstairs because he thought she might be less mad if he hadn't left any of it behind.

“Nigga carried it like flowers,” she told me later. She laughed then too. I hated the dead nigga for making her laugh with something he'd done years before I met her, and I wanted to hear it again anyway.

In Red's room, she wiped her eyes with the back of her wrist.

“You don't know how he talked to me.”

“I know what he was.”

“Then talk about that. Talk about your dead nigga. You don't get to talk about mine.”

Red came close enough that she could smell the drink.

“You keep making him a saint.”

“No. I'm remembering my husband. That nigga stole money, lied badly, and left his socks in the bed. I loved him. You think telling me he was a bad man changes something?”

She touched the watch again.

“Tell me something I don't fucking know.”

“That nigga Dante said the same shit,” Red went on. “A house. A different life. Music. That nigga was gonna buy you a microphone and call himself a husband.”

Shawna turned back.

“That nigga was my husband.”

“I paid for the wedding.”

“You paid for the room.”

“I paid for everything.”

“Then you should've kept the receipts. Maybe the preacher would've married you.”

Red threw the glass.

It hit the wall beside the horse and broke. Shawna said she didn't move, which she was proud of until she told me why. Her legs had gone stiff. She couldn't move.

He came toward her.

“You used to know how to talk to me.”

“I used to need you to stop talking.”

He caught her face in one hand. That's where the picture came from. He called one of his men in and told him to take it while he held her there.

Shawna stared into the phone.

“He'll see you touching me,” she said.

“That's the point.”

“I know.”

The man took the picture and left. Red's ring dragged against the edge of her jaw when he let go.

She looked at it.

“You wearing it now?”

His hand closed.

“Wearing what?”

“All that money, and you still want somebody to notice.”

He put his hand in his pocket.

A telephone rang outside the room, and Red's face changed when the man at the door told him who was calling.

Red looked at her.

She knew the name. Had heard it in enough penthouse conversations to recognize which uniform Red meant when he said the city would be reasonable.

The man at the door whispered something.

Red took the phone so hard the man stepped backward.

“What do you mean arrested?”

Shawna leaned back in the white chair.

Nigga, when she told me this part later, she made me listen twice. Said the first time I'd smiled so much I hadn't heard the whole sentence.

Red kept listening.

“Knox said what?”

Shawna lifted her cigarette.

“My captain?”

He covered the phone.

“Shut up.”

“I'm just wondering which nigga finally made your policeman nervous.”

He went into the hallway to finish the call. Left the door open three inches. Left the man who'd taken the picture inside with her.

The man looked tired. Thirty-eight, name of Arlo, a little gray beginning in his beard. She knew him too. He'd once carried her groceries upstairs when the elevator broke and then apologized because Red heard him asking whether she was all right.

He looked at the door.

“It's my job.”

“Nigga, your job's turning into a funeral.”

Red shouted her name.

She kept running.

Her borrowed dress caught the corner of a table. She pulled free and left a strip of blue silk behind her. At the far end, the service doors were open. Light came through from a stairwell. She could hear men below, radios, a woman crying.

She hit the door with her shoulder and nearly fell over a waiter sitting on the landing.

He was holding his wrist. Blood marked his white cuff. Beside him stood a young-looking woman who told Shawna she was twenty-four when Shawna asked if she could lift him. Both of them had been trying to get past the men downstairs.

“Where's the outside door?” Shawna asked.

“Two floors,” the woman said. “But they won't let us—”

“Get him up.”

They lifted the waiter together. Shawna took his good side. The woman took the other. They started down the stairs, three people moving awkwardly, the blue dress dragging through dust and somebody else's dropped flowers.

Behind them, Red came through the service door.

“Shawna!”

She didn't look back.

“Nigga, if you want to help, get his fucking feet.”

Red caught her at the next landing. The woman with the waiter stopped. He pointed toward the stairs below.

“You two. Go.”

They stared.

“Go!”

Shawna held on to the waiter until the woman had him steady. Then she let them pass. She watched them reach the lower door, watched the woman look up at her once, watched them disappear into the light.

Red grabbed her arm.

“You made me run.”

“You should try it more.”

He pulled her toward him.

“Think you're him now?”

She looked at his hand, then his face.

“Nigga, I know exactly who I am. That's what's upsetting you.”

He tried to drag her back up the stairs. She planted her feet and hit him with Dante's watch across the wrist holding her. He swore and loosened his grip. She drove an elbow into his chest, got one step away, and nearly reached the lower door.

Arlo stood in it.

One eye was swelling. Blood ran from his nose. He looked at Shawna, looked at Red behind her, and for one second she thought he might move.

Then he raised his gun.

She stopped.

He couldn't make himself look at her while he pointed it.

“I'm sorry,” he said.

“Then get better at it.”

Red pulled her back upstairs. Her shoulder struck the rail. She bit the inside of her cheek to keep from giving him a sound he could enjoy.

When he put her back into the white chair, the room looked different. The cigarette box lay on the floor. The little patch of carpet where it had stood was clean and bright amid the mess.

Red bent over her.

“You get one more fucking idea—”

She looked up at him.

“I got several. You're gonna need a longer night.”

He stepped away.

Shawna told me her hands were shaking after that. She put them under her thighs so he wouldn't see. She had failed to get out. Two other people had made it.

She held on to that while he paced in front of her.

When I asked what she'd been thinking, she put her feet beneath my leg and looked at me like the answer ought to have been obvious.

“What my nigga would've done,” she said. “Only in a better dress.”


At the house, I didn't know any of this yet. Marcellus was still looking at the same photograph, holding my phone so close to his face I thought he might press his forehead through it.

“It's the stone,” he said.

“Nigga, tell me you sure.”

“Yes.”

“Nigga, we were sure at the party.”

He looked at me over the top of the phone.

“You were.”

I wanted to throw him through the rest of the door. I also wanted him to be right more than I'd wanted anything besides hearing her voice.

“Tell me,” I said.

He set the decoy on the table. In that room it looked worth forty million dollars. It looked worth killing for. Red could've shown it to a king, and the king would've started moving money.

Marcellus looked at it like an old sin.

“Nigga changed the setting on the original. Put dark stones around it. Made it look smaller by giving you other things to see.”

“Nigga, you can read forty million through my phone?”

“I can tell you he didn't do a good job.”

His hands had steadied. For the first time that night, he was somewhere he'd spent more years than prison. He turned the picture toward the light.

“I saw the ring at the gala. Only from the side. I thought I recognized the shoulder of the mounting. Here.”

He touched the screen.

“The nigga kept that part.”

“Because it's gold?” Tank asked.

“Because that nigga's an idiot.”

Rosa let out a breath through her nose.

“Finally, some good fucking news.”

Marcellus showed us where the blue metal curved under the stone. Not a secret map. Not a number to somebody's money. The old curve of a mounting he had shaped himself. Red had reused one tiny part when he changed the necklace into a ring. Marcellus knew the work the way I knew the sound of Shawna coming down our stairs.

“Will we, my nigga? Say the whole promise.”

“Yeah.”

“Her.”

“I burn it.”

“Dante said something very much like that.”

I grabbed the edge of the table.

“Nigga, I'm not Dante.”

“I know.”

“Then stop putting that nigga between me and every fucking thing I do.”

Rosa said my name.

I shook my head.

“No. I love her. Right now. Me. I'm the nigga standing here bleeding. I'm the nigga going out there. You can remember him all you want, but when her phone rings, I want her to know who's calling.”

My voice had gone loud enough to shake. I hated all three of them hearing it.

Marcellus took the papers we had brought out of the gala and laid them beside Celeste's old letter. Rosa handed him the original order from Dante's watch, still inside its envelope. He kept the different pages apart. One held a dead woman's handwriting. The others held proof that Red had wanted another man dead. They'd cost more nights than I could count, and I would have set them all on fire if it got Shawna through that door.

“Then give her something to answer,” he said.

He slid my phone back to me.

I opened her name.

The last message I'd sent before the gala was still there.

*You look good as fuck.*

She had sent back a picture of one eye, half her forehead, and her middle finger. She'd been trying to take a photograph of the whole dress and got mad at the phone.

Rosa stood beside me.

“She sent me six of those,” she said quietly. “Bitch got the bathroom ceiling in one.”

I wiped my face with the heel of my hand.

“Nigga,” I whispered, though I wasn't talking to anybody now.

I started typing.

*I'm coming. Wear whatever you want.*

Then I deleted the second sentence. She couldn't choose what she was wearing. She couldn't choose where she was sitting. Red would read it first, and I wanted to take his head off for being between us even inside a fucking phone.

I sent only the first two words.

At the Saint, Shawna said Red watched the message arrive. He held the phone where she could see it and smiled.

“Nigga thinks he's in a love song.”

“Then don't stand in the chorus.”

He took the phone away.

She asked for her purse. He laughed at that. She asked for water instead, and he brought it because he wanted to look like the kind of man who brought water.

“Tell me you were lying to that nigga,” he said.

She drank.

“About what?”

“All of it.”

“That's a lot of lying.”

“You lied to Valentino.”

She set the glass down.

“Yeah.”

“You lied to me.”

“Yeah.”

“Then tell me you lied to that nigga.”

He waited so hard that Shawna could see him believing her before she even opened her mouth. She said that was the ugliest thing in the room. He'd hurt her, trapped her, scared her, and still wanted her to make his heart feel better.

She almost gave him what he wanted. Not because it was true. Because she knew how to do it. She knew exactly how soft to make her voice. She could've called him baby and watched his shoulders come down.

Instead she said, “I told Aiden I wasn't scared.”

Red leaned closer.

“There you go.”

“I told him I didn't think about Dante when we were together.”

He smiled.

“And?”

“I told him I didn't like that stupid fucking cross.”

“You see? You see what I'm saying?”

She looked straight at him.

“I told him I could live without him.”

His smile went away.

She said his name then. Not mine. Red's. She wanted him to know he was the one hearing it.

“That was a lie.”

He knocked the water out of her hand.

For a second she thought he was going to hit her again. He stood there breathing, staring at the wet front of her dress. Then he picked up his jacket.

“Get up.”

“Where?”

“You wanna see your nigga so bad, let's go see him.”

He opened her purse and took out the little silver pistol. He looked at it, then at her, and put it in his jacket.

“Taking this too,” he said.

He took her back into the hall. She left the cigarette burning in his silver box. She told me that part twice. Of everything he'd taken away from her that night, she wanted me to know he'd forgotten to make her put it out.

At ten thirty-seven, he sent me another message.

*On our way. Don't disappoint her.*

I looked around the room at Tank, Rosa, Marcellus, and the three million dollars that had already buried too many people.

“Call everybody,” I said.

Tank picked up his phone.

“Everybody?”

“Everybody who ever called me brother.”

## 15. Ten Toes, Thirty Engines

By eleven, the street outside the house was full of cars.

The world could interview better men in the morning.

She tried to raise it again. Her hand was shaking so badly the barrel fell below his chest.

Red was still standing.

He lifted his pistol.

I had mine back in my hand by then.

I put him down.

I fired until his gun fell out of his hand. Until his knees hit the balcony. Until the nigga who had taken years from her life was on the ground looking up at a sky he wasn't ever going to own.

Then I went to her.

She was on her knees, still holding the silver gun.

"Baby."

She didn't look up.

"Shawna."

Her hand wouldn't open.

I put mine around it.

"It's done."

She shook her head.

I wanted her to say my name. Nigga, I wanted it so bad I could hardly breathe. She kept saying Dante's. I put my gun down beside hers and got both arms around her.

I held her on the floor while she cried for Dante.

And I held her tighter because I was the one who was there.

Downstairs, somebody shouted that Red was dead.

Then somebody shouted my name.

More voices picked it up. Through the hall. Across the casino. Out into the parking lot where all those headlights were still burning.

Aiden.

Aiden.

My nigga, I had blood in both shoes and her face against my neck, and they were calling me the king of Vegas.

I believed them.

Before we left, I took Blue Mercy off Red's hand. He'd stopped reaching for anything by then. Forty million dollars in my fist, and I closed it so hard the setting cut my palm.

***

Tank was alive when we got back downstairs.

Rosa had come after us with Dr. Vega. She told me later she'd sat in the house watching the clock until she couldn't fucking stand it, then gone to get the doctor and driven out behind the convoy. They came through the doors after the shooting stopped. Vega was bent over a wounded man near the entrance when I saw her.

Tank lay beside the roulette table with Rosa holding a folded tablecloth against him. Her dress was covered in blood. She looked at me like she was ready to fight God if he tried to take another person out of that room.

"Where the fuck you been?"

"With her."

She looked past me. Saw Shawna. Her face broke, and she had to press her mouth shut before she could talk again.

"Come here, baby."

Shawna went down beside her.

Tank opened his eyes when I knelt.

"My nigga. You got her?"

"Yeah."

"Red?"

"Dead."

He tried to smile. It hurt him.

"You better pay my fucking hospital bill."

I took his hand.

"Every penny."

"And the car."

"What car?"

"I want one."

"Nigga, a hundred. Your nigga will buy you a hundred fucking cars."

He shut his eyes again.

"Black ones."

I held on until the doctor came.

I kept my hand around his. The last nigga who called me family had put a bullet in me before the night was over. Tank had taken one instead. Every time a voice came through the door, I looked up expecting somebody to tell me what else I could pay to keep him breathing.

I wasn't letting him wake up alone.

***

Marcellus came into the casino after the shooting stopped.

He walked through the broken glass slowly. His shoes were old and polished. He looked at the walls, the marble, the great staircase Red had built with other people's lives.

Then he looked at me.

I put the ring in his hand.

Blue Mercy flashed under the work lights.

For a long time he didn't speak.

His thumb moved across the setting. Red had put her stone in his own ring. Marcellus turned it in his hand, held it beneath the light, and closed his eyes.

Then he took Celeste's letter out of his coat. He laid the ring against the paper and bent his head over both of them.

I let him take all the time he needed.

"What are you going to do?" I asked.

He closed his hand around the ring.

"Take my wife home."

I nodded.

Forty million dollars. I put forty million dollars in an old man's hand and watched him walk away because the woman I loved had just killed a man while saying another man's name, and for the first time that night I understood why some things couldn't be bought back.

Everything else in that casino was mine. Knox came through the entrance with Bell after the last guns were down. She looked at Shawna in my arms, the gold badge under the blood, and the men who had changed sides because a living captain had offered them more future than Red.

"You brought her home," she said.

"Told you my nigga doesn't forget."

She saluted. Bell followed. Then every officer in the doorway stood straight while the men inside the casino began saying my name again.

Captain. King. Aiden.

Shawna heard all three and held me tighter.

I made sure every nigga understood that too.

## 17. Ten Toes Down Forever

They put Tank in a private room with a view of the mountains. I paid for the room next door for Rosa because she wouldn't leave him.

"I'm here for Shawna," she said.

Tank looked at me from his pillows. "Nigga, tell Shawna I'm doing better."

"Shut up."

"Tell Shawna you called me handsome."

"You were bleeding. I was keeping you interested."

"Nigga, write that down," Tank told me. "She's interested."

Rosa adjusted his blanket over the bandage, gently enough that both of us knew better than to mention it.

I bought my nigga a black Cadillac. Then I bought him another because he called and said the first picture didn't do the color justice.

"Nigga, you buying me a car or showing me a car?"

"Look out your fucking window."

Rosa opened the blinds. I heard Tank laugh, stop because it hurt, then make the bad decision again.

"Two?"

"One for whichever side of the hospital you recover on."

"Aiden," Rosa said, "this nigga needs to learn to walk."

"That's why I put them outside."

Tank asked her which one she liked. Rosa called him an idiot and stayed at the window choosing.

Nigga, I'd seen that man take punches that would've put another man's shoes in the obituary. He'd never sounded as nervous as he did asking whether she preferred the left Cadillac.

I went back to the house with Shawna three days after Red died.

The lake was quiet. There were flowers at the gate, flowers on the stairs, flowers in the bedroom. Niggas I'd never met sent bottles and watches. Women sent silk, perfume, diamonds. Somebody sent a white horse with a blue ribbon tied into its mane.

I had the horse taken around to the back where she could see it from the balcony.

Shawna stood at the bedroom window and looked down at it.

"You buy that?"

"Somebody sent it."

"Why?"

"Because they know who you are."

She watched it walking beside the water.

"I don't know how to ride."

"Then we'll learn."

She turned toward me.

For a second, I thought she was going to cry again.

Then she laughed.

I had missed that sound so bad. I had been hearing gunfire in my sleep, hearing T say son, hearing Red tell me she'd have got in the car with anybody. I had started thinking that was all the world had left to say to me.

Then she laughed and came across the room and kissed me.

I picked her up. She wrapped her arms around my neck, and I carried her away from the window while the sun went down behind the mountains. My side hurt. I didn't put her down. Nigga, I'd carried worse for men who loved me less.

Later, we lay awake listening to the water.

Her watch rested on the nightstand.

I looked at it for a while.

She saw me looking.

"You want me to put it away?"

I didn't answer right away.

I wanted everything. Her mornings. Her nights. The way she turned her head when somebody said my name. I wanted to be the man she thought of when a song came on. I wanted to reach back three years and be the one carrying that cake into the club. I wanted to put myself in every photograph that had ever made her happy.

I couldn't.

And all the money in Vegas couldn't help me with that. I'd bought half a fucking city, and I couldn't buy one night three years ago.

"No," I said.

She rested her head on my chest.

"Sometimes I miss him when I'm happy."

I looked at the ceiling.

"With me?"

"Yeah."

That hurt. I'm not going to lie to you. That shit went straight through me.

She lifted her head.

"Because I wish he knew I wasn't hurting all the time anymore."

I looked at her then.

She touched the cross against my chest.

"I wish he knew somebody stayed."

I took her hand and held it there.

Neither of us said anything for a long time.

Then I asked her to marry me. Right there. No shirt, no ring, her dead husband's watch where both of us could see it.

She sat up.

"Aiden."

"I mean it."

"You just bought me a house."

"I'll buy you another one."

"That's not what I'm saying."

"Then tell me what you're saying."

She looked at me with tears in her eyes.

"You can't fix every bad thing that ever happened to me."

"I know."

"You gonna be mad when you find that out for real?"

I sat up beside her.

"I'm gonna be mad about a lot of shit, baby. I'm a fucked-up man. You know that."

She nodded.

"I'm asking you to marry me, baby. Bring the watch. Bring the pictures. Bring every fucking thing you can't leave behind. Just come with me."

She put her hand over my mouth.

Then she started laughing and crying at the same time.

"Yes," she said.

I kissed her palm.

"Say it again."

"Yes. I'll marry you."

I had fifty niggas outside that house ready to die for me and more money than I could count in one room, and I swear to you none of it had ever made me feel like that one word.

***

We got married beside the lake.

Three weeks after I walked into the Saint. The flowers cost more than every rent payment I'd ever made put together. Shawna came toward me in a white dress while a woman sang the song she used to sing in her mother's kitchen.

Blue roses and the gardenias I had promised her covered the aisle.

There were diamonds at her throat. Dante's watch on her wrist.

I wore the cross.

Tank stood beside me in a black suit with his arm still healing under the jacket. Rosa sat in the front row wearing a blue dress and trying not to look at him. He looked at her enough for both of them.

Every table had a bottle of Hennessy on it. Every nigga at the gate had a gun. Behind the flowers, my people watched the road while men from other cities came to shake my hand and find out whether the stories were true.

Nigga, they were true enough that none of them tried me.

I paid the singer more than she asked. Then I paid her band. Then I bought the place where she'd been playing because Shawna said the owner had treated her like shit once, years ago, when she was trying to get an audition.

I didn't bring him to the wedding.

I made that nigga listen to it from the parking lot.

Shawna reached me and put her hands in mine.

For a moment I forgot everybody else was there.

The preacher asked me if I would love her in sickness and in health.

I looked at the scar above her eyebrow. At the mark still fading on her mouth. At the wrist she had held against my bleeding side when all I had to give her was a promise about a house near the water.

"I will."

He asked her the same.

"I will."

That's how it happened. Her hand was shaking in mine. Tank kept wiping his face with the sleeve on his good arm. Rosa was crying into a napkin she'd stolen off his table. My girl said yes in front of the whole fucking city.

I kissed my wife while the band played and my people shouted my name.

The wedding presents arrived in a convoy longer than the one we'd taken into the desert.

Knox brought a gold-plated pair of handcuffs in a velvet box and told Shawna she trusted her to decide when they were necessary. Shawna held them up, looked at me, and smiled in a way that made Tank cover his eyes.

"Nigga, I'm trying to eat."

"Then look at your plate," Rosa told him.

Professor Halcyon came with Principal Mercer and six graduates who'd helped carry people out of the auditorium. They brought a framed photograph of my Lamborghini coming through the doors with fire behind it. Mercer had signed the bottom in gold.

*Our valedictorian.*

"Nigga, you signed this like you made me," I told him.

He began crying again.

Shawna took the frame before either of us dropped it.

"I know exactly where this goes."

"Over the bed?"

"Over the fireplace. You already think enough of yourself in bed."

Mercedes screamed laughing. Porsche nearly dropped her drink. Mercer didn't seem to understand the sentence, but he nodded like the academic quality of it was beyond dispute.

Rex Crown arrived with a white limousine full of fighters who all wanted to shake my hand. He introduced me to each one by telling them exactly how quickly I had put him down. Every time he told it, the time got shorter and his expression got prouder.

"One punch," he said to the last man. "Before I got comfortable."

"Rex, nigga, you weren't comfortable when you arrived."

"That's what I'm telling him. I never stood a chance."

The fighter looked at me, looked at Shawna, and bowed his head.

"Congratulations, Captain."

"See?" Rex said. "Nigga learns fast."

Little Saint brought a motorcycle painted dark blue with my initials on the tank. He told me it was for days when four wheels felt like too much company. Tank immediately told him no day in my life required fewer wheels.

"You jealous?" Little Saint asked.

"Nigga, I have watched this man drive. I am informed."

Leon brought a bicycle.

A real bicycle. Green. A little basket on the front. He wheeled it through the gate in a tuxedo and looked straight at Porsche.

"This one floats."

She kissed him on the cheek.

"Baby, so did the first car for a minute."

He shut his eyes while everybody laughed, then opened them on the house, the music, the lake full of lights.

"I wouldn't have missed any of it," he said.

Nigga, neither would I.

***

For our first dance, Shawna wanted a song she could hear without remembering a room somebody else had paid for.

So we made one.

She had written the melody in the week before the wedding. I'd found her at the piano after midnight, barefoot, chewing the end of a pencil while she tried to make the bridge stop sounding like she was saying goodbye.

I sat beside her and played the left hand while she sang.

"There," I said when her voice rose.

"Where?"

"Don't turn back. Keep going."

She tried it. The note opened. Her face changed.

"Baby."

"That's us."

We worked until the sun came up. No dead men in the song. No promises somebody had made with one hand on a door. Just a woman hearing a car in the driveway and knowing the person she wanted was finally coming in.

Now the band played it beside the lake while my wife put her hand in mine.

The first steps were easy. The next ones belonged to her. Shawna moved like music had been waiting all those years for somebody to give her enough room, and I followed until she laughed at how serious I looked.

"Nigga, you know how to dance."

"I know how to dance with you. Different subject. Higher degree."

She leaned back in my arm. I caught her, turned, brought her close again while the whole wedding cheered.

Rosa stood up first. Tank tried to follow and remembered his shoulder. She came to him instead, took his good hand, and led him onto the edge of the floor.

"I don't dance," he told her.

"You do tonight."

"Rosa—"

"Nigga, you got shot for these people. Survive four steps with me."

He survived them. Badly at first. Then better when he stopped looking at his feet and looked at her face.

I watched my brother discover that being large wasn't the only way to take up a woman's whole world.

He looked over at me once, embarrassed and helplessly happy.

I nodded.

Your nigga got it, I was telling him. Your nigga had finally fucking got it.

At the last chorus, the fireworks began over the water.

Blue and gold. Huge flowers opening in the sky above the house I'd bought before I knew where to put my own clothes. The guests turned to look. Shawna stayed looking at me.

I put my forehead against hers.

"You missing the expensive part."

"No, baby."

She touched the cross.

"I'm looking right at him."

Nigga, I didn't have a single smart thing left in my mouth. She kissed me before I could find one.


Then we went inside and got so drunk the sun came up before anybody remembered there had been a schedule.

There was cocaine in the upstairs bathroom, cash changing hands on the terrace, hookers dancing with men who had brought other women to the wedding. Somebody drove a convertible onto the lawn. Tank fell asleep with his good arm around Rosa, and she threatened to kill the first motherfucker who took a picture.

I bought every picture the next morning.

I kept one. Nigga looks at it now and swears he was awake. Rosa has the same picture by her bed.

***

People tell this story like everything ended when Red hit the ground.

It didn't.

The calls kept coming. The money kept coming. The west side still needed a boss on Monday. A man in another city still thought Red dying meant he could take what Red used to sell. Somebody still owed somebody, and somebody's brother still wanted blood.

I didn't become a good man because I put a ring on Shawna's finger.

I was still the nigga who walked into a hotel with blood on his collar and told another man's people the girl was coming with him. I still had a temper. Still had a gun. Still woke up some nights with my hand on the place T had shot me.

And I still wanted everything.

But now I knew who I was bringing it home to.

One night that week, I came back from handling business and found Shawna in the room we'd made for her music.

She had all the lights off except the little lamp beside the piano. There were papers on the floor. A half-finished drink on the windowsill. She was singing something I hadn't heard before.

I stood in the doorway and listened.

When she finished, she turned around.

"How long you been there?"

"Long enough."

"You like it?"

"Yeah."

"You always say that."

"I always mean it."

She held out her hand.

I went to her.

Outside, there were niggas guarding the gates and a city waiting for me to make the next decision. In that room, she pulled me down beside her and put my hand against her heart.

"Stay a minute," she said.

I stayed all night.

***

Aight, my nigga. Now you know which nigga you riding with.

You know about the money in the pool. You know about the blood in the car. You know why I wear a dead man's cross and why my wife still wears his watch. You know which nigga called me son and tried to kill me, and which nigga took a bullet before he'd let me stand alone.

Don't let anybody tell you I came out of that desert clean. I came out alive. I came out with Shawna's hand in mine and Tank bleeding in the back of an ambulance, and I made the whole fucking city learn our names.

My nigga, I gave away the most expensive diamond I ever held. Then I took everything the man wearing it thought he was going to leave behind. That's what happened. That's my money in the walls of that house. That's my name on the cars. That's my wife singing with the window open when the lake goes black at night.

And when she wakes up crying, I'm there. When Tank calls me at three in the morning, I answer. When some motherfucker thinks the years made me soft, I let him get close enough to see the scar.

You can call that love. You can call that crazy. Call it whatever the fuck you want. I was crazy before she loved me. I had blood on me before she touched me. She didn't make me dangerous. She gave all that danger somewhere to come home.

I remember Dante. I never met him, but I remember him every time that gold cross hits my chest. I remember T. I wish I could remember him without the gun. I remember Red. I remember what he looked like when he found out there was something in that room he couldn't buy.

And I remember her in the Mercedes. One hand on the wheel. One hand trying to keep my life inside me. She could've left me. She could've taken what she could carry and gone. But she stayed. My girl stayed.

So your nigga stayed too. What the fuck else was I going to do with a home?

Ten toes down, my nigga. In the club where twelve niggas tried me. In the desert where thirty engines answered. In that hospital hallway waiting for my nigga Tank to open his eyes and start requesting Cadillacs. A nigga can stand in a kingdom; a nigga can stand beside a bed. I stood in both. Ten toes down when the money came, and ten toes down when no nigga could buy the thing I was waiting to hear.

And if another man comes through that gate thinking he's going to make her cry like that again, he better bring every goddamn prayer he knows.

Because I'm home now.

Aiden.

Ten toes down. Forever.

The invitation arrived the morning we were packing for our honeymoon.

Blue envelope. Silver seal. Nobody had come through the gate. It lay on the hood of my police Lamborghini when I came outside carrying Shawna's suitcase and my smaller bag.

Tank pointed at it.

"If that's another college, I'm taking the test."

"Nigga, start with the envelope."

He picked it up. "Sky High."

"Club?"

"School."

"Nigga, I just graduated. They got a return policy?"

Shawna came down with Rosa on her arm. Mercedes and Porsche stood by the cars arguing over suitcases. Mercedes had brought enough to make a nigga check whether somebody had sold her apartment.

"It's their honeymoon," Porsche said.

"I'm aware."

"Then why you have four bags?"

"Because I'm aware it's their honeymoon. I need my own activities."

Marcellus stood beside the white horse, feeding it something the trainer had approved. Blue Mercy was safe in a proper case, and the old nigga had finally stopped carrying the case from room to room. He let the horse finish chewing before he looked over.

"Trouble?"

"Education," Tank said.

"Ah. Expensive trouble."

I opened the envelope. A photograph slid out.

Campus. Lawns. Football field. Towers. All above the clouds, on a huge piece of ground with no apparent interest in rejoining the rest of the fucking planet.

Tank bent over it.

"Nigga, that's up."

"I see."

"No. That's UP up. Our school had steps. These niggas got weather."

Rosa took the photograph.

"What's underneath it?"

"A lawsuit if a nigga drops his keys," Mercedes said.

The letter came from Dean Seraphine Storm. Advanced adult hero program. Every incoming student at least twenty-two. She'd seen the graduation footage, the hospital rescue, the convoy, and enough of my other work to think I'd been taking the long way to the right school.

She wanted me after the honeymoon.

She wanted my people too.

I read that again. My people. Nigga, I liked a dean who understood the application included attachments.

"All of us?" Tank asked.

I handed him the page. He read it, then looked at Rosa, trying to make the looking casual.

Rosa pointed at the photograph. "They got rooms?"

"Whole residences," Shawna said, reading the next page.

"Piano?" I asked.

"Conservatory."

"That's more piano?"

"Yes, baby. More piano."

Porsche took off her sunglasses. "Cars?"

I found the line.

*Your automobile may accompany you, Captain. We would prefer that its first landing be supervised.*

"Nigga," Tank said, "they know you personally."

I started smiling.

Shawna caught it.

"Honeymoon first."

"Absolutely."

"A real honeymoon. With a bed. The bed touches a floor. The floor touches the fucking earth."

"Whatever my wife wants."

"And when I say beach, I mean beach. You will not enroll the sand in college."

"Baby, if the sand tests well—"

She held out her hand for the keys.

I gave them to her. Nigga, marriage was going beautifully. Clear expectations. Prompt consequences.

Tank handed back the photograph.

"You think you can be best in that school too?"

I folded the letter beside my badge. The gold cross settled against my chest.

"Tank."

"What?"

"Those niggas invited me. They know."

Shawna opened the Lamborghini.

"Get in, valedictorian. I'm driving before somebody gives you a planet."

I got in. My niggas were still arguing over luggage. My wife was starting the engine. A college in the sky wanted the whole unreasonable collection of us.

Nigga, I had a wife, a badge, a kingdom, and somewhere new to be magnificent.

And she still made me carry the bags.
