## 11. Blue Mercy

Shawna's face changed before the song reached its first word.

Aiden watched her fingers tighten in Red's hand as the band began. She seemed to know this one. Red leaned toward her ear. She looked down at their joined hands, then let him turn her beneath the chandelier. Close enough for a ballroom. Too close for Aiden.

He wanted to pull the song out of the speakers.

Tank brought him a glass.

“For drinking,” Tank said. “Don't throw it.”

“Brought it where I have hands,” Aiden pointed out.

“Might surprise me once,” Tank said. “Keep hoping.”

Aiden swallowed, felt the drink warm his throat, and lowered the glass when his side gave its opinion. Across the room, Marcellus spoke to a woman wearing a necklace so heavy it seemed to be arresting her slowly. The old man's new suit made him look at home among the flowers and glass. Aiden knew Celeste's letter was inside that suit.

Red turned Shawna again. Her dress moved around his legs. She smiled at something Aiden couldn't hear.

Aiden glanced at Tank.

“I see,” Tank told him before he spoke.

“Don't know what I'm saying,” Aiden said.

Tank drank his whole glass. “Getting an idea.”

***

The man who interrupted wore six championship rings and a gold mouthguard on a chain.

“Captain Aiden,” he said.

“Depends who's bleeding,” Aiden answered.

“Rex Crown,” the man announced.

Aiden recognized him. He'd watched enough fights to remember Rex winning heavyweight titles, losing them, and returning to ask for them back. At forty-five, he still filled out his jacket like he expected another chance at the conversation.

Tank looked at the rings. “Bring the whole display?”

Rex ignored him.

“Heard you think you can fight,” Rex told Aiden.

“Heard you get paid for it,” Aiden said. “Congratulations. Have a drink.”

“Asking you,” Rex said.

Friends arranged themselves behind the boxer, forming an audience without buying tickets. Mercedes and Porsche stood among them. Mercedes was smiling already.

“Champ asked something,” one of Rex's friends told Aiden.

“He answered,” Mercedes said to the man. “Want him to sing it? Plays piano too.”

Tank nodded. “Ruined a breakfast.”

Rex moved closer. “Five thousand says I put you down before your woman finishes dancing.”

Aiden looked toward the floor. Green jacket against blue silk. Red's hand at Shawna's back. The glass felt very thin between Aiden's fingers.

“Aiden,” Tank said quietly.

He set it down.

“Five thousand?” Aiden asked Rex. “You're famous. Don't bring the introductory offer.”

“Twenty,” Rex said.

Aiden turned toward Mercedes. “Front-row ticket cost more when he used to win?”

“Apparently,” Mercedes answered.

“Hundred thousand,” Rex said.

A friend caught his sleeve. Rex shook the hand away. Aiden watched the sleeve lose its attempt to save the rest of the suit.

Porsche put down her drink. “Anybody hear that besides me?”

“Everybody heard,” Rex told her.

“Good,” Porsche said. “Mercedes, get a pen.”

Aiden took off his jacket and held it toward Tank.

“Your side,” Tank reminded him.

“Hold it on the good side,” Aiden said.

“Jacket hasn't been shot,” Tank told him. “You have.”

“Easier week, then,” Aiden said. “Let it help.”

Tank took the jacket. The badge flashed inside its lining. Rex's eyes stopped on the gold.

“You're a cop?” he asked Aiden.

“Called him Captain when you came over,” Tank said. “Thought he captained breakfast?”

Rex's friends cheered. Aiden looked past them to the open space beside the tall windows. He moved there. Rex followed, shedding his own jacket.

The music continued. Across the floor, Red looked toward them and smiled. Shawna didn't. She shook her head once at Aiden over Red's shoulder.

Aiden lifted one finger.

One.

She closed her eyes. He lowered the finger before she opened them again.

Rex rolled his shoulders, removed the mouthguard from its chain, and put it between his teeth.

“Jewelry he can chew,” Tank murmured.

Mercedes bent forward laughing.

Rex swung.

The punch came straight, fast, and properly aimed. Aiden saw the shoulder move, stepped out of its line, and felt the air pass the cut on his mouth. He moved inside Rex's reach and struck once beneath the ribs.

The impact wasn't especially loud. The breath leaving Rex was.

The champion folded. Aiden caught the back of his collar before he hit the champagne table. He held him there while Rex tried to straighten, failed, then tried to speak with no air available for it.

The watching circle went quiet.

“Nigga,” Porsche whispered.

“Nigga,” Mercedes agreed.

Tank looked down at Rex. “About the whole sentence.”

Aiden guided the boxer into a chair. His own side throbbed, but he kept standing until Rex was settled. One of the champion's friends checked his watch as though time might explain what had happened.

“You said before the dance ended,” Aiden told Rex. “Got two verses left. Enjoy them.”

The circle erupted. People came from farther tables to see. A woman touched Aiden's shoulder and told him she'd watched every one of Rex's title fights and never seen him sit so respectfully.

Rex finally got a breath deep enough to speak.

“Again,” he said.

“Breathe first,” Aiden told him.

Rex gripped the chair arms. “Again.”

“Rematch or hospital bracelet?” Aiden asked.

The boxer looked up at him. His jaw unclenched slowly.

“What the fuck are you?” Rex asked.

Aiden took his jacket from Tank and put it back on, easing the sore arm through its sleeve. Then he looked toward Shawna.

“Her man,” he answered.

People followed his gaze. Shawna stood beside Red at the edge of the dance floor. Her hand remained in his, but she was looking directly at Aiden and smiling. He held her eyes for the time it took Red's smile to disappear.

Rex opened a checkbook with hands that still weren't steady. He wrote the amount, signed, and held the check out.

“Don't welch,” Rex said.

Aiden accepted it and passed it to Mercedes.

“You and Porsche,” he told her. “Got us out that first night.”

Mercedes looked at the number. “Hundred thousand dollars.”

“Want it smaller?” Aiden asked.

“Don't you fucking dare,” she told him.

She kissed one cheek. Porsche kissed the other. Rex sat in the middle of the laughter, watching the check move away from him.

A thin man behind the chair drew a pistol.

“Give it back,” the man ordered Mercedes.

She stepped behind Aiden. He looked at the gun, then at Rex.

“Yours?” Aiden asked.

Rex pushed himself out of the chair.

“Put it away, Silas,” he said.

“Made you look—” Silas began.

“Beat me,” Rex corrected him. “Put it away.”

Silas kept the gun raised.

Aiden caught his wrist as Tank seized his shoulder. The shot went into the ceiling. Crystal rained from a small chandelier. Aiden drove the wrist down while Tank put the rest of Silas on the floor.

The pistol slid away. Porsche kicked it beneath the edge of an empty table and held it there with her shoe. Mercedes put the check into her purse, closed it, and hit Silas with the bag.

“Made me bend down,” she told him.

Rex stood over his friend with his fists closed.

“Pull a gun because I lost?” Rex asked.

“Helping,” Silas said from the floor.

“Ruining my reputation twice,” Rex told him.

Two of Knox's officers hurried in from the lobby. Aiden stood and opened his jacket to show the badge. Tank kept Silas's wrists behind him until the officers reached them.

“Captain?” one officer asked.

“His evening's over,” Aiden said.

Porsche moved her shoe for the second officer to collect the pistol. They took Silas out, and after a frightened pause the band found its song again.

Rex offered Aiden his hand.

“Need somebody in your corner, call,” Rex said.

Aiden shook it. “Stay out of mine first.”

Rex laughed carefully, pressing a hand beneath his ribs, then went toward the bar.

Aiden looked back at the floor. Shawna was dancing with Red again. A heavyweight beaten, a check given away, an arrest made. None of it had moved the thing he kept watching.

***

Red said something against Shawna's ear. Her shoulder made a small hard line beneath his hand. They stopped turning for a moment while couples went around them. Aiden couldn't hear the words. He watched her lift her chin and answer.

Red looked toward him.

Shawna put a hand against Red's cheek.

Aiden retrieved his glass from the table and finished his drink. He knew what the gesture was meant to accomplish. Red had to believe her. He also knew how easy the man looked beneath her touch, and the knowledge did nothing useful to his hands.

He set down the glass and started walking.

Tank called his name. Rosa moved toward him. Aiden went past, telling himself he would only ask Shawna whether she was all right. Take her aside for a second. Make Red acknowledge that a living man was standing here.

“Dance is over,” Aiden told Red.

Red kept turning. “Band's still playing.”

Aiden put a hand on his shoulder. Now Red stopped.

Shawna looked at Aiden's hand, then at his face. Her eyes held his, urgent and steady. He saw the warning. He didn't move his hand.

“Take it off me,” Red said quietly.

“Yours off her first,” Aiden told him.

Three men in dark jackets came from the edge of the room. Tank moved with them. The singer lost a word, but the musicians carried the beat past it.

Shawna stepped between Aiden and Red.

“Go sit down,” she told Aiden.

He stared at her. “What?”

“You're embarrassing me,” Shawna said.

They had discussed this that afternoon in front of the bedroom mirror. She might have to reject him publicly. They had even agreed to one slap if she needed it to keep the disguise intact. She refused twice before Aiden insisted he could take a slap if it kept Red believing her.

Remembering that agreement didn't stop the room from feeling suddenly too close.

“Mean that?” Aiden asked her.

Shawna gave him the slap they had agreed upon.

The sound cracked across the music. Aiden turned back to her. Her hand was shaking.

“Told you to listen to me,” Shawna said.

Anger shone through the performance. He could see it and had earned it. Behind her, Red was watching with a smile that made Aiden's stomach tighten.

Aiden stepped back.

Shawna held his eyes one second longer, then turned toward Red.

“Finish our dance,” she told him.

They moved away. Aiden stood with half the gala watching his face. His cheek burned. He had never needed a gun less or wanted one more.

Tank caught his sleeve.

“Come on,” Tank said.

“Don't,” Aiden answered.

Tank kept his grip gentle. “Come on.”

Aiden let him lead him to the side of the room. For a few steps, looking at Tank's shoes was easier than choosing where to put his own.

Rosa joined them.

“Kept you alive,” Rosa told him. “That's what she just did.”

“Know,” Aiden said.

“Act like it,” Rosa replied.

Across the room, Red kissed Shawna's hand again. Aiden kept his feet where Tank had brought him.

***

At nine, Red stopped the music.

He went onto the low presentation stage with Shawna beside him. Attendants drew a curtain behind them. A glass case stood under white light. Inside, a blue necklace rested on velvet.

Aiden had heard about forty million dollars, a marriage, a prison, Dante left in the desert. He'd almost expected the stone to rise and announce itself.

It was smaller than he'd imagined.

That made the lives around it seem larger. He looked at Marcellus. The old man's mouth hung slightly open. His hands stayed still at his sides.

Red took the microphone.

“Some things wait a long time to come home,” he said, looking at Shawna.

People applauded. Aiden watched the pauses Red left for them to fill.

“This isn't an auction,” Red told the room. “Nobody here can afford what it means to me.”

A man near the front laughed too loudly. Red didn't turn toward him.

“Tonight is a promise,” Red said.

He lifted Shawna's hand. The watch slid along her arm. Tank muttered beside Aiden, too quietly for him to make out the words.

Marcellus approached with the jeweler's case they'd brought him. An attendant gave his borrowed name and profession. He inclined his head and stopped beneath the stage while Red studied him.

“Approve?” Red asked.

Marcellus looked at the display. “I'd like to see it closer.”

“Later,” Red said.

“A piece of this age—” Marcellus began.

“Later,” Red repeated.

His ring flashed when he lowered the microphone. Aiden saw Marcellus's gaze drop toward that hand before he stepped away from the stage.

Shawna turned and found Aiden in the audience. She held his eyes. He made himself nod. They still had a plan, and she was still inside it.

***

An attendant approached after the presentation.

“Mr. Redmond would like you upstairs,” the man told Aiden.

“Can come get me,” Aiden said.

Tank touched his arm. Across the room, Shawna was speaking with Rosa beside the stage. Both women looked toward them when the attendant repeated the invitation. Shawna nodded once.

Aiden agreed to go. Tank came with him, and so did Marcellus. The attendant objected to the old man.

“Giving away forty million, you can let a jeweler look at it,” Aiden told him.

The man led all three upstairs.

Red's office had dark wood, wide city windows, and a desk large enough to lay a king across. Aiden could hear the party through the floor. A second door stood partly open on the far side; Shawna was already inside near it, holding a small blue box.

Red waited behind the desk.

“Thought you might prefer privacy,” he said.

“For what?” Aiden asked.

“The end,” Red told him.

Shawna took a step toward Aiden. Red came around the desk and rested a hand on her shoulder.

“Tell him,” Red said.

Her face was pale. Aiden couldn't tell how much she was performing anymore.

“Take the box,” Shawna told him.

“Come here,” Aiden said to her.

She held it farther out. “Take it.”

Her hand shook. He accepted the box, wanting that hand free to hold his. Inside lay a blue stone in a setting that looked like the one in the display. From here, he couldn't tell them apart.

“Forty million,” Red said. “Start again anywhere.”

Aiden looked up. “Think I'm selling her?”

“Think she just sold you,” Red answered.

Marcellus stepped forward. Red raised a finger, and two men entered beside the door. The old man stopped.

Shawna's eyes went from Red to the guards, then back to Aiden. He closed his fingers around the box. Red wasn't guarding it, bargaining over it, asking where the money was. He was handing over the very thing they'd come to take.

Aiden looked at the guards again.

Red opened a drawer.

“Since your friends enjoy history,” he said.

He laid a leather folder on the desk. A white label across it read DANTE.

Shawna made a small sound Aiden hadn't heard from her before. He reached for the folder.

Red let him take it.

The ease of that permission frightened Aiden more than the men at the door.
