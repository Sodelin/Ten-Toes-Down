On the third afternoon, the nurse at the private entrance called his room.

“Mr. Aiden, there's an item here for you,” she said.

“Flowers?” Aiden asked.

“It's larger than flowers,” the nurse told him.

“Piano?” he suggested.

She paused. “It's a coffin.”

Tank stood so fast his lunch hit the floor. He didn't look down. Aiden had seen him defend individual fries; this seemed serious.

“Don't open it,” Aiden told the nurse.

“I wasn't planning to try it on,” she said.

Shawna took the phone and told her to move people away. Aiden reached for his trousers. Putting them on had required breaks and commentary that morning. Now he tried to do it like nobody had put a bullet in him.

Tank caught his arm.

“No,” Tank said.

“Get my shoes,” Aiden told him.

“You got hospital socks,” Tank pointed out.

“Then get shoes worthy of the fucking socks,” Aiden said.

Mercedes and Porsche had come with fruit, champagne nobody was allowed to open, and an enlarged photograph of the Mercedes above the boat. Aiden had inspected it at length. Shawna was screaming. His own face maintained an impressive relationship with catastrophe.

Mercedes put the photograph facedown and went to the window with Porsche. Aiden joined them, one hand held tightly against his side.

Below, two men were wheeling a black coffin into the glass-fronted atrium. Silver handles gleamed. He could make out his name across the lid. Somebody had stood in a store selecting hardware for his ass.

“Even your enemies know you're expensive,” Mercedes said beside him.

Porsche watched the two white sedans outside. Four men stood near them, looking toward the entrance instead of the street. One turned his whole body to keep something beneath his jacket out of sight.

“Those aren't delivery men,” Porsche said.

The first shot broke the glass downstairs.

Tank pulled Aiden away from the window. More shots cracked inside the atrium. A scream climbed through the hollow space, followed by the alarm. From the phone in Shawna's hand came a clatter and a man's order to get on the floor.

Aiden took his pistol from the clothes Tank had kept beside the chair. Shawna looked from the gun to his face.

“Bathroom,” Aiden told her. “With Rosa. Lock it.”

“I'm not—” Shawna began.

“Be alive when I come back, baby,” Aiden said. “Then cuss me the fuck out.”

She watched him for a moment longer. Rosa reached for her hand. Mercedes lifted the heavy vase from the table, dumped the flowers and water into the empty basin, and followed both women into the bathroom.

Porsche dragged the wheelchair away from the wall.

“He can't walk fast,” she told Tank. “He can roll.”

Aiden appreciated a woman who understood transportation.

Tank helped him sit, pulled his shoes on over the hospital socks, and lifted his feet onto the rests; leaving one behind seemed like the sort of extra this hospital would charge for. Tank checked the hall, then waved Porsche out with the chair.

The corridor led to glass doors opening onto the upper gallery of the atrium. Near those doors, an enclosed stairwell entered the corridor from one side; opposite it was an alcove and the service lift, wide enough for a hospital bed. Through the gallery glass, Aiden could see a second route down: an open staircase curving along the far wall to the lobby. The gunfire below made that staircase look considerably less attractive than the architect probably intended.

A nurse was struggling with an older patient's bed near the alcove. His leg was in a heavy cast. One wheel had caught against the wall, turning the bed across the corridor while the back of his gown surrendered more territory.

Porsche let go of Aiden's chair to help the nurse free the wheel.

“Stay with them,” Aiden told her.

Porsche looked toward his chair. “Who pushes you?”

“Nigga, I got hands,” he said.

He put them on the rims and managed a distance a proud snail might have covered. His side burned. He laid the pistol across his lap to push, then picked it up whenever Tank stopped at a door.

The stairwell door opened.

A gunman came through with his weapon low. He saw Tank and checked his stride. Then his eyes found Aiden in the chair, and he smiled.

“That's the king?” the gunman asked.

Tank backed toward the wall. “That's the nigga.”

The gunman's hand came up. Aiden leaned out and fired. The chair rocked beneath him. The man struck the stairwell door with his shoulder and fell back through it. His pistol skidded across the hall. Somebody else shouted from the stairs.

The patient on the bed had turned to watch. Both hands clutched his gown shut.

“Don't look down,” Aiden told him.

“Wasn't planning to, son,” the old man said.

“Good,” Aiden replied. “Your nigga's got this.”

A second gunman fired from the stairwell doorway. The shot struck a sprinkler overhead. Water hit the corridor in a cold sheet.

Tank grabbed the wheelchair handles and ran. The wheels hissed through the wet floor. Each seam went straight into Aiden's wound. Behind them, Porsche and the nurse pushed the bed toward the gallery doors, away from the gunman standing between them and the lift.

Aiden twisted far enough to see the second attacker slide as he stepped into the hall. The first was moving in the stairwell doorway again, one arm hanging low, reaching for his fallen pistol with the other.

“Tank!” Aiden shouted.

“I see him!” Tank answered.

“Then quit sightseeing and push!” Aiden said.

The front of the chair struck the glass doors. They banged open onto the gallery. Water ran from Aiden's hair into his eyes. He wiped it away with his wrist and looked down.

One delivery man was pulling the coffin trolley sideways for cover. A wheel hit the low rim of the fountain. The coffin tipped off and dropped into the shallow water with a splash that echoed under the glass roof. The second delivery man raised his pistol toward the gallery.

Aiden fired first. The man dropped behind a column. Shots came back, biting tile and striking the joint of a railing panel. Tank hauled the chair sideways. One little front wheel caught a planter, and Aiden went over with it.

He caught an armrest, rolled badly, and landed on one knee. For a moment he couldn't pull air into his chest. The pistol remained in his hand. His other palm lay flat on wet tile, doing nothing useful when he told it to push him up.

Porsche and the nurse had got the bed just inside the gallery doors. Porsche held a steel visitor's chair by its back, legs aimed toward the corridor. Beyond her, the second gunman was advancing from the stairwell.

Tank turned away from the railing and fired through the open gallery doors. The attacker lurched back behind the stairwell wall. The wounded man crawled in after him, abandoning his pistol.

“Lift,” Tank told the nurse. “I'll hold them.”

Porsche passed him the chair. He ran to the stairwell, kicked the loose pistol into the alcove, and pulled the door shut. He jammed the steel chair against it, the back beneath the handle and its feet braced against the opposite wall. The door bumped from inside. Tank stood to one side with his pistol trained on the opening it would make.

From the gallery floor, Aiden watched Porsche and the nurse wheel the bed past Tank into the lift alcove. The bed scraped the wall. The nurse pressed the button. Above the doors, a little arrow glowed.

“It opens downstairs by the fountain,” she called to Tank.

Aiden looked over the damaged railing. Of course it fucking did. Every route ended at a man with a gun.

The coffin floated just enough to knock against the fountain's marble rim. One attacker crouched behind it. The other held the column opposite the lift doors. If the patient came out now, both men would have him boxed between them.

“Who sent the ugly-ass coffin?” Aiden shouted down.

The man behind the box looked up. “Who you think?”

“I think your taste is fucking awful,” Aiden told him.

He braced his back against the planter and kicked the overturned wheelchair toward the broken railing. It caught. He kicked again. Something hot spread beneath his dressing. The chair struck the loose section, took it free, and fell.

Metal hit coffin. Water leapt into the air. The box slid sideways, exposing the gunman behind it. Aiden fired as the man jumped toward the column. Both attackers disappeared behind the marble.

The center of the lobby was clear for a moment.

Behind him, the service lift arrived. Aiden looked back and saw Porsche helping the nurse roll the bed inside. Tank kept his gun on the wedged stairwell.

“Hold there till I call,” Tank told the women. Porsche kept one hand across the lift's doorway while the nurse straightened the bed inside.

Aiden got a hand around the sound part of the railing and dragged himself upright. The pain rose with him. It seemed to have grown its own fucking legs.

Tank looked across from the stairwell and saw him looking at the coffin.

“Don't you dare,” Tank said.

Aiden glanced once at the open staircase on the far side of the gallery. Tank could get down that way once the others were in the lift. The coffin was closer. It already had his name on it. If this went badly, at least nobody would misplace the paperwork.

“Catch up,” Aiden told him.

He went over the rail.

The drop emptied his stomach, then filled his whole body with impact. His shoes punched through the coffin lid. Black wood cracked around his shins. Water slapped his legs. His knees buckled, but the broken edges held him upright for one impossible second.

He was standing on his own name with a pistol in his hand.

A woman on the lobby floor lifted her face from behind a bag and stared. Aiden would have liked to say something magnificent. He was occupied finding out whether his spine still came in one piece.

The gunman behind the column rushed him before he got a foot free. Aiden saw the man's hand reaching for his pistol wrist. He wrenched a section of broken lid loose with his other hand and swung it.

Wood cracked against the attacker's shoulder. The man fell into the fountain and pulled Aiden after him.

The water tasted of coins and cleaning fluid. A hand closed at his throat. Aiden shoved a knee between them, drove the man off, and brought the pistol tight against his body.

The hand stopped reaching.

“You finished?” Aiden asked him, coughing water.

The man nodded.

“Use your words,” Aiden told him.

“Finished,” the gunman said.

“Then stay fucking finished,” Aiden ordered.

He kept the pistol raised as he pushed himself against the fountain rim. The second man was still behind the column; Aiden could see one shoe and the end of a gun. He aimed toward that edge until the weapon slipped onto the floor and two empty hands appeared.

“Bring him down!” Tank shouted from above.

His feet thundered along the open gallery stairs. Across the lobby, a bell sounded and the service lift doors opened at ground level. Porsche and the nurse rolled the old man's bed out. Aiden watched them cross behind the fountain, clear of the column now that Tank stood between them and its surrendering occupant.

The old man raised a fist toward Aiden.

“That's my boy!” the patient shouted.

Aiden didn't know him from anybody. Still, the pride in that voice made something hurt beneath the part of him already hurting. He was collecting fathers faster than the bad ones could die.

Tank reached over the fountain rim and got an arm under his. Aiden's shoes dragged through the water, then struck marble. He tried to stand. His legs declined. Tank kept him upright.

Aiden put his pistol back in his waistband while Tank held him upright.

Outside, the two white sedans began to move. The men who'd stayed with them were trying to leave.

The first reached the gate as a police cruiser hit its side. The crash boomed through the broken entrance. The sedan stopped diagonally across the exit, trapping the second behind it.

A woman stepped out of the cruiser carrying a long gun.

“Hands where I can fucking see them!” she shouted.

Hands appeared at every window. One man pushed his elbows outside too, apparently unwilling to risk a misunderstanding.

Two minutes later, Chief Gloria Knox came through the entrance. Aiden recognized her from enough photographs to know that the silver at her temples hadn't softened anything. Her uniform remained neat after using a cruiser to change another car's plans.

Officers went past her toward the fountain and the stairs. She looked from the broken rail to the coffin, then to Aiden.

“You jumped from there?” Knox asked.

“Nigga was in my way,” Aiden said.

She tilted her head. “And that solved it?”

The man in the fountain groaned as an officer pulled his arms behind him.

Knox smiled. “Apparently.”

An officer above called that the corridor and stairwell were secure. Vega arrived with Shawna, Rosa, and Mercedes. Aiden watched Shawna come toward him fast enough that the doctor had to put an arm across her while checking his wound.

Vega made a sound that wasn't a word but seemed to include his name, his stupidity, and the bill.

“Everybody's alive,” Aiden said, nodding toward the patient and the nurse.

“You were in bed,” Vega reminded him.

“Didn't have wheels,” Aiden said.

Porsche lifted her wet hand. “I tried a chair.”

Vega closed her eyes. When she opened them, she looked at the nurse, then back at Aiden.

“You saved my nurse,” the doctor said.

“She saved my ass first,” Aiden told her.

The nurse began crying with both hands still locked around the bed rail. The patient wanted Aiden's hand. Tank helped him close enough to offer it, and the old man's papery fingers squeezed warmly around his.

Knox slipped a card into Aiden's hand after the patient released it.

“When you're tired of people sending coffins, come see me,” she said. “I'd rather give you a badge.”

Tank stared at her. “This nigga?”

“Especially this nigga,” Knox told him.

Shawna finally got both arms around Aiden. Carefully at first, then tightly enough for him to feel her shaking. She kissed his wet face and called him impossible, laughing and furious. He tried to answer, but her mouth reached his before the words did.

He stood dripping fountain water and bleeding again, his woman's cheek against his, while people stared up at the broken railing and back at him. He could feel their attention from every part of the lobby. It was a good deal less useful than Tank's arm holding him upright.

That evening, Aiden watched workers carry the coffin out on a squealing trolley. Knox had it loaded onto a truck as evidence. Two cuffed attackers waited nearby with officers; more police moved between the cars and the hospital wing. The man Aiden had fought in the fountain kept looking toward his window.

Aiden gave him a little wave from the bed.

Vega caught him doing it.

“Rest,” she said.

He rested. He had no doubt she could finish what those men had started without disturbing one hair on her head.

***

Vega let him leave on his fifth day in the hospital. Leaving, she made clear, was not healing.

Aiden could walk slowly. He could sit. He could get tired halfway across a room and pretend he'd stopped to admire a lamp. The hospital had a lot of lamps. He'd become a fucking expert.

Shawna sat beside him for the instructions. She asked questions and made him listen again when he looked toward the window. Tank stood behind the wheelchair with two duffels and Rosa's red coat over one arm.

“No heroics,” Vega told Aiden.

“He won't,” Shawna promised.

Vega looked at her. “You either.”

Aiden watched them exchange a look. He didn't especially like how well they appeared to understand each other.

Before Tank moved the chair, Shawna unfastened the cross. She leaned over Aiden, passed the chain behind his head, and worked the clasp shut. The gold settled against his clean shirt. Her fingers held it there a moment, then smoothed his collar.

“There,” she said.

He took her hand before she could draw it away.

Six black cars waited beneath the entrance canopy. The broken glass was gone. Men straightened beside the cars as Tank wheeled Aiden into the warm, dry air. One opened the Mercedes door.

“Welcome back,” the man said.

Back. Like Aiden had owned a kingdom long enough to be missed. These men had learned his name while he was bleeding through somebody else's sheets.

He put his hands on the chair arms and stood because he wanted Shawna to see him stand. His knees held. His side made its opinion known. She took his arm without congratulating him, giving him something to lean against without making a production of it.

“Where we going?” Shawna asked.

The cross rested warm beneath Aiden's collar. Somewhere inside, a telephone rang. He looked down at the woman beside him.

“Home, baby,” he told her. “We just gotta get one.”
