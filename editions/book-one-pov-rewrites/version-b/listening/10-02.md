Four cars took them to the Saint: Aiden and Shawna in the police Lamborghini, Tank in the black Mercedes, Rosa driving Marcellus, Porsche bringing Mercedes in the green Bentley.

Aiden had wanted six cars. Tank asked who would drive the extras. When Aiden suggested putting one person in each, Tank asked which people. Apparently this was the night he'd chosen to become a mathematician.

Four cars still looked good beneath the canopy.

Aiden stepped out in his white suit, the cross against his chest and the badge inside his jacket. Shawna took his hand. Cameras flashed. He'd prepared himself for guns; the flashes still made his shoulders tighten.

“Keep walking,” she whispered.

He did, slowly enough to make the sidewalk look like part of his purchase.

A photographer stopped them against a wall of white roses. Shawna put a hand on his shoulder. Aiden settled his at her waist. A reporter asked whether he was taking over T's interests.

“Anybody owes them anything, owes me,” Aiden said.

“Relationship with Mr. Redmond?” the reporter asked next.

Redmond. The motherfucker had brought a government name to the party.

“Ask at the end,” Aiden told him.

Rosa moved between them and the reporter before another question arrived. Tank followed closely enough for Aiden to feel his jacket brush his own.

“Anything happens,” Tank said quietly, touching Aiden's arm, “stay with me.”

“Already the idea,” Aiden told him.

Tank didn't answer.

Inside, a curved staircase surrounded a statue of a naked woman holding a bowl of real fire. Aiden paused long enough to appreciate the expense and the commitment to making the building nervous.

Jewels moved through pools of warm light. A preacher Aiden recognized from television stood beside a man he knew dealt in stolen cars. The preacher had the larger watch. Beyond an open lounge door, champagne bottles crowded a table dusted with cocaine.

A man inside raised his glass. Aiden remembered a long weekend with him before Shawna, and the two women sitting beside him brought the rest of it back.

“Disappear when you fall in love?” the man called to Aiden.

“Got promoted,” Aiden told him.

The women laughed. Shawna looked at all three, then at Aiden.

“All of them?” she asked.

“Long weekend,” he said.

She shook her head. “Nasty motherfucker.”

“Love me,” Aiden reminded her.

She squeezed his fingers. “Don't get confident.”

He felt confidence arrive anyway. By the time Red descended the far staircase in a dark green jacket, Aiden was smiling as though he'd come to welcome him.

Red looked past that smile to Shawna.

“There she is,” Red said.

He held out both hands. Shawna gave him one. He kissed it above Dante's watch.

Aiden imagined Red's head driven through the flowers. The green jacket burning beside the statue. His own hands putting the man on the floor while the television preacher advised God to mind His business.

He kept his hands at his sides and smiled.

“Good party,” Aiden told Red. “Shame about your future.”

Red finally looked at him.

“Captain,” Red said, giving the word an awkward pause. “Clean up well.”

“Still talking,” Aiden answered.

Shawna's free hand tightened around his arm. He felt the pressure through his sleeve and let it hold him still.

Red called for a photograph. All three, he specified. He guided Shawna into the middle and rested his right hand against her upper arm. A broad dark ring held a blue stone almost flush against that hand.

Aiden glanced toward his friends while the photographer arranged them. Marcellus stood a few feet away, perfectly still. Then the photographer asked Aiden to face forward.

The flash went off.

“Another,” Red told the photographer.

Aiden looked straight into the lens. Keeping his face still took work. Shawna's hand stayed against his sleeve until the second flash.

An attendant brought glossy prints while Red greeted Rosa. Aiden watched her take two, slide them into her purse, and smile at the host.

“Promised this woman a dance,” Red said, offering his arm to Shawna.

She looked at Aiden.

He remembered the plan. Her hand over his at the dining table. Red had to feel victorious. Close and foolish, she'd explained. Aiden had agreed to every word.

He also remembered that he had hands.

“One dance,” Aiden said.

Red smiled. “Let's see what she wants.”

Shawna took Red's arm. Aiden watched them go onto the floor, following every step as though something inside his chest had left with her and he needed to see where it was being carried.
