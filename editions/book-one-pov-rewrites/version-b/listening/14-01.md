## 14. Two Words to Send

Marcellus held Aiden's phone beneath the dining-room lamp.

The old man's thumb moved slowly across the photograph, enlarging Red's hand until Shawna's face disappeared beyond the screen. Aiden wanted him to put her back. He stood close enough to see a curved strip of blue metal beneath the stone.

“The mounting,” Marcellus said. “There.”

“Tell me you're sure this time,” Aiden said.

Marcellus looked over the phone. “Sure about my work.”

“We were sure at the party.”

“You were,” Marcellus replied.

Aiden tightened his grip on the chair. He wanted to shake the answer out of him. He wanted the old man to be right almost as badly as he wanted Shawna to speak again.

“Show me,” Aiden said.

Marcellus opened the decoy's box on the table. Under the lamp, it looked rich enough to make a sensible man reconsider being sensible. Aiden could still feel the impact it had made against the guard's head. Its one honest achievement all evening.

The old man set the phone beside it.

“Changed the jewelry around the other stone,” Marcellus said. “Dark stones at the edges. Makes your eye measure the whole thing, then think the blue part's smaller. But kept this piece beneath it.”

He pointed to the curved metal in the photograph.

“Saw the shoulder of it at the gala,” Marcellus continued. “From the side. Couldn't get close enough. This picture gives me the underside.”

Tank leaned over his other shoulder.

“Kept it because it's gold?” Tank asked.

“Kept it because he's an idiot,” Marcellus said.

Rosa let out a short breath. “Finally. Good fucking news.”

The old man didn't smile. He moved the phone to show Aiden the curve under the stone, then the corresponding shape on the decoy. It wasn't a hidden map. It was something a hand had made and its maker remembered.

“Both stones fit that work,” Marcellus reminded him. “This one on the table is the copy. The ring is where I'd look for the original.”

“You can tell forty million through my phone?” Aiden asked.

“Can tell you he hid familiar work badly,” Marcellus answered. “To swear to the diamond, I need the diamond in my hand. Need the flaw inside it. No photograph gives me that.”

Aiden looked at the phone again. Red's fingers curved under Shawna's chin. He might be wearing the thing Dante had died trying to find. Touching her with it. Letting a camera record the joke.

Aiden pushed the chair back.

“We'll get it,” he said.

Marcellus looked at him without reaching for the box.

“Say the whole promise,” Marcellus told him.

“What?” Aiden asked.

“If he says her or the diamond?”

“Her,” Aiden said.

The answer came before he could consider whether it hurt the old man.

“If he wants the letter burned?” Marcellus asked.

“Burn it,” Aiden answered.

Marcellus held his eyes. “Proof of what he did?”

“Gets it,” Aiden said. “Want my promise? Bringing her home. Everything else finds a way to come with us.”

The old man's mouth tightened. He nodded once.

“Dante said something very much like that,” Marcellus told him.

Aiden caught the edge of the table.

“Not Dante,” he said.

“I know,” Marcellus answered.

“Then stop putting him between me and every fucking thing I do.”

Rosa said Aiden's name. He shook his head before she could continue.

“Love her now,” Aiden said. “Me. Standing here bleeding. Going out there. Remember him all you want. When her phone rings, want her to know who's calling.”

His voice had risen until he could hear it shake. He hated the sound, hated all three of them being present for it.

Marcellus lowered the phone. He took Dante's folder from the end of the table, where Tank had put it after bringing it in from the broken doorway. The old man laid the gala records beside Celeste's letter. Rosa gave him the envelope containing the original order from the watch.

He kept the papers separate.

A dead woman's handwriting. A dead man's warning. The records of men who had decided death belonged in the same columns as money. Aiden watched the old hands arrange them and knew he meant what he'd said. He would burn every page if Shawna walked through the door while they burned.

Marcellus slid the phone toward him.

“Give her something to answer,” the old man said.

***

Aiden opened Shawna's messages.

The last one he'd sent before the gala read: *You look good as fuck.*

Her reply was a photograph of one eye, part of her forehead, and her middle finger. He had expected a picture of the dress. Instead the phone had apparently insulted her, and she had documented the argument.

He stared at it until the screen blurred.

Rosa stood beside him, looking down.

“Sent me six,” Rosa said softly. “Bathroom ceiling in one. Whole gorgeous ceiling, bitch nowhere in sight.”

Aiden tried to laugh. The sound failed halfway out. He wiped his face with the heel of his uncut hand, then lifted the phone so neither of them could keep looking at him.

He typed: *I'm coming. Wear whatever you want.*

His thumb stopped over Send.

She couldn't choose what to wear. Couldn't choose the room or the chair. Red might read it first. The man was between them even in this little lighted rectangle, and Aiden wanted to reach through it and close both hands around his neck.

He deleted the second sentence.

Two words remained.

*I'm coming.*

Aiden sent them.

The message appeared beneath her ridiculous photograph. He watched for an answer. None came.

Rosa guided his injured hand onto the towel.

“Let me finish,” she told him.

He sat. She wiped blood away from his fingers and wrapped the cuts. It hurt more now that someone was touching them gently. Aiden kept the phone in his other hand, screen turned up.

Tank knelt beside the dining table and checked for money beneath the chairs. When he rose, he placed the last loose bills in the open bag instead of claiming there had never been any on the floor.

“She answer?” Tank asked.

Aiden shook his head.

Tank looked toward the front window. Through the broken door, Aiden could hear the lake wind move the pieces of glass still hanging in the frame. This was the house he had promised her from the bloodied car. A room for singing. Water outside. Now a towel covered his hand and her chair stood empty beside three million dollars.

He looked at the piano instead of the door.

Shawna had brought herself here. She had picked candles, complained about keys, left a shoe where nobody sensible would put one. Red couldn't make all those choices disappear by telling him she belonged somewhere else.

Aiden set the phone faceup beside the towel.

“When we go,” he told Tank, “drive like you know who's in the car.”

Tank met his eyes. “I do.”

Aiden let that answer remain between them. He wasn't ready to make it forgiveness. He was ready to use it.

Marcellus closed the decoy's box. The small click made Aiden look over. The old man held it a moment before putting it beside the records.

“Keep those straight,” Aiden told him, nodding toward the papers.

“They are,” Marcellus said.

Rosa finished wrapping Aiden's hand. He flexed his fingers, stopped when she glared, and picked up the phone again.

Still nothing.

He could call. Make Red answer. Hear her for a second and pay for it with whatever begging the man wanted. But the last call had ended with terms already set. He looked at the time, then at the bags, measuring how much of the night remained.

The phone vibrated in his palm.

Ten thirty-seven.

Red's message filled the screen: *On our way. Don't disappoint her.*

Aiden stood. Rosa let him. Tank had already reached for his own phone.

“Call everybody,” Aiden told him.

Tank paused with the screen lit in his hand.

“Everybody?” Tank asked.

Aiden looked at the empty piano bench, the bags on the table, and the people still beside him.

“Everybody who ever called me brother,” he said.
