At the head of the ballroom stairs, Aiden saw a guard pull the main doors shut.

Guests packed the space in front of him. One man held a chair over his head for protection. A woman had climbed onto a table, found that no safer, and was begging someone to help her down. The guard planted his back against the doors as though this was an argument over the dress code.

“Open them!” Rosa shouted from beside Aiden.

The guard raised his gun.

Tank pulled Aiden behind the banister as a bullet struck the wall above them. Plaster fell into his collar.

Aiden had left Shawna upstairs. That fact filled him so completely he could scarcely fit the room around it. He had taken the box and the papers. He had followed her instructions out of the place where she needed him. Now someone was shooting at Rosa to keep the rest of the guests from leaving too.

He pushed the box into the deep inside pocket of his jacket. He slid the folder under his belt, between shirt and trousers, and pulled his badge clear.

“Put me down,” Aiden told Tank.

“Aiden—” Tank began.

“Not going upstairs,” Aiden said. “Getting these people out. Put me on my fucking feet.”

Tank lowered him. Aiden gripped the rail until the stairs stopped tilting, then drew his pistol.

“Police!” Aiden called toward the doors.

The guard laughed.

Aiden opened his jacket with his free hand. Gold caught the light.

“Captain,” he corrected the man. “Try the joke with my title.”

The guard's gaze moved between badge and pistol. For a moment Aiden thought the man might discover intelligence in time to use it.

Then he fired again.

Aiden swung over the lower section of banister and landed hard beside a table. Pain shot up his side. He crouched behind the table. Tank drew his own pistol and came down the stairs, firing toward the doors.

A woman in diamonds caught Aiden's sleeve.

“Are we going to die?” she asked him.

Aiden glanced at the guard's bullet holes. “Not with him shooting. Get down.”

She crawled behind the table. Her husband followed so closely he left one shoe in the open and made no attempt to honor its service.

A second guard appeared in a side corridor. He started bringing his gun around the corner toward Aiden.

Rex Crown hit him from behind.

They crashed into the champagne tower. Glass poured down their shoulders. Rex drove the man against the wall and held him there with a forearm across his chest.

“Captain!” Rex shouted.

Aiden fired toward the first guard, forcing him behind a planter beside the doors. Tank reached the opposite door and wrenched it open. Guests ran past him into the lobby.

At the champagne table, the second guard struck Rex across the brow with his pistol. The boxer stumbled. His grip broke. The guard rolled away and began rising, weapon aimed at Rex's head.

Aiden holstered his own gun as he crossed behind him. He caught the gun arm with his right hand and drove the heavy blue box, still wrapped in the lining of his left jacket pocket, against the man's temple.

The guard collapsed.

Whatever Marcellus had seen inside that box, it had just bought one living boxer.

Aiden pulled the pistol from the man's slack fingers and kicked it beneath the bar. He settled the box back into its pocket before helping Rex sit up.

“Twice,” Rex told him. Blood ran from the boxer's eyebrow.

“Keeping score?” Aiden asked.

“Beat me. Then saved me,” Rex said.

“Keep up,” Aiden told him. “Night ain't over.”

Rex laughed, showing blood on his teeth, and put a shoulder beneath Aiden's good arm. They moved toward Tank's open door. Rosa was pushing Marcellus ahead of her through the fleeing guests.

A grinding sound came from above the stairs.

Aiden looked up. The bronze woman mounted beside the landing had begun leaning away from her base. She held a bowl of decorative flame over her head. Now burning fuel ran down her raised arms, spilled over the flowers beneath her, and lit them with a soft, terrible rush.

He didn't see what had loosened the statue. He saw where it was falling.

Mercedes stood beneath it, trying to pull a fallen man through the passage toward the lobby.

“Move!” Aiden shouted.

She looked up.

Aiden pulled away from Rex and ran the few steps to her. The bronze struck the banister as he reached them. Wood split. He shoved Mercedes and the man through the passage, following with his shoulder against both of them.

They hit the floor beyond the opening. Behind Aiden, the lower section of railing and stair trim crashed onto the carpet. The bowl landed separately, sending fire across the flowers and spilled alcohol.

Heat rolled over his back. His wound seemed to open all the way through him.

Mercedes took his face in both hands.

“You got me?” she asked. “Aiden, you got me?”

“Got you,” he said.

“All of me?” Mercedes asked.

“Count your fucking legs,” Aiden told her.

She looked down. Two legs. One shoe. Her expensive skirt had acquired a hole large enough to qualify as a second entrance.

Mercedes kissed his forehead so hard he saw a flash of light.

“Beautiful, stupid, magnificent motherfucker,” she said against his skin.

He tried to get up and couldn't. Rex and Tank reached him together. They lifted him while Mercedes helped the other man stand.

Porsche held the outer lobby door with one leg, shouting directions over the alarms. People obeyed her with the speed and seriousness the guard had failed to obtain with a gun.

The woman in diamonds caught Aiden's hand as he passed. She pushed her wedding ring into his palm.

“For saving us,” she said.

Aiden closed her fingers around it again.

“Keep your husband nervous,” he told her. “Got a woman waiting.”

She laughed through her tears.

Cold air struck his face outside. Shawna wasn't there.

He looked through the doorway toward the stairs. Tank caught his shoulder before he could turn all the way back.

“Not that way,” Tank said.

Aiden stared at him.

“She told me to get you out,” Tank continued. “You're alive enough to go get her somewhere else. That's what we have.”

The alarms were deafening. Beyond them, sirens approached. Aiden let Tank take him to the Mercedes.

Rosa went toward another car with Marcellus, then came back to Aiden's open passenger window. She pushed one of the gala photographs into his hand.

“She gave me this,” Rosa said.

Shawna stood between him and Red in the picture. Red's ring caught the flash beside her arm. Aiden's hand rested at her waist. They were all still standing where the photographer had put them.

Rosa pressed her lips together before she could finish.

“For when you two got married,” she said. “Told me to keep one.”

Aiden couldn't answer her.

Tank started the Mercedes. Through the window Aiden saw Knox's officers beside the police Lamborghini. One came to the window.

“We'll bring your car, Captain,” the officer told Aiden. “Soon as the road's clear.”

Tank followed Rosa's car out into traffic.

***

Marcellus called before they had gone far.

Aiden laid the photograph on his lap and set the phone to speaker in the console. He took the box out again. His fingers stuck to its edge.

“Listen carefully,” Marcellus told him. “The stone in your hand is the duplicate.”

Aiden opened it beneath the dashboard light. Blue looked back at him, beautiful and silent.

“Sure?” he asked.

“I made it,” Marcellus said.

Aiden shut the lid. They had gone inside with Shawna and come out carrying a lie in velvet.

“Red know?” Tank asked.

Marcellus paused. “He expected you to take it.”

Streetlights passed over Aiden's hands. He watched them because each time he stopped, he saw Red's arm across Shawna's chest.

Rosa came onto the speaker.

“We still have the original order,” she told Aiden. “And you have his records.”

He pulled the bent folder from beneath his belt. Numbers. Payments. Valentino settled in red ink. It was probably enough to mean something to someone, somewhere, with a chair and time to read.

“Don't want his papers,” Aiden said.

“I know,” Rosa told him.

“Want her.”

“I know, Aiden,” Rosa said.

Another call lit the screen. Unknown number. Aiden ended the first and answered before the ring finished.

“Shawna?” he asked.

Red breathed into the phone.

“Noticed my gift?” Red asked.

Aiden pressed the phone to his ear. “Let me hear her.”

“Your friend has my three million,” Red said. “You have my papers. Someone has the original note.”

“Let me hear her,” Aiden repeated.

He heard movement, a door, a faint scrape. Then her voice came through.

“Baby,” Shawna said.

Aiden closed his eyes. “I'm here.”

“I know,” she said.

“Coming,” Aiden told her.

“I know,” Shawna said again.

The phone moved away from her.

“Unfinished casino in the desert,” Red told Aiden. “Midnight. Money, papers, original. All of it.”

Aiden looked at the dashboard clock. Less than three hours.

“Hurt her and—” he began.

“Come before I get bored,” Red interrupted.

The call ended. Aiden put the phone in the console, slid the folder back under his belt, and picked up the photograph from his lap.

Tank drove with both hands on the wheel. Aiden held the photograph in one hand and the box in the other. His suit was bloody. His cheek still burned where Shawna had struck it. The parts of him she had touched seemed easier to find than the rest.

“Take me to the money,” Aiden told Tank.

“Yeah,” Tank said.

“All of it.”

“Yeah,” Tank repeated.

Aiden put the photograph inside his jacket, against the cross. At the next light, traffic stopped. A city full of people sat obediently beneath a red circle while Shawna was somewhere he couldn't reach.

“After we get her,” Aiden said.

Tank glanced at him. “We are getting her.”

Aiden watched the light until it changed.

“After,” he told Tank, “there won't be enough desert left to hide what we do to him.”
