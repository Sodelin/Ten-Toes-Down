The Mercedes had scarcely cleared the drive when a black Charger came up behind it without headlights. Shawna caught it in her mirror.

“Aiden. Don't look,” she said.

He looked. He'd owned the car barely eleven minutes. He was checking everything that got close to it.

Two men sat in front of the Charger. A third leaned across the back seat with a long gun. Aiden saw the barrel find the gap between the front seats.

His rear window burst inward.

Shawna folded over the case. Aiden ducked and dragged the wheel with him. The right fender hit the fountain's edge, throwing water across three men in suits. The Mercedes bounced into the street. Glass slid down Aiden's collar. The horn would not stop screaming.

“Your people?” he shouted to Shawna.

“You think I'd be riding with you if they were my people?” she shouted back.

“You met me tonight!” Aiden said, correcting the wheel.

“I'm trying to meet you tomorrow,” Shawna told him. “Drive!”

A yellow Ferrari came from the next street and pulled beside them. Its driver smiled. His passenger lifted a pistol. Aiden resented the comfort of the seat the man was trying to kill him from.

“Move your hand,” he told Shawna.

She looked toward him.

“The horn,” Aiden said.

Her wrist had been pressing it since the fountain. She lifted her hand. For one beautiful second, there was only wind, engine noise, and the wheel warm beneath his palms.

He drove toward the Ferrari.

The passenger saw it first. His pistol jerked upward. The driver turned too late. Yellow metal struck a pole, folded at the nose, and spun across a valet entrance. Sparks skittered under people suddenly finding reasons to run.

“That's one,” Aiden said.

Shawna stared out her window. “That's a Ferrari!”

“One Ferrari,” he told her. “Keep up.”

The Charger stayed behind them. In the mirror, Aiden saw the long gun move again. A red convertible shot out of a hotel drive and cut across the Charger's nose.

Mercedes was standing in the passenger seat with her heels in one hand.

“I told you about my fucking flowers, Aiden!” she shouted.

Porsche drove, red hair blowing against a red dress. Aiden had once seen her leave a party in a sports car a man bought her, while the man stood crying because she actually meant to drive away. Aiden had watched him try to apologize to the taillights.

“Sit down, Mercedes!” Porsche shouted now.

“He owes me!” Mercedes shouted back.

“He owes the whole street right now!” Porsche told her.

Mercedes dropped into the seat. Porsche moved left when the Charger tried left, right when it tried right. Aiden watched her keep the black car behind the red one with quick, exact turns.

“Your friends?” Shawna asked him.

“Good people,” Aiden said.

She looked back and laughed once. A happy sound, brief and startled. He felt it beneath the engine noise.

Then the Charger hit the convertible's rear bumper.

The red car slid sideways. Mercedes grabbed the windshield frame. One silver heel flew from her hand, flashed beneath a streetlight, and landed among tables outside a restaurant.

Aiden braked.

“Aiden!” Shawna cried, bracing the case against her knees.

“Not leaving them,” he told her.

He dropped behind the convertible and put the Mercedes between it and the Charger. In the mirror, the driver's face grew large. A white scar crossed one eyebrow. He bared his teeth.

“Choose me, nigga,” Aiden muttered.

The Charger obliged. The impact drove Aiden's head into the seat. Metal screamed behind them. Shawna caught his arm with her free hand. The rear bumper began dragging against the street.

Ahead, Porsche caught the convertible before it struck a wall. One finger rose above her door. Aiden grinned despite his split lip.

A truck crossed the intersection carrying a silver speedboat. Its bow rode above the cab. Beside the road, the delivery ramp of a closed hotel rose toward a low roof. Aiden looked at the ramp, then at the boat moving across the gap beyond it.

“No,” Shawna said, watching his face.

“You don't know what I'm thinking,” Aiden told her.

“I know exactly what you're thinking,” she said.

He tightened his grip on the wheel. “Then hold the case.”

The ramp hit beneath the car like a fist. His teeth shut. The headlights climbed the wall ahead, then had nothing left to touch.

The Mercedes left the ground.

For an instant the Strip filled the windshield below them, bright rows of hotel windows hanging in the dark. The boat's little flag snapped beneath the hood. Shawna screamed his name. Aiden couldn't get air deep enough to answer. His stomach rose against his ribs while the wheel felt suddenly loose in his hands.

The ground came back with a crash. The seat drove into his spine. A tire burst, and the wheel kicked hard against his thumbs. He fought the turn, caught it, kept the car pointed down the road. Rubber began slapping the pavement.

Behind them, the Charger tried the turn too late. Aiden saw it slide into the bare rear section of the trailer. The impact boomed through their missing window. A black wheel rolled free into the intersection, travelling farther than the car that lost it.

Shawna twisted to look behind them. Nothing followed.

At the next light Porsche drew alongside. Mercedes held her remaining heel like she was looking for somebody large enough to deserve it.

“You jumped a fucking boat!” Porsche shouted across to Aiden.

“Had to get over it somehow,” he called back.

Porsche hit her steering wheel with one palm. “With a car, Aiden!”

A man waiting for a taxi began applauding. The couple beside him joined in. Then a whole line of people put down their luggage and clapped at the wrecked Mercedes.

Aiden lifted a hand. Shawna caught it and put it back on the wheel.

“They can admire you moving,” she told him.

Mercedes pointed at Shawna. “Keep him. He's expensive, but he comes with a show.”

Porsche blew Aiden a kiss and turned away. He took the damaged car toward the desert. The tire struck a steady complaint into the road. Shawna reached over and touched his cheek.

“You could've kept going when they hit the girls,” she said.

“Wouldn't have been me,” Aiden told her.

She watched him for a moment. Her hand moved from his cheek to his thigh and stayed there. He kept both hands on the wheel now without being reminded.

***

Beyond the last casinos, Shawna told him to stop. He said T's place was farther out.

“I know where his fucking house is,” she answered. “Pull over.”

Aiden turned behind a closed wedding chapel. Half its sign had burned out. The surviving bulbs cast pink light across an empty lot and left the chapel windows black. He stopped the car. The engine ticked hard beneath the hood. Through the missing rear glass came desert air and the smell of hot rubber.

Shawna opened the case across her knees.

Money filled it in tight, orderly bundles. Three million dollars, she told him. Aiden stared. That afternoon he'd eaten a gas-station sandwich in a borrowed car. He hadn't considered asking the sandwich whether it knew this was possible.

“T said—” he began.

“T says a lot,” Shawna interrupted.

She took an envelope from beneath the cash. Inside was a photograph folded across the middle. Aiden recognized the chapel behind the couple before he recognized her. Shawna in a white dress. A man with his arm around her waist.

The man's cross was hanging from Aiden's neck.

“Dante,” Shawna said. “T's brother. My husband.”

Aiden looked from the photograph to the building outside. He touched the cross. She had been turning it over at the party, pressing those little letters against his chest, while he tried to make her laugh.

Her lighter sparked twice without catching. He took it, cupped the flame, and held it for her cigarette. She watched the flame a moment before leaning in.

She told him she'd come from Phoenix at twenty-three with eight hundred dollars, a suitcase, and a number somebody said could help her sing. Valentino's number.

Aiden knew the name. White suits. A diamond in one tooth. Hotels full of women watching his hands while men laughed around him.

“Clothes, a room, people he knew in music,” Shawna said, counting the things on the fingers holding her cigarette. “Then every fucking thing he'd given me was something I owed him.”

Aiden watched ash lengthen above the case. She tapped it through the open window.

“Dante came into the club on my birthday,” she continued. “Everybody wanted something. He asked what kind of cake I liked.”

Her mouth almost curved. She looked at the photograph instead. Dante came back the next night, she told Aiden, carrying an entire carrot cake. He set it among the bottles. Valentino laughed at him. Dante knocked out his diamond tooth.

“My nigga,” Aiden said before he could stop himself.

Shawna turned toward him. “That's what I said. Exactly what I fucking said.”

She wiped one eye with her thumb. Six weeks after the cake, they'd married in this chapel. Three years ago. T stood beside his brother. Red paid for the room. Aiden studied the smiling couple in the photograph while she described the wedding around them.

“Then Dante took money from a deal,” she said. “Three million. Said we were leaving. Said he had a brother, so he didn't have to be scared.”

She turned the picture over. On the back were the words Damn, boy. Magnificent.

Aiden opened his hand around the cross. Shawna pointed at the little letters and told him about Dante seeing himself in the chapel mirror after the ceremony. He'd said the words so seriously she nearly dropped her flowers laughing. The next morning she had the initials engraved.

“He was right about you,” Aiden said.

“He was talking about himself,” Shawna corrected him.

Aiden looked down at the gold. “Then he finally got it on the right neck.”

Her laugh came out sharp and bright. She covered her mouth with the hand holding the photograph. For a moment she looked at him without looking past him, and he wanted to keep that moment exactly where it was.

Then she pressed the picture against her chest.

They had left Dante in the desert, she told him. Valentino came with his watch and said Dante died begging somebody to look after her.

Aiden's hand closed on the cross. He wanted Valentino in the car. Wanted enough room to turn and put his hands on the man, and no room at all for Valentino to get away.

Shawna said she'd waited eight months. Let Valentino think she came back because she had nowhere else to go. Let him believe, eventually, that she loved him.

“Last Tuesday I shot him in his car,” she told Aiden. “He was talking. Kept talking after the first shot, so I shot him again.”

She raised her wrist. The watch hung too loose and slid toward her hand.

“Glove compartment,” she said. “That's where he kept it.”

Aiden watched the face catch the chapel's pink light. Red had promised to protect her afterward, she explained. Tonight she'd taken the three million back. T said he could get her out of Vegas.

Aiden remembered T's cologne, his hand on Aiden's neck. Don't fall in love with her. What else had he left out of the assignment?

“You believe him?” Aiden asked.

“I believed his brother,” Shawna said.

She put the photograph inside her dress and shut the case. The catches snapped in the quiet. Then she leaned across and kissed him.

Smoke touched his mouth. Her fingers held his jaw. The money disappeared from his attention. So did the broken car, the promises upstairs at the club, the hands that had put this gold around his neck.

Shawna drew back without letting go of his face.

“Don't take me to that house,” she said.

“Then I'm not taking you,” Aiden told her. “Give me somewhere else.”
