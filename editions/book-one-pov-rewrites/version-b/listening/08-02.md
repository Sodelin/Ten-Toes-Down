Marcellus worked with a small tool beneath the lamp. His fingers had the patience Aiden had lacked with the clasp. While he worked, he explained that Dante had asked in prison about keeping a letter somewhere safe. After Dante's release, an old client arranged a legal repair commission that brought the watch to Marcellus. He made the hidden space and returned it with the engraved cross.

Aiden listened closely to the word returned. Dante had planned to wear it again. To put something away and live long enough to take it out.

A thin folded paper lay inside.

Marcellus placed it on the table and flattened his hands beside it. Shawna opened it.

The scrap had been torn from a longer note. Red ink. Crowded, slanting handwriting Aiden recognized from the invitation he'd carried that morning.

He read over her shoulder.

*T — He has the withdrawal and Vale's story. If he gets the stone, he can name every one of us. Finish Dante tonight. No more brother shit. Valentino takes the watch. Keep her alive. I'll deal with her. R.*

Beneath it, in darker ink, Dante had added four words.

*Shawna. If I'm wrong.*

She read twice. On the third pass, her eyes stopped moving.

“No,” Shawna said.

Aiden reached for her hand. She pulled away and stood so sharply that the chair fell behind her.

“He knew?” she asked Marcellus.

The old man looked at the note. “He suspected.”

“He fucking knew,” Shawna said.

Aiden picked up the paper. Red's breakfast voice came back to him, explaining protection and the dress he'd bought. Here was another arrangement in the same handwriting: kill the husband, distribute his things, keep the widow alive to be dealt with.

He didn't know how Dante had obtained the note. Didn't know why he'd read it and still gone to T. Yet he'd written if, leaving room for a version that didn't mean what the words said.

Aiden understood that part too well. He'd waited beside the motel pool for T to tell him a lie he could believe.

Shawna stood by the window with both hands against her head.

“He went to talk to his brother,” she said. “Said there was a mistake to straighten out. I was angry he wouldn't stay for dinner.”

Marcellus closed his eyes.

“I told him I'd throw his plate away,” Shawna said.

Aiden rose.

“Shawna,” he said.

She turned toward him. “Don't say it.”

“I wasn't—” he began.

“Don't say there was nothing I could do,” she told him. “I could've locked the door. Gone with him. I could've—”

“Died too,” Aiden said.

She slapped him across the mouth.

The split lip opened. Blood touched his tongue. He stood with his head turned, breathing through his nose. When he looked back, her hand was still raised.

“Aiden,” Shawna said.

He took her wrist, lowered her hand, and let go. Marcellus watched from the table. Aiden didn't trust the first thing gathering in his throat to become words.

“I'm going outside,” he said.

He went.

***

Tank stood beside the lake throwing pieces of bread to the birds. He looked up and saw Aiden's mouth.

“What happened?” Tank asked.

“Don't,” Aiden said.

Tank put the bread down. They stood together until Aiden could breathe without imagining his hands closing around something breakable.

“She hit you?” Tank asked.

Aiden looked at him. “Said don't.”

“All right,” Tank answered.

A bird walked between them, took the whole remaining roll, and waddled away with it.

Tank watched it go. “That was mine.”

“Tell him,” Aiden said.

Tank stayed where he was. Aiden nearly laughed. His mouth hurt too much, so he sat on the low wall instead.

“Red ordered Dante killed,” Aiden told him. “His own handwriting.”

Tank stared at the water.

“You know?” Aiden asked.

“No,” Tank said.

“Hear anything?” Aiden pressed.

“That Dante took money,” Tank answered. “That was what people said around me. T had me moving things, driving people. I wasn't in every room.”

Aiden wanted to believe him. He wanted one person he cared about to finish telling him something before another person brought the missing piece.

“I'm taking the diamond,” Aiden said.

Tank's jaw shifted. “And then?”

“Look at how poor Red got,” Aiden told him.

“Forty million isn't everything he has,” Tank said.

“Then I'll stay busy,” Aiden answered.

The back door opened. Shawna came down with a wet cloth and the watch in her other hand. Tank picked up the empty bread bag and left them alone.

She stopped in front of Aiden.

“I shouldn't have done that,” Shawna said.

“No shit,” he answered.

She lifted the cloth. “Can I see?”

He let her turn his chin. The cloth was cool against his mouth. He looked past her shoulder because looking directly at her made the cruel things he might say feel too available.

“I love him,” Shawna said.

“I know,” Aiden told her. “I fucking know.”

Her hand stopped against his chin. “I love you too.”

He looked at her then.

“That last night with Dante,” Aiden asked. “Did you get to tell him you loved him?”

Shawna shook her head. “I was angry about dinner.”

She put the cloth on the wall and touched his face with her empty hand.

“I love you,” she said again.

Aiden took that hand and pressed it over his heartbeat. She stayed still beneath his fingers.

“Don't hit me again,” he told her.

“I won't,” Shawna said.

He looked toward the watch in her other hand.

“And don't take that off to shut me up,” Aiden told her. “Put it back. I want the whole thing when you tell me something.”

She opened the clasp and fastened it around her wrist while he watched.

***

They ate the food Tank had brought. Marcellus placed an old letter beside his plate in a clear sleeve. When Aiden asked, he slid it across.

“Celeste's,” Marcellus said.

Aiden drew the paper from its sleeve. The folds had worn thin. Here were the words themselves, including the parts Shawna couldn't remember. He read what Celeste had written to her husband.

*My love,*

*You think I never understood that stone. I did. I understood every hour you spent getting it right. I know the little words you hid beneath the blue mounting. I know you were waiting for me to find them, and I know you were disappointed when I liked the front so much I forgot to look behind it.*

*I found them the night you fell asleep with your shoes on. I was angry with you for missing supper. I took the necklace off and nearly dropped it, and there you were underneath, saying what you used to say at my door.*

*I came for you.*

*I wore those words against me every night afterward. You never asked whether I'd found them. You just watched me wear it and smiled.*

*I am so tired now, Marcellus. I can't keep driving out there. Don't let anybody tell you I stopped because I stopped loving you. I am stopping because I am sick, and I want the strength I have left for things that don't require me to pass through a locked door.*

*If you get your beautiful stone back, put it down before you touch somebody you love. I know your hands. They are warmer without all that metal in them.*

*And if you are still angry when you come home, be angry. You always were. Just open the curtains. Pay the piano man what I owe him. There is chicken in the freezer because I never learned how to cook for one.*

*I would take the ride again.*

*Celeste.*

Aiden slid the paper carefully into its sleeve. Marcellus took it with clean fingertips and put it inside his coat. The movement reminded Aiden of Shawna protecting Dante's photograph.

“You want your stone?” Aiden asked him.

“Yes,” Marcellus said.

“Want Red dead?” Aiden asked.

The old man looked toward Shawna before answering.

“Yes,” Marcellus said.

Aiden nodded. “We can work together.”

Shawna spread the blue invitations on the table. The necklace appeared on the front.

“Three nights,” she said. “He's displaying it.”

Marcellus picked up an invitation. “He's displaying something.”

“Two stones,” Aiden said.

“Yes,” Marcellus answered.

“You can tell which?” Aiden asked.

Marcellus looked at him over the paper. “Of course.”

“Then you're coming,” Aiden said.

The jeweler touched his face. “He knows this.”

“Knows the man he put inside eight years ago,” Aiden told him. “We give you another suit and another name.”

Rosa had returned while they ate. She inspected Marcellus from her end of the table.

“I know somebody who can make you look rich,” Rosa said.

Marcellus looked around Aiden's dining room. “Apparently people are easy to confuse.”

Tank covered his mouth with a napkin. Aiden let the old man have it. He'd earned one.

Marcellus explained the mounting again. The outer blue setting could be removed. A buyer might wear the stone differently. The diamond's tiny internal flaw was the thing he remembered precisely. The copy didn't have it.

“This necklace?” Shawna asked, touching the printed picture.

“Could hold either,” Marcellus said.

Aiden leaned back. “We get you close enough to look.”

Shawna sat forward. He recognized the movement before she began speaking and disliked what he expected to hear.

“I can get Red to bring us closer,” she said.

“No,” Aiden told her.

“Hear it first,” Shawna said.

He looked at the invitation beneath her fingers. “He wants you back.”

“He wants everybody to see me go back,” she corrected him. “That's what we use.”

She reached across the table and took his hand. She would tell Red she'd made a mistake, that Aiden frightened her, that she was bringing him to the gala so Red could deal with things quietly.

“He believes that?” Tank asked her.

“He wants to,” Shawna said.

Aiden's fingers tightened around hers. “What else?”

“I know where you're keeping the money,” Shawna said. “That's what I tell him.”

“I haven't got his money,” Aiden reminded her.

“He doesn't know that,” she answered.

Tank set his glass down with a soft, careful click. Aiden glanced at it, then back at Shawna.

“I'm tired of running,” she continued. “I'll return if he clears the debt and leaves you alive.”

“Why would he leave me alive?” Aiden asked.

“Because you can give him the west side,” she said.

He let go of her hand. The room quieted.

Shawna leaned closer.

“I'm saying what he'll listen to,” she told him. “You know that.”

He did. It still felt like watching her put on the dress Red bought.

Marcellus folded the invitation. “If he thinks she can deliver you, he'll want you close enough to take.”

“Let him try,” Aiden said.

Rosa pointed between him and Shawna.

“We get near the jewel,” she said. “Marcellus checks it. We leave with his property. Nobody decides to kill somebody halfway through because they get mad.”

Shawna stayed quiet. Rosa kept her finger aimed at Aiden.

“You too,” Rosa added.

“Why's everybody looking at me?” he asked.

“The face,” Rosa said.

Tank nodded as if that settled the matter.

***

Shawna made the call in their bedroom with Aiden beside her. She put it on speaker. Red answered on the second ring.

“There she is,” Red said.

Her eyes closed. Aiden rested his hand on her knee.

“Can you talk?” Shawna asked.

“For you?” Red said.

Aiden wanted to put the phone through the wall. He left his hand where it was.

“I think I made a mistake,” Shawna said.

Red took a moment to answer. Their reflection floated in the dark window, her holding the phone, Aiden sitting rigidly beside her.

“He hurt you?” Red asked.

Shawna looked at the cut on Aiden's mouth.

“I don't know what he'll do next,” she said.

“Come home,” Red told her.

“Can't just leave,” Shawna answered.

“Yes, you can,” Red said.

She steadied the phone in both hands. “You want the money and his streets. I can bring him. You have to promise me something.”

“Anything,” Red told her.

Aiden looked down at the hand on her knee. Anything had come easily from the man who wrote a husband's death into a few lines.

“You let him live,” Shawna said.

Red sighed. “Too good to these boys.”

Aiden moved his hand. Shawna caught it before he could rise.

“I know where the money is,” she told Red. “I'll get him to bring what you need. I'll stay near you at the gala. Everybody can see I'm back. Then you talk to him.”

“And you stay?” Red asked.

Shawna looked at Aiden.

“Yes,” she said.

A long breath came from the speaker. Aiden heard satisfaction in it and had to make himself remain seated.

“Wear the blue dress,” Red said. “The one I liked.”

“I will,” Shawna told him.

“And Shawna?” Red said.

“What?” she asked.

His voice softened. “Knew you'd remember who loved you.”

She ended the call without answering. The sudden silence made the sound of the air conditioning seem enormous.

Aiden knew every word had been a lie. He'd helped arrange it. His chest hurt anyway.

Shawna put the phone down and took his face in both hands.

“Look at me,” she said.

He did.

“I'm here,” Shawna told him.

He pulled her against him too hard. His side burned. He loosened his arms enough to breathe and kept her close.

Three nights. He looked past her at the lake and tried to make three nights feel like something he could wait through.
