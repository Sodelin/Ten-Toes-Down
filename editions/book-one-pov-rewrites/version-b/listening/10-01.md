## 10. Forty Million and One Woman

The van was blocking his Lamborghini.

Aiden stood outside the coffee shop with a cup in each hand, looking across the hood at the vehicle parked sideways in front of him. An alarm was ringing inside the casino across the street. Four men came through its doors carrying bags. One pointed a gun backward. Another had blood on his lip and the look of a man disappointed by customer service.

Shawna sat in the Lamborghini wearing sunglasses and Aiden's captain's jacket over her dress. She'd claimed it because the gold braid felt good against her wrists. He had moved the badge to his shirt before handing it over. The department hadn't had time to miss the jacket yet.

Aiden looked at the coffee, the van, and Shawna.

“Hold these, baby,” he said, passing her both cups.

“Getting involved?” she asked.

“Involved me when they parked,” Aiden told her.

He approached the van. Its driver stood by the open front door, waiting while two men threw bags through the rear. The fourth, carrying the gun, backed toward them from the casino.

“Keep walking, pretty boy,” the driver told Aiden.

Aiden opened his shirt enough to show the badge pinned inside.

The driver's laugh stopped halfway out. “Police?”

“Captain,” Aiden said.

The man looked him over. “Don't look like police.”

“Police improving,” Aiden told him. “Move the van.”

The armed man came alongside his driver and raised the pistol. Aiden caught the open door and drove it into the gunman's forearm. The shot hit the street. Aiden slammed him against the side panel, and the pistol fell beneath the van.

The driver scrambled behind the wheel. The injured gunman dropped to one knee, then dragged himself through the passenger door while the two bag carriers clambered into the rear. Aiden saw all four inside as the van lurched forward.

The rear doors remained open. Bags slid toward the threshold. One man grabbed for them, and Aiden caught a door handle as the van gathered speed.

“Aiden!” Shawna shouted from the Lamborghini.

“Coffee!” he shouted back.

His shoes struck the curb, found purchase, lost it. He pulled himself toward the rear step while a man inside tried to kick his hand loose.

“Let go!” the robber yelled.

“You let go,” Aiden told him.

The man kicked again. “My van!”

“Should've moved it,” Aiden said.

He got a foot onto the step and drove upward. His shoulder caught the man's stomach. Both fell among the money bags. Aiden's side flared, bright enough to blur the open doors behind him.

The driver looked back. The van climbed halfway onto the sidewalk and struck a newspaper stand. Papers burst across the windshield. The driver fought the wheel while yesterday's headlines tried to cover his view.

The second man in the back reached for a pistol. Aiden planted a foot on his wrist, took the gun, and turned it toward both rear passengers.

“Don't,” Aiden said.

The man beneath his shoe looked up.

“You're Aiden,” he said.

“Easier morning remembering that at the coffee shop,” Aiden told him.

The van turned into a parking structure. Concrete closed over them. Tires screamed on the first ramp. Aiden braced against the front seat, keeping the captured pistol on the two men behind him. The injured gunman in front cradled his arm and looked back without attempting to use it.

A siren followed them inside.

Aiden glanced through the open rear doors. Dark blue paint flashed beneath the strip lights. Shawna had the Lamborghini behind them, gaining on the wide van at every turn.

“She's still there!” the driver shouted as they reached the second floor.

“Thorough,” Aiden told him.

The driver glared in the mirror. “Who asked you?”

“You brought me,” Aiden said.

On the third floor, the van took the outer bend around a row of pillars. Aiden saw the Lamborghini cut through the empty inner parking bays. It disappeared beyond a column, then reappeared through the windshield, turning across the next upward ramp.

The blue car stopped sideways in their path.

The driver braked. Aiden caught the front seat with his free hand before he hit it. The two men behind him slid into the bags with a heavy, financially painful sound.

Shawna got out holding her silver pistol.

“Everybody out,” she ordered.

The driver stared through the glass. “She's police too?”

“Worse,” Aiden told him. “Disappointed.”

The man's hands left the wheel.

Ten minutes later, Knox's officers found four robbers lying on the concrete, the stolen money inside the van, and the Lamborghini blocking the ramp. Aiden handed over the captured gun and watched a gray-mustached sergeant inspect the men.

“Been after this crew for months, Captain,” the sergeant told him.

“Parked wrong,” Aiden explained.

The sergeant looked at the van. “Should've tried that.”

Shawna retrieved the coffee from the Lamborghini's cup holders and brought Aiden his. The captain's jacket lay safely over the seat. Not a drop had touched it.

He took a drink. Still warm.

The sergeant saluted Shawna too.

“Ma'am,” he said.

“You can call me Shawna,” she told him.

“Yes, ma'am,” the sergeant answered.

Aiden watched her give up on improving that instruction.

***

The casino owner came to the station before lunch carrying two gold watches and an envelope. Aiden gave the watches to the two employees the robbers had knocked down. He opened the envelope, thanked the owner, and used the money to buy lunch for the shift.

Officers took pictures beside the Lamborghini while food arrived in bags large enough to satisfy Tank. Knox watched from her office door.

“First morning going well?” she asked Aiden.

“Need a bigger cup holder,” he told her.

She laughed. “Anything else?”

“Raise,” Aiden said.

“Been here four hours,” Knox reminded him.

“Look what the four hours did for you,” he answered.

By the next afternoon, officers were offering to wait outside the Saint in case he needed company at the gala. He told them to stay close to Knox. The badge was inside his jacket again. He wanted Red to see it there.

***

The tailor kept offering black.

Aiden stood on the fitting platform in front of three mirrors while the man held another dark jacket against his shoulders. When black failed, he tried blue.

“White,” Aiden told him. “Want the whole room seeing who came.”

“Your injury,” the tailor began.

“Suit going to heal me?” Aiden asked.

The tailor stopped discussing the injury.

Shawna sat behind them in a small velvet chair with champagne in one hand and her phone in the other. She had told him he looked handsome in everything without looking up once.

Near the door, Tank reached for a peppermint. A seam in the suit they'd bought forty minutes earlier gave way with a soft tearing sound. The tailor pretended not to hear.

Aiden pointed. “Another one for him too.”

“Larger?” the tailor asked.

“Whatever size a cathedral is,” Aiden said.

Tank put the peppermint down.

“Eat,” Aiden told him. “Can afford the candy and whatever seam goes with it.”

Shawna laughed and finally looked up. For a second, Aiden had her attention without a diamond, a dead husband, or Red's name between them. Just the pins in his trousers and the biggest man in the room being defeated by a sweet.

He would have bought the actual cathedral to keep that laugh going.

Her phone lit again. The sound brought back the calls she'd made to Red over the past days. Aiden knew the plan. He'd approved the plan. Then he'd sat beside her while she told Red she missed him and discovered how little approval helped.

After one call, Aiden had complained about the way she spoke to him.

“Like what?” Shawna had asked.

“With your mouth,” Aiden told her.

She'd stared until he suggested Red had a name and she could use that instead of baby. Then she kissed him, and he stopped arguing. The next time he began, she kissed him again. By the latest call, she had simply told him to come over without starting the fight first.

Now she was typing beneath the tailor's light.

“Him again?” Aiden asked her.

“Rosa,” Shawna said.

He held out a hand. “Let me see.”

She turned the screen. Six gowns lay across a bed beneath Rosa's question: Your nigga paying for all this?

“Tell her yes,” Aiden said.

“Don't know the price,” Shawna reminded him.

“Capital fucking letters,” he told her.

She put the phone away, stood, and stepped around the tailor to straighten his collar herself.

“Allowed to be scared tonight,” Shawna said quietly.

“I'm not,” Aiden told her.

“Okay,” she said.

He looked at her hands against his shirt. “You are?”

“Yes,” Shawna answered.

He caught her hand before she could step away. The watch slid against his fingers. He let it.

“Then I am too,” Aiden said.

She kissed the uncut corner of his mouth.

“Good,” she told him. “Maybe you'll listen.”

He looked at her a moment longer before letting the tailor turn him toward the mirrors again.

***

At home, Rosa had turned the bedroom into a room Aiden apparently needed permission to enter. Shoes occupied the chairs. Perfume clouded the air. Champagne stood in the bathroom sink beside his toothbrush. His pistol had been moved from the dresser to make room for a heated hairbrush.

“Why are you in here?” Rosa asked when he reached the door.

“Sleep here,” Aiden told her.

“Not while we're busy,” Rosa said.

She wore red with diamonds at her throat. Aiden looked past her for Shawna.

“Need my woman,” he said.

Rosa turned toward the dressing screen. “Your man needs patience.”

“Bought some,” Aiden told her. “Delivery tomorrow.”

Rosa picked up a shoe. He retrieved his pistol, tucked it into the holster beneath his jacket, and left before finding out whether the shoe was meant for her foot.

Downstairs, Marcellus sat by the windows in a gray suit, shoes polished until they looked wet. The new clothes changed the outline of him. They did less for the tightness around his mouth.

Celeste's letter lay on the table beneath his hand.

“Ready?” Aiden asked him.

“For years,” Marcellus said.

Aiden stopped beside the chair. “Not what I asked.”

The old man looked up and almost smiled.

“Yes, Aiden,” he answered.

Aiden sat opposite him, lowering himself carefully. The bullet made its presence known despite the care.

“She knows how much that hurts?” Marcellus asked.

“Knows everything,” Aiden said.

The jeweler waited.

Aiden looked toward the stairs. “Enough.”

Marcellus folded the letter into its sleeve and returned it to his inside pocket.

“The stone can't love you back,” he said.

“Got somebody for that,” Aiden told him.

“Remember which one you came for,” Marcellus said.

Tank entered with two phones. He turned one facedown when he saw them.

“Cars ready,” Tank said.

“Who's calling?” Aiden asked.

“Nobody,” Tank told him.

Aiden looked at both devices. “Need two phones for nobody?”

“In case somebody does,” Tank said.

Aiden watched him pocket them. On another day he might have laughed. This time he was preparing another question when he heard Shawna on the stairs.

He turned.

Dark blue cloth followed her body and trailed behind her. Diamonds touched her ears. Her hair was up, leaving the small scar in her eyebrow plainly visible. Aiden knew exactly where that scar lay even when he couldn't see it. He watched her fingers travel down the rail, the watch bright at her wrist.

“Rosa's dress,” Shawna said when she saw him looking. “Never wore it.”

“Said I'd buy you anything,” Aiden reminded her.

“Wanted this,” she said.

Rosa lifted the train clear of the last stair.

“Want it back,” Rosa told them. “Both hear me?”

“Yes,” Shawna said.

Rosa came forward holding a small envelope. She showed them the original murder order from Dante's watch, then slid it inside and put the envelope in her purse.

“Nobody gets this off me,” Rosa said.

Shawna showed Aiden the copy she was taking. She folded it into her own purse beside the small silver pistol. Celeste's original letter remained with Marcellus. Red's original order would stay with Rosa. Aiden watched both women close their bags, wanting to remember where every piece of the truth was going.

“Turn around,” he told Shawna.

She did, carefully gathering the train. He watched the blue fabric move around her legs.

“Again,” Aiden said.

Shawna gave him a look. “Aiden.”

“Trying to remember who I need to thank,” he explained.

She came close and put both hands on his chest.

“Tear this dress and I'll kill you before Red gets the chance,” she told him.

“Buy the factory,” Aiden promised.

“Kill you in the factory,” Shawna said.

He kissed her. Tank cleared his throat. Aiden kissed her again. Tank didn't determine the length of this part of the evening.
