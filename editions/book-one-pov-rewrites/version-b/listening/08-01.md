## 8. Let Him Think You Love Him

The seam moved beneath Shawna's thumbnail.

Aiden leaned forward and caught the watch before she closed the clasp again. He'd spent days studying it across tables and beds, beneath her hand on his cheek. He knew the way it sat on her wrist better than he wanted to admit.

“Wait,” he said. “That piece moved.”

Shawna held it beneath the lamp. “Where?”

He pointed to the inner clasp. “There. Not the hinge. Beside it.”

“You know how it opens?” she asked him.

“No,” Aiden admitted.

She took it from his fingers. “Then give it back before you're the next thing I hit with it.”

Aiden spread his hands. “Man makes a discovery and the museum throws him out.”

Shawna called Rosa for a jeweler she trusted. Aiden could hear Rosa through the phone.

“Trusted to do what?” Rosa asked. “Different men.”

“Open a watch without breaking it,” Shawna said.

“Oh. I have him,” Rosa told her. “Owes me money.”

Aiden leaned nearer the phone. “Makes him trustworthy?”

“Makes him findable,” Rosa answered. “Do you want reliable or found?”

“Send the findable man,” Aiden said.

An hour later, Rosa's jeweler sat at their table turning the clasp under a loupe. Aiden reached once. Shawna moved the watch out of reach without looking at him.

The jeweler lowered the loupe. “Who made this?”

“Marcellus Vale,” Shawna said.

“Know where he is?” the man asked.

“Prison,” she answered.

The jeweler shook his head. “Got out nine days ago. Came by my shop asking about his old bench.”

Shawna gripped Aiden's arm. He covered her hand with his.

“Call him,” Aiden told the jeweler.

“I'm not—” the man began.

“Call him whatever you are,” Aiden said. “Just call the fucking man.”

The jeweler held up his phone. “I was saying I'm not certain he answers this number.”

Shawna looked at Aiden.

“I'm excited,” he explained.

“You're letting him dial,” she told him.

Aiden sat back. The jeweler made the call, explained about the watch, and passed the phone to Shawna when she gave her name.

“Dante's wife?” Marcellus's voice came sharply from the speaker.

She covered her mouth and nodded. Aiden touched her wrist. She looked at him, then remembered the man on the phone couldn't see.

“Yes,” Shawna said.

Aiden held her other hand while she talked. For once, that seemed a large enough thing to be doing.

Marcellus reached the gate before sunset in a brown coat too heavy for the weather. Aiden watched from the front steps. Tall and narrow, close gray hair, a paper bag held carefully in one hand. Then a car stopped behind him and three men got out.

The tallest caught Marcellus by the coat. The paper bag fell. A clean shirt slid into the dust.

Aiden called Tank's name.

Tank came through the side door with bread in his mouth. He saw the gate and removed it.

“Jeweler's friends?” Tank asked.

“Holding him like enemies,” Aiden said.

Shawna came out behind them. Aiden heard her say Marcellus's name and looked back long enough to see her hand tighten on the doorframe. Mercedes appeared in the doorway behind her. Then Aiden crossed the drive.

One iron gate leaf stood partly open, swung outward over the entrance. The tall man held Marcellus in the gap between its edge and the car. His purple suit shone in the low sun. Rings covered all but one finger, the only part of him apparently attempting humility.

“Go inside,” the man told Aiden.

“Came outside for you,” Aiden said. “Don't make it disappointing.”

The man looked at Tank and shifted his grip on Marcellus.

“Private business,” he said.

“Private gate,” Aiden answered. “See how I keep turning up in your problem?”

One of the other men opened his jacket, displaying a pistol at his belt. Aiden looked at it, then at his face.

“Buy another for when that one leaves you?” Aiden asked.

The man drew.

Tank struck before the gun cleared the jacket. The man's back hit the car hard enough to break a window. The third attacker swung at Aiden. Aiden caught his shoulder, turned with the force of it, and drove him face-first into the stone gatepost.

The tall man pulled Marcellus in front of him. A pistol appeared against the old man's coat.

Aiden stopped. Behind him, Shawna said don't. He couldn't tell whom she meant.

Marcellus looked down at the shirt in the dust.

“Pick it up,” he told the man holding him.

The man blinked. “What?”

“My shirt,” Marcellus said.

“You out your fucking mind?” the man asked.

“I've been out nine days,” Marcellus told him. “That's the only shirt I've bought.”

The gunman looked at Aiden as though expecting him to restore some respect for the pistol.

Aiden held out an empty hand.

“Let him get the shirt,” he said.

“Think I'm stupid?” the man demanded.

“Giving you a chance to surprise me,” Aiden told him.

The man dragged Marcellus another step across the gate's swing. Marcellus's shoe caught the fallen bag. He stumbled. The muzzle shifted away from his chest.

Aiden put both palms against the gate and drove it outward.

Iron struck the gunman's forearm. The pistol fired into the driveway, chipping stone. Marcellus dropped clear as Aiden caught the man's wrist against the bars. The man tried to twist. His rings scraped the metal. Aiden held until the gun fell, then drove him against the gate again and let him slide down.

Pain pulled beneath Aiden's bandage. He stood very still for a breath, keeping the dropped pistol out of the man's reach with his shoe.

Tank had the other two on the ground. One was swearing; the other made small, cautious attempts at breathing. Shawna ran to Marcellus and got her hands beneath his arms.

“Are you hurt?” she asked.

Marcellus looked at a tear in his sleeve. “My coat.”

“Fuck the coat,” Shawna said. “Are you hurt?”

He shook his head. Aiden looked past them and saw Mercedes bend to collect the clean shirt. She shook dust from its sleeves and held it toward the light.

“Little wash,” Mercedes told Marcellus. “Handsome again.”

The old man watched her brush it with her palm. His mouth moved as though he meant to thank her, but no words came at first.

Aiden turned to the man sitting against the bars.

“Name,” he said.

“Felix Barrow,” the man answered, staring at his empty hand.

“What do you want with him?” Aiden asked.

Felix swallowed. “Owes people.”

“Which people?” Aiden pressed.

“Inside,” Felix said. “He made things. Things got promised.”

Aiden looked toward Marcellus.

“I promised him nothing,” Marcellus said.

Aiden nudged the fallen pistol farther away. “He hasn't got your promise. You haven't got your gun. Everybody learned something.”

Felix looked past him toward the cars and the house, then at Tank standing over the other men.

“You're that Aiden,” Felix said.

“Which one were you hoping for?” Aiden asked.

“The boat. The hearse,” Felix said.

“You're behind,” Aiden told him. “Two boats.”

Mercedes laughed. Tank joined her. Even Marcellus turned to look at him.

Felix rested his head against the bars. “Should've asked where he was going.”

“Should've stayed home,” Aiden said.

Tank called Knox's people. Aiden waited until the three men were securely under Tank's gun, then helped Marcellus toward the house. Shawna held one of the old man's hands. Mercedes carried the shirt beside him.

At the door, Aiden offered his name properly.

“I know,” Marcellus said.

“People talking?” Aiden asked.

“Dante's widow said your name six times on the phone,” Marcellus told him.

Aiden glanced at Shawna. He liked the old man considerably more already.

Marcellus touched the cross at Aiden's chest with one fingertip.

“I engraved those letters,” he said. “Dante sent it with the watch. Said his wife thought he was magnificent and he wasn't arguing.”

Aiden looked down at the gold. Dante had evidently improved his own side of that story too.

“Come inside,” Aiden told him.

Shawna stopped just short of Marcellus in the living room. For a moment they stood looking at one another. Then he opened his arms. She stepped into them and cried against his coat.

Aiden watched the old man's hand settle between her shoulders. His eyes closed. Neither of them had been beside Dante when he died; Aiden knew that from what they'd told him. Each had brought part of the man into this room. He stood a little aside while they held on.

Tank returned after the police arrived, carrying the bag of food he'd left by the side door. He saw Shawna and Marcellus and stopped.

“Marcellus,” Aiden told him quietly.

Tank glanced toward the entrance. The old man looked at Tank for a long moment over Shawna's shoulder.

“Your friend?” Marcellus asked Aiden.

“Yeah,” Aiden said.

“I see,” Marcellus replied.

Tank put the food down and went outside again. Aiden watched him leave, then turned back to the watch waiting on the table.
