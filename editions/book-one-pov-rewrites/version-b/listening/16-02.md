The corridor divided around an unfinished fountain. A shallow layer of dirty water sat at the bottom among scraps of tile. Red's white jacket disappeared through the far doors with Shawna pulled against him.

Aiden followed with his left hand against the wall. Blood warmed the bandages under his shirt.

A man stepped beside the fountain holding a shotgun.

Aiden dropped as it fired. The fountain's edge burst. Water and stone fragments struck his face. He shoved his pistol into its holster as he lunged into the man's legs before the barrel could come down toward him.

They hit the floor together. The shotgun slid across the concrete.

The man drove an elbow into Aiden's wounded side. Pain spread so wide that the corridor vanished. Aiden couldn't see the doors. Couldn't find the next breath.

Shawna shouted his name from beyond the wall.

He heard it and struck the man beneath him. Again. Again, until the grip at his ribs opened. His knuckles hurt through the bandage. He hit with the other hand. The man's head struck concrete, and both arms fell away.

Aiden got one foot beneath himself and rose.

The man caught his ankle.

Aiden looked down.

“Don't make this your whole life,” Aiden told him.

The fingers opened.

Another man appeared at the far doorway, firing. Aiden crouched, took the shotgun from the floor, and fired toward the marble stacked beside him. The shooter dropped behind the slabs. Aiden left the shotgun and drew his own pistol again as he passed the fountain.

Ahead, desert light showed through a room without windows. A thin railing divided the floor from the darkness beyond. Aiden could hear Red breathing hard.

“Don't get tired?” Red called from inside.

Aiden reached the doorway.

“Keep giving me somewhere to be,” Aiden answered.

***

The upper room opened straight onto the unfinished balcony. No glass. No doors along that edge. Below it lay concrete, steel, and the lights of their cars.

Red held Shawna against the railing. As he hauled her backward, a small silver pistol fell from inside his jacket and struck the floor beside his shoe.

Hers.

Aiden stopped in the doorway with his own gun raised.

“Down,” Red ordered him.

“Let her go,” Aiden said.

Red pulled her higher against the rail. She gripped his arm, and one heel scraped for purchase on the floor.

“Gun down or she goes over,” Red said.

Shawna looked at Aiden. Her eyes were wide. Her mouth worked around a breath she couldn't finish. He could see the fear without needing her to say it.

“Put her feet down,” Aiden told Red.

“Gun first.”

Aiden crouched slowly and laid his pistol on the concrete beside the doorway. He opened his hand and stood.

Red watched the weapon settle. Then he smiled.

“How your husband died,” Red told Shawna. “Hands open. Begging. T couldn't even look at him.”

Shawna turned her head enough to speak.

“Not for himself,” she said.

Red tightened his arm. “What?”

“Said he wanted somebody to look after me,” Shawna told him. “Wasn't begging for himself.”

“Shut the fuck up,” Red said.

She drove her head backward into his face.

Red staggered. His gun fired into the open sky. Shawna dropped from his arm onto the floor, reached for her silver pistol, and came up with it in both hands.

She shot his leg.

Red buckled against the railing. She fired again, into his chest. His free hand reached toward her, fingers closing on nothing.

“Dante,” Shawna said.

She tried to lift the pistol again. Both hands were shaking so badly that the barrel fell beneath his chest.

Red remained against the rail. Blood spread through the white shirt. His gun hand began rising.

Aiden had dropped back to his own pistol the moment Shawna broke free. He took it from the concrete and fired before Red's muzzle came level with her.

Red's gun fell. Aiden fired again. The man's knees struck the balcony floor. He toppled sideways along the base of the railing and lay still, looking into a sky that hadn't agreed to belong to him.

Aiden went to Shawna.

She knelt with the little gun in front of her. Her mouth moved around Dante's name.

“Baby,” Aiden said.

She didn't look up.

“Shawna,” he tried again.

Her fingers wouldn't open. He knelt beside her and closed his free hand around them.

“Done,” Aiden told her. “It's done.”

She shook her head.

He wanted his name. Wanted it from her mouth so badly he could hardly breathe. She kept saying Dante's. Aiden eased the silver pistol from her hands, laid it beside his own, and put both arms around her.

She folded against him and cried.

He held her on the dusty floor while she grieved for her husband. There was room for his jealousy; he could feel it hurting. There was more room for the woman shaking in his arms.

He held her tighter because he was here.

Voices sounded in the corridor. Someone reached the doorway, saw Red, and shouted that he was dead. The words went down the stairs. Aiden heard his own name follow them.

Through the hall. Across the gaming floor. Out toward the headlights.

His shoes were wet with blood. Shawna's face pressed against his neck. Below, men were calling him king, and he found that he could believe them without letting her go.

***

When Shawna could stand, Aiden retrieved both pistols. He put his own in its holster and kept hers tucked securely in his jacket. Then he went to Red's body.

The right hand lay open on the floor. Aiden pulled the blue ring from it.

He didn't know its worth yet. He knew what Marcellus needed to see. He closed his hand around it and felt the setting press into his palm.

Shawna waited beside the doorway. He went back to her, put his free arm around her, and helped her down the stairs.

The shooting had stopped. Men stood with hands raised among the tables. Weapons lay on the floor. Aiden saw Malik near the landing, speaking to an officer with his own gun held by the barrel.

Tank was alive beside the roulette table.

Rosa knelt over him with a folded tablecloth pressed below his shoulder. Dr. Vega was tending another wounded man near the entrance. Aiden saw them and almost stopped walking.

Rosa looked up.

“Where the fuck you been?” she demanded.

“With her,” Aiden said.

Rosa looked past him and saw Shawna. Her face changed so suddenly that Aiden felt his own mouth begin to shake.

“Come here, baby,” Rosa told her.

Shawna knelt beside her. Rosa kept one hand on the tablecloth and reached the other around Shawna's shoulders. They pressed their foreheads together.

“Couldn't sit in that house,” Rosa said, looking up at Aiden. “Got Vega. Followed after you. Came in when it stopped.”

He nodded. He couldn't imagine the room without her now.

Tank opened his eyes when Aiden knelt. Aiden put the ring into his trouser pocket so he could take Tank's hand.

“Got her?” Tank asked.

“Yeah,” Aiden said.

“Red?”

“Dead,” Aiden told him.

Tank tried to smile. Pain caught the expression before it finished.

“Pay my hospital bill,” Tank said.

Aiden gripped his hand. “Every penny.”

“Car too,” Tank added.

“What car?” Aiden asked.

“Want one.”

“Hundred,” Aiden said. “Buy you a hundred fucking cars.”

Tank closed his eyes.

“Black ones,” he said.

Aiden held on until Vega reached them. The last man who'd called him family had put a bullet in him. Tank had taken one instead. Aiden watched the doctor's hands and listened for every breath beneath them.

***

Marcellus came through the entrance after the weapons were down.

He stepped carefully over the glass, the blue box in one hand and his coat closed over the papers. He looked at the unfinished marble, the bare walls, the great staircase. Then his eyes found Aiden beside Tank.

Vega had attendants preparing Tank to move. Aiden rose slowly and took the ring from his pocket.

He put it in Marcellus's palm.

The old man didn't speak. He brought out his jeweler's lens, held the ring beneath a work light, and bent over it. Aiden waited while the old hands turned the stone a fraction at a time.

Marcellus drew a long breath.

“There,” he said. “Inside. The flaw. This is her.”

He lowered the lens. His eyes had filled. He took Celeste's letter from his coat and laid the ring against the paper, bending his head over both.

Now Aiden knew. Forty million dollars stood in an old man's hand, and for once the number didn't tell him what he was looking at.

“What will you do?” Aiden asked.

Marcellus closed his fingers around the ring and letter.

“Take my wife home,” he said.

Aiden nodded and let him have the silence that followed.

Shawna came to stand beside Aiden. She put her arm around his waist, carefully avoiding the wound, and leaned into him. He kissed her hair.

Knox entered with Bell after the last men had lowered their guns. The chief stopped in front of them. Her eyes moved from Shawna to the badge beneath the blood on Aiden's jacket.

“Brought her home,” Knox said.

“Don't forget a promise,” Aiden told her.

Knox saluted. Bell followed. Other officers straightened in the doorway, and from the tables the men began calling Aiden's name again.

Captain. King. Aiden.

Shawna tightened her arm around him at the sound. He drew her closer and stood where every one of them could see.
