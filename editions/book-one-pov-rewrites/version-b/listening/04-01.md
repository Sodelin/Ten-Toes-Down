## 4. The Palace of the Woman I Just Met

The agent lost a shoe before she got them inside.

Her heel sank into the lawn, her foot came free, and for a moment she stood on one leg with the brochure pressed to her chest. Tank bent, pulled the shoe out of the grass, and handed it back. Mud clung to the heel. She looked at his hand, the line of cars, and Aiden holding himself carefully upright beside Shawna.

“Will anyone else be joining the viewing?” the agent asked.

“You want more of us?” Tank said.

Aiden put his hand over Shawna's. “Show her the water.”

The agent found a patch of stone for both feet and began again. Five bedrooms. A private dock. A heated pool. The piano could be negotiated separately.

“Why?” Aiden asked. “Owner in a band?”

“He may wish to retain it,” the agent explained.

“Tell him to wish for a smaller instrument,” Aiden said.

Shawna squeezed his fingers. “Let her get the door open first, baby.”

Beyond the door stood a curved staircase and a chandelier with enough glass in it to make Aiden check the ceiling. White curtains moved above the lake-facing doors. The water lay blue beyond the lawn, carrying sunlight in little broken pieces. A black piano stood close to the glass. Someone sitting there could look up from the keys and see the far shore.

Shawna let go of his hand.

Aiden watched her cross the room. Her pace slowed at the piano. She put two fingers on the keys without pressing them. The agent was still explaining something about furnishings, but he couldn't keep the words in order while looking at Shawna's face.

“You like it?” he asked.

She turned toward him. “Aiden, this is a whole house.”

He came closer, one careful step at a time. “I noticed. You like it?”

“Yeah,” Shawna said quietly.

Aiden looked at the agent. “We have a yes. Do whatever paper follows yes.”

“We haven't discussed the price,” she reminded him.

“Don't say it louder than she said yes,” Aiden told her.

Tank went outside to inspect the dock. Shawna sat on the piano bench. Aiden lowered himself beside her, got halfway through pretending it didn't hurt, and felt her catch his arm.

“Slowly, you ridiculous nigga,” she said.

“This is slowly,” he told her. “Normal speed, we'd own next door already.”

She laughed and looked toward the lake. Her hand remained around his forearm.

“We're doing this backward,” Shawna said.

“Turn around, then,” Aiden suggested.

She swung a leg over the bench and faced him properly. “We barely know each other.”

“I know you want water,” he said.

Shawna looked through the doors again. “Dante wanted water.”

Aiden's smile stayed in place after he stopped feeling it. Outside, Tank was testing the first dock plank with one shoe. A duck watched him from the water. Tank tested again. The duck moved six inches away without giving up its position.

“You said it to me,” Aiden told Shawna.

She put her palm on his knee. “I know.”

He looked down at that hand. He could sit through the truth if she kept touching him while she said it.

The agent returned with a number and a folder. Aiden called the men handling T's accounts. The accountant confirmed that the club still had money coming in, but T's house had been promised elsewhere. Aiden told him this purchase would be different. He wanted Shawna's name on it.

“And yours?” the agent asked while he ended the call.

“My nigga lives where I live,” Shawna said before he could answer.

Aiden turned toward her.

“That the arrangement?” she asked him.

“That's the whole constitution,” he said.

She patted his knee. “Then stop arguing over my piano.”

Aiden looked back at the folder. “Put the keys in her hand. Piano stays. My wife said—”

Shawna raised an eyebrow.

“My future,” he corrected himself. “My future said.”

She kissed him hard enough that he had to grip the bench. Through the glass, Tank finally put both feet on the dock. The duck left.

Aiden watched it go over Shawna's shoulder. Two men had secured waterfront property. He liked his method better.

***

By evening, Aiden had bought a house without buying himself a replacement shirt. The shirt on his back came from Rosa's hospital bags. The shirt he had arrived at the hospital wearing had dried into something no living person ought to put on again.

Rosa stood in the new foyer with her sunglasses still on. She tilted her head back to inspect the chandelier.

“This bitch gets shot at one time and comes home with a staircase,” Rosa said.

Shawna screamed laughing. Aiden turned toward the sound. He'd heard her laugh in the car and at the hospital, but this came from deeper, loose enough to make her bend at the waist. He hadn't known she sounded like that.

“Come see upstairs,” Shawna said, catching Rosa's hand.

“You got upstairs too?” Rosa asked.

Tank pointed at the obvious evidence. “You're looking at the stairs.”

Rosa pointed back at him. “And furniture that talks.”

Tank looked toward Aiden. Aiden couldn't give him anything useful through the grin on his face.

The women went up. Soon their voices were moving from room to room. Rosa called down to announce which bedroom she had chosen. Then she appeared on the landing carrying a painting of a gray hillside and a solitary gray man.

“The dead owner had problems,” Rosa said, resting it against the rail.

“He moved to Arizona,” Aiden told her.

Rosa studied the painting. “Then he can take his weather with him.”

She took it out of sight. Aiden sat in the living room with a towel-wrapped ice pack against his side, listening to women reorganize the palace.

Tank sat opposite him, watching the stairs.

“You sure about all this?” Tank asked.

“House?” Aiden said.

“Woman,” Tank clarified.

Aiden's fingers tightened around the towel. “Watch it.”

“I been watching,” Tank said.

Aiden waited. Tank kept his eyes on the empty staircase until Aiden told him to say the rest.

“She loved Dante,” Tank said.

“He's dead,” Aiden answered.

Tank looked directly at him. “That's what I'm telling you.”

Aiden threw the ice pack. Tank caught it without leaving the chair.

“I got a bullet in me,” Aiden reminded him. “You want me getting up to fight your big ass?”

“No,” Tank said.

“Then stop saying shit I can't sit down for,” Aiden told him.

Tank leaned over and put the ice pack back against his side. Gently. That made Aiden angrier.

“Somebody's got to tell you something,” Tank said.

Shawna laughed upstairs again. Both men looked toward it.

Aiden adjusted the towel and stared at the water beyond the doors.

“Tell me where T's funeral is,” he said.

***

The party began before anyone found the towels.

Mercedes arrived with twelve women, two men, and a case of champagne she described as a gift with enough force to discourage further questions. Porsche followed in a white Bentley with the roof down. A man in the back seat was crying into one hand.

Tank met them beside the drive. Aiden could hear him from the patio.

“Who that?” Tank asked Porsche.

“His car,” she said. “My evening.”

The man lifted his wet face. “We're friends.”

“Blink if she bought you too, nigga,” Aiden called.

The man laughed so suddenly his crying stopped. He came over and introduced himself as Leon, thirty-seven, owner of a steakhouse. Porsche supplied the rest while taking a bottle from the back seat: he'd proposed during appetizers. She'd declined the ring and accepted permission to drive.

“That's growth,” Mercedes told Leon, taking the bottle from Porsche.

People spread over the patio. Aiden watched women enter the pool wearing diamonds, their hands glittering above the water while they held their drinks dry. A dealer he knew caught Rosa near the kitchen door and made her inspect his enormous watch. She lifted it to her ear.

“Can't hear the personality in it,” Rosa told him.

Tank laughed from the doorway.

The dealer turned toward him. “You got something to say?”

“She said it,” Tank replied. “I'm enjoying it.”

The dealer looked from Tank's face to his shoulders. His own smile arrived a moment later.

Aiden rested in a patio chair with his shirt open over the dressings. Shawna sat beside him. People brought bottles, flowers, a horse statue nobody could explain. A refrigerated truck arrived with steaks because, the driver told him, somebody heard Aiden had a yard now and wanted that properly recognized.

He looked over the bags of ice and the plates and the people making themselves comfortable. The private drive came down the side of the house and ended beside the patio; Porsche had left the Bentley there with its top open. Beyond it lay the lawn, a stone garden border, and the dock. The lake carried the lights from the far shore in long broken lines.

“Baby, is all this yours?” Shawna asked him.

He turned toward her. “Whatever you can point at.”

She pointed at the horse statue. “Who the fuck brought that?”

“I'm finding out,” Aiden promised.

A red speedboat came around the point before he could question anyone. Its driver stood at the wheel with his white shirt open to his waist. A chain lay on his chest thick enough to lower over the side if the anchor failed. Two men sat behind him with rifles across their knees.

Tank set down his plate.

“Everybody inside,” he said.

The music kept playing. Some guests turned, others went on talking. The boat hit the dock hard enough to lift a run of planks. Now everybody turned.

“Where's the new owner?” the driver shouted.

Aiden raised his glass from the chair. “Home.”

“You bought from my cousin,” the man called back.

“He looked old enough to make that decision,” Aiden said.

The man pointed toward the house. “Brock had no right selling it.”

Shawna stood. “Your name on anything?”

“I'm talking to the man,” the driver told her.

Aiden leaned forward. His side pulled, but he kept his voice level.

“Then look at me while you regret that,” he said.

The driver's eyes found his. Behind the man, a rifle shifted. The music stopped between songs, leaving a brief, hard silence. Women in the pool held the edge with glittering hands. Someone set a glass down very carefully.

“Roman Pike,” the driver announced. “You can ask around.”

“You can go around,” Aiden told him. “My dock's occupied.”

Roman stepped out onto the damaged planks. “I got a claim.”

“So does my insurance company,” Aiden said. “You just hit the fucking dock.”

Roman spread both hands as though explaining something to a slow child.

“Twenty thousand a month,” he said. “My cousin paid. You pay. Nobody has trouble.”

Aiden looked at Shawna.

“Sold us a house and left a subscription,” he said.

She laughed into her hand. Roman's face darkened. His right hand went beneath his shirt.

Tank moved as the pistol came out. Rifle shots cracked from the dock. The patio doors exploded behind Aiden. He pulled Shawna off her feet and dropped from his chair with her against him.

They struck the stone hard. White pain filled his sight. He kept his body over hers until the next shots passed, then lifted his head enough to see.

Mercedes hauled two women out of the pool and shoved them toward the kitchen. Porsche caught Leon by the collar and dragged him behind the Bentley.

“My car!” Leon shouted.

“Your head first!” Porsche shouted back.

Rosa was crawling beside the low table where Aiden had left his pistol. He saw her reach up, take it, and slide it across the stone toward him.

“Housewarming gift,” Rosa said.

Aiden caught it. “You shouldn't have.”

“I didn't,” Rosa told him. “It's yours.”

Roman ran back to the speedboat. His two men fired from the dock, covering him. Tank answered from behind the stone barbecue. Splinters jumped around the gunmen's feet. A bottle on the shelf above Tank burst, pouring champagne over his shoulders.

“Now I'm fucking mad!” Tank shouted.

Aiden looked toward the Bentley. Fifty feet of grass lay between its front wheels and the dock, with two chairs and the raised stone border in the way. The boat's engine roared as Roman prepared to pull off. Aiden measured its angle with his eyes.

“Again?” Shawna asked beside him.

“Different boat,” he told her.

She gripped his shirt. “Aiden.”

“Get inside, baby,” he said, putting a hand over hers. “Go with Rosa.”

Shawna kissed him, hard and quick. For exactly that long, he forgot his side.

“Then come back in my house,” she told him.

He watched Rosa take her through the kitchen door before he moved. Then he ran bent at the waist toward the Bentley, every step pulling beneath the dressing. Porsche opened the driver's door as he arrived. Leon held out both hands toward the car as though he could catch its future personally.

“It's new!” Leon cried.

“So's my house!” Aiden told him.

He tucked the pistol into his waistband and got behind the wheel. Porsche ran around the hood and dropped into the passenger seat.

“I know this car,” she said.

“Introduce us fast,” Aiden told her.

Roman's boat backed off the dock. The red hull swung broadside a few feet beyond its end. Aiden pressed the accelerator.

The Bentley crossed the lawn. Grass tore beneath its tires. Both chairs disappeared under the front bumper. Porsche braced one hand against the dashboard and the other on the door.

“Beautiful, baby, beautiful!” she shouted as Aiden drove through the flowerbed.

The raised border hit beneath the wheels. The nose lifted. Planks flashed below the hood, then red fiberglass.

The Bentley landed across the speedboat's rear deck.

The noise filled Aiden's teeth. Fiberglass shattered. The hood folded. The stern plunged beneath the Bentley's weight, and the bow kicked upward. Roman struck the steering wheel and went over the side. Water struck the open car in a white sheet.

Aiden gripped the wheel until the spray fell away. He was sitting in a car on a boat beside a house he had bought that day. Behind him, people were shouting his name. His side hurt enough to make the whole scene pulse at its edges.

Porsche lifted her wet head.

“You owe Leon a car,” she said.

“Roman owes me a dock,” Aiden answered.

The boat settled until its damaged stern touched the shallows. Roman came up beside it, spitting water. One rifleman had jumped into the shallows too. The other crouched on the bow with empty hands raised; Tank stood at the broken end of the dock holding his gun on both men.

Aiden leaned over the Bentley's door.

“Roman,” he called. “House still yours?”

Roman shook his head, wet hair across his eyes.

“Speak,” Aiden told him. “I bought acoustics.”

“Your house!” Roman shouted.

“And whose car?” Aiden asked.

Roman stared at him.

Aiden looked toward the patio. “Wrong answer. Leon! Come get your speedboat!”

Leon stood with both hands on his head. Mercedes climbed onto a chair and raised two bottles. Rosa had brought Shawna back outside and held an arm around her. Tank began laughing, then pointed harder at the man on the bow when he moved.

“Stop fucking looking at me,” Tank told his prisoner.

Shawna came onto the sound section of the dock. Aiden got out of the Bentley, planted a foot on the boat's buckled rear seat, and climbed toward her. The boat shifted under his weight. She held out both hands across the short gap.

“Most ridiculous man I've ever met,” Shawna said.

Aiden took her hands and stepped onto the dock. “Best-looking too.”

She pulled him close. “I married a dream tonight and I haven't even married you.”

His arms closed around her. Beyond her shoulder he could see Leon inspecting the wreck from shore, laughing with the same wet face he'd arrived with.

“Never loved a car more!” Leon announced. “Never want to see it again!”

Tank helped Porsche out of the Bentley and across the broken end of the dock. Then he kept Roman and both gunmen together beside the drive until Knox's officers came and took all three into custody. Aiden sat inside with Shawna while the officers took statements and their friends carried the party around the broken doors.

Knox called him that night.

“Two boats in five days?” she asked.

“First one I cleared,” Aiden told her.

“I'm beginning to understand the distinction,” Knox said.

Shawna curled against his good side on the sofa. Aiden watched her draw her feet beneath her.

“Anything else, Chief?” he asked.

“One question,” Knox said. “Can you do that in uniform?”

Aiden smiled toward the lake.

“Depends how nice you make the uniform,” he told her.
