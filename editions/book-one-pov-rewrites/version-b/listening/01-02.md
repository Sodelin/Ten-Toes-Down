Aiden carried a tray of shrimp out of the kitchen. Its underside was hot against his palm; butter had worked beneath the paper along one edge. He held it level. People would move for refreshments before they moved for trouble, and for the moment he could be refreshments.

Shawna saw his shirtsleeves. Her eyes flicked to Silas, then to the aisle. She drew her chair back enough to let him pass. Across the aisle, Tank put his plate down with both hands.

A woman at the first table looked from Aiden to the coffin.

“Lord,” she breathed.

The man beside her crossed himself.

“Don't do that,” the woman whispered to him. “He got shrimp.”

“Could still be spiritual,” her companion said.

She shoved his chair with her foot. “Move, nigga.”

Aiden passed without losing a shrimp. The drummer held one stick over his snare. The smallest guard stood at the foot of the steps, watching Mercedes instead of the aisle.

Aiden offered the tray. The man took a shrimp in his right hand, keeping the empty case in his left.

“Sauce?” Aiden asked.

The guard nodded. Aiden tilted the tray toward the little cup. The man dipped, stepped back, and began chewing.

Above them, Silas turned at the sound of Aiden's voice. His fur collar slapped his mouth. He stared down from beside the coffin with the drive still in his hand.

Forty dollars, Aiden thought. Worth it.

“Nigga, it's my motherfucking funeral,” he said.

Silas jammed the microphone into its stand and reached into his coat.

Aiden took the three shallow steps. The pistol cleared the fur just as he reached Silas beside the coffin. He drove the tray up beneath the man's wrist. The shot punched into the ceiling. The gun spun across the platform and skidded beneath the piano.

The room folded downward. Guests dragged one another under tables. Flowers shook above their heads. Plaster hit Aiden's neck, coarse and warm. Tank caught two people by the shoulders and got them behind a table while somehow keeping his drink above the whole arrangement.

Aiden grabbed a fistful of Silas's coat.

“Everybody else brought flowers!” he shouted.

Silas snatched the bottle from the coffin and swung it into his shoulder.

The blow went deep before the glass broke. Aiden lost his breath. Then heat ran down his upper arm, and liquor found the cut with a cold sting that turned vicious almost at once. His grip tightened in the fur. He hit Silas across the face with the tray.

Metal bent against bone. Sauce scattered over the white collar. The drive flew out of Silas's free hand, flashing under the chandeliers. Mercedes caught it against her chest with both arms and stumbled into the stage rail.

“Got it!” she shouted to Aiden.

The biggest guard came out of the backstage doorway and strode along the platform toward the steps. Porsche followed close behind him with her duplicate case. As he reached the top step, she swung it low across his advancing ankle. His shoe caught the handle. His upper body kept going, taking him down the steps toward Shawna.

She had lifted the chair she'd moved out of Aiden's way. Its legs met the guard's face and broke around it. A tooth clicked across the floor. He landed seated in the splinters, pressing one hand over his mouth.

“Stay,” Shawna told him.

He stayed.

In the mirrored wall, Aiden caught a moving hand behind his own reflection. Another guard. A gun rising. Aiden turned, but Mercedes was already pushing the drive into her jacket. She grabbed the microphone stand in both hands.

“Down, Aiden!” she shouted.

He ducked. The shaft passed over his head and hit the guard across the chest. The man staggered into the drum kit. A cymbal folded under him; a stand rang against the platform. The bass drum rolled until the keyboard player stopped it with his shoe.

The drummer looked at the broken cymbal, then at the guard crawling out from under it.

“That's vintage, nigga,” he said.

He lifted his stool and brought it down on the man's back. The guard flattened among the drums.

Silas twisted out of his fur coat. Aiden was left holding the collar as the man fled around the coffin toward the steps. His shoulder pulsed; his fingers had gone wet inside the fur.

Tank rose from behind the guests' table, bounded onto the stage, and met Silas at the top step. He caught him around the waist. With his free hand, Tank swung the coffin lid open and tipped the struggling man into the satin.

The box rocked. Tank dropped the lid.

A squeal came from inside.

“Shit,” Tank said.

The lid flew up. A little man in a gold tracksuit sat upright beneath Silas, wearing Aiden's spare sunglasses crooked on his face. One lens had fogged over. He shoved Silas off his legs with both hands.

“NIGGA, I TOLD YOU NINE-FIFTEEN!” the little man screamed at Tank.

Aiden looked from the tracksuit to his friend. Tank began studying his shoes.

“You hired a corpse?” Aiden asked him.

“Resurrection package,” Tank explained.

Aiden waited, the coat hanging from his fist.

“It included the dance,” Tank added.

“And fog,” the performer shouted, pointing over Silas's back.

Tank waved toward the plaster dust drifting through the lights. “We got atmosphere.”

“That's ceiling,” the performer told him.

“Work with the venue, nigga,” Tank said.

The little man climbed out over Silas's stomach. He straightened his jacket and tugged both gold cuffs down over his wrists.

“I do the surprise. I do the dance. I leave,” he informed Tank. “Ain't nobody said wrestling.”

At the far side of the room, the fourth guard had begun edging toward the entrance. Tank pointed at him.

“Sit your ass down. The nigga hasn't danced,” Tank ordered.

The guard sat at an empty table and put both palms flat on the cloth.

Aiden turned toward the smallest man. He was still beside the steps with a shrimp tail in his right hand and the open, empty case in his left. He lowered the case to the floor without making a sound.

“I'm with catering,” the guard said.

From the kitchen doorway, the cook answered him. “No the fuck you are not.”

***

Tank and Porsche collected the weapons and tied the guards' wrists behind them. A waiter crouched beside the piano, wrapped the fallen pistol in a cloth, and passed it up to Tank. Broken chairs came out of the aisle. A guest helped another guest retrieve a shoe from beneath a table. The bartender rose behind his counter, inspected the bottles, and announced that he was still open.

Aiden watched people begin standing properly again. Seven minutes after the first shot, somebody was complaining about a drink. That seemed encouraging.

Silas tried to climb out of the coffin. The hired corpse put a sneaker against his forehead and pushed him gently back into the satin.

“Stop touching my face,” Silas snapped.

“Stop being in my office, nigga,” the performer said.

Mercedes had gone to the sound desk along the side wall. Aiden saw her take the drive from her jacket and accept a cable from the young technician. Porsche stood beside her. Folders filled the small screen. Under the edge of the table, Porsche found Mercedes's hand.

Aiden took a step toward them. Shawna caught two fingers in his belt and held him still.

He waited beside her. His shoulder was beginning to throb in time with his pulse. Each beat tugged the wet cloth stuck to the wound. Across the room, Mercedes leaned nearer the screen. She selected something, listened with one headphone against her ear, and closed her eyes.

Aiden wanted to see her smile. He stayed where Shawna had stopped him.

Mercedes opened her eyes and nodded to Porsche. Then she lifted one hand toward Aiden and Shawna.

“Every track,” Mercedes called across the room. “All the originals. They're here.”

The tightness beneath Aiden's ribs eased. He watched her unplug the headphone and turn toward the stage.

The drummer was tightening a spare cymbal into place. Mercedes asked whether he was good.

“You doing something slow?” he asked her.

“No,” Mercedes said.

The drummer sat behind his repaired kit. “Then yeah.”

She gave the technician a track number and pointed at Aiden's portrait beside the entrance.

“Turn that nigga around,” Mercedes said. “I need the room.”

Aiden went to the frame. It was heavier than it looked. He used his good arm, but the turn still pulled at his injured shoulder. He set the policeman and the angel wings against the wall and left the plain brown backing facing the guests.

A hiss came through the speakers, then a saxophone. The breath at the start of the note was still there, rough beneath the warmth. Aiden recognized the recording. The live bass found its place under it. The drummer settled into the beat.

Mercedes began quietly. People had to stop talking to hear the first line. She put one heel against the monitor and lifted her chin. Her next breath filled her jacket at the ribs, and the next line came out strong enough to draw a woman from her chair.

Aiden watched the spaces between the tables fill. Hands caught other hands. People stepped around chair splinters and made room with their hips. Mercedes wasn't explaining the fight. She was singing.

The little performer shut Silas's coffin and climbed onto the lid.

“Get off!” Silas shouted from inside, pounding beneath his shoes.

“Nine-fifteen, bitch!” the performer shouted back.

His gold trousers flashed above the dark wood. He danced hard enough to shake flowers off one corner. Tank raised both arms toward the stage.

“That nigga earned his deposit!” Tank called.

Porsche tipped her face up and laughed. The cook came out with another tray, Aiden's jacket over his shoulder. A woman took the tray, put it on a table, and caught both his hands.

“I'm working,” the cook told her.

“Not anymore,” she said, pulling him toward the music.

The cook looked back at the kitchen. Nobody came to claim him. He let the woman turn him around.

Shawna slid her fingers between Aiden's. Her other hand touched the dark patch on his sleeve, and he flinched.

“You're bleeding,” she said.

“Little bit,” Aiden told her.

She looked along his arm. “We'll get it looked at.”

“After the song,” he said.

Shawna moved to his good side. On the last chorus, Mercedes's younger voice caught through the speakers. The little break Aiden remembered. Onstage, Mercedes sang the same words beside it and smiled. Porsche stopped laughing. Her hand came up to the base of her throat and stayed there until the line was finished.

Shawna leaned close to Aiden's ear.

“Eleven sponsors is a lot of mouths to feed,” she said.

He laughed and hurt his shoulder, then brought her closer anyway.

At the entrance, the ticket man admitted another group.

“Sixty dollars,” he told them.

Aiden pointed across the room. “Nigga, it was forty!”

The man nodded toward Mercedes, the dancer on the coffin, and Aiden standing beside his own widow.

“Live show now,” the ticket man said.

***

By the time police took Silas away, the band wanted overtime. Tank was negotiating for the remaining shrimp. Officers led the guards down the central aisle past guests who wouldn't stop eating for them. The performer stood beside the empty coffin counting his pay. He had finally taken off Aiden's sunglasses, leaving two damp oval marks around his eyes.

Aiden sat near the sound desk, holding a clean folded napkin against his shoulder. The young technician brought over two glasses. He looked about twenty-three, with a narrow beard and headphones resting around his neck. His eyes kept moving between Shawna and the purple stamp on Aiden's wrist.

“Can I ask something?” the technician said.

“Apparently you can charge for it,” Aiden told him.

The young man smiled. “How'd you two meet?”

Shawna laughed before Aiden could answer.

“Let me tell it,” she said.

Aiden turned toward her. “Baby, I was there.”

“So was I,” Shawna reminded him. “One of us remembers who saved whom.”

The technician pulled up a chair. Tank called for somebody to get him a plate.

“I'm on the clock,” the young man told Tank.

“So am I,” Tank said. “I organized a death.”

Mercedes brought more napkins. Porsche moved a bottle within reach. Tank dragged over another chair, its legs catching on the place where champagne had dried. Shawna settled beside Aiden's uninjured shoulder and took one of the glasses.

The outer doors stood open. Night air moved through the room, bending the candle flames toward the kitchen. Aiden could smell extinguished wicks and the sweetness of flowers getting warm. Under the table, plaster dust lay among petals and chips of ice.

He looked at the technician waiting across from him.

“First night in Vegas,” Aiden said. “You want the whole thing?”

The young man nodded.

“Start before you became reasonable,” Shawna advised.

“We still waiting on that chapter, nigga,” Aiden told her.

Tank laughed into his shrimp.

Aiden took a drink. The liquor warmed the back of his throat, and for a moment the bass in this room seemed to be coming through another wall. He could remember hot concrete beneath his shoes. The cars lined up in the dark. Big T's hand settling on his shoulder, heavy enough to make an offer feel like a command.

He set the glass down and began with the twelve men waiting behind the club.
