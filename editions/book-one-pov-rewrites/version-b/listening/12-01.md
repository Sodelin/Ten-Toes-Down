## 12. The Money at the Bottom

The folder smelled of leather and the cigarettes Red hadn't smoked in front of his guests.

Aiden opened it against the desk. Dates filled the first page. The second carried amounts and names. On the third, someone had written *settled* beside Valentino in red ink, pressing hard enough to mark the sheet underneath.

A copy of the murder order lay behind it.

The same order. The same cold arrangement of a brother, a watch, and a woman to keep alive. Aiden had seen Shawna read those words in her own house. Now here they were in Red's office, filed neatly enough for a man to find between drinks.

“You kept a copy,” Aiden said.

Red watched his face. “Careful with my papers.”

Shawna pulled the folder toward her. Aiden let go. She turned the page, then turned it back. Her thumb stopped beside Dante's name.

“This lived in your desk?” Shawna asked Red.

“T kept records,” Red said. “You knew that.”

“You're sitting behind the desk.” Shawna lifted the order. “You. Right fucking here.”

Red said her name as though she had broken something at dinner.

She threw the sheet at his chest. It drifted onto the blotter. Aiden wanted it to land harder. Wanted paper to do what he could barely stop his hands from doing.

“Was trying to make this generous,” Red told him.

“Try shutting your mouth,” Aiden said.

Tank stepped closer. Both men guarding the outer door shifted their feet. Red looked from Tank to Aiden, and a small pleasure appeared at the corners of his mouth.

“Ask your friend about generosity,” Red said.

Tank stopped moving.

Aiden turned toward him. “What is he talking about?”

Tank's eyes stayed on Red. “Aiden.”

“That's my name,” Aiden said. “Answer the fucking question.”

Red took a remote from beside the telephone. A screen lit on the wall behind Aiden. He turned toward the light and saw the motel's empty swimming pool.

For a second, the office floor seemed to tilt into those blue tiles. He could feel T's pistol pressed into his side. Cash moved in the recorded wind. The balcony rail cut across the top of the picture.

Their Mercedes had already left.

Tank climbed down into the pool carrying an empty bag.

Aiden watched the man beside him bend on the screen and begin gathering money.

***

The recording had no useful sound. That made the small office noises enormous: Red's thumbnail clicking against the remote; Shawna drawing a breath and failing to finish it; the leather box creaking in Aiden's hand.

On the screen, Tank filled the bag.

“Turn it off,” Aiden told Red.

Red let it play. Recorded Tank climbed out and returned with another bag. He stepped around the place where T had fallen, bent, and picked up a loose bundle beside his shoe.

“Turn the fucking thing off,” Aiden said.

The screen went black.

“Three million,” Red told them. “Your friend doesn't leave a tip.”

Aiden looked at Tank's hands. He'd watched those hands bring him clothes, repair cars, open doors. One had steadied him when he couldn't stand straight. On the screen, both had worked without hesitation around the blood in the pool.

“You took her money,” Aiden said.

Tank met his eyes. “Took it out the pool.”

“Don't make that sound different.”

“I'm not,” Tank said.

Aiden moved toward him. “You watched her hold me together. Watched me bleed in the car you brought. We left to keep me alive, and you went back down there shopping?”

Tank's jaw worked once. “Yes.”

Aiden hit him in the face.

The punch pulled across his wounded side so violently that he nearly folded after it. Tank's head turned. His feet stayed planted. When he faced Aiden again, blood shone at one corner of his mouth.

Aiden hit him again.

“Hit back,” Aiden demanded. “Look at me and do it.”

“No,” Tank said.

“Now you got limits?” Aiden asked.

Shawna caught Aiden's right forearm. He still held the blue box in his left hand, gripping it so hard the edge had left a groove in his palm. He hadn't thought about it once while he struck Tank.

Tank looked at Shawna, then back at him.

“I'm not hitting you,” Tank said.

Red leaned against the desk. “Touching.”

Aiden turned toward him. Shawna tightened her grip.

“Another word,” Aiden told Red. “Give me one.”

Red glanced at the box. “Going to die rich?”

Aiden lifted it, suddenly ready to put the corner through the man's teeth. The lid had sprung partly open in his grip. Blue caught the desk lamp.

Marcellus stepped near enough to look inside.

The old man's face changed. Aiden saw it and held still.

“Close the box,” Marcellus told him.

“What?” Aiden asked.

“Close it,” Marcellus repeated.

His eyes came up to Aiden's. Whatever he had seen under the lamp, he wasn't saying it here.

Aiden pressed the lid shut.

***

Tank wiped his mouth with the back of his hand.

“It's at the house,” he told Aiden. “Back of my closet. Moved it when we moved in.”

Shawna's fingers loosened on Aiden's arm.

“How much is left?” she asked Tank.

“All of it,” Tank said. “Every dollar.”

“Then why?” Shawna asked. “Why let us think it was gone?”

Tank looked at the carpet. Aiden took a step toward him, and the bigger man made himself look up.

“Thought you'd leave,” Tank told Aiden.

Aiden stared at him. “Was busy fucking dying.”

“Know that,” Tank said.

“You keep saying you know things.”

Tank drew a breath through his nose. Blood moved at his lip again.

“T was dead,” Tank said. “You had her. Had the car. Started talking about houses by water, taking her anywhere. Thought one morning I'd wake up and you'd be gone.”

Aiden felt Shawna beside him, still enough that her sleeve scarcely touched his. He couldn't put what Tank was saying beside the man he had trusted. The shapes refused to meet.

“So you kept three million for company?” Aiden asked.

“Thought I'd have something,” Tank said. His eyes had filled. “If you left. That's all the answer I got.”

Aiden wanted the tears gone. They made Tank look like someone he might hurt instead of a solid thing he could hit until an explanation came out.

Red tapped a finger against the desk.

“Finish the story,” Red told Tank.

Tank turned on him. “You called me.”

“You answered,” Red said.

“Sent that video to my phone.”

“And you answered again,” Red said.

Shawna moved away from Aiden. Her voice had gone level.

“Did you tell him where we were?” she asked Tank.

“No,” Tank said at once.

She waited, staring at him.

“Didn't tell him about the lake house,” Tank continued. “Didn't give him your plans. He knew tonight because you called him yourself. Didn't give him that.”

Red's smile thinned.

“Offered him his place back,” Red told Aiden.

“What place?” Aiden asked.

“Yours,” Red said.

Aiden remembered the second phone at the bedroom door. Tank watching him call men who had once answered to T. All those ordinary moments waited in his head, suddenly available for a different explanation. He couldn't tell which deserved it.

“You want it?” Aiden asked Tank.

Tank didn't answer.

“Look at me,” Aiden told him. “I'm here. You want what he offered?”

Tank looked at Aiden's white jacket. Aiden followed his eyes and saw a small red stain spreading near the wound. The punches had opened something. Shawna reached toward it, and he moved before her hand arrived.

Tank shook his head.

“Wanted you to ask me to stay,” Tank said. “Me. Not the driver.”

Aiden had an answer for theft. For blackmail. For a man choosing a bigger chair. This stopped him with his anger still raised and nowhere certain to put it.

Red opened a drawer and tossed a car key onto the blotter.

“Still time,” Red told Tank. “Bring my money. Walk out with a future.”

Tank picked up the key.

Aiden's stomach went cold.

Then Tank hurled it at the window behind Red. A white star cracked across the glass. The key bounced onto the carpet; the pane stayed in its frame.

Tank looked at the failed exit his throw had made. Then he looked at Red.

“Fuck your car too,” Tank said.

***

The guard nearest Tank grabbed his shoulder. Tank turned and drove him into the wall. A framed photograph jumped sideways.

The other guard reached inside his jacket. Shawna took the desk lamp in both hands and smashed it across his forearm. The bulb burst. The shade rolled over the papers, trailing smoke.

Red caught her from behind.

Aiden lunged toward them. The first guard had slipped out of Tank's grasp; he struck Aiden's side with the hard butt of his pistol. Aiden hit the desk and couldn't breathe. The folder spread beneath his right palm. He clamped down on it because the floor was moving and the desk was the one thing he could find.

Shawna's shoes scraped the wood as Red dragged her toward the inner door. She drove an elbow back at him. He twisted away and kept hold of her.

“Let her go,” Aiden tried to say.

The words came out without enough air to carry them.

Tank tore the first guard backward. Marcellus caught Aiden's sleeve and helped him off the desk. Aiden gathered the folder under his right arm. The blue box remained in his left hand.

The second guard fired. A chip of wood struck Aiden's knuckles. The report filled the office, and then screaming rose through the floor from the ballroom.

Red reached the inner doorway with Shawna in front of him. He had drawn a pistol. Its muzzle pressed against her body.

Aiden stopped.

Tank released the man he had been holding. Even the guards paused.

“There,” Red said. “Everybody learns.”

Shawna's hair had come loose. One earring was gone. She looked at Aiden over Red's arm, her mouth closed hard. He couldn't stand that look. Couldn't stand the distance between her outstretched hand and his.

“Take me,” Aiden told Red.

Red regarded him as though he'd offered the wrong currency.

“Don't want you,” Red said.

“Let her walk. I'll stay.”

“Heard you,” Red said.

Shawna shook her head, sharp enough to pull hair across her cheek.

“Aiden,” she said.

He kept watching the pistol.

“Take it and go,” Shawna told him.

“Not leaving you,” Aiden said.

“Go,” she repeated.

The inner door opened behind Red. A third man stood beyond it. Shawna glanced back, then looked directly at Tank.

“Get him out,” she ordered.

Red pulled her through the doorway.

Aiden went after them. Tank caught him around the chest and hauled him backward. Aiden struck him in the face, the shoulder, anywhere his right fist could reach without losing the folder. He heard himself threaten to kill him. Tank kept moving toward the outer door.

A shot struck the wall behind them. Marcellus had the door open. Rosa stood in the corridor, her red skirt gathered above her shoes.

“Where is she?” Rosa demanded.

“Red has her,” Marcellus said.

Rosa tried to pass him. He caught her hand.

“Not through there,” Marcellus told her.

Tank dragged Aiden into the corridor. For a few steps Aiden could still hear Shawna somewhere past the office. Then the corridor turned, another shot sounded, and her voice was gone.
