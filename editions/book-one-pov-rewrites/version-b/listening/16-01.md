## 16. The Last King of Vegas

Tank came around the Mercedes and stood beside Aiden.

Behind them, headlights burned through the settling dust. Men got out of cars wearing blue at their wrists and throats. Aiden recognized faces from years ago and others who had started calling him boss eleven days earlier. Their guns were visible now. Nobody had come dressed for peace.

Above the entrance, Shawna stood beneath a chandelier that had never been lit.

Red hadn't finished building the place. Marble covered one wall; the next showed naked concrete. Plastic moved in the dark openings where windows belonged. The columns were ready for a king, but the dust beneath them still carried workers' boot prints.

Aiden lifted his good hand toward Shawna. She found it among the headlights and held his eyes.

Red came closer to the balcony edge. He wore white from his shoes to his collar. The blue stone on his right hand flashed as he put that hand on her shoulder.

“Brought everybody,” Red called down to Aiden.

“Told them there was a dead man here,” Aiden answered.

Tank looked at him. Aiden could read the warning well enough without words. He was already leaning forward, ready to move wherever Red gave him an opening, and Tank's hand had found his sleeve.

“Inside,” Red told them. “You and Tank. That's all.”

Aiden took a step. Tank held him for one second.

“Get her and leave,” Tank said.

“Get her,” Aiden answered.

Tank studied his face, then let go.

***

Men stood between roulette tables covered in plastic. Others waited beside the unfinished bar, beneath the staircase, along the walls. Aiden counted thirty as he and Tank crossed the room.

His badge showed inside the ruined jacket. Several men looked at it before looking at his gun. From outside came a short burst of police siren. Through the entrance behind him, he could see Knox and Bell directing officers around the parked cars.

Red brought Shawna down the staircase by her elbow. Every tightening of his fingers made Aiden want to tear that arm out of the white sleeve.

“Let her walk,” Aiden told him.

“Walking,” Red said.

“To me,” Aiden answered. “Let her walk to me.”

Red stopped halfway down.

“Haven't paid for anything,” Red said.

Tank stepped beside Aiden. “Brought the money.”

Red laughed.

“Still think it's three million?” Red asked him.

“Kept asking for it,” Tank said.

“Asked for respect,” Red replied. “Money was easy.”

He turned back to Aiden.

“How much did she tell you about that first night?” Red asked.

Shawna closed her eyes.

Aiden looked at Red. “Enough.”

“Supposed to get into a car with whoever T sent,” Red said. “Could've been you. Tank. Anybody with a pulse and somewhere to hide a bag.”

“Don't,” Shawna told Red.

He leaned toward her.

“Being honest,” Red said. “What lovers want, isn't it?”

Aiden heard Tank draw a slow breath beside him.

Red lifted Shawna's wrist. Dante's watch slid against her skin.

“House, cars, army,” Red said to Aiden. “Whole parking lot ready to die because they think you're something. Still wears his watch.”

Aiden had looked at that watch in bed. At breakfast. Against his cheek when Shawna touched his face. He'd wanted it to stop mattering and had sometimes mistaken not speaking about it for succeeding.

It mattered now. Red could see that. The man's eyes stayed on Aiden's face as he held up her wrist.

“Tell him whose chain brought you over,” Red ordered Shawna.

She looked at Aiden.

“Dante's,” Shawna said.

Tank turned toward him. Aiden kept watching her.

“First time I kissed you,” Shawna continued, “I was looking at his cross. Wanted something of him back. Didn't know what I was doing.”

Red smiled. His gaze moved to Aiden's hand, waiting.

Aiden's throat hurt. He let it.

“Now?” Aiden asked her.

Shawna swallowed. Blood marked the corner of her mouth.

“Looking at you,” she said.

Red's smile disappeared.

“Don't give him that shit,” Red told her.

Shawna held Aiden's eyes.

“You, Aiden,” she said.

He nodded once.

“Keep looking, baby,” Aiden told her.

Red hit her.

Aiden moved before he could find a thought to stop himself. Tank caught his arm, but Aiden pulled free. Guns rose around the room. Men shouted. Red shouted over them not to fire yet.

Shawna caught the banister instead of falling. She straightened slowly and turned toward Red. Aiden could see the blood at her mouth and the hard, fixed look she gave him.

Red drew his pistol and put it beneath her jaw.

Aiden stopped at the foot of the stairs. His heart struck the place where T's bullet remained with every beat.

“One more,” Red warned him.

“You win,” Aiden said.

Tank stared at him. “Aiden.”

“Let her come down,” Aiden told Red. “West side, house, money. Take them. Want my name? Have that too.”

Red watched him.

Aiden meant it. He could sleep in the car with her. Find another room for the piano later. Nothing he owned had ever made him feel as rich as her hand reaching for his.

“Hear him?” Red asked Shawna. “Everything.”

“Take it,” Shawna said. “Let me go.”

Red shook his head.

“Dante offered everything too,” Red said.

The room seemed to tighten around the words. Aiden watched Shawna's face.

Red glanced down at his ring, then toward Tank.

“Her husband thought a brother made him untouchable,” Red told Aiden. “You brought a parking lot. Everybody thinks somebody won't sell.”

He smiled at Tank.

“How much from that pool?” Red asked.

“Gave it back,” Tank said.

“After I told him,” Red replied.

“Gave it back,” Tank repeated.

Red beckoned with his free hand.

“Come here,” Red told Tank. “Don't die for someone you met eleven days ago.”

Tank looked at Aiden. Aiden said nothing. He had asked for the car, the money, the drive, the return to this room. He couldn't make this choice for him too.

Tank put his hand on Aiden's shoulder, where T had rested his hand that first night.

“My brother,” Tank said.

Red shot him.

***

Tank struck Aiden as he fell. His weight knocked Aiden sideways, taking him below the first guns that fired across the room.

Aiden drew his pistol and fired toward the stairs. Red pulled Shawna across his body and backed upward. Aiden stopped shooting when she covered the line to him. Red kept retreating through the doorway at the top.

Tank caught Aiden's ankle.

“Go,” Tank said.

Blood spread below his shoulder. Aiden dropped beside him for half a second, unable to make his body choose.

“Get her,” Tank told him. “Go.”

The front doors burst inward under the men outside. Glass came down across the entrance. Blue cloth moved between the tables. Someone fell onto a roulette wheel. A figure disappeared through the plastic draped over the bar.

Aiden went up the stairs with Tank's voice still in his ears.

A man in a brown jacket rounded the first turn and fired too close. The shot broke the rail beside Aiden's hand. Aiden drove his left shoulder into the man's chest and crushed him against the wall, keeping his own pistol turned away from the people below. The man's weapon struck the steps and bounced down behind them.

Aiden kept climbing.

Through the stairwell opening he saw Rex arrive with a man gripped by the jacket and another by the belt. The boxer slammed them into each other hard enough to bring both down beside the nearest table.

“Captain!” Rex shouted.

“Stairs!” Aiden called back.

Rex came toward them.

Red was above Aiden with Shawna, dragging her toward the unfinished upper corridor. She stumbled, caught herself, and looked back for half a second. Her eyes found him. He climbed another flight.

A gunman waited between two empty wall fixtures at the top. His pistol pointed directly at Aiden's chest.

Aiden stopped one step below him.

“Move,” Aiden told him.

The man glanced at the badge.

“Captain Aiden?” he asked.

“Terrible place to introduce yourself,” Aiden said. “Move.”

“Red said you weren't really—” the man began.

A shot came from deeper in the corridor.

The gunman flinched. Aiden caught his collar and hauled him down onto the step beside him. A second bullet struck the wall where the man's head had been. White dust fell over his shoes.

“Generous boss,” Aiden told him.

The man looked from the fresh hole to the corridor. Aiden fired toward the distant shooter, driving him behind a pillar. The man beside Aiden turned his own weapon away from Aiden and toward that pillar.

“Go,” the man said.

Aiden looked at him. “Decide properly.”

The man raised his voice.

“Go get her, Captain,” he said.

“Name?” Aiden asked.

“Malik,” the man answered.

Aiden nodded and went past him. Behind him, Malik shouted at the other gunmen to put their weapons down. Aiden heard one man argue and another pistol strike the floor. He couldn't tell what the rest chose.

Before he turned into the corridor, he looked through the open side of the stairwell. Porsche was dragging a wounded man behind the roulette table where Tank lay. Mercedes stood beside them with a pistol, watching the dark bar.

“Rosa's coming!” Porsche shouted up to Aiden.

He lifted a hand to show he'd heard. He didn't know how she knew, but the women were beside Tank now. He made himself turn away.

Little Saint came through a side entrance below with three more men. Aiden heard his voice rise over the gunfire.

“Our man up there!” Saint shouted. “Clear him a road!”

Aiden was already on it.
