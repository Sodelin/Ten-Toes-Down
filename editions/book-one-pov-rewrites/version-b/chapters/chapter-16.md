## 16. The Last King of Vegas

Tank came around the Mercedes and stood beside Aiden.

Behind them, headlights burned through the settling dust. Men got out of cars wearing blue at their wrists and throats. Aiden recognized faces from years ago and others who had started calling him boss eleven days earlier. Their guns were visible now. Nobody had come dressed for peace.

Above the entrance, Shawna stood beneath a chandelier that had never been lit.

Red hadn't finished building the place. Marble covered one wall; the next showed naked concrete. Plastic moved in the dark openings where windows belonged. The columns were ready for a king, but the dust beneath them still carried workers' boot prints.

Aiden lifted his good hand toward Shawna. She found it among the headlights and held his eyes.

Red came closer to the balcony edge. He wore white from his shoes to his collar. The blue stone on his right hand flashed as he put that hand on her shoulder.

“Brought everybody,” Red called down to Aiden.

“Told them there was a dead man here,” Aiden answered.

Tank looked at him. Aiden could read the warning well enough without words. He was already leaning forward, ready to move wherever Red gave him an opening, and Tank's hand had found his sleeve.

“Inside,” Red told them. “You and Tank. That's all.”

Aiden took a step. Tank held him for one second.

“Get her and leave,” Tank said.

“Get her,” Aiden answered.

Tank studied his face, then let go.

***

Men stood between roulette tables covered in plastic. Others waited beside the unfinished bar, beneath the staircase, along the walls. Aiden counted thirty as he and Tank crossed the room.

His badge showed inside the ruined jacket. Several men looked at it before looking at his gun. From outside came a short burst of police siren. Through the entrance behind him, he could see Knox and Bell directing officers around the parked cars.

Red brought Shawna down the staircase by her elbow. Every tightening of his fingers made Aiden want to tear that arm out of the white sleeve.

“Let her walk,” Aiden told him.

“Walking,” Red said.

“To me,” Aiden answered. “Let her walk to me.”

Red stopped halfway down.

“Haven't paid for anything,” Red said.

Tank stepped beside Aiden. “Brought the money.”

Red laughed.

“Still think it's three million?” Red asked him.

“Kept asking for it,” Tank said.

“Asked for respect,” Red replied. “Money was easy.”

He turned back to Aiden.

“How much did she tell you about that first night?” Red asked.

Shawna closed her eyes.

Aiden looked at Red. “Enough.”

“Supposed to get into a car with whoever T sent,” Red said. “Could've been you. Tank. Anybody with a pulse and somewhere to hide a bag.”

“Don't,” Shawna told Red.

He leaned toward her.

“Being honest,” Red said. “What lovers want, isn't it?”

Aiden heard Tank draw a slow breath beside him.

Red lifted Shawna's wrist. Dante's watch slid against her skin.

“House, cars, army,” Red said to Aiden. “Whole parking lot ready to die because they think you're something. Still wears his watch.”

Aiden had looked at that watch in bed. At breakfast. Against his cheek when Shawna touched his face. He'd wanted it to stop mattering and had sometimes mistaken not speaking about it for succeeding.

It mattered now. Red could see that. The man's eyes stayed on Aiden's face as he held up her wrist.

“Tell him whose chain brought you over,” Red ordered Shawna.

She looked at Aiden.

“Dante's,” Shawna said.

Tank turned toward him. Aiden kept watching her.

“First time I kissed you,” Shawna continued, “I was looking at his cross. Wanted something of him back. Didn't know what I was doing.”

Red smiled. His gaze moved to Aiden's hand, waiting.

Aiden's throat hurt. He let it.

“Now?” Aiden asked her.

Shawna swallowed. Blood marked the corner of her mouth.

“Looking at you,” she said.

Red's smile disappeared.

“Don't give him that shit,” Red told her.

Shawna held Aiden's eyes.

“You, Aiden,” she said.

He nodded once.

“Keep looking, baby,” Aiden told her.

Red hit her.

Aiden moved before he could find a thought to stop himself. Tank caught his arm, but Aiden pulled free. Guns rose around the room. Men shouted. Red shouted over them not to fire yet.

Shawna caught the banister instead of falling. She straightened slowly and turned toward Red. Aiden could see the blood at her mouth and the hard, fixed look she gave him.

Red drew his pistol and put it beneath her jaw.

Aiden stopped at the foot of the stairs. His heart struck the place where T's bullet remained with every beat.

“One more,” Red warned him.

“You win,” Aiden said.

Tank stared at him. “Aiden.”

“Let her come down,” Aiden told Red. “West side, house, money. Take them. Want my name? Have that too.”

Red watched him.

Aiden meant it. He could sleep in the car with her. Find another room for the piano later. Nothing he owned had ever made him feel as rich as her hand reaching for his.

“Hear him?” Red asked Shawna. “Everything.”

“Take it,” Shawna said. “Let me go.”

Red shook his head.

“Dante offered everything too,” Red said.

The room seemed to tighten around the words. Aiden watched Shawna's face.

Red glanced down at his ring, then toward Tank.

“Her husband thought a brother made him untouchable,” Red told Aiden. “You brought a parking lot. Everybody thinks somebody won't sell.”

He smiled at Tank.

“How much from that pool?” Red asked.

“Gave it back,” Tank said.

“After I told him,” Red replied.

“Gave it back,” Tank repeated.

Red beckoned with his free hand.

“Come here,” Red told Tank. “Don't die for someone you met eleven days ago.”

Tank looked at Aiden. Aiden said nothing. He had asked for the car, the money, the drive, the return to this room. He couldn't make this choice for him too.

Tank put his hand on Aiden's shoulder, where T had rested his hand that first night.

“My brother,” Tank said.

Red shot him.

***

Tank struck Aiden as he fell. His weight knocked Aiden sideways, taking him below the first guns that fired across the room.

Aiden drew his pistol and fired toward the stairs. Red pulled Shawna across his body and backed upward. Aiden stopped shooting when she covered the line to him. Red kept retreating through the doorway at the top.

Tank caught Aiden's ankle.

“Go,” Tank said.

Blood spread below his shoulder. Aiden dropped beside him for half a second, unable to make his body choose.

“Get her,” Tank told him. “Go.”

The front doors burst inward under the men outside. Glass came down across the entrance. Blue cloth moved between the tables. Someone fell onto a roulette wheel. A figure disappeared through the plastic draped over the bar.

Aiden went up the stairs with Tank's voice still in his ears.

A man in a brown jacket rounded the first turn and fired too close. The shot broke the rail beside Aiden's hand. Aiden drove his left shoulder into the man's chest and crushed him against the wall, keeping his own pistol turned away from the people below. The man's weapon struck the steps and bounced down behind them.

Aiden kept climbing.

Through the stairwell opening he saw Rex arrive with a man gripped by the jacket and another by the belt. The boxer slammed them into each other hard enough to bring both down beside the nearest table.

“Captain!” Rex shouted.

“Stairs!” Aiden called back.

Rex came toward them.

Red was above Aiden with Shawna, dragging her toward the unfinished upper corridor. She stumbled, caught herself, and looked back for half a second. Her eyes found him. He climbed another flight.

A gunman waited between two empty wall fixtures at the top. His pistol pointed directly at Aiden's chest.

Aiden stopped one step below him.

“Move,” Aiden told him.

The man glanced at the badge.

“Captain Aiden?” he asked.

“Terrible place to introduce yourself,” Aiden said. “Move.”

“Red said you weren't really—” the man began.

A shot came from deeper in the corridor.

The gunman flinched. Aiden caught his collar and hauled him down onto the step beside him. A second bullet struck the wall where the man's head had been. White dust fell over his shoes.

“Generous boss,” Aiden told him.

The man looked from the fresh hole to the corridor. Aiden fired toward the distant shooter, driving him behind a pillar. The man beside Aiden turned his own weapon away from Aiden and toward that pillar.

“Go,” the man said.

Aiden looked at him. “Decide properly.”

The man raised his voice.

“Go get her, Captain,” he said.

“Name?” Aiden asked.

“Malik,” the man answered.

Aiden nodded and went past him. Behind him, Malik shouted at the other gunmen to put their weapons down. Aiden heard one man argue and another pistol strike the floor. He couldn't tell what the rest chose.

Before he turned into the corridor, he looked through the open side of the stairwell. Porsche was dragging a wounded man behind the roulette table where Tank lay. Mercedes stood beside them with a pistol, watching the dark bar.

“Rosa's coming!” Porsche shouted up to Aiden.

He lifted a hand to show he'd heard. He didn't know how she knew, but the women were beside Tank now. He made himself turn away.

Little Saint came through a side entrance below with three more men. Aiden heard his voice rise over the gunfire.

“Our man up there!” Saint shouted. “Clear him a road!”

Aiden was already on it.

***

The corridor divided around an unfinished fountain. A shallow layer of dirty water sat at the bottom among scraps of tile. Red's white jacket disappeared through the far doors with Shawna pulled against him.

Aiden followed with his left hand against the wall. Blood warmed the bandages under his shirt.

A man stepped beside the fountain holding a shotgun.

Aiden dropped as it fired. The fountain's edge burst. Water and stone fragments struck his face. He shoved his pistol into its holster as he lunged into the man's legs before the barrel could come down toward him.

They hit the floor together. The shotgun slid across the concrete.

The man drove an elbow into Aiden's wounded side. Pain spread so wide that the corridor vanished. Aiden couldn't see the doors. Couldn't find the next breath.

Shawna shouted his name from beyond the wall.

He heard it and struck the man beneath him. Again. Again, until the grip at his ribs opened. His knuckles hurt through the bandage. He hit with the other hand. The man's head struck concrete, and both arms fell away.

Aiden got one foot beneath himself and rose.

The man caught his ankle.

Aiden looked down.

“Don't make this your whole life,” Aiden told him.

The fingers opened.

Another man appeared at the far doorway, firing. Aiden crouched, took the shotgun from the floor, and fired toward the marble stacked beside him. The shooter dropped behind the slabs. Aiden left the shotgun and drew his own pistol again as he passed the fountain.

Ahead, desert light showed through a room without windows. A thin railing divided the floor from the darkness beyond. Aiden could hear Red breathing hard.

“Don't get tired?” Red called from inside.

Aiden reached the doorway.

“Keep giving me somewhere to be,” Aiden answered.

***

The upper room opened straight onto the unfinished balcony. No glass. No doors along that edge. Below it lay concrete, steel, and the lights of their cars.

Red held Shawna against the railing. As he hauled her backward, a small silver pistol fell from inside his jacket and struck the floor beside his shoe.

Hers.

Aiden stopped in the doorway with his own gun raised.

“Down,” Red ordered him.

“Let her go,” Aiden said.

Red pulled her higher against the rail. She gripped his arm, and one heel scraped for purchase on the floor.

“Gun down or she goes over,” Red said.

Shawna looked at Aiden. Her eyes were wide. Her mouth worked around a breath she couldn't finish. He could see the fear without needing her to say it.

“Put her feet down,” Aiden told Red.

“Gun first.”

Aiden crouched slowly and laid his pistol on the concrete beside the doorway. He opened his hand and stood.

Red watched the weapon settle. Then he smiled.

“How your husband died,” Red told Shawna. “Hands open. Begging. T couldn't even look at him.”

Shawna turned her head enough to speak.

“Not for himself,” she said.

Red tightened his arm. “What?”

“Said he wanted somebody to look after me,” Shawna told him. “Wasn't begging for himself.”

“Shut the fuck up,” Red said.

She drove her head backward into his face.

Red staggered. His gun fired into the open sky. Shawna dropped from his arm onto the floor, reached for her silver pistol, and came up with it in both hands.

She shot his leg.

Red buckled against the railing. She fired again, into his chest. His free hand reached toward her, fingers closing on nothing.

“Dante,” Shawna said.

She tried to lift the pistol again. Both hands were shaking so badly that the barrel fell beneath his chest.

Red remained against the rail. Blood spread through the white shirt. His gun hand began rising.

Aiden had dropped back to his own pistol the moment Shawna broke free. He took it from the concrete and fired before Red's muzzle came level with her.

Red's gun fell. Aiden fired again. The man's knees struck the balcony floor. He toppled sideways along the base of the railing and lay still, looking into a sky that hadn't agreed to belong to him.

Aiden went to Shawna.

She knelt with the little gun in front of her. Her mouth moved around Dante's name.

“Baby,” Aiden said.

She didn't look up.

“Shawna,” he tried again.

Her fingers wouldn't open. He knelt beside her and closed his free hand around them.

“Done,” Aiden told her. “It's done.”

She shook her head.

He wanted his name. Wanted it from her mouth so badly he could hardly breathe. She kept saying Dante's. Aiden eased the silver pistol from her hands, laid it beside his own, and put both arms around her.

She folded against him and cried.

He held her on the dusty floor while she grieved for her husband. There was room for his jealousy; he could feel it hurting. There was more room for the woman shaking in his arms.

He held her tighter because he was here.

Voices sounded in the corridor. Someone reached the doorway, saw Red, and shouted that he was dead. The words went down the stairs. Aiden heard his own name follow them.

Through the hall. Across the gaming floor. Out toward the headlights.

His shoes were wet with blood. Shawna's face pressed against his neck. Below, men were calling him king, and he found that he could believe them without letting her go.

***

When Shawna could stand, Aiden retrieved both pistols. He put his own in its holster and kept hers tucked securely in his jacket. Then he went to Red's body.

The right hand lay open on the floor. Aiden pulled the blue ring from it.

He didn't know its worth yet. He knew what Marcellus needed to see. He closed his hand around it and felt the setting press into his palm.

Shawna waited beside the doorway. He went back to her, put his free arm around her, and helped her down the stairs.

The shooting had stopped. Men stood with hands raised among the tables. Weapons lay on the floor. Aiden saw Malik near the landing, speaking to an officer with his own gun held by the barrel.

Tank was alive beside the roulette table.

Rosa knelt over him with a folded tablecloth pressed below his shoulder. Dr. Vega was tending another wounded man near the entrance. Aiden saw them and almost stopped walking.

Rosa looked up.

“Where the fuck you been?” she demanded.

“With her,” Aiden said.

Rosa looked past him and saw Shawna. Her face changed so suddenly that Aiden felt his own mouth begin to shake.

“Come here, baby,” Rosa told her.

Shawna knelt beside her. Rosa kept one hand on the tablecloth and reached the other around Shawna's shoulders. They pressed their foreheads together.

“Couldn't sit in that house,” Rosa said, looking up at Aiden. “Got Vega. Followed after you. Came in when it stopped.”

He nodded. He couldn't imagine the room without her now.

Tank opened his eyes when Aiden knelt. Aiden put the ring into his trouser pocket so he could take Tank's hand.

“Got her?” Tank asked.

“Yeah,” Aiden said.

“Red?”

“Dead,” Aiden told him.

Tank tried to smile. Pain caught the expression before it finished.

“Pay my hospital bill,” Tank said.

Aiden gripped his hand. “Every penny.”

“Car too,” Tank added.

“What car?” Aiden asked.

“Want one.”

“Hundred,” Aiden said. “Buy you a hundred fucking cars.”

Tank closed his eyes.

“Black ones,” he said.

Aiden held on until Vega reached them. The last man who'd called him family had put a bullet in him. Tank had taken one instead. Aiden watched the doctor's hands and listened for every breath beneath them.

***

Marcellus came through the entrance after the weapons were down.

He stepped carefully over the glass, the blue box in one hand and his coat closed over the papers. He looked at the unfinished marble, the bare walls, the great staircase. Then his eyes found Aiden beside Tank.

Vega had attendants preparing Tank to move. Aiden rose slowly and took the ring from his pocket.

He put it in Marcellus's palm.

The old man didn't speak. He brought out his jeweler's lens, held the ring beneath a work light, and bent over it. Aiden waited while the old hands turned the stone a fraction at a time.

Marcellus drew a long breath.

“There,” he said. “Inside. The flaw. This is her.”

He lowered the lens. His eyes had filled. He took Celeste's letter from his coat and laid the ring against the paper, bending his head over both.

Now Aiden knew. Forty million dollars stood in an old man's hand, and for once the number didn't tell him what he was looking at.

“What will you do?” Aiden asked.

Marcellus closed his fingers around the ring and letter.

“Take my wife home,” he said.

Aiden nodded and let him have the silence that followed.

Shawna came to stand beside Aiden. She put her arm around his waist, carefully avoiding the wound, and leaned into him. He kissed her hair.

Knox entered with Bell after the last men had lowered their guns. The chief stopped in front of them. Her eyes moved from Shawna to the badge beneath the blood on Aiden's jacket.

“Brought her home,” Knox said.

“Don't forget a promise,” Aiden told her.

Knox saluted. Bell followed. Other officers straightened in the doorway, and from the tables the men began calling Aiden's name again.

Captain. King. Aiden.

Shawna tightened her arm around him at the sound. He drew her closer and stood where every one of them could see.
