## 2. First Night in Vegas

The rear door shut behind Aiden, turning the nightclub bass into a thick pounding inside the metal. Big T's hand rested on his shoulder. Ahead of them, men waited between crookedly parked cars, stretching their fingers and grinding cigarettes beneath their shoes.

Twelve men, counting T. Aiden counted again without moving his lips. It would have been embarrassing to let them see him worrying about arithmetic before they beat his ass.

Heat rose from the concrete. Blue rags hung from pockets and circled wrists. One of the men had once borrowed ten dollars from Aiden and used both hands to promise he'd return it. Now he was using those hands to loosen his neck.

“Once you're in, you're in,” T told Aiden. His fingers tightened. “Understand?”

“I understand,” Aiden said.

T looked around the waiting men. “Summer ends. A body drops. Suddenly a nigga's got somewhere else to be.”

“I got nowhere else to be,” Aiden told him. “That's why I'm standing out here instead of enjoying the fucking music.”

Somebody laughed behind him. T's hand stayed heavy.

“You think this funny?” T asked.

Aiden counted the faces one last time. “I think twelve is a committee. Which one of you takes the minutes?”

T hit him against a Cadillac.

That answered it. T took the minutes. The other eleven arrived with amendments.

A fist drove Aiden's lip into his teeth. An elbow struck behind his ear. The security light bloomed sideways, white against the block wall. Somebody hooked a leg behind his knee, and he went down with both palms open. Grit bit through skin. He got an elbow beneath him before the shoes found a comfortable arrangement around his ribs.

He pushed up swinging. A jaw knocked against his knuckles. Another man folded around a punch to the stomach. Neither result made the circle smaller. The men moved aside and let fresh fists through. Blood crept from Aiden's eyebrow into one eye, turning the security light red every time he blinked.

He wanted in. That was the simple, ugly part. At twenty-four, he had a burned car, cocaine dust in his shirt, and a watch with another man's blood worked beneath its glass. His ex-boyfriend had decided the burned car outside his apartment meant Aiden wanted him back. Aiden mostly wanted another fucking car.

T had people. When he called, they answered. When he entered a room, somebody made space. Aiden wanted to belong somewhere a man couldn't cross out his name the minute he left.

His knee hit the concrete again.

“Stay down,” the man beside him said.

“Y'all just told me to stay in,” Aiden answered, getting a foot beneath himself. “Pick an instruction.”

Tank hit him under the ribs as he rose. The breath left Aiden so hard he thought something important had gone with it. Tank was twenty-eight, broad enough to make the lot feel narrow, his shirt stretched shiny across his shoulders.

Aiden caught that shirt in both hands and drove forward. Backward meant the whole committee. He kept his forehead against Tank's chest and worked his fists beneath the bigger man's elbows.

They struck another car. An alarm began screaming. Tank's back dented the door.

“My paint!” someone shouted from the circle.

Aiden turned his head just enough to answer. “Should've thought about the paint before you started throwing people at it!”

Tank tried to turn him. Aiden stayed close, denying him room to use those arms. Their shoes slid in spilled beer beside the tire. The car alarm shrieked into Aiden's ear.

“Get this nigga off me!” Tank shouted.

T laughed from somewhere over Aiden's shoulder.

“You said he was little,” T reminded Tank.

“I have new information!” Tank yelled back.

T pulled Aiden away before that information could become fatal. Aiden stood with his fists still raised, breathing through his mouth. His hands didn't seem to understand the stopping part. Every knuckle pulsed.

“Enough,” T said. He looked around the men. “Twelve of us and he's still shopping.”

“I'm done,” Aiden told him.

T glanced at his fists. “You sure?”

“Waiting for my hands to hear it,” Aiden said.

The men laughed. Then T brought a gold chain out of his pocket, and the laughter thinned. Tank pushed himself off the damaged car. Aiden stayed still while T lowered the chain over his head. A little cross settled against his bloody shirt.

“My brother wore that,” T said. “Real nigga.”

Aiden lifted the cross between two fingers. “Where's he at? I should thank him.”

“Dead,” T said.

The car alarm stopped. In the sudden quiet, T's breathing seemed close. His hand moved to the back of Aiden's neck, the thumb warm beneath his ear.

“Don't make me bury you too,” T told him.

Aiden closed his fist around the cross. Tears crowded the same eye he'd been trying to keep blood out of. A minute ago this man had punched him against a Cadillac. Now the same man was giving him something his dead brother wore. Aiden couldn't get those two things to occupy separate places inside him.

“Not this nigga,” he said.

T kissed his forehead and pointed at the door.

“Come get your money,” T told him, then raised his voice to the others. “Make room for my son.”

Tank held the door open. Aiden reached for it too, still trying to do something useful with his hands.

“Walk through,” Tank said. “I can still defeat a door.”

Aiden went inside.

***

Blue light spread through the smoke in the back room. Cash stood in stacks on a glass table beside bottles and wrapped cocaine. Men counted with their heads bent, tapping bundles square before setting them down. Three women sat beside a man with two pistols tucked into the same belt. Whenever he laughed, he leaned back to give his stomach clearance.

One woman lifted her glass to Aiden.

“Damn, baby. You survive a war?” she asked.

“Yeah,” Aiden told her. “And I won.”

The room cheered. Somebody gave him a bottle, somebody else a lit blunt. He drank and coughed when the liquor found his split lip. The chair took his weight. Pain began introducing itself from places he'd forgotten owned nerves.

T announced that Aiden had beaten Tank. The woman leaned over and tucked a folded thousand-dollar bill into Aiden's shirt pocket. He looked down at it, then back at her. Apparently a broken face paid better indoors.

Tank arrived with ice against his jaw. He took Aiden's bottle, drank, and returned it.

“Who taught you to fight dirty like that?” Tank asked.

“Same nigga taught you to fight unconscious,” Aiden said.

The laughter hit the walls. Tank stared at him. Aiden braced his shoes beneath the chair, wondering whether he'd just scheduled another meeting outside.

Then Tank laughed too. Blood showed between his teeth. He dragged a chair close enough that their shoulders touched, and they drank from the same bottle. Aiden let his feet relax. The huge shoulder beside his felt different now that it wasn't coming at him.

T tossed a set of keys across the table. Aiden caught them against his stomach. A silver star turned against his fingers.

“Black Mercedes,” T said. “Yours.”

Aiden pressed one of the buttons with his thumb. The key had weight. Somewhere outside, an entire car was supposed to respond to that little movement.

“You serious?” he asked T.

“West side needs a nigga,” T told him. “West side got you.”

Aiden sat straighter. The cross and the thousand-dollar bill pressed against the same place on his chest. An hour ago, he'd needed permission to join the crew. Now T was handing him territory like he'd found extra city in a pocket.

T opened a photograph on his phone and passed it over. A woman stood beside a pool at night, dark hair over one shoulder, a black dress following the length of her body. Diamond earrings caught the lights behind her. She wasn't smiling. Aiden scarcely looked at the millions of dollars the city had spent on the rest of the view.

“Shawna,” T said. “Twenty-six. Works for Red.”

“Doing what?” Aiden asked.

“Whatever Red wants,” T said. He enlarged the photograph. “Saint penthouse. She's bringing a case down at eleven. You take her and the money to my place in Henderson. That's all.”

Across the table, Tank's bottle stopped halfway to his mouth.

Aiden looked up from the screen. “Why's she leaving Red?”

“Finally got smart,” T said.

“Red know about this family pickup?” Aiden asked.

T took the phone back. “If he knew, we wouldn't be having the conversation.”

He caught Aiden behind the neck and drew him close. For a second, cologne covered the smoke.

“Don't fall in love with her,” T said.

Aiden laughed. T didn't.

“Look at me,” T told him. “I'm serious.”

Aiden spread his free hand. “Look at me, nigga. Do I look like somebody who falls in love?”

T let go. Aiden turned the keys again, feeling the little points bite into his palm. Love wasn't an assignment anybody had given him. He had a woman to collect, money to deliver, and a car outside that expected him specifically. That seemed like enough work for one night.

***

Gold-lit water rose from the Saint's fountain. Valets slipped between polished hoods with their jackets open. Above them, Red's penthouse sat behind bulletproof glass. Aiden had heard enough about the service doors and the men who disappeared through them to know why the carpet inside was thick.

He left the Mercedes directly in front. The valet glanced at the blood on his collar.

“Need a doctor?” the young man asked.

“My car needs you first,” Aiden told him, handing over a hundred. “Nothing happens to it.”

Upstairs, music struck him as the elevator doors opened. Powder streaked mirrored tables. Bank-wrapped money changed hands over cards. A man in white fur was doing cocaine off a trophy clamped between his knees. Whatever he'd won, he wasn't sharing.

Mercedes stood by the bar. Aiden recognized her before she turned: thirty, beautiful, and still owed an apology. He'd called her by her sister's name in a hotel room. She had left with his shoes. The parking lot had been hot through his socks the following morning.

Her eyes went to the car keys in his hand.

“You finally got a Mercedes you can afford, baby?” she asked.

“Come see it,” Aiden said.

Mercedes lifted her chin. “You still owe me flowers.”

He took five hundred from his pocket and offered it to her. “Buy the whole fucking garden.”

She kissed his cheek, then touched her lip and inspected the red smear.

“You're bleeding on the good side of your face,” Mercedes told him.

“Both sides good,” Aiden said.

He left her laughing and crossed the room. Shawna stood by the window with a silver case against her ankle. A small scar broke the line of one eyebrow. She held a cigarette away from her dress while a man beside her talked. Her gaze stayed on Aiden as he approached.

He had known beautiful people. Men who set guns down carefully before coming to bed. Women who left lipstick on a glass and somebody else's diamonds on his pillow. He'd considered himself reasonably prepared for the effect of a face.

Shawna looked directly at him, and his next breath seemed to find less room than the previous one.

“T sent me,” Aiden said.

“I know,” she told him.

“How?” he asked.

Her eyes dropped to the cross. The cigarette stopped halfway to her mouth. She reached for the gold and turned it against his shirt, her fingers cool on his chest. Three little letters showed on the back: D. B. M.

“Where'd you get this?” Shawna asked.

“T. Tonight,” Aiden said.

Her thumb pressed into the engraving. She asked whether he knew what it meant. He shook his head.

Shawna let the cross fall. “Drive before you find out.”

He picked up the case. She took his free hand. As they crossed the party, he saw two men reach for their phones. Shawna's grip tightened near the elevator.

A guard stepped in front of the doors.

“Where the fuck you taking her?” the guard asked Aiden.

“With me,” Aiden told him. “You need a picture?”

“I'm asking her,” the guard said, turning toward Shawna.

Aiden hit him in the throat. The man's mouth opened without producing anything useful. Aiden drove him against the wall and pushed him down before his hand found the inside of his jacket. Beside them, Shawna pressed the elevator button.

The doors opened. Aiden stepped over the guard with the case. Another man rounded the corner, looked at the body on the floor, and reached beneath his coat.

Shawna drew a small silver pistol from her purse and held it toward his chest.

“You get one chance,” she told him.

The man stopped. The elevator closed between them. Shawna kept the gun in her hand as the numbers changed above the door.

Aiden could feel her shoulder touch his whenever the car swayed.

“Men chase you out of every party,” he asked her, “or did I get the special one?”

“Only the ones I survive,” Shawna said.

He looked at the silver pistol, then at her face. There was a whole sensible set of questions he could have asked. Instead he found himself wishing the elevator took a little longer.

***

The Mercedes had scarcely cleared the drive when a black Charger came up behind it without headlights. Shawna caught it in her mirror.

“Aiden. Don't look,” she said.

He looked. He'd owned the car barely eleven minutes. He was checking everything that got close to it.

Two men sat in front of the Charger. A third leaned across the back seat with a long gun. Aiden saw the barrel find the gap between the front seats.

His rear window burst inward.

Shawna folded over the case. Aiden ducked and dragged the wheel with him. The right fender hit the fountain's edge, throwing water across three men in suits. The Mercedes bounced into the street. Glass slid down Aiden's collar. The horn would not stop screaming.

“Your people?” he shouted to Shawna.

“You think I'd be riding with you if they were my people?” she shouted back.

“You met me tonight!” Aiden said, correcting the wheel.

“I'm trying to meet you tomorrow,” Shawna told him. “Drive!”

A yellow Ferrari came from the next street and pulled beside them. Its driver smiled. His passenger lifted a pistol. Aiden resented the comfort of the seat the man was trying to kill him from.

“Move your hand,” he told Shawna.

She looked toward him.

“The horn,” Aiden said.

Her wrist had been pressing it since the fountain. She lifted her hand. For one beautiful second, there was only wind, engine noise, and the wheel warm beneath his palms.

He drove toward the Ferrari.

The passenger saw it first. His pistol jerked upward. The driver turned too late. Yellow metal struck a pole, folded at the nose, and spun across a valet entrance. Sparks skittered under people suddenly finding reasons to run.

“That's one,” Aiden said.

Shawna stared out her window. “That's a Ferrari!”

“One Ferrari,” he told her. “Keep up.”

The Charger stayed behind them. In the mirror, Aiden saw the long gun move again. A red convertible shot out of a hotel drive and cut across the Charger's nose.

Mercedes was standing in the passenger seat with her heels in one hand.

“I told you about my fucking flowers, Aiden!” she shouted.

Porsche drove, red hair blowing against a red dress. Aiden had once seen her leave a party in a sports car a man bought her, while the man stood crying because she actually meant to drive away. Aiden had watched him try to apologize to the taillights.

“Sit down, Mercedes!” Porsche shouted now.

“He owes me!” Mercedes shouted back.

“He owes the whole street right now!” Porsche told her.

Mercedes dropped into the seat. Porsche moved left when the Charger tried left, right when it tried right. Aiden watched her keep the black car behind the red one with quick, exact turns.

“Your friends?” Shawna asked him.

“Good people,” Aiden said.

She looked back and laughed once. A happy sound, brief and startled. He felt it beneath the engine noise.

Then the Charger hit the convertible's rear bumper.

The red car slid sideways. Mercedes grabbed the windshield frame. One silver heel flew from her hand, flashed beneath a streetlight, and landed among tables outside a restaurant.

Aiden braked.

“Aiden!” Shawna cried, bracing the case against her knees.

“Not leaving them,” he told her.

He dropped behind the convertible and put the Mercedes between it and the Charger. In the mirror, the driver's face grew large. A white scar crossed one eyebrow. He bared his teeth.

“Choose me, nigga,” Aiden muttered.

The Charger obliged. The impact drove Aiden's head into the seat. Metal screamed behind them. Shawna caught his arm with her free hand. The rear bumper began dragging against the street.

Ahead, Porsche caught the convertible before it struck a wall. One finger rose above her door. Aiden grinned despite his split lip.

A truck crossed the intersection carrying a silver speedboat. Its bow rode above the cab. Beside the road, the delivery ramp of a closed hotel rose toward a low roof. Aiden looked at the ramp, then at the boat moving across the gap beyond it.

“No,” Shawna said, watching his face.

“You don't know what I'm thinking,” Aiden told her.

“I know exactly what you're thinking,” she said.

He tightened his grip on the wheel. “Then hold the case.”

The ramp hit beneath the car like a fist. His teeth shut. The headlights climbed the wall ahead, then had nothing left to touch.

The Mercedes left the ground.

For an instant the Strip filled the windshield below them, bright rows of hotel windows hanging in the dark. The boat's little flag snapped beneath the hood. Shawna screamed his name. Aiden couldn't get air deep enough to answer. His stomach rose against his ribs while the wheel felt suddenly loose in his hands.

The ground came back with a crash. The seat drove into his spine. A tire burst, and the wheel kicked hard against his thumbs. He fought the turn, caught it, kept the car pointed down the road. Rubber began slapping the pavement.

Behind them, the Charger tried the turn too late. Aiden saw it slide into the bare rear section of the trailer. The impact boomed through their missing window. A black wheel rolled free into the intersection, travelling farther than the car that lost it.

Shawna twisted to look behind them. Nothing followed.

At the next light Porsche drew alongside. Mercedes held her remaining heel like she was looking for somebody large enough to deserve it.

“You jumped a fucking boat!” Porsche shouted across to Aiden.

“Had to get over it somehow,” he called back.

Porsche hit her steering wheel with one palm. “With a car, Aiden!”

A man waiting for a taxi began applauding. The couple beside him joined in. Then a whole line of people put down their luggage and clapped at the wrecked Mercedes.

Aiden lifted a hand. Shawna caught it and put it back on the wheel.

“They can admire you moving,” she told him.

Mercedes pointed at Shawna. “Keep him. He's expensive, but he comes with a show.”

Porsche blew Aiden a kiss and turned away. He took the damaged car toward the desert. The tire struck a steady complaint into the road. Shawna reached over and touched his cheek.

“You could've kept going when they hit the girls,” she said.

“Wouldn't have been me,” Aiden told her.

She watched him for a moment. Her hand moved from his cheek to his thigh and stayed there. He kept both hands on the wheel now without being reminded.

***

Beyond the last casinos, Shawna told him to stop. He said T's place was farther out.

“I know where his fucking house is,” she answered. “Pull over.”

Aiden turned behind a closed wedding chapel. Half its sign had burned out. The surviving bulbs cast pink light across an empty lot and left the chapel windows black. He stopped the car. The engine ticked hard beneath the hood. Through the missing rear glass came desert air and the smell of hot rubber.

Shawna opened the case across her knees.

Money filled it in tight, orderly bundles. Three million dollars, she told him. Aiden stared. That afternoon he'd eaten a gas-station sandwich in a borrowed car. He hadn't considered asking the sandwich whether it knew this was possible.

“T said—” he began.

“T says a lot,” Shawna interrupted.

She took an envelope from beneath the cash. Inside was a photograph folded across the middle. Aiden recognized the chapel behind the couple before he recognized her. Shawna in a white dress. A man with his arm around her waist.

The man's cross was hanging from Aiden's neck.

“Dante,” Shawna said. “T's brother. My husband.”

Aiden looked from the photograph to the building outside. He touched the cross. She had been turning it over at the party, pressing those little letters against his chest, while he tried to make her laugh.

Her lighter sparked twice without catching. He took it, cupped the flame, and held it for her cigarette. She watched the flame a moment before leaning in.

She told him she'd come from Phoenix at twenty-three with eight hundred dollars, a suitcase, and a number somebody said could help her sing. Valentino's number.

Aiden knew the name. White suits. A diamond in one tooth. Hotels full of women watching his hands while men laughed around him.

“Clothes, a room, people he knew in music,” Shawna said, counting the things on the fingers holding her cigarette. “Then every fucking thing he'd given me was something I owed him.”

Aiden watched ash lengthen above the case. She tapped it through the open window.

“Dante came into the club on my birthday,” she continued. “Everybody wanted something. He asked what kind of cake I liked.”

Her mouth almost curved. She looked at the photograph instead. Dante came back the next night, she told Aiden, carrying an entire carrot cake. He set it among the bottles. Valentino laughed at him. Dante knocked out his diamond tooth.

“My nigga,” Aiden said before he could stop himself.

Shawna turned toward him. “That's what I said. Exactly what I fucking said.”

She wiped one eye with her thumb. Six weeks after the cake, they'd married in this chapel. Three years ago. T stood beside his brother. Red paid for the room. Aiden studied the smiling couple in the photograph while she described the wedding around them.

“Then Dante took money from a deal,” she said. “Three million. Said we were leaving. Said he had a brother, so he didn't have to be scared.”

She turned the picture over. On the back were the words Damn, boy. Magnificent.

Aiden opened his hand around the cross. Shawna pointed at the little letters and told him about Dante seeing himself in the chapel mirror after the ceremony. He'd said the words so seriously she nearly dropped her flowers laughing. The next morning she had the initials engraved.

“He was right about you,” Aiden said.

“He was talking about himself,” Shawna corrected him.

Aiden looked down at the gold. “Then he finally got it on the right neck.”

Her laugh came out sharp and bright. She covered her mouth with the hand holding the photograph. For a moment she looked at him without looking past him, and he wanted to keep that moment exactly where it was.

Then she pressed the picture against her chest.

They had left Dante in the desert, she told him. Valentino came with his watch and said Dante died begging somebody to look after her.

Aiden's hand closed on the cross. He wanted Valentino in the car. Wanted enough room to turn and put his hands on the man, and no room at all for Valentino to get away.

Shawna said she'd waited eight months. Let Valentino think she came back because she had nowhere else to go. Let him believe, eventually, that she loved him.

“Last Tuesday I shot him in his car,” she told Aiden. “He was talking. Kept talking after the first shot, so I shot him again.”

She raised her wrist. The watch hung too loose and slid toward her hand.

“Glove compartment,” she said. “That's where he kept it.”

Aiden watched the face catch the chapel's pink light. Red had promised to protect her afterward, she explained. Tonight she'd taken the three million back. T said he could get her out of Vegas.

Aiden remembered T's cologne, his hand on Aiden's neck. Don't fall in love with her. What else had he left out of the assignment?

“You believe him?” Aiden asked.

“I believed his brother,” Shawna said.

She put the photograph inside her dress and shut the case. The catches snapped in the quiet. Then she leaned across and kissed him.

Smoke touched his mouth. Her fingers held his jaw. The money disappeared from his attention. So did the broken car, the promises upstairs at the club, the hands that had put this gold around his neck.

Shawna drew back without letting go of his face.

“Don't take me to that house,” she said.

“Then I'm not taking you,” Aiden told her. “Give me somewhere else.”

***

The motel had six rooms along its upper balcony. Aiden paid for all six. The clerk counted the bills twice, then asked whether he meant the whole floor.

“All of it,” Aiden said. “Nobody comes up.”

The clerk slid the keys across the counter and saved whatever else he might have asked for a cheaper customer.

Shawna set the case on one bed and her pistol on the nightstand. Aiden locked the door. Trucks rolled along the highway beyond the lot. Their noise came through the wall without losing much of itself. The room smelled of old smoke and fresh cleaning fluid, neither quite defeating the other.

Shawna unbuttoned his shirt. Her hands stopped when she saw the bruises over his ribs.

“Jesus, Aiden,” she said.

“I'm good,” he told her.

She touched the edge of the blood on his chest. “You're bleeding.”

“Been bleeding all night,” Aiden said. “Consistent service.”

She wet a towel in the bathroom and cleaned his face. He flinched when she reached his mouth. She apologized; he told her she didn't have to. The water darkened the cloth. He watched her turn it to a clean corner before touching him again.

He lifted the chain over his head and put the cross in her hand.

Her fingers closed. Her mouth trembled, and she tried to turn her face away before he could see. Then the breath she pulled in broke apart. She pressed her forehead against his bare chest.

Aiden held her. The towel lay cold against his back where she had forgotten it in her hand. He could feel her shoulders shaking. There wasn't anybody in the room to threaten. No amount of standing larger would make Dante walk out of the bathroom and ask why she was crying.

So he stood there with his arms around her until her breathing eased.

“Stay,” Shawna said against his chest.

“I'm here,” he answered.

She lifted her face. “Tonight. I mean tonight.”

“Right fucking here,” Aiden told her.

She put the cross safely inside her purse, then drew him down to kiss her. He tasted salt at the corner of her mouth. His shirt slid from his shoulders. She switched off the lamp herself.

After they made love, she lay against him with her head clear of his bruised mouth. Their guns were close enough to reach; the money stayed on the other bed. Blue light from the motel sign pulsed through a gap in the curtains. Each passing truck made the window tremble in its frame.

“What do you want?” Shawna asked.

“You,” Aiden said.

She lifted her head from his chest. “You don't know me.”

He brushed her hair away from his mouth. “Tell me more, then.”

She told him about songs she wanted to sing. Her mother in Phoenix, who thought she worked in a restaurant. The house Dante had wanted near water, although neither of them could swim.

Aiden watched the sign's blue light cross her face. He told her they would buy the house. They could learn to swim. She could have a room for her music, windows where she wanted them. He meant it with an intensity that seemed, by itself, almost enough to produce the deed.

His phone lit up beside her gun.

T.

Aiden let it ring. Shawna watched the white glow until the screen went dark.

Then a photograph arrived: the Mercedes below their balcony, its rear glass broken, its body listing over the damaged tire.

Underneath, T had written: Come down, son.

***

T waited beside the empty pool. Tank stood behind him with two other men. A fence surrounded the cracked blue tiles. The deep end held nothing but shadow.

Aiden came down the stairs with his hands open. His shirt hung loose over his trousers. T was wearing the same clothes as at the club; a little of Aiden's blood still marked one cuff.

“Where's the money?” T asked.

“Upstairs,” Aiden said.

“The girl?” T asked him.

“Upstairs,” Aiden repeated.

T nodded slowly. “One thing. I gave you one fucking thing to do.”

Aiden stopped a few feet away. “She told me about Dante.”

Tank looked toward the pool. T kept watching Aiden.

“Dante stole from his family,” T said.

“He was your brother,” Aiden told him.

“Then he should've known better,” T answered.

Aiden had wanted a lie. Something he could climb inside and call a misunderstanding. He had been wearing the cross less than a night, but already he wanted to keep the man who gave it to him separate from the story Shawna told. T wasn't offering him enough room to do that.

“That woman put him in the ground,” T said. “Now you're ready to follow him.”

“You put him there,” Aiden said.

T stepped closer. The familiar cologne reached Aiden again.

“Who made you?” T asked him. “Who put you in that car? Money, a car, a name in this city. You think she would've looked at you without it?”

Aiden couldn't answer. Every gift changed shape while T named it. The keys in his pocket felt heavier.

“She's upstairs with my three million,” T continued. “That's what she loves.”

Tank shifted behind him. Aiden's eyes moved. T noticed.

“Last chance,” T said. “Get her.”

The door opened above them. Aiden looked up.

Shawna stood barefoot on the balcony with the silver case resting against the rail.

“You want your money?” she called down to T.

T raised his face toward her.

She threw the case.

It hit the dry pool and burst. Bundles scattered across the tiles. Loose bills lifted in the night air, drifted onto the steps, and settled along the empty gutter. Aiden watched a piece of paper worth more than his dinner slide under a broken chair.

“There,” Shawna told T. “Now tell him what I love.”

T pulled his pistol.

Aiden struck his arm as the shot went off. A window shattered above them. Tank grabbed Aiden from behind, locking his arms against his sides. One of the other men stepped in swinging. Aiden drove his forehead into the man's face. Pain opened over his eyebrow.

He twisted hard. Tank's grip loosened enough for him to free an arm. He shoved the big man into the fence. Wire rang against the posts.

Shawna fired from the balcony. One of T's men dropped. The other recoiled from the stairs.

T pushed the muzzle into Aiden's side.

The shot struck inside him before the noise seemed to arrive.

His knees loosened. Breath stopped halfway in. T's face remained close and clear while the lights beyond it began to move away. Heat spread beneath Aiden's shirt.

“Should've listened,” T said.

Aiden caught the gun wrist with both hands. T wasn't lifting that pistol toward the balcony. Whatever his body was trying to do, it could finish this first.

Tank struck him again. Aiden felt the movement more than the pain. He drove his knee into T, freed one hand, and drew his own pistol from his waistband.

For one stupid instant, he still wanted T to stop. Let go. Become the man who kissed his forehead and called him son.

He fired.

T fell against the fence. Aiden fired again. T's hand remained lifted toward him as he slid down. The wire stopped shaking.

Tank stared at the body, then at Aiden's pistol.

“Aiden—” Tank began.

“Back up,” Aiden told him. His voice sounded far away. “Tank, back the fuck up.”

Tank stepped away.

Shawna ran down the stairs with the silver pistol in her hand and her purse knocking against her hip. She saw the blood and nearly slipped on the last step. She pushed the pistol into the purse, then pressed both palms against Aiden's side before he could say anything reassuring.

He tried anyway. Nothing came out.

“Baby. Look at me,” she said, putting her face where T's had been. “Look at me.”

Then she turned on Tank.

“Get the fucking car!” Shawna shouted.

Tank looked down once more. Then he ran for the Mercedes.

Aiden tried to keep his eyes on Shawna. The man who'd called him son lay dead at his feet. Dante's widow was holding him together with both hands. Money moved in the empty pool whenever the air stirred. Nobody bent to pick it up.

***

Tank brought the car close enough to the pool fence that Shawna could help Aiden into the passenger seat. She pressed a towel from the room against his side and put his hands over it. Then she ran around the hood and took the wheel.

The money stayed in the pool. Aiden could see it through the open door until Tank shut him inside.

On the road, Shawna kept one hand on the wheel and reached for the towel whenever she could. He held it when she needed both hands to steer. The cloth grew heavy between his fingers. The ruined tire hammered the road with an uneven beat.

“Stay with me,” Shawna said.

“I'm here,” Aiden told her.

She looked toward him, then sharply back at the road. “Keep talking.”

He told her about the house. The room for her music. Learning to swim. His mouth tasted of blood, and he had to stop between sentences to find enough breath. Shawna was crying. He couldn't do anything about the blood, but he could keep describing the windows.

“Which way?” she asked him, wiping one cheek against her shoulder. “The windows, baby.”

“Sunrise,” Aiden said. “They'll face the sunrise.”

At the edge of town she pulled over. She opened her purse, took out the cross he'd given her, and leaned across the console to fasten it around his neck. Her fingers shook against the clasp.

“Don't,” he said. “That's his.”

“I know,” Shawna told him.

She closed his hand around it, kissed his mouth, and got behind the wheel again.

Gray light was entering the desert. Aiden watched the watch slide down her wrist as she turned the car back onto the road. His phone rang. Stopped. Rang again. The screen was too bright, the names difficult to keep still.

He answered the third call.

“Aiden,” he said.

The man on the other end went quiet. Aiden recognized a voice from the club. After a moment the man asked what he wanted them to do.

Shawna's jaw was tight as she watched the road. Her hand reached toward the towel beneath his. Aiden forced enough air into his chest to finish what he needed to say.

“Somebody find me a doctor,” he told the man. “And tell everybody the girl is with me.”
