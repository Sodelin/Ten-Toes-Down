## 9. Our Valedictorian Has a Gun

Aiden was deciding which pistol went with breakfast when Tank brought the cream envelope into the kitchen.

A gold crest shone above his name. Somebody had given the capital A more attention than Aiden had given certain relationships.

“School,” Tank said, laying it beside the plate.

“Whose?” Aiden asked.

“Yours,” Tank told him.

Aiden put one pistol down. “I got a school?”

Shawna stopped pouring coffee. Rosa turned from the counter. Mercedes, still occupying the guest room and now occupying its owner's breakfast, lowered her toast.

“Las Vegas College,” Tank read.

“Oh,” Aiden said. “Those people.”

Shawna set the pot down. “You went to college?”

“Went to the building,” Aiden told her.

“When?” she asked.

“Twenty-two,” he said. “Free lunch. Excellent chicken.”

Tank opened the second page.

“Says valedictorian,” he told Aiden.

Aiden nodded. “Paying attention.”

Rosa reached for the pages. “Give me that.”

“Don't wrinkle my achievement,” Aiden warned her.

“Looking for the payment demand,” Rosa said. “Mercedes, check whether he's on a milk carton too. Been somewhere two years and forgot.”

Mercedes leaned over Rosa's shoulder.

“Highest marks in the college's history,” she read aloud.

Tank looked up. “Marks in what?”

“History,” Aiden told him. “She just said.”

Shawna turned his face gently toward hers.

“Baby, what did you do to these people?” she asked.

He thought about the chicken.

Two years earlier, a friend told him the college's adult scholarship day served food. The program took people twenty-two and older. Aiden was exactly twenty-two and hungry enough to have a future if it came with a plate.

The registration woman told him lunch was for applicants. He became one before she finished the word. She gave him a booklet: mathematics, reading, music with something wrong on the page, a question about two promises that couldn't both be kept.

He remembered eating and writing. Correcting the music while waiting for another piece of chicken. Taking two rolls when he handed everything in. He explained this to the kitchen.

Nobody spoke for a moment.

“You ate a degree,” Tank said.

“Thought while I chewed,” Aiden told him. “Acting like it's forbidden technology.”

Mercedes turned the invitation over. “They want you there at eleven.”

Shawna checked the clock. Nine twenty.

“We need clothes,” she said.

“Got plans,” Aiden reminded her.

Porsche entered carrying a garment bag, sunglasses still on.

“Your plans just got a hat,” she said. “You're going.”

Aiden tapped the blue gala invitation beside his plate. “Red.”

“Two nights away,” Porsche told him. “Graduation's in a hundred minutes. You can count that far.”

Tank lifted a finger. “He has the highest—”

“Don't help,” Rosa told him.

Shawna kissed Aiden. “I want to see my man graduate.”

He looked at her smiling above him. She could have asked him to graduate from the moon with that face.

“Get dressed,” he said, standing carefully. “We're going to college.”

Tank looked at his plate.

“Bring it,” Aiden told him. “Apparently that's the entrance exam.”

***

Tank drove the Mercedes. In the mirror, Aiden watched Porsche follow in Leon's newest Bentley, dark green this time. Leon had sent a note explaining that white brought back memories. Mercedes held a camera in the passenger seat. Behind them came Rosa in yellow bright enough to underfund the campus flowers, and Marcellus wearing the clean shirt she'd helped choose a tie for.

Shawna wore white again. Aiden preferred this occasion for it. He'd chosen black trousers and a jacket that covered the bandages, his shirt open enough for the cross. The pistol sat beneath the jacket, clear of the wound.

Graduates in blue gowns crowded the campus gates. Grown people carried bouquets and checked their phones, trying to arrange themselves for photographs before their relatives arranged them worse.

As Tank stopped near the fountain, Aiden heard a man point him out.

“That's him,” the man said to his friend. “Mercedes nigga.”

His friend looked up from a program. “It's a college, Marcus. Seventeen Mercedes in the parking lot.”

“Flying Mercedes,” Marcus clarified.

The friend looked properly, then looked back at the program and struck Marcus's shoulder.

“Our valedictorian?” he asked.

“That's what I'm saying,” Marcus told him.

By the time Aiden got out, people around the fountain were turning. A woman came down the steps with a blue robe folded over her arms. Silver glasses, dark skin, hair pinned with a precision that made Aiden straighten his own collar.

“Professor Yvonne Halcyon,” she said, reaching him. “We met at your admission.”

“You had the chicken,” Aiden told her.

Her smile widened. “You remember.”

“Don't forget a good plate,” he said.

She gave him the robe. “We've been looking for you.”

“Been around,” Aiden said.

Halcyon glanced at the Mercedes. “We've seen.”

Principal Percival Mercer came down behind her in academic blue with enough gold to make Aiden wonder whether a crown came with it. He had a gray beard and a deep voice that failed him on the first attempt at Aiden's name. His eyes filled before they shook hands.

“At last,” Mercer said.

Tank leaned close to Aiden. “What the fuck was in that booklet?”

“Apparently me,” Aiden said.

Mercer took his hand in both of his.

“Your work changed this college,” the principal told him.

“Which part?” Aiden asked.

“The final page,” Mercer said.

Aiden tried to remember. “Blank one?”

“You corrected the examination on it,” Mercer explained.

“There was a mistake,” Aiden said.

“Six,” Mercer told him. “We'd found two in twenty years.”

He opened a folder. A copy of Aiden's impatient handwriting lay inside, complete with the grease mark from lunch. Mercer said they'd framed the original.

Halcyon explained that the scholarship board had accepted Aiden's independent work in place of the standard course sequence. His essays had circulated through four departments. The music analysis had repaired the conservatory's examination. Professor Lorne had taught his final solution since the previous spring.

“Hope Lorne has a nice voice,” Aiden said.

A man behind Halcyon raised a hand. “I do my best.”

Shawna turned to Aiden, her eyes bright.

“You didn't tell me any of this,” she said.

“Didn't know they kept it,” he told her.

A graduate nearby took off his cap and laughed into it.

“Came every night for three years,” the graduate said.

Aiden looked at him. “You're here, aren't you?”

The man straightened. “Yeah.”

“Come on, then,” Aiden told him. “We're graduating.”

The graduate put his cap back on and joined them walking toward the buildings.

***

Mercer insisted on taking Aiden through the science hall on the way to the auditorium. Gold letters above the entrance announced AIDEN HALL. Aiden stopped beneath them while Tank raised his phone.

“Got a hall,” Aiden told Shawna.

“Several,” Mercer corrected him.

Rosa turned on the principal. “Don't tell him. He'll name the doors.”

Inside, three professors stood over a long table covered in papers. Their discussion stopped in pieces as the visitors entered. Lorne picked up a drawing and looked hopefully at Aiden.

“Perhaps you could settle something,” he said.

“He has a ceremony,” Halcyon reminded him.

“One minute,” Lorne promised.

Aiden came to the table. “Everybody needs one minute until I get there. Show me.”

It was a drawing of the auditorium roof. Lines crossed beneath columns of figures. Someone had circled the same span three times in red. Lorne explained that the model produced two different answers.

Aiden looked through the window at the auditorium, then back at the paper.

“Old roof,” he said.

Lorne touched a line. “The dimensions—”

“Changed when they moved the stage,” Aiden told him. “That window's eight panels wide. You've drawn seven.”

All three professors looked outside.

Aiden took the pencil. He moved the line, followed the load across the corrected drawing, and changed the two numbers that no longer needed to fight.

Lorne read what he'd written. The professor beside him compared it with another sheet and sat so suddenly his chair rolled backward.

“He's right,” the seated man said.

“Say it like you enjoyed finding out,” Aiden told him.

The professor looked up. “He's right!”

The room laughed. Mercer began crying again.

Aiden turned to Shawna. “Your man got a roof.”

She caught the edge of his robe. “Get under it before they name another building.”

At the door, Lorne touched Aiden's arm gently.

“What do you do for a living?” Lorne asked.

“Improves transportation,” Tank answered before Aiden could.

***

The auditorium was full. A choir in white stood on risers behind the stage. Blue banners hung beneath the balcony. Halcyon told Aiden the youngest graduate was twenty-two, the oldest sixty-eight. Looking into the room, he could see what a long day each family had dressed for: flowers wrapped in paper, phone cameras held ready, hands carefully smoothing somebody else's gown.

His people took the front row. Shawna sat in the middle. Tank chose the aisle and lowered himself into a chair that looked surprised to receive him.

Knox waited beside the stage.

“Following me to school now?” Aiden asked her.

“Invited,” she said.

“Graduating?” he asked.

“Recruiting the valedictorian,” Knox told him.

Behind her, something low and wide stood under a dark cloth on the stage's display area. Aiden looked at the shape beneath the fabric. That nose. Those wheels. A Lamborghini could wear a bedsheet to church and he would still recognize it.

Knox watched his face.

“Speech first,” she said.

“Cruel, Chief,” Aiden told her.

“Motivation,” Knox answered.

She went to join Mercer. Aiden took his place near the curtain while the ceremony began. Names rose through the microphone. People crossed the stage, taking careful steps in shoes chosen for the photograph. Applause kept arriving from different parts of the room.

Shawna looked back toward him. Each time, he felt himself stand taller. Two nights until the gala. For now, a whole college was preparing to give him a microphone on purpose.

The lights flickered.

Aiden looked up. A man in a gray suit stood in the upper aisle, watching the doors instead of the ceremony. Beneath the balcony, another man waited beside an exit. Their jackets hung with a shape Aiden knew better than his own curriculum.

He caught Knox's eye and looked toward the balcony.

She followed his gaze.

The man below drew his pistol as flame climbed the curtain behind the choir.

***

Blue cloth turned orange. A choir member screamed. The gunman by the exit fired into the ceiling, and the audience tried to stand all at once.

Aiden grabbed the microphone.

“Off the stairs!” he shouted. “Get low! Heads down!”

His voice filled the room over the gunshot's echo. Knox fired from beside the stage. The man at the exit fell behind a row of seats. Her officers moved toward him. Tank pulled their front row down while Shawna dragged Rosa clear of the aisle.

Above them, the balcony gunman raised his weapon.

Aiden drew and fired. The man struck the rail and dropped behind it. His pistol fell into the empty aisle below.

The choir was coming off its platform through smoke. Aiden set the microphone on the lectern and holstered his pistol before running to the steps. A woman with a cane had stopped halfway down, unable to manage the next drop while people pressed behind her.

“Give me your arm,” he told her.

She caught his shoulder. Another chorister took her other side. Together they carried her down to the stage while sparks settled on the white robes behind them.

Mercer stood by the lectern holding Aiden's diploma against his chest.

“Principal, move!” Aiden shouted.

Mercer stared through the smoke. “Your degree—”

“Need somebody alive to sign it!” Aiden told him.

The principal moved. A burning crossbeam from the hanging decorations fell across a side exit. Wood broke. Sparks burst upward. The doorway disappeared behind it.

Aiden looked toward the main aisle. Overturned seats blocked the front rows from the doors at the back. People farther away were escaping, but those near the stage had no clear route through the jam. Behind the choir, the fire had reached the loading doors.

He looked at the covered car.

Knox was already pulling off the cloth.

Dark blue paint. A gold shield on each door. Low black wheels. Lights across the windshield. Aiden stared for a second longer than the emergency required.

Knox threw him the keys.

“Don't scratch it,” she said.

“Don't limit me,” Aiden answered.

Tank reached him as he opened the door.

“Driving in the school?” Tank asked.

“Improving the exit,” Aiden told him.

Tank looked across the blocked aisle. “Went to the wrong fucking college.”

He dragged the nearest overturned seats away from the bumper. Knox and her officers moved people toward the side walls, shouting for them to clear the central aisle. Aiden watched the last crouching graduate crawl out of the car's path before he lowered himself behind the wheel.

The engine's roar cut through the screaming. Heads turned toward it. Shawna stood at the edge of the stage holding Mercer's arm. Her hand trembled against his sleeve. She looked from the fire to the car and found Aiden through the windshield.

Then she smiled at him.

He drove.

The Lamborghini crossed the display area, traversed the stage, and took the shallow steps with a hard drop that went straight into Aiden's side. The bumper struck the jammed seats. Wood snapped. Chairs scraped outward across the cleared floor. He kept the nose pointed at the distant doors.

A curtain fell across the hood. Flame washed over the windshield. Orange filled his sight for one second, then the fabric slid away and the far exit appeared ahead.

He pressed down.

Both doors tore from their hinges. Daylight struck his eyes. The car bounced down the broad front steps and landed in the courtyard with its siren screaming.

Aiden braked, got out, and ran back toward the opening. People were coming through behind him: choir members first, graduates supporting other graduates, Tank carrying the woman with the cane. Rosa and Mercedes shoved a loose bench away from the route. Porsche stood near the doors with an extinguisher in each hand, directing people around the smoke like she owned the available air.

Knox emerged with Mercer. Shawna followed, one hand over her mouth and Aiden's cap in the other.

“Baby!” she called.

He caught her at the top of the steps. She hit his good shoulder with the cap, then set it on his head.

“Nearly forgot something,” Shawna told him.

“Making an entrance,” Aiden said.

“Made an exit,” she corrected him.

“Great man does both,” he told her.

She kissed him while the courtyard filled with people.

Fire crews arrived. Officers brought the attackers out in cuffs, one limping, the other holding a bloodied sleeve against his arm. They passed near the Lamborghini as Aiden adjusted his cap.

“We burned the building,” the first attacker said to the other.

“He came through it,” the second man replied.

“With a police car,” the first insisted.

“I saw,” the second told him.

Aiden watched the officers lead them away. They'd come to ruin the morning and were now reviewing it together.

He turned toward Mercer.

“Got a microphone out here?” Aiden asked.

***

They moved the lectern into the courtyard. Mercer stood beside it with wet cheeks while Halcyon rested a hand on his back. Lorne looked from the smoke rising above the auditorium to the drawing still folded in his hand.

Graduates gathered in front of Aiden. Soot marked blue gowns. Some people held shoes. Nearly everybody held somebody's hand.

He leaned into the microphone.

“Aight,” Aiden said. “Where we at?”

Laughter rolled toward him, loud and uneven. Tank stood behind Shawna with his arms folded. Marcellus had a hand on Rosa's shoulder. Mercedes lifted her camera.

“I wasn't here much,” Aiden admitted.

“Not here at all!” a graduate called back.

“Look what happened the morning I came,” Aiden told him. “You're welcome.”

A man in the third row waved his cap overhead. The laughter loosened into cheering. Aiden waited until he could hear the microphone humming again.

He looked at the adults before him. People who'd come back to classrooms after work, after failures, after whoever told them to stop trying. He had watched them carrying one another minutes ago. He didn't need an examination to explain what that meant.

“You came for your name,” Aiden said. “Worked all night and came anyway. Some of you had people saying this wasn't your kind of room. Some got somebody missing today who ought to be here making too much fucking noise.”

Shawna looked down at her watch, then back at him.

“But you're here,” Aiden continued. “When the doors caught fire, you started carrying each other. Saw it myself. That part belongs to you. Keep it. Don't let anybody make you little enough to forget.”

He touched the cross.

“Three letters on this,” he told them. “D. B. M.”

Tank's eyebrows lifted. Shawna began smiling before Aiden explained.

“Damn, boy. Magnificent,” he said.

The courtyard shouted back. Aiden spread his free hand toward the gowns.

“You too,” he told them. “Every grown man and woman standing here with a cap and a dream. Look in the mirror tomorrow and say it properly. Don't mumble greatness in your own house.”

“Say it again!” Mercedes called.

“Damn!” Aiden shouted into the microphone.

The graduates answered with boy. He raised both arms.

“Magnificent!” Aiden called.

They shouted the word back loudly enough to lift the pigeons from Aiden Hall. He watched the birds scatter above the gold letters while Mercer put the diploma into his hand.

The principal was still crying. Aiden had driven through a burning auditorium and delivered the speech before Mercer finished the first breakdown.

Education was going beautifully.

***

Knox came beside the lectern. Two officers carried a blue velvet box; another brought a dark uniform jacket with gold braid. The talking quieted in stages as people turned toward them.

“You've made this offer difficult to postpone,” Knox told Aiden.

“Busy graduating,” he said.

“You've also protected this city's people,” she said. “And shown a relationship with automobiles I've never seen in a man who still has all his limbs.”

Porsche applauded first.

Knox opened the box. A gold captain's badge lay inside. Aiden looked at it, then at her face.

“Captain Aiden,” Knox announced. “Las Vegas Police Department. Actual command. Actual badge. Actual trouble for anybody who thinks otherwise.”

Tank bent forward with both hands on his knees. Rosa caught his arm.

“You all right?” she asked.

“This nigga's a cop,” Tank said.

“Heard,” Rosa told him.

Tank shook his head. “My nigga's the police.”

“Stand up before he arrests your posture,” Rosa said.

Knox asked Aiden to raise his right hand. He did. She gave him the oath in short phrases, and he repeated each one: keep faith with his people, stand when somebody needed standing for, use the badge's authority with his whole name behind it.

He said the words before the chief, the officers, the graduates, and Shawna. Then Knox pinned the badge inside his jacket.

“Captain,” she said.

Aiden looked down at the gold. His mother would have needed to sit down twice.

Shawna came up beside him, took his face in both hands, and kissed him slowly. Officers began applauding before the graduates joined them.

“My man,” she whispered.

“Your captain,” Aiden told her.

“Still my man,” Shawna said.

He could have stood there letting her settle the rank all day.

Knox put the Lamborghini keys back in his hand. From the lectern, he could see a scratch along one door. The hood was blackened by smoke, but the lights still flashed across the windshield.

“Yours,” Knox told him.

“Noticed,” Aiden said.

She leaned close enough to lower her voice.

“Red's people thought they owned every uniform that mattered,” Knox told him. “Let him see yours.”

Aiden looked directly at her. She smiled.

“Know who I recruited, Captain,” she said.

He drove away with the diploma on Shawna's lap and the badge inside his jacket. Tank followed in the original Mercedes. The other cars came behind, horns sounding along the campus road.

At the fountain, Marcus raised his cap.

“Our nigga!” Marcus shouted.

“Our captain!” his friend called beside him.

Aiden touched the siren for both of them. Shawna rested her hand on his leg as the gates passed behind the car.

“What are you doing tomorrow?” she asked.

He looked at her, then at the open road.

“People better not schedule anything they can't afford,” Aiden told her.
