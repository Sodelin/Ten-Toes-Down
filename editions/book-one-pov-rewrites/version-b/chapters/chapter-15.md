## 15. Ten Toes, Thirty Engines

By eleven, headlights filled the street outside the lake house.

Aiden stood just inside the broken door and watched the men come up the path. Leather jackets. Silk shirts. Coats borrowed in a hurry from people with different shoulders. One man had shaving foam behind his ear. Another wore pajamas patterned with little white moons, a gold chain caught between the buttons.

Tank looked down at the pajamas.

“Asleep?” Tank asked.

“Participating in my relationship,” the man replied.

“Relationship's open at the bottom,” Tank told him.

The man buttoned his shirt without stopping.

“Where she at?” he asked Aiden.

“Red has her,” Aiden said.

The man's hands stopped on the buttons.

“Aight,” he said. “What we need?”

Aiden moved aside to let him in.

He couldn't know why every man had come. A few had worked for T long enough to prefer another person standing in Aiden's place. Some might have answered Red's call just as quickly. There were men coming through the door who couldn't be trusted with an unlocked refrigerator.

But they had come.

The man with shaving foam stopped over the broken glass.

“Red do that?” he asked.

“I did,” Aiden said.

The man nodded. “About to get separately mad.”

“Stay with the original mad,” Aiden told him. “Deadline.”

The man wiped his ear on his sleeve and went inside. Aiden watched someone he'd once fought move a chair so Marcellus could sit. Another man picked glass out of Rosa's path before her shoe reached it.

Better men could arrive in the morning and explain their schedules. These were here.

***

Tank waited beside the dining table until the room had filled. The bags stood open in front of him.

“Money from the motel,” Tank told the gathering. “I took it.”

Conversations stopped. A man beside the piano leaned forward.

“You did what?” the man asked.

Tank kept his eyes on the room.

“All of it,” Tank said. “Let Aiden believe it was gone. Red found out. Tried to use it against him tonight.”

“Tried?” a man near the windows asked.

“Tried,” Tank repeated.

The man by the piano looked toward Aiden.

“Still breathing?” he asked, pointing at Tank.

Aiden stepped between them.

“Look at me when you say that,” Aiden told him.

The man lowered his hand.

“Tank stands with me,” Aiden said.

“Just saying—” the man began.

“Say he stole,” Aiden interrupted. “Did. Say he lied. Did that too. Say you're touching him tonight, find out how fast I forget your name.”

The room went still. Behind him, Tank didn't speak.

The man by the piano lifted both hands.

“Your house,” he said.

“Yeah,” Aiden answered. “My house. Money on my table. My girl with Red. Any of that bother you, door's already broke. Won't hurt its feelings leaving.”

Nobody left.

Rosa stood beside the stairs in the borrowed coat, studying faces. Marcellus sat behind her. The papers were inside his jacket; the closed blue box rested on his knees. He looked tired enough that Aiden wanted to offer him a bed, but the old man caught his eye and shook his head before he could.

Aiden put his good hand on the money.

“Some of you owe me,” he told the room. “Some think Red owes you. Some heard there'd be money.”

“All three,” a man at the back said.

Aiden looked at him. The man stood by his answer.

“Good,” Aiden said. “Knows why he came.”

He began putting stacks into hands.

He didn't throw them. Each man had to come to the table, take the weight, and look at him. The woman they were going to bring home had carried this money out before any of these people arrived to speak about courage. He wanted them to remember whose absence had opened the bags.

Cash went into coats and trouser pockets. The man in pajamas packed bundles inside his shirt until the little moons strained against their buttons.

“Get a bag,” Tank told him.

“Good,” the man said.

“Look pregnant,” Tank replied.

The man held the shirt closed. “Good.”

Aiden kept paying. Some men counted at once. Some laughed. One only held the stack between his palms. Aiden recognized him from a hospital corridor, sitting with his head in his hands. Tonight he kissed the money before putting it away.

Aiden didn't ask what waited at home for it. He needed a woman brought back to his own house. There could be room for more than one desperate thing on the table.

He and Tank had separated the bundles by their bands. When two million lay distributed or set aside for the people in the room, Aiden stopped. The last million remained together inside one bag.

“Hers,” Aiden told them, pulling the zipper shut.

No one argued.

Tank hadn't taken his allotted share. Aiden picked it up from the money already set aside and held it toward him.

Tank shook his head. “No.”

“Take it before I love you louder,” Aiden told him.

“Aiden,” Tank said.

“Poor and guilty?” Aiden asked. “Giving you one bad feeling. Pick.”

Tank glanced at the watching men.

“Don't want it,” Tank said.

“Spend it on somebody who does,” Aiden answered.

He pressed it against Tank's chest until the big man took it. Tank looked down, then placed the money beside the closed bag.

“For her,” Tank said.

Aiden left it there. He wasn't going to count the gesture back out while everybody watched.

Rosa stepped away from the staircase.

“Before you start singing about brotherhood,” Rosa told the room, “she's my friend.”

Several men straightened. The one holding his pajama shirt stopped smiling.

“See her, bring her,” Rosa said. “Don't stop to tell Red how hard you are. Don't explain a grudge from 2018. Bring her.”

The man who'd arrived with shaving foam nodded.

“Got you, Rosa,” he said.

“Better have her,” Rosa answered.

Aiden pushed Rosa's share across the table. It came from the two million already separated, not the bag he had closed.

She looked down.

“What is that?” Rosa asked him.

“Yours,” Aiden said.

“For what?”

“Whatever you want,” he told her.

Her face tightened. “Paying me to stay home?”

“Because she loves you,” Aiden said.

Rosa looked up sharply.

“Don't do that right now,” she told him.

“When she gets out that car,” Aiden said, “want somebody here she knew before every room had me in it.”

Rosa closed one hand around the borrowed coat. He could see how tired she was now. One earring gone. Dress marked by the gala. Her face held still with visible effort.

“Bring her,” Rosa said.

Aiden nodded.

She slapped his cheek. The sound stopped a whisper at the back of the room.

“With your mouth, Aiden,” Rosa told him.

He swallowed.

“Bringing her,” Aiden said.

Rosa kissed the same cheek.

“Go, baby,” she told him.

Aiden turned toward the men.

“Anybody think Red owns Vegas?” he asked.

“Fuck Red,” someone near the door answered.

“Anybody think he owns my wife?” Aiden asked.

Rosa made a sound behind him. He turned halfway back.

“What?” Aiden asked her.

“Asked her yet?” Rosa said.

“Going to.”

“Quit inviting these men before me,” Rosa told him.

Laughter moved through the room. Men called out wedding advice, named people who couldn't be allowed near the bar, argued about suits for a ceremony nobody had agreed to hold. Aiden let them have a few seconds. The future had become large enough to stand among them.

Then he raised his hand.

“Listen,” Aiden said.

They quieted.

“Not asking you to die because I fell in love,” he told them. “Asking you to stand with me because he thinks seeing something makes it his. Took a man's wife. Took another man's stone. Had Dante taken into the desert and left there. Tonight, took Shawna.”

He closed his fingers around the cross through his shirt.

“Keeps calling this dead man's jewelry,” Aiden said. “Fine. Let him look at it while I get my girl.”

Tank came to stand beside him.

“Ten toes,” Tank called.

“Down,” the room answered.

Tank raised his voice. “Ten toes.”

The men answered louder. “Down.”

The damaged door rattled in its frame. Aiden could feel the voices against his chest. He wanted them to reach every room Red owned.

He brought the cross to his mouth, kissed it, and went outside.

***

Tank put the closed bag in the Mercedes's back seat, adding his returned share before closing it again. The rescue money was now the reserved million plus what Tank had given back. Red had demanded three million. He was getting what remained after the men coming for him had been paid.

Aiden took the front passenger seat. His side wouldn't let him settle comfortably against the backrest. His wrapped hand lay in his lap beside the phone.

Marcellus opened the rear door.

Aiden turned toward him. “Sure?”

“No,” Marcellus answered.

“Can stay with Rosa.”

“Waited enough,” the old man said.

He got in and shut the door. The box rested beside him. The letters and records stayed inside his coat.

Rosa came to Aiden's open window. She had left her money on the dining table.

“Gardenias,” Rosa told him.

He looked at her.

“For the wedding,” she said. “Likes gardenias. Will lie and tell you roses because men always buy roses.”

Aiden gripped the door.

“Gardenias,” he repeated.

“And don't have that old man give her away,” Rosa added. “I'll do it.”

Marcellus leaned toward the window from behind Aiden.

“Hadn't volunteered,” Marcellus said.

Rosa pointed at him. “Keep it that way.”

She stepped back. Tank eased the car down the drive.

More than thirty engines waited along the street. Aiden recognized Rex in a black coupe and Little Saint on a motorcycle with an unpainted tank. Saint called through the open window that he'd borrowed it. Aiden looked at the bare metal and hoped its owner had an interesting definition of a return policy.

Mercedes waved from the green Bentley beside Porsche. At the head of the gathering, Bell sat behind the wheel of the returned police Lamborghini, with Knox in the passenger seat. The chief lifted two fingers in acknowledgment before the dark blue car moved out.

Tank followed. Headlights filled both mirrors.

For several blocks nobody spoke. Vegas passed from tall lights to low signs and gas stations. Beyond those, the highway ran into darkness.

“Doctor after,” Tank said at last.

“After,” Aiden agreed.

“Saying it now.”

“Heard you,” Aiden told him.

Tank nodded. Both hands stayed on the wheel.

Aiden looked at the cross.

“When you went back to the pool,” he asked Tank, “T look like himself?”

Tank drove several seconds before answering.

“No,” he said.

“Good,” Aiden said.

Tank's fingers tightened around the wheel.

“Don't have to say that,” Tank told him.

“Know,” Aiden replied.

The dashboard lit the side of Tank's face. Aiden remembered him standing behind T at the initiation, waiting for orders. Now the road ahead belonged to a decision he'd made himself.

“Glad you're here,” Aiden said.

Tank's jaw moved.

“Yeah,” he answered.

“Don't make me say it twice.”

“Heard you,” Tank said.

In the back seat, Marcellus turned the closed box between his hands.

“Celeste wore blue the last time I took her dancing,” Marcellus said. “Was late. She'd danced with three other men by the time I arrived.”

Aiden turned halfway toward him.

“Three?” Aiden asked.

“Counted them for me,” Marcellus said.

Tank looked in the mirror.

“Did something to them?” Tank asked.

“Asked for the fourth dance,” Marcellus answered.

Aiden looked out at the black desert.

“Could've done that myself,” Aiden said.

“Didn't,” Marcellus replied gently.

The old man rested against the seat. Aiden let the words stay with him through the next stretch of road.

***

At eleven fifty-four, Red called.

Aiden put the phone on speaker.

“Coming?” Red asked.

“Hear the cars?” Aiden answered.

“Told you to come ready to apologize,” Red said.

“Am,” Aiden told him.

Tank glanced over.

“Told her I'd never let you touch her again,” Aiden continued. “Got plenty to apologize for.”

Red breathed into the line.

“Think all those men love you?” Red asked.

“Enough to leave the house,” Aiden said.

“Money,” Red told him.

“Should've paid better,” Aiden answered.

Silence followed. Aiden heard Shawna somewhere behind the phone. He couldn't make out the words, only the sound of her voice before Red moved away from her.

“Let me talk to her,” Aiden said.

“See her soon,” Red answered.

“Let me—” Aiden began.

The call ended. He gripped the phone until Tank reached over and took it from his hand. Tank put it in the console, screen up.

Ahead, past the last gas station, headlights shone across the road instead of along it.

Two trucks blocked the highway. Men stood beside them without uniforms. Between the trucks, flames rose from a row of barrels. The glare hid part of the road beyond.

Tank slowed. Behind them, the engines changed pitch together.

Knox's name appeared on the phone. Aiden answered on speaker again.

“See them,” Knox said. “Stay in the convoy.”

“Convoy is me,” Aiden told her.

“Then don't make me explain a road accident to your wife before you reach her,” Knox answered.

A shot struck the Mercedes's hood.

Tank pulled left. Another bullet starred the windshield in front of him. Aiden ducked. In the back seat, Marcellus dropped below the windows, pulling his coat closed over the papers and drawing the box down with him.

Bell brought the Lamborghini alongside them. Through its windshield Aiden saw Knox leaning forward, her eyes fixed on the roadblock. The siren rose.

One of the men stepped backward. Another raised his gun anyway.

“Hardheaded motherfucker,” Aiden muttered.

Tank accelerated off the road.

Dust struck the windows. The Mercedes dropped into a shallow ditch and came out hard enough to throw Aiden upward against his seat belt. His head clipped the roof. Blood touched his tongue.

Behind them the line spread across the desert. Cars swept to both sides of the trucks, headlights bouncing over scrub and loose stone. Red's men had blocked a road. Thirty engines were making an argument about how much ground remained.

Rex's black coupe came past on the right. Little Saint rode beside it, standing slightly above his motorcycle seat over the rough ground. The green Bentley emerged through the dust with its roof down. Mercedes held her hair away from her face in one hand and a pistol in the other.

“Keep him down!” Mercedes shouted, pointing toward Marcellus.

“Old man is down,” Marcellus called from below the rear window. “Thoroughly fucking down.”

Aiden laughed before he could help it. The laugh hurt, but it was his. Red had not managed to take every sound out of the car.

The nearer truck began moving.

Its driver turned off the highway toward the cars passing on that side. Rex braked. Little Saint swerved clear. The truck's front wheel climbed the edge of a ditch, bringing its huge nose across the coupe's escape route.

Aiden could see Rex looking up through his windshield. Saint had turned his bike back toward him.

“Tank,” Aiden said.

“See it,” Tank answered.

“Believe you this time,” Aiden told him.

Tank drove into the truck's rear corner.

The impact kicked the Mercedes sideways. Aiden's shoulder struck the door; pain tore across his side. The truck's rear swung out in the loose dirt, pulling its nose away from Rex long enough for the coupe to get clear.

Little Saint shot through the opening beside it and raised a fist.

“Aiden!” Saint called.

“Tank!” Aiden shouted back.

Tank glanced at him. “What?”

“Giving your fucking credit,” Aiden told him. “Enjoy it.”

Tank grinned despite his swollen lip.

The truck stopped against a low rise. Its driver jumped from the cab with a gun, looked at the cars surrounding him, and dropped it into the dirt.

Bell braked beside him. Knox got out with her weapon raised. Tank had slowed close enough for Aiden to hear her through his open window.

“Blocked my captain's road,” Knox told the driver.

The man stared at her uniform.

“Your captain?” he asked.

Knox pointed at Aiden. “That one.”

The driver turned. His face fell with an honesty Aiden almost appreciated.

“Didn't say it was him,” the man told Knox.

“What part of thirty cars looked like a small mistake?” Aiden shouted from the Mercedes.

Rex pulled beside them with his window lowered. Blood still marked his eyebrow.

“Three times,” Rex told Aiden.

“That was Tank,” Aiden answered.

Rex looked at the driver. “Owe the family, then.”

Tank lifted two fingers from the wheel. He made no effort at modesty, and Aiden was glad of it.

Knox's officers moved around the trucks. The second crew had run. Aiden saw four flashlight beams jumping through the desert, growing smaller. Bell got out to help secure the man beside the first truck while Knox directed the other officers.

The cars found the highway again. Tank waited long enough to count the familiar headlights joining behind them. Rex's coupe. Green Bentley. Saint's borrowed motorcycle. More cars emerging from dust, scraped and loud, but coming.

“All back,” Tank said when he finished counting.

Knox returned to the Lamborghini with Bell. The police car took the lead while cruisers stayed behind at the roadblock. Bell sounded the siren once, and horns answered along the line.

Marcellus sat up carefully.

“Finished doing that?” he asked Aiden.

“For now,” Aiden said.

The old man looked down at his collar.

“Tie gave up,” Marcellus told him.

At the next slow bend, Aiden turned and straightened it with his good hand.

“Meeting forty million,” Aiden said. “Look ready.”

Marcellus looked at the cross hanging outside Aiden's shirt.

“Damn, boy,” Marcellus said.

Aiden smiled. “Magnificent.”

The old man nodded and settled back with the box and the bag beside him.

***

At eleven fifty-nine, the unfinished casino rose ahead.

White columns stood under floodlights. Empty window openings made black rectangles in the walls. Plastic sheeting moved inside the upper floor, and a dry fountain lay beyond cars parked across the entrance.

Men stood between those cars.

Above them, a balcony ran beneath an unfinished crown of stone. Aiden saw blue at the railing. His body recognized her before his eyes finished the distance.

Shawna.

Her hair blew across her face. Red stood beside her and raised the hand with the ring, waving as if they had come for dinner.

Tank stopped the Mercedes. Behind them, engine after engine went quiet.

Aiden opened his door. His hand hurt. His side hurt. Fear had found every place anger wasn't holding and filled it.

He put both feet on the ground.

Shawna stepped toward the railing. Red caught her arm.

“Aiden!” she called.

He looked straight up at her.

“Here, baby,” Aiden answered. “I'm here.”
