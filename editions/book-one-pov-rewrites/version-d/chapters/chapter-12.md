## 12. The Money at the Bottom

The folder smelled of leather and old cigarettes. I laid it open on the visitors' side of Red's desk, keeping the blue box in my left hand while I turned the pages with my right. Dates on the first page. Amounts and names on the second, arranged in clean little columns.

Shawna leaned close enough to read beside me. For a second her shoulder touched mine. I remembered that touch afterward much more clearly than I remembered any of the numbers.

On the third page, beside Valentino's name, somebody had written *settled* in red ink.

Underneath was a copy of the order from Dante's watch.

Red had kept his own.

I looked across the desk. “Nigga, you sick motherfucker.”

Red's hands rested flat on the wood. “Careful where you point that mouth.”

Shawna took the loose pages from the folder. She found the order, laid the others back on the leather and turned the single sheet over. Its blank back faced me for a moment. She turned it again and held it closer.

I had watched her read the original at our table. Knowing the words hadn't prepared me for finding a clean copy here, in the same drawer a man might keep stamps. I couldn't imagine what it did to her. Her hand began shaking hard enough that the edge of the paper tapped against the leather folder.

“You kept this?” Shawna asked Red.

He looked toward Tank. “T believed in records. Nigga kept everything.”

Shawna moved the paper into his line of sight. “You're sitting behind the desk.”

Red turned back to her. “Shawna.”

She raised her voice. “You're sitting behind the fucking desk.”

She threw the page at his chest. It caught his jacket and slid down onto the blotter. He looked at it without moving his hands, then raised his eyes to me.

“I was going to make this generous,” Red said.

I reached across his desk, took the order off the blotter and put it back with the other pages. “Try shutting your mouth.”

Tank moved up beside my shoulder. Both men at the outer door shifted forward. I heard a shoe scrape behind me and looked back long enough to see one of them watching Tank's hands.

When I faced Red again, he was smiling.

“Ask your man what generosity looks like,” Red told me.

Tank stopped moving.

I turned toward him. “What the fuck is he talking about?”

Tank said my name.

I waited. He looked past me toward the desk. That was all he did, and I felt something cold open beneath my ribs before I knew what belonged there.

“Nigga,” I said to Tank. “What?”

Red picked up a remote. A screen on the wall beside his desk lit up with a wide, grainy view of an empty swimming pool.

I knew the pale motel wall behind it. I knew the balcony rail and the blue tiles at the deep end. My body recognized the place before I saw the money: the ache in my side sharpened, and I caught myself taking the short breaths I'd taken against that fence.

Then Tank came into the picture.

Our Mercedes was already gone. T lay near the fence. The recording had no sound I could hear, just the quiet room around me and Tank on the screen, stepping down into the pool with a bag.

He bent over a bundle of money.

He picked it up.

I watched him put it inside the bag, turn, and reach for another.

The terrible thing about a recording is that it doesn't give a fuck when you've understood. It keeps going. The man I'd asked to help me was still bending and gathering and stepping carefully around the place where I'd nearly died.

“Turn it off,” I told Red.

On the screen, Tank climbed out with a full bag. He came back with another. One loose bundle lay beside T's shoe. Tank leaned over and took that too.

“You heard enough breathing in this room,” I said to Red. “Turn it the fuck off.”

Red pressed the remote. The screen went dark. I could hear his thumbnail clicking against the remote and Shawna trying to finish a breath. The little box creaked in my hand.

“Three million,” Red said. “All of it. Your friend is thorough.”

I turned toward Tank. He looked back at me. His arms hung loose beside his jacket.

“You took her money?” I asked him.

Tank swallowed. “I took it out the pool. I'm standing here saying that.”

I stepped closer. “Don't play with me.”

“I'm not,” Tank said.

I could smell the peppermint he'd been eating downstairs. I hated that ordinary smell. Those hands had brought me clothes, held doors, steadied me when I couldn't stand. On the screen they'd moved just as carefully around the blood beside the pool. I couldn't make the two things fit, and he was standing close enough to hit.

“You watched her hold me together,” I told Tank. “You saw her drive off with me bleeding. Then you went back for the fucking money?”

His jaw moved before the word came out.

“Yes,” Tank said.

I hit him with my right hand.

His head turned. Pain went through my side so hard my knees bent with it. I caught my balance and hit him again. He rocked back half a step, then came upright with blood on his lip.

I still held the blue box in my left hand. It seemed impossible that I'd remembered how to keep something so useless from falling.

“Look at me and hit me back,” I told Tank.

Tank looked at me. “No.”

I raised my fist again. “Hit me the fuck back.”

Shawna caught my wrist with both hands. I felt her weight against my arm before I looked down and saw her there.

Tank wiped his mouth. “I'm not hitting you.”

Red leaned against the far edge of his desk. “Touching.”

I turned toward him. Shawna's grip tightened.

“You say one more word,” I told Red.

He glanced at the box. “And what? You'll die rich?”

I looked down at the jewel. It lay where it had been, blue and bright in a velvet bed, having contributed absolutely fucking nothing to the evening except reasons for everybody to behave worse.

I wanted to throw it through his face.

Marcellus stepped close enough that his shoulder brushed mine. He bent toward the open box under the lamp. His eyes narrowed, then lifted quickly toward Red.

“Aiden,” Marcellus said.

I looked at him.

“Close it,” he told me.

I kept the lid open. “What?”

Marcellus put two fingers against its edge. “Close the box.”

I let the lid meet his fingers, and he withdrew them. He looked into my face. Whatever he'd seen, I took that look to mean he wanted to discuss it somewhere else.

Behind me, Tank spoke again.

“It's at your house,” Tank said. “Back of my closet. I moved it when we moved in.”

Shawna let go of my wrist and turned toward him.

“All of it?” she asked.

Tank nodded. “Every fucking dollar.”

Shawna stood very still. “Why didn't you tell us?”

Tank looked at the floor. I stepped toward him, and he raised his head before I reached him.

“I thought you was going to leave,” Tank said.

I stopped. “What?”

He rubbed his wet mouth with the back of his hand. “You got her. T was dead. You had the car. Then you started talking about water and houses, taking her anywhere. I thought you was gone.”

“I was fucking dying,” I told him.

Tank's eyes shone. “I know.”

I leaned toward him. “Do you?”

I'd seen Tank angry, drunk, half asleep in a doorway, laughing with food in his mouth. I hadn't seen him stand with tears gathering while he waited for me to decide whether to hit him again.

It made me angrier. I needed him solid. I needed something I could strike until the room stopped changing shape.

“I thought if you left, I'd have something,” Tank told me. “That's it. That's the whole thing.”

Red made a little impatient sound behind us.

“Tell him the rest,” Red said.

Tank looked past me. “You called me.”

Red nodded. “You answered.”

Tank took a breath through his nose. “You sent me that video.”

Red's smile came back. “And you answered again.”

Shawna moved beside me. “You told him where we were?”

Tank turned to her immediately. “No.”

She said his name and waited.

“No, Shawna,” Tank told her. “I didn't tell him about the house by the lake. Didn't give him your plans. He knew you was coming here because you fucking told him.”

Red straightened. “I offered him his place back.”

I kept my eyes on Tank. “What place?”

Red answered from behind me. “Yours.”

I heard the band through the floor. It had begun another song. Somebody downstairs laughed, and the ordinary sound reached the office like proof of a world none of us could get back into.

I thought about Tank sitting outside my bedroom. The spare phone. The way he'd watched me call men who used to answer to T, men who now waited for me to finish speaking before they asked what I wanted done.

“You want it?” I asked Tank.

He said nothing.

I stood closer. “I'm right here. You want it?”

His eyes dropped toward my waist. I followed them and saw a red mark spreading through the side of my white suit. I hadn't felt the wetness. Now that I could see it, the cloth seemed to cling to the wound.

Tank shook his head.

“I wanted you to ask me to stay,” Tank said. “Me. Not whoever drove the car.”

I'd been ready to hear he'd sold us. I'd been preparing my answer with my fists. I wasn't ready for him to stand there bleeding and tell me he wanted to stay in my house.

I didn't have an answer for that.

Red opened a drawer and tossed a car key onto the desk. It skidded across the blotter and stopped beside the open folder.

“Still time for an intelligent decision,” Red told Tank. “Bring me my money. Walk out with a future.”

Tank looked at the key.

He reached past me and picked it up.

My hands went cold.

Then he turned and threw it at Red's window.

The key struck with a sharp crack. A white star spread through the glass, but the pane held. The key bounced back and landed on the carpet beside the desk.

Tank watched it settle, then looked at Red.

“Fuck your car too,” Tank said.

The nearest guard grabbed Tank's shoulder. Tank spun and drove him into the wall. A framed photograph jumped sideways on its hook. The second guard reached inside his jacket, and Shawna snatched the lamp off the desk with both hands.

She brought it down across his forearm.

The bulb burst. For an instant the shade lit from within; then the office lost its warm pool of light and the windows reflected only the ceiling lamps. The guard shouted and bent his arm against his chest.

Red came around the desk while Shawna still held the broken lamp. He caught her from behind and pulled her against him. The lamp fell between their feet. I moved toward them.

The guard Tank had thrown into the wall twisted one arm free. I saw the pistol only as its heavy butt struck my wounded side.

The room left my lungs.

I folded against the desk. Loose pages slid under my right palm. I saw Shawna's shoes scrape the floor while Red hauled her toward the inner door. One heel came off. She kicked backward with the bare foot and twisted hard enough to knock a photograph from a shelf.

“Let her go,” I said.

There wasn't enough breath behind it to reach the doorway.

I pushed against the desk. “Let her fucking go.”

Tank tore the guard away from me. Marcellus caught my sleeve and helped me straighten. I pressed the loose papers together, closed the folder around them and gripped it in my right hand. The box stayed clenched in my left. I wasn't making sensible decisions about evidence. I was holding things because I couldn't stand the thought of losing anything else.

A gun went off.

Wood jumped from the desk beside my hand. I jerked back and saw Red at the inner doorway with his pistol pressed against Shawna. Smoke hung briefly in the light between us.

Downstairs, a scream cut through the band. More voices followed. The music stopped.

Tank stopped with one guard held against the wall. The man Shawna had hit cradled his injured arm and stayed where he was.

“There,” Red said. “You can learn.”

Shawna's hair had come loose. One earring was missing. Red's forearm lay across the upper part of her chest; his other hand held the gun against her body. She looked at me over his arm. Her eyes were wet, but she kept her mouth shut tight.

I didn't know what she was trying to tell me. I knew only that I could see her breathing and that he had a hand on the thing that could stop it.

“Take me,” I told Red.

His eyebrows rose. “Nigga, I don't want you.”

I moved one foot forward. “Let her walk. I'll stay.”

Red drew her tighter. “I heard you.”

Shawna shook her head once, hard.

“Aiden,” she said.

I couldn't look away from the gun.

“Take it and go,” Shawna told me.

I looked into her face. “I'm not leaving you.”

Her voice broke on the next word. “Go.”

The inner door opened wider. A third man waited beyond it. Shawna looked over her shoulder, then back past me toward Tank.

“Get him out,” she told Tank.

Red pulled her through the doorway.

I went after them.

Tank let the guard drop and caught me around the chest before I reached the desk's far corner. I struck him with my elbow, then with the fist holding the folder. Its leather edge scraped his face. I told him I'd kill him. I meant it with everything I had left, and he kept dragging me toward the outer door.

Behind us, a man shouted. A shot struck the wall near the inner doorway. I couldn't see whose gun had fired. Tank turned his body between me and the sound and pulled harder.

Marcellus reached the hall first. Rosa appeared there with her red gown gathered in one hand so she could climb the last steps. She looked at us, then into the room behind us.

“Where is she?” Rosa asked.

Marcellus caught her wrist. “He has her.”

Rosa tried to pull free and go past him.

“Not this way,” Marcellus told her.

Tank hauled me into the hall. I could still hear Shawna beyond the inner door. Then another door shut somewhere farther inside, and I couldn't hear her at all.

By the time we reached the ballroom stairs, people were pressing toward the exit below us. Chairs lay in their path. A waiter tried to move one and nearly disappeared beneath the crowd pushing around him.

One of Red's men pulled the ballroom doors shut and put his back against them.

I saw him do it. A room full of people trying to get away from gunfire, and that motherfucker decided the most urgent thing in the building was the fact that nobody had given them permission.

“Open them!” Rosa shouted from the stairs.

He raised his gun toward her.

Tank pulled me down behind the banister. The shot struck the wall over our heads and showered pale dust onto Rosa's hair. Marcellus drew her against the stairwell wall.

I could still hear Shawna telling me to go. I'd brought the box and the papers. I'd left the only person in that office I wanted, and now a fool downstairs was aiming at Rosa as if the evening still had a seating plan.

I shoved the blue box into the deep inner pocket of my jacket. The folder went inside my waistband, flat against my stomach. It hurt to bend far enough to put it there. Everything hurt. I was beginning to resent the variety.

“Put me down,” I told Tank.

His grip tightened. “Aiden.”

I caught his wrist. “I ain't going back upstairs. I'm getting these people out. Put me on my fucking feet.”

Tank lowered me until my shoes touched the step. I held the rail while the room finished tilting. Then I took my pistol from its holster and looked over the banister.

“Police!” I shouted toward the doors.

The guard laughed.

I opened my jacket with my free hand. The badge caught the ballroom light.

“Captain, nigga,” I told him. “Try your joke with the right title.”

His eyes went from the badge to the pistol. For half a second I thought intelligence might have reached him in time.

Then he fired again.

I went over the low banister beside the last few steps. My shoes hit the carpet, my knee followed, and I dropped behind a table while the shot struck wood above me. Tank came down the remaining steps with his own gun drawn. He fired toward the doorway, forcing the guard behind a planter beside it.

People crawled away from the line between them. A woman in diamonds caught my sleeve.

“Are we going to die?” she asked me.

I looked past her shoulder to make sure her husband was down. “Not with that nigga shooting. Behind the table. Both of you.”

She crawled. Her husband followed so closely that he lost a shoe and didn't even pause to negotiate with it.

A second guard came through a side corridor near the bar. I saw his arm before the rest of him. He was bringing his gun around the corner toward me when Rex Crown hit him from behind.

They crashed into a champagne tower.

Glass fell in a bright waterfall around their shoulders. Rex drove the guard across the table and into the wall, holding on with both enormous arms. The champagne ran down his back. If the man wanted a rematch with anybody, he had chosen a much better opponent.

“Captain!” Rex shouted.

The first guard rose behind his planter. I fired toward him, well clear of Rex on my other side, and he dropped out of sight again. Tank reached the door and hauled it open. Guests began running through to the lobby.

Rex pulled the second guard to the floor. The guard twisted beneath him and struck him across the face with the pistol. Rex's head snapped sideways. His grip opened, and the man rolled free onto one knee.

I crossed the gap between our table and the bar before he could stand.

He raised the gun toward Rex. I shoved my own pistol into its holster as I came up beside him, caught his gun wrist with my right hand and drove it away from Rex's head. With my left, I pulled the blue box from my jacket.

Then I hit him beside the temple with it.

Forty million dollars made a solid fucking sound. I would learn soon enough that it wasn't worth forty million. At that moment it was worth exactly one living boxer.

The guard fell onto his side. I set my foot on his gun hand until his fingers opened, then kicked the pistol deep beneath the bar. I put the box back inside my jacket and offered Rex my free hand.

Blood ran from his eyebrow.

“Twice,” Rex said, looking up at me.

I braced myself while he got his feet beneath him. “Keeping score now?”

Rex put a hand on the bar. “You beat me. Then you saved me.”

I glanced toward the open doors. “Keep up. Night ain't over.”

He laughed with blood on his teeth, put his shoulder beneath my good arm, and helped me toward the exit. Tank held the ballroom door. Rosa pushed Marcellus into the passage ahead of her. I could see Porsche beyond the lobby, waving people toward the street.

Then metal ground against stone above the staircase.

I looked up.

The bronze woman holding the bowl of fire had tilted forward on her base. Something had struck it; I hadn't seen what. Burning liquid spilled down her arms and fell onto the flowers below. Flame climbed through the dry arrangement in a sudden sheet.

Mercedes stood beneath her, trying to pull a fallen man to his feet.

“Mercedes, move!” I shouted.

She looked up. The statue began to fall.

I pulled free of Rex and ran the few steps back toward her. The bronze woman's arm struck the banister. Wood snapped. Mercedes had one hand beneath the man's shoulder, his other arm still dragging behind him.

I drove into both of them from the side, catching Mercedes around the waist and shoving the man ahead of us through the open passage. His shoes slid. We fell together beyond the doorway as the statue struck the lower stairs behind us.

The bowl flew off. Burning liquid splashed across the carpet we'd just left. Part of the broken banister crashed down after it.

Heat rolled over my back. Pain spread through my side so completely that, for a moment, I couldn't tell where the bullet was anymore. It felt like the whole fucking thing had gone into business across my body.

Mercedes rolled toward me and caught my face in both hands.

“Nigga,” Mercedes said. “Nigga, you got me?”

I tried to draw breath. “Got you.”

She gripped my cheeks. “You sure?”

I looked down at her. “Baby, count your fucking legs.”

She did. Two legs. One shoe. A dress she was going to mention for the rest of my natural life.

Then she kissed my forehead hard enough that I thought the bullet might come out from gratitude.

“You beautiful, stupid, magnificent motherfucker,” Mercedes told me.

The man we'd pushed clear sat against the wall, coughing and looking at the burning doorway. I saw him move both arms. Mercedes reached for him again while I tried to stand.

I couldn't do it the first time.

Rex and Tank reached me together. Each took an arm, and between them they got me up. The pistol still pressed against my waist; the blue box weighed down my jacket. Mercedes stayed beside the other man, helping him stand against the wall while we cleared the passage.

People had found their way through the passage. Porsche held the outer lobby door open with one leg and shouted directions at a frightened crowd that, for once in its expensive fucking life, followed instructions.

The woman in diamonds stopped beside us. Her husband stood behind her with one bare foot. She took my hand and pressed her wedding ring into it.

“For saving us,” she told me.

I pushed it back into her palm. “Keep your husband nervous. I got a woman waiting.”

She laughed through her tears. Her husband looked at the ring returning to her hand and nodded at me with an intensity I understood perfectly.

Then the cold air touched my face.

My own woman wasn't waiting outside.

The people around us kept moving, calling for friends, touching one another to make sure they had come out whole. Somebody called me Captain. Somebody else tried to shake the hand Tank was using to keep me upright.

I turned toward the building.

Tank caught my shoulder. “Not that way.”

I looked at the lit windows above the ballroom. “She's in there.”

Tank stepped in front of me. His mouth had swollen where I'd hit it. “She told me to get you out. You're alive enough to go get her somewhere else. That's what we got left.”

I didn't forgive him beside that car. I didn't decide the money didn't matter. I let him take my weight because I couldn't stand without help, and I needed to stay alive long enough to find her.

Rex helped him lower me into our Mercedes. Tank went around to drive. The police Lamborghini stayed at the Saint with Knox's officers; one of them told Tank they'd bring it back when the way was clear.

Rosa came to my open window before getting into another car with Marcellus. She pushed one of the gala photographs into my hand.

“She gave me this,” Rosa said.

Shawna stood between me and Red in the picture. His ring caught the light beside her arm. My hand rested at her waist. I looked at my own face, at how sure I'd been that standing close was the same as keeping her.

Rosa touched the edge of the picture.

“Told me to keep one for when you two got married,” she said.

I couldn't look up at her.

Tank started the car.

We hadn't gone far when Marcellus called. I balanced the photograph against my thigh, took out the phone and put him on speaker. Then I set it in the console between us.

“The stone,” Marcellus said. “Aiden, listen carefully. The one you have is the duplicate.”

Tank glanced toward me before looking back at the road.

I took out the blue box and opened it beneath the dashboard light. My fingertips left a red mark on the lid.

“You sure?” I asked Marcellus.

His voice sharpened through the speaker. “Nigga, I made it.”

I shut the box.

We'd gone in with Shawna and come out with a fucking lie in velvet.

“Red know?” Tank asked toward the phone.

Marcellus paused. “He expected you to take it.”

Streetlights moved across the windshield. I counted them until I realized what I was doing. Every time I stopped looking at something in front of me, I saw Red's arm across Shawna's chest.

Rosa's voice came through next.

“We still have the original order,” Rosa said. “And you have his papers.”

I pulled the folder from my waistband and laid it across my lap. Its corners were bent. A page had torn where my fist had held it. I saw Valentino's name again, then a list of payments tied to the jewel transaction, figures arranged so neatly that they almost concealed the bodies underneath.

“I don't want his fucking papers,” I told Rosa.

Her voice softened. “I know.”

I put my hand over the page. “I want her.”

Rosa answered me by name. “I know, Aiden.”

Another call lit the screen. Unknown number.

I picked the phone up, ended the first call and answered before the next ring could finish.

“Shawna?” I said.

Red breathed into my ear.

“Wondered how long you'd take to notice the gift,” Red said.

I pressed the phone so hard against my head that its edge hurt. “Let me hear her.”

Red kept speaking. “Your friend has my three million. You have papers that belong in my desk. Somewhere, somebody has a very old note.”

I held the box against my leg. “Let me hear her.”

Movement came through the line. A door, footsteps, the brush of something against the microphone.

Then Shawna spoke.

“Baby,” she said.

I closed my eyes. “I'm here.”

Her breath caught. “I know.”

I leaned forward against the phone. “I'm coming.”

Shawna answered softly. “I know.”

Red came back on the line before I could say anything else.

“The unfinished casino in the desert,” Red told me. “Midnight. Bring the money, the papers and the original note.”

I looked at the dashboard clock. Less than three hours.

“If you hurt her,” I began.

Red cut across me. “Come before I get bored.”

The call ended.

For a while neither Tank nor I spoke. He drove with both hands on the wheel. I laid the phone on the folder and held the photograph in one hand, the closed blue box in the other. The suit we'd chosen that afternoon had blood on the sleeve and the side. My cheek still held the heat of Shawna's slap.

Every place she'd touched me that night seemed like the only part of my body still alive.

“Take me to the money,” I told Tank.

He nodded at the road. “Yeah.”

I turned toward him. “All of it.”

Tank swallowed. “Yeah.”

I put the photograph inside my jacket, over the cross. I'd gone into the Saint believing I could steal a fortune and a future in one night. Now he had my future locked in a room somewhere, and all the money in Vegas couldn't make the next red light change.

“Tank,” I said.

He glanced at me. “What?”

I kept my hand against the picture. “After we get her.”

Tank faced the road again. “Nigga, we are getting her.”

I looked out at the city.

“After we get her,” I told Tank, “there isn't going to be enough desert left to hide what we do to him.”
