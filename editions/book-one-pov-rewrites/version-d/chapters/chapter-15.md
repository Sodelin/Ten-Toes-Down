## 15. Ten Toes, Thirty Engines

By eleven, the street looked like every bad idea in Vegas had found my address.

Headlights up the drive. Doors slamming. Men calling to each other through the dark. Some wore leather, some silk, and one had arrived in pajamas covered with little white moons. His gold chain was caught between the buttons. Another man still had shaving foam behind his ear.

Tank looked at the pajamas.

“You were asleep?” he asked.

“Participating in my relationship,” the man said.

“Relationship's open at the bottom.”

The man buttoned himself without slowing down.

“Where she at?” he asked me.

“Red has her.”

His hands stopped on the shirt.

“Aight,” he said. “What we need?”

That was why I'd called. I wasn't under the impression every man on my porch was good. Some would've asked Red the same question if his invitation had arrived first. A few had carried T's coffin. Some owed the women at home answers to messages they'd had time to read six fucking times.

But they came. I could work with came.

The man with shaving foam saw the door.

“Red did that?” he asked.

“I did.”

“Okay. Was about to get separately mad.”

“Stay with the original mad,” I told him. “Deadline.”

He wiped his ear on his sleeve and went in. I watched a man I'd once fought move a chair so Marcellus could sit more comfortably. Somebody else picked glass out of Rosa's path before her shoe found it.

My girl was missing. These were the people who answered.

The world could interview better men in the morning.

***

Tank opened both money bags on the dining table. He waited for the room to fill, then spoke without looking at me first.

“This is from the motel,” he said. “I took it.”

The room changed its sound. Men stopped talking and started listening to each other stop.

A man beside the piano leaned forward.

“You took what?” he asked.

“All of it,” Tank said. “Let Aiden think it was gone. Red knew. Tried using it against him tonight.”

“Tried?” a man near the windows asked.

“Tried.”

The man by the piano pointed at Tank and looked at me.

“Still breathing?” he asked.

I stepped between them.

“Look at me when you say that.”

He did. Lowered his hand too.

“Tank stands with me,” I told him.

“Just saying—”

“Say he stole it. He did. Say he's a lying motherfucker. He was. Say you're touching him tonight, find out how fast I forget your name.”

Tank stood behind me without saying a word. I could feel him there. Had spent half the night wishing that were still a thing I could count on. Was choosing to count on it now.

The man by the piano lifted his hands.

“Your house,” he said.

“Yeah. My house. Money on my table. My girl with Red. Any of that bother you, door's already broke. Won't hurt its feelings leaving.”

Nobody moved toward it.

Rosa watched from the staircase in the borrowed coat. Marcellus sat behind her with the blue box on his knees and the papers inside his jacket. The old man looked exhausted. When I glanced at the stairs as if to offer him a bed, he shook his head. Apparently I was the third person that evening who needed to stop deciding where other people belonged.

I put my hand on the money.

“Some came because you owe me,” I told the room. “Some because Red owes you. Some heard there was money.”

“All three,” said a man at the back.

I looked at him. He stood by it.

“Good,” I said. “Knows why he came.”

Then I started paying them.

I didn't throw it in the air. Every man came to the table. Every man looked at me while I put the weight into his hands. I wanted them to remember whose money it had been before they arrived to talk about brotherhood. Shawna had carried it out of a room full of killers. Now she needed something more useful than a room full of men saying they were dangerous.

Bundles disappeared into coats. Into pockets. Into the pajama shirt, where the little moons began straining to escape.

“Get a bag,” Tank told that man.

“I'm good.”

“Look pregnant.”

“I'm good,” the man repeated, holding himself closed.

Some counted immediately. Some laughed. One just held the cash in both hands. I remembered seeing him in a hospital corridor with his head bowed and nothing to say. Tonight he kissed the stack before putting it in his coat.

I didn't ask which part of his life I'd helped. Had one woman to bring home. The rest of the city could bring its problems along if it brought its heart too.

Tank and I had separated the bundles by their bands. When two million had gone into hands or sat aside for the people still waiting, I stopped. The final million remained together in one bag.

I pulled the zipper closed.

“Hers,” I said.

Nobody argued.

Tank hadn't taken his share from the money we'd already set aside. I picked it up and held it toward him.

He shook his head. “No.”

“Take it before I love you louder.”

“Aiden.”

“Poor and guilty? I'm allowing one bad feeling. Pick.”

He looked at the men around us.

“Don't want it.”

“Spend it on somebody who does,” I told him.

I pressed it against his chest until he accepted it. He looked down, then set the whole share beside the closed bag.

“For her,” Tank said.

I left it there. Wasn't going to turn the thing he'd finally given willingly into another argument.

Rosa stepped away from the stairs.

“Before you start singing about brotherhood,” she told the room, “she's my friend.”

Men straightened. The one holding his pajamas stopped smiling.

“See her, bring her,” Rosa said. “Don't stop to tell Red how hard you are. Don't explain a grudge from 2018. You bring her.”

The man who'd arrived with shaving foam nodded.

“We got you, Rosa.”

“Better have her, baby.”

I pushed Rosa's share across the table. It came from the two million we'd separated. The million in the closed bag stayed untouched.

She looked down at it.

“For what?” she asked.

“Whatever you want.”

“Paying me to stay home?”

“Because she loves you,” I said.

Rosa looked up sharply.

“Don't do that right now.”

“When she gets out of the car, I want somebody here she knew before every room had me in it.”

Her fingers tightened around the borrowed coat. I could finally see how tired she was. One earring missing, dress dirty, holding herself straight because we'd all fallen apart whenever she stopped supervising.

“Bring her,” Rosa told me.

I nodded.

She slapped my cheek.

“With your mouth, Aiden.”

Nobody laughed. I swallowed.

“I'm bringing her.”

Rosa kissed the same cheek.

“Go, baby.”

I turned to the room.

“Anybody think Red owns Vegas?”

“Fuck Red,” somebody by the door said.

“Anybody think he owns my wife?”

Rosa made a little noise behind me.

I turned halfway back. “What?”

“Asked her yet?”

“Going to.”

“Quit inviting these men before me.”

The room broke open. Men laughed, slapped shoulders, discussed suits and weddings and who had to be kept away from an open bar. For a few seconds, we had a future large enough to crowd the furniture.

I let them have it. Needed some myself.

Then I raised my hand.

“Listen. I'm not asking you to die because I fell in love. Asking you to stand with me because Red thinks seeing something makes it his. Took a man's wife. Took another man's stone. Had Dante left in the desert. Took Shawna tonight.”

I closed my hand over the cross beneath my shirt.

“Keeps calling it a dead man's jewelry. Fine. Let him look while I come get my girl.”

Tank stepped beside me.

“Ten toes,” he called.

“Down,” the room answered.

“Ten toes.”

“Down.”

The broken door rattled in its frame. I felt their voices against my chest and wanted every penthouse in Vegas to hear them. Every little room where somebody was waiting for a man to come home different.

I kissed the cross and went outside.

***

Tank drove the Mercedes. I took the front passenger seat because I couldn't lean back comfortably and because I wanted to be the first person Red saw.

Marcellus opened the rear door.

“Sure about this?” I asked him.

“No.”

“Can stay with Rosa.”

“Waited enough,” he said, and shut himself in before I could start managing his life again.

The blue box rested beside him. The letters and records stayed inside his coat. Tank put the closed money bag in the back, adding his returned share first. One million plus Tank's part. Red had demanded three million. He was getting what remained after I paid the men coming to take his fucking kingdom.

Rosa came to my open window. Her share was still on the dining table.

“Gardenias,” she said.

“What?”

“Wedding. She likes gardenias. Will lie and tell you roses because men always buy roses.”

I gripped the door. “Gardenias.”

“And don't have that old man give her away. I'll do it.”

Marcellus leaned forward behind me.

“Hadn't volunteered,” he said.

Rosa pointed at him. “Keep it that way.”

She stepped back. Tank started down the drive.

The cars stretched along the street until it looked like somebody had called a funeral and forgotten to agree who was dying. Thirty engines, maybe more. Rex Crown sat in a black coupe. Little Saint rode beside him on a motorcycle with an unpainted tank.

“Borrowed it,” Saint called through my window.

I nodded. The owner had either been very generous or badly informed about our return policy.

Mercedes waved from the green Bentley beside Porsche. Bell was driving my returned police Lamborghini at the front, with Knox beside her. The chief lifted two fingers toward me before they pulled away.

Headlights filled both our mirrors.

For several blocks, Tank said nothing. The towers became signs, the signs became gas stations, and beyond those the road went black.

“Doctor after,” Tank said eventually.

“After.”

“Saying it now.”

“I heard you.”

He nodded and kept both hands on the wheel.

I looked down at the cross.

“When you went back to the pool,” I asked, “T look like himself?”

Tank drove a few seconds before answering.

“No.”

“Good.”

His fingers tightened.

“Don't have to say that,” he told me.

“Know.”

The dashboard lit his face. I remembered him behind T that first night, big as a wall, waiting for another man's decision. Now he had made his own and was driving me toward it.

“Glad you're here,” I said.

His jaw moved.

“Yeah.”

“Don't make me say it twice.”

“Heard you,” Tank said.

Marcellus turned the box between his hands in the back.

“Celeste wore blue the last time I took her dancing,” he said. “I was late. She'd danced with three other men.”

I turned halfway around. “Three?”

“Counted them for me.”

Tank glanced in the mirror.

“Did something to those men?” he asked.

“Asked for the fourth dance,” Marcellus said.

I looked back at the road.

“Could've done that myself.”

“Didn't,” the old man said gently.

We drove the next stretch without speaking. I had a whole convoy behind me, but that answer fit in the car beside me better than all their guns.

***

At eleven fifty-four, Red called.

I put him on speaker.

“Coming?” he asked.

“Hear the cars?”

“Told you to come ready to apologize.”

“I am.”

Tank glanced over.

“Told her I'd never let you touch her again,” I said. “Got plenty to apologize for.”

Red breathed into the phone.

“Think all those men love you?”

“Enough to leave the house.”

“That's money.”

“Should've paid better.”

He went quiet. I heard Shawna behind him. Not the words, just her voice before he moved the phone away.

“Let me talk to her,” I said.

“See her soon.”

“Let me—”

The call ended. I gripped the phone so hard Tank took it before I could break that too. He set it in the console.

Past the last gas station, headlights shone across the highway instead of down it.

Two trucks. Men beside them, no uniforms. Burning barrels between the trucks. Red had arranged the road the way he arranged a dining room: everybody would stop where he told them and appreciate the view.

Tank slowed. Thirty engines behind us changed their voices.

Knox called. I answered on speaker.

“See them,” she said. “Stay in the convoy.”

“Convoy is me.”

“Then don't make me explain a road accident to your wife before you get there.”

A shot hit our hood. Tank pulled left. Another made a white star in the windshield directly in front of him.

Marcellus dropped below the rear windows, pulling his coat over the papers and taking the box down with him. I ducked as Bell brought the Lamborghini alongside us. Knox leaned forward in the passenger seat, staring at the trucks.

The siren rose.

One man stepped backward. Another raised his gun anyway.

“Hardheaded motherfucker,” I said.

Tank put his foot down.

The Mercedes left the road in a sheet of dust. We dropped into a shallow ditch and came out hard enough to throw my head against the roof. My seat belt caught the rest of me. I tasted blood.

Behind us, the convoy spread to both sides of the trucks. Headlights bounced over scrub and stone. Red's men had expected a line.

Got a fucking horizon.

Rex's coupe came through on our right. Little Saint rode beside it, up off his seat over the rough ground. The green Bentley emerged from the dust with its roof down. Mercedes held her hair in one hand and a pistol in the other.

“Keep the old man down!” she shouted as they passed.

“Old man is down,” Marcellus called from the back. “Thoroughly fucking down.”

I laughed. Bullet hole in the glass, girl waiting inside a nightmare, and that old man was still correcting the way my people talked to him. I loved him for giving me a sound Red hadn't put in my mouth.

Then the nearer truck moved.

Its driver turned toward the cars passing through the desert. Rex braked. Saint swerved. One of the truck's front wheels climbed a ditch, bringing its nose across the coupe's escape route.

I saw Rex look up through the windshield. Saw Little Saint turn his bike back.

“Tank.”

“See it.”

“Believe you this time.”

Tank drove straight into the truck's rear corner.

Metal struck metal. Our car jumped sideways. My shoulder hit the door, and every stitch in my side tried leaving without me. The truck's rear swung through the dirt, pulling its nose away long enough for Rex to get clear.

Little Saint shot through the gap and raised a fist.

“Aiden!” he shouted.

“Tank!” I shouted back.

Tank looked at me. “What?”

“Giving you your fucking credit. Enjoy it.”

He grinned through the blood on his lip.

The truck stopped against a low rise. Its driver jumped out with a gun, took one look at the cars surrounding him, and dropped it.

Bell braked beside him. Knox got out with her weapon raised. Tank had slowed close enough that I heard her through my open window.

“Blocked my captain's road,” Knox told him.

The man stared at her uniform.

“Your captain?”

She pointed at me. “That one.”

He looked over. His face fell so completely I almost respected the honesty.

“Didn't say it was him,” he told Knox.

“What part of thirty cars looked like a small mistake?” I shouted.

Rex pulled up beside us with his window down. The cut on his eyebrow had opened again.

“Three times,” he told me.

“That was Tank.”

Rex looked at my driver. “Owe the family, then.”

Tank raised two fingers from the wheel. Didn't bother trying to look modest. Would've annoyed me if he had.

Knox's officers moved around the trucks. The second crew had run before the first finished moving. I could see four flashlights bouncing through the desert, trying to become somebody else's problem.

Bell helped secure the driver while Knox directed the others. Then they returned to the Lamborghini as our cars found the highway. Tank counted headlights coming through the dust: coupe, Bentley, borrowed bike, everybody else, scraped and louder but still moving.

“All back,” he said.

The police car took the lead. Cruisers stayed to hold the roadblock. Bell gave the siren one short burst, and horns answered along the line.

Marcellus sat up carefully.

“Finished doing that?” he asked.

“For now.”

He looked down at his collar.

“Tie gave up.”

At the next slow bend, I turned and straightened it with my good hand.

“Meeting forty million,” I told him. “Look ready.”

He looked at the cross on my chest.

“Damn, boy,” Marcellus said.

I smiled. “Magnificent.”

He nodded like the last repair of the evening had finally been completed.

***

At eleven fifty-nine, the unfinished casino rose out of the dark.

White columns beneath floodlights. Empty windows. Plastic moving behind them. A dry fountain beyond the cars parked across the entrance. Men standing between those cars.

Above it all, a balcony ran beneath an unfinished crown of stone.

I saw blue at the railing.

Shawna.

My whole body knew before my eyes finished crossing the distance. Her hair blew across her face. Red stood beside her and lifted the hand with the ring, waving like he'd invited us to dinner.

Tank stopped. Behind us, one engine after another went quiet.

I opened the door. My side hurt. My hand hurt. Everything in me that knew how to be scared was awake and screaming.

I put both feet on the ground anyway.

Shawna stepped toward the railing. Red caught her arm.

“Aiden!” she called.

I looked straight up at her.

“Here, baby,” I said. “I'm here.”
