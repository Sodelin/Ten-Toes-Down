## 4. The Palace of the Woman I Just Met

I bought Shawna a house before I bought myself another shirt.

We went to Lake Las Vegas after the hospital. I had managed to stand for the men by the cars, sit through the drive, and convince myself that looking at property counted as resting because the property generally stayed where you put it. Then I got out beside the lawn and discovered my side had objections to real estate as well.

The agent came toward us in heels. One sank into the grass and stayed there when she took her next step. She caught herself against Tank’s forearm. He steadied her, pulled the shoe out of the lawn, and handed it back with a plug of earth still attached.

She looked at the size of his hand, the line of black cars, and the dressing visible where my borrowed shirt fell open.

The agent cleared her throat. “Will anybody else be joining the viewing?”

“You want MORE of us?” Tank asked.

I told her we were fine. “Show my woman the water.”

She stepped onto the stone path before describing the house. Five bedrooms. Private dock. Heated pool. The piano could be negotiated separately.

“Why?” I asked. “The nigga in a band?”

The agent explained that the owner might wish to retain it.

I waved a hand at her property folder. “Tell him to wish for a smaller instrument.”

Shawna squeezed my hand. “Baby. Let her do her job.”

I let the woman get the key into the lock without offering further terms.

The foyer opened beneath a curved staircase. Light fell through a chandelier large enough to make me wonder whether the ceiling had applied for financing. White curtains moved at the far end of the living room, where glass doors stood open to the patio. Beyond the pool and the grass, the lake lay bright and almost still.

The black piano sat beside those doors.

Shawna let go of my hand and walked toward it slowly, looking between the water and the keys. I stayed by the last step down into the room and watched her find the bench. She placed two fingers on the ivory without pressing. Her mouth opened, then closed. I remembered touching the hood of a clean car when I was little, then taking my hand away before the owner came out. I wanted her to play the fucking thing.

“Aiden,” she said.

I went to her. “You can't buy a house because I touched a piano,” Shawna said.

“Touch a smaller thing next time.”

She looked up at me. I could see her trying not to laugh.

I touched the end of the bench. “You like it?”

Shawna looked through the doors again. “Yeah. Look at this light, baby.”

I turned toward the agent. “We have a yes. Do whatever paper follows yes.”

The agent opened the folder she carried. “We haven't discussed the price.”

I held up a hand. “Don't say it louder than she said yes.”

Tank wandered outside to inspect the dock while the woman went to fetch the details. I lowered myself onto the piano bench beside Shawna and got halfway through pretending it was easy before she caught my arm.

Shawna guided my elbow down. “Slowly, you ridiculous nigga.”

I settled onto the bench with my teeth together. “That is slowly. Normal speed, we’d own next door already.”

She laughed, and I concentrated on breathing through my nose until the pulling beneath the bandage eased. The room carried sounds differently from the motel. A footstep had space to travel. Water touched the dock in small, regular strokes. There was no argument below the window, no engine idling for somebody who might be coming upstairs.

Shawna looked from my bandage to the room around us. “We doing everything backward.”

I patted the space beside me. “Then turn around.”

She swung one leg across the bench to face me. “We barely know each other.”

I nodded toward the open doors. “I know you wanted water.”

Shawna took her fingers off the piano. “Dante wanted water.”

My smile stayed in place a moment after it should have left. Outside, Tank put one shoe onto the dock and tested his weight. A duck stood on the far end watching him. Neither seemed prepared to recognize the other’s claim.

“You said it to me,” I told her.

She put a hand on my knee. “I know.”

I wanted to stand up and buy a different lake. One he hadn't thought of. Instead I sat there with her hand on my knee while Tank sized up a duck. After a while, Shawna pressed one of the keys. The note hung in the empty room, and I stayed to hear it finish.

The agent returned with the price. I sent her to the men handling T’s accounts. The club and its income had survived him. His house was promised elsewhere; this one would carry Shawna’s name. I wanted her to have a door that was hers to close, a place nobody could take back because she stopped pleasing him.

The agent asked where my name would go.

“My nigga lives where I live,” Shawna said before I could answer.

I turned toward her. Shawna met my eyes. “That the arrangement?”

I laid my hand over hers. “That’s the whole constitution.”

Shawna faced the agent again. “Then stop negotiating over my piano.”

I told the agent to put the keys in Shawna's hand. “Piano stays too. My wife said.”

I caught Shawna's raised eyebrow and tried again. “My future wife. Look, lady, leave the fucking piano.”

She leaned across the bench and kissed me hard enough that I needed to catch the edge with my good hand. Outside, Tank committed his full weight to the dock. The duck stepped off into the water and swam away, surrendering the property without meeting his asking price.

By that evening, the house had begun filling with the objects and people that would make it ours. Rosa arrived in the red velvet coat, stopped under the chandelier, and kept her sunglasses on while she examined the height of the room.

Rosa lowered her sunglasses just far enough to look at us. “This bitch gets shot at one time and comes home with a staircase.”

Shawna screamed laughing. I had heard her laugh in the car. This time she bent forward, braced a hand against the wall, and let the sound fill both floors. I wanted the agent back so I could tell her that was what I'd paid for.

Shawna took Rosa's hand and tugged her toward the first step. “Come see upstairs.”

Rosa looked up along the curve of the banister. “You got upstairs too?”

Tank, standing near the first step, gestured at the evidence. “You looking at the stairs.”

Rosa pointed at him without breaking stride. “And furniture that talks.”

He looked at me for support. I gave him none. I was sitting down at the time and had become very selective about wars.

Within an hour Rosa came down to tell us she'd chosen her room and found a bathroom light that respected her cheekbones. She carried a painting she said was depressing everybody. She referred to the previous owner as if he had died in it. I told her he had moved to Arizona. She leaned the painting against the wall facing inward anyway. Apparently the location of his remains was not central to her criticism.

Tank sat across from me in the living room. I held an ice pack wrapped in a towel against my side and listened to the two women moving above us. A drawer opened, laughter traveled down the stairwell, and Rosa shouted a question about towels nobody had found yet.

“You sure about this?” Tank asked.

“If that chandelier comes with a monthly payment, no.”

He looked toward the stairs. “Her, Aiden.”

“Watch it,” I said.

Tank kept his voice low. “I been watching.”

He kept his eyes on the stairs. I sat through several seconds of silence, deciding whether I wanted him to finish. I told him to say it.

Tank leaned forward, elbows on his knees. “She loved Dante.”

I pressed the ice pack harder against my side. “He’s dead.”

Tank looked back at me. “That’s what I’m telling you.”

I threw the ice pack at him. He caught it without rising, which denied me even the small pleasure of inconveniencing his ass.

I pointed at the dressing below my shirt. “I got a bullet in me. You want me jumping up to fight you?”

Tank shook his head. “I want you sitting right there.”

I gripped the arm of my chair. “Then stop saying shit I can't sit down for.”

He came over and put the ice pack back against my side. Gently. The care made me angrier. It was harder to dismiss him while he was helping me breathe.

Tank adjusted the towel beneath the ice. “Some nigga gotta tell you something.”

Shawna laughed upstairs. We both looked toward the sound.

I asked him where T’s funeral would be. It was the first change of subject I could find large enough to hide inside.

The housewarming began before we had answered the towel question.

Mercedes arrived with twelve women, two men, and a box of champagne whose origins she described as a gift with enough emphasis to discourage witnesses. Porsche followed in a white Bentley with the top down. A man sat in the back wiping his eyes.

Tank leaned toward her window and asked who he was.

“His car,” Porsche said. “My evening.”

The man lifted a hand. “We’re friends.”

I told him to blink if she had bought him too. He laughed so hard he stopped crying and got out to shake my hand.

He introduced himself as Leon, owner of a steakhouse. While he shook my hand, Porsche told me he'd proposed over appetizers.

“I said you could drive,” Leon reminded her, his hand still in mine.

Porsche tapped the Bentley's hood. “And here we are. Keep up, baby.”

She told me she'd declined the ring. Looking at Leon's damp eyes, I thought he might still be hoping the evening came with a second answer.

Mercedes took a champagne bottle from his free hand before he could set it down.

“That’s growth,” she told him.

Twenty minutes later, the patio had people in every available space. Women went into the pool wearing diamonds that nobody had tested for recreational use. A dealer tried to impress Rosa with his watch. She held his wrist beside her ear, listened, and told him she couldn't hear the personality inside it.

Tank laughed from the door. The dealer turned toward him with a sharp little movement of his chin. Then his gaze traveled upward over all that Tank and stopped at his face.

“Funny lady,” the dealer said, and joined in. He laughed so enthusiastically I thought Rosa might have to charge him for it.

I rested in a chair with Shawna beside me and a clean shirt open over my bandages. Gifts accumulated around us: flowers, liquor, a horse statue nobody claimed, and enough steaks in a refrigerated truck to suggest somebody believed ownership of a yard was a public catering commitment.

Shawna leaned close and gestured across the patio, taking in the house, the gifts and the lights coming on along the water. “All this yours?”

The lake lay beyond the dock. Lights on the other shore had begun appearing between the houses. I looked at what she was seeing and felt the dangerous pleasure of being able to give an answer large enough for it.

I rested my hand over hers. “Whatever you can point at.”

She pointed at the horse. “Who the fuck brought that?”

I promised to investigate.

Before I could, an engine rose over the music. A red speedboat came around the point and headed directly toward our dock. Its driver stood behind the wheel with his white shirt open to the waist. Two men sat behind him with rifles across their knees.

Beside me, Tank stopped eating. His plate went onto the nearest table as he turned toward the boat.

Tank spread his arms toward the party. “Everybody inside.”

Half the party kept talking. Then the boat hit the dock. Planks jumped against their fixings, and the people nearest the water turned toward the sound.

The man at the wheel cupped a hand beside his mouth. “Where the new owner at?”

I raised my glass. “Home.”

The driver pointed up at the house. “You bought this from my cousin.”

I looked from him to the house, giving the matter all the examination it deserved. “Your cousin looked old enough to sell it.”

The man gripped the wheel. “Brock had no right. That's my fucking house.”

Shawna stood beside my chair. “Your name on anything?”

The driver turned his head away from Shawna. “I’m talking to the man.”

I sat forward. “Then look at me while you regret that.”

His eyes came to mine. One of the riflemen shifted his weapon across his lap. Between songs, the music stopped and the silence spread over the patio. Women in the pool held the edge, jeweled hands gripping stone. One woman in the pool began moving hand over hand toward the steps. Another followed, keeping below the edge. I looked at the kitchen door and hated how much open patio they had to cross.

The visitor thumped a hand against his chest. “Roman Pike. Ask around.”

I pointed along the shore. “Go around. Dock's full.”

Roman put one foot on the planks. “I have a claim.”

I pointed at the splintered timber below him. “So does my insurance company. You just hit the fucking dock.”

He climbed out onto the damaged planks. The riflemen rose behind him and stepped onto the dock while he approached the patio. He came with his chin up and his shirt blowing open, as though he'd come over to inspect a purchase I hadn't been informed about. The gold chain against his chest bounced with every step. I could see the little marks his bare feet left on my dock.

Roman stopped at the end of the dock and lifted both empty hands. “Twenty thousand a month. Brock paid it. You pay it. We won't have no trouble.”

I looked past those empty hands at the two rifles still pointed our way. His idea of no trouble had brought its own witnesses.

I turned to Shawna. “He sold his house and left a subscription.”

She laughed into her hand. Roman watched her do it. His face tightened, and he drew the pistol from beneath his open shirt.

Tank moved first. Roman’s men lifted their rifles. Shots broke the quiet and the glass doors behind my chair exploded inward.

I pulled Shawna down with me. We struck the patio hard. The pain washed my sight white, but I kept my body over hers until the next shots went past. Glass hit the stones around us. Somewhere close, a woman was crying. I heard her say she'd only come for a drink, and I wanted to get every person on that patio inside before there was something worse to cry about.

Mercedes hauled two women out of the pool and drove them toward the kitchen door. Porsche caught Leon by his collar and dragged him behind his Bentley at the end of the patio drive.

Leon tried to look over the Bentley's hood. “My fucking car!”

Porsche put a hand on his head and shoved him down. “Your head first!”

I'd seen Rosa take my pistol off the table as Roman drew his. Now she came across the patio on her side, keeping low, with the pistol flat against the stone ahead of her.

Rosa pushed the grip against my fingertips. “Housewarming gift.”

I closed my hand around it. “You shouldn't have.”

Rosa flattened herself behind a planter. “I didn't. It’s yours.”

Roman ran back along the dock. His riflemen covered him while he got behind the wheel. Tank fired from the stone barbecue, striking planks around their feet and making them draw back toward the boat. A round broke a champagne bottle above him. He flinched as it poured over the shoulder of his suit.

Tank wiped champagne off his cheek with his shoulder. “Now I’m fucking mad!”

I looked toward the Bentley. It stood at the end of the short patio drive. Fifty feet of lawn separated it from the dock, broken by two chairs and a low stone border around a raised bed. Beyond that border, the damaged end of the dock sat almost level with the boat’s rear deck.

The boat’s engine screamed. Roman was turning away from the shore, bringing the hull broadside for a moment before it could leave.

Shawna followed my gaze from the car to the water, then caught my sleeve. “Again?”

I turned my pistol's muzzle toward the ground before getting up. “Different boat.”

Shawna tightened her hold. “Aiden.”

I looked toward the kitchen. “Get inside, baby.”

She caught my face and kissed me, hard and brief. Then Shawna looked me right in the eyes. “Then come back in my house.”

She moved toward the kitchen while I ran for the Bentley, bent over the wound. Porsche saw me coming and opened the driver's door. Leon held out both hands as if the coming damage were a physical object he might persuade us to hand back.

Leon put a palm against the open car door. “It’s new!”

I ducked under his arm. “So’s my house!”

I dropped into the seat and tucked the pistol between my thigh and the console. Porsche climbed in beside me. “I know this car.”

I grabbed the wheel. “Then introduce us fast.”

Porsche pointed out the selector, then pulled her seat belt across her chest. I fastened mine, put the car in drive, and pressed the accelerator. The rear tires tore out of the drive and onto the lawn, throwing clods behind us. The chairs went beneath the bumper. At the dock, Roman looked over his shoulder. His mouth fell open. He pulled hard at the boat's wheel while I came across the lawn in Leon's proposal.

The front wheels struck the raised border. The Bentley pitched upward, cleared the broken end of the dock, and came down across the boat’s rear deck.

Fiberglass burst beneath us. The hood folded. The speedboat lurched beneath the weight, its stern driven deep and its bow kicking upward. Roman struck the wheel, lost his hold, and went over the side. Spray covered the windshield. Porsche ducked behind the dash while the seat belt drove into my chest and every injured part of me demanded a separate explanation.

For a moment, we stopped moving.

The spray cleared. I was sitting in a car on a boat outside a house I had only just bought. Through the cracked windshield, I could see people clustered at the kitchen door. Several were screaming my name. One man held a steak over his head. I didn't know what kind of help he thought the steak would be, but I appreciated his willingness to commit it.

Porsche lifted her head. “You owe Leon a car.”

I pointed through the windshield at Roman's head coming out of the water. “Nigga owes me a dock.”

Roman coughed beside the boat. Both his hands were open as he tried to keep his head above water; I couldn't see the pistol. One rifleman had jumped into the shallows and stood with his hands up. Tank held his gun on the other at the end of the broken dock.

I leaned out of the Bentley and called Roman’s name. He pushed wet hair out of his eyes.

I braced my elbow on the window frame. “House still yours?”

Roman shook his head, coughing.

I leaned a little farther out. “Speak. I bought acoustics.”

Roman spat water and shouted up at me. “Your house!”

I tapped the Bentley's door. “Whose car?”

Roman stared at me. His mouth opened, but all that came out was another cough.

“Wrong answer.” I looked past him toward the patio. “Leon! Come get your speedboat!”

Leon stood with both hands on his head. Behind him, Mercedes climbed onto a chair she had found still usable and raised two bottles. Rosa held Shawna around the shoulders. Tank started laughing while keeping his gun on the prisoner, then told the man to stop fucking looking at him, as if the captive had caused the problem by witnessing joy.

Shawna came to the edge of the broken dock and held out her hands. I unfastened my belt, retrieved the pistol from beside my thigh, and secured it inside my waistband. Then I climbed out slowly. There was nothing impressive about that part. My legs shook when they took my weight, and the car shifted beneath me as the boat settled lower.

She caught both my hands and drew me across the last gap.

Shawna held on while my feet found solid dock. “You are the most ridiculous man I’ve ever met.”

I tried to stand straight for her. “Best-looking too.”

She brought my forehead against hers. “I married a dream tonight and I haven’t even married you.”

Over her shoulder, I saw Porsche climbing out onto the crushed rear deck. Tank took her forearm and helped her step onto the dock. She walked past us holding one broken fingernail up for inspection.

Behind us, Leon announced that he had never loved an automobile more and never wanted to see it again. The boat sank another inch beneath the Bentley, politely ending the option.

Knox's officers came down the side path while I sat on the patio, and I watched them take custody of Roman and his men. Roman left wet. I hoped the seats in the police car were cloth. My new dock was broken, the lawn looked excavated, and one of the patio doors hung in glittering pieces. The kitchen remained full of friends arguing about who had screamed first. I held on to that last fact while Shawna pressed a fresh towel against my side.

Knox called that night.

Knox got straight to the part that concerned her. “Two boats in five days?”

I shifted the phone to my other ear. “First one I cleared.”

“That distinction may save me a form,” Knox said. I heard paper move beside her phone. “Could you do that in uniform?”

Shawna lay curled against me on the sofa. The house was still awake around us. I looked at the top of her head and smiled.

I looked at my bandages beneath the open shirt. “Depends how nice you make the uniform.”

The club opened for me the following night.

I wore white because I wanted the room to know I wasn't expecting to bleed. This was a clothing decision, not a medical prediction. Shawna wore blue silk with her back bare and long earrings that touched her shoulders. Rosa had done her hair. I had bought the earrings. Dante’s watch remained on her wrist, loose beneath the new beauty we had arranged around her.

Blue lights ran the length of the sidewalk. Above the entrance, my name stood in letters taller than the apartment windows I had grown up behind. They had put it there without asking. I got out and looked up long enough for a driver behind us to lean on his horn.

Shawna took my arm. “They waiting on you.”

I kept looking up at my name. “Let them.”

Inside, the west side had dressed for its own imagined future. Velvet jackets crowded the tables. Champagne bottles sweated in silver tubs. Through the open door of a private room, I saw cash beside a mirror striped with cocaine, all of it lit more gently than the customers deserved. Women I knew from bad months wore shoes that cost more than those months. I recognized men who owed half the room money. They were buying bottles with both hands. Apparently their plan was to get the creditors too drunk to count.

A dancer stepped down from her platform and kissed my cheek. “You made it, Aiden.”

I reached into my jacket. “We made it.”

I put a stack of cash in her hand. She held it against her chest before climbing back up. Shawna saw the gift and moved closer to my side.

At a rear table, a man in a silver shirt stood when we approached. We had spent a reckless weekend together the previous year, broke and drunk, promising things off his balcony that neither of us could afford. I remembered telling him one day I would own the view. He looked at the room behind me as if checking whether I had finally made the down payment.

He kissed the corner of my mouth before I could turn. Shawna’s arm stiffened through mine.

The man in the silver shirt put a hand on my shoulder. “Look at you. Knew you had something.”

“He still has it,” Shawna told him.

The man smiled at her. “Keep it, girl. Cost me a weekend’s sleep.”

At our table, Shawna stopped with her hand on the back of a chair. “How many people in here you fucked?”

I looked around. Purely to improve accuracy.

Shawna caught my chin and turned it back toward her. “Don’t look around. Answer me.”

I pointed at my mouth, seeking a useful limit. “We counting kisses?”

Shawna hit my good shoulder with her purse. “I want you sitting your ass down.”

I sat. Surviving T had not made me interested in fighting Shawna in those earrings.

She settled sideways on my lap. Pain pulled me forward before I could hide it. Shawna immediately lifted her weight off me. Then she moved into the chair beside me, keeping her hand on my thigh. Whenever someone approached, she drew me a little closer by the collar before letting go.

I loved being claimed in public. That is the honest part. People who had wanted me for a weekend could look across the table and see that somebody expected me on Monday. I had spent so much of my life feeling leaveable that even her jealousy looked like a place to put my coat.

Then the music stopped.

Rosa stood onstage in the red velvet coat, gold shining beneath it. She took the microphone and said somebody owed her a song.

Shawna shook her head before her name was mentioned.

“I ain’t said your name yet, bitch,” Rosa told her.

People began clapping. Rosa held out her free hand toward our table. She left it there without adding another word. The clapping continued while Shawna stared at that hand.

Shawna turned toward me. “You set this up?”

“Wasn't me,” I told Shawna. “I'd have bought an orchestra. There'd be a nigga standing on your dress with a tuba.”

She laughed once, then looked back at Rosa's outstretched hand. Shawna placed her own hand against her stomach. I moved my chair so she had room to get past.

She pushed her chair back and rose. Her first steps looked harder than mine coming out of the hospital. At the stage, Rosa kissed her cheek and passed her the microphone.

Rosa leaned close enough to Shawna that the microphone caught her voice. “You know the one.”

Shawna closed her eyes. The piano began.

She sang about a porch light left burning. About a car passing the house. About getting dressed for someone who wasn't coming home.

Her first line trembled. She steadied the microphone with her other hand and kept going. By the second verse, men had stopped pouring drinks. A woman at the bar bent over her own hands. Shawna’s voice moved through the room without needing to be loud. It made every table feel close to where she stood.

The watch slid down her wrist when she lifted the microphone.

Behind me, a man at the next table leaned toward his companion. I heard him whisper that it was Dante's song.

I turned so fast the wound pulled. He wasn't even looking at me. His face was lifted toward the stage; he wiped one eye with his thumb and went still again. I couldn't find a sneer, a challenge, any little sign I could take as permission to be angry at him. The ugly feeling in my chest belonged to me.

My name glowed outside. Inside, the woman I had brought home sang for her husband.

I swallowed what was in the glass before me. Then Vega’s instructions arrived in my head and I set it down, furious that I needed a doctor to supervise my jealousy while a dead man got a whole fucking song.

On the last verse, Shawna opened her eyes and looked directly at me.

I didn't know which of us she saw. She held my eyes through the end of the verse, and I hated that I couldn't just be happy she was looking at me.

When the song ended, the club rose. People struck their tables and shouted. Rosa cried without hiding it. I stood slowly and clapped until my palms hurt.

Shawna came back smiling. She was still holding the microphone until Rosa hurried after her and took it from her hand. Shawna laughed at herself, then reached for me.

“Did you like it?” Shawna asked. Her face was bright; she'd barely seemed able to cross the room before the song, and now she stood close enough for me to feel her breathing.

I kissed her before I answered. Long enough for people to see. Long enough to turn a question about her singing into a demonstration of who stood beside her.

“Beautiful,” I said when I let her go.

She touched my face. “Why you looking at me like that?”

“Like what?” I asked.

Shawna's smile faded. She took her hand from my cheek. “Never mind, baby.”

Later, a man from the cash tables caught Rosa by her coat as she tried to leave him. I saw her stop, turn, and remove his hand. He put it back.

“I paid,” he said.

Rosa peeled his fingers off her coat again. “You paid for last time.”

The man caught a fresh handful of velvet and pulled Rosa a step back toward his chair.

I was out of the chair before Tank reached me. Shawna said my name behind me, but I was watching the man's fingers bunch Rosa’s velvet. He looked over his shoulder, saw me coming, and continued to hold on.

I hit him once.

The blow sent him to the carpet. Something beneath my bandages pulled hard enough to turn my stomach. Tank caught my elbow before I could throw a second punch.

“Take your hands off her,” I told him.

He wasn't holding anything by then. I pointed at him anyway. “You hear me?”

The man nodded from the floor.

Rosa straightened her coat. Her hands shook. Our eyes met, and she tucked both hands into her pockets.

“Get him out,” I told Tank.

“Sit down,” Tank told me.

I pointed at the man again. “His ass first.”

Tank beckoned to two of our men and passed the customer to them. Then he took my arm and walked me into the office while Shawna and Rosa followed.

The people we passed had gone quiet. A woman moved her chair to clear our path. A man started to raise his glass, saw the red spreading down my shirt, and set it on the table. I had come in imagining a whole night of people admiring me. Now Tank had to hold me upright because I'd thrown one punch. Somewhere behind us, Rosa's heels kept striking the floor, and I listened until I knew she was following.

In the office, Shawna unbuttoned my jacket and found red coming through the shirt.

Shawna pressed a folded towel over the blood. “You dumb motherfucker.”

I leaned back against the office chair. “I'm okay.”

Shawna looked up at me without taking pressure off the towel. “You don't know another fucking sentence?”

Rosa stood by the door with both hands hidden inside her coat. She started to say she hadn't needed me to do that.

“I know,” I told her. “I wanted.”

She pressed her lips together. Then she came over and kissed the top of my head again.

Rosa kept her mouth against my hair a moment. “Stupid nigga.”

She stayed while Shawna found a clean shirt. Tank stood in the doorway. When someone came to ask about the music, he told them to turn it back on, and the bass reached us faintly through the office wall.

At home, the lake was black beneath the windows. Shawna stood at the bathroom counter removing the earrings. She laid them side by side, then began opening the clasp of Dante’s watch.

I watched from the doorway. I was tired enough to feel my knees shaking. That should have warned me to sit down and stop talking. Instead I offered to buy her a watch that fitted.

She held the clasp open against her palm.

Shawna held up the watch. “I have one.”

I looked at the loose links gathered in her palm. “It hangs off you.”

Shawna lowered her hand. “I like it.”

I rubbed the doorframe with my thumb. “I can see that.”

She turned toward me. The room had enough marble to carry a whisper, and suddenly every small movement sounded accusatory.

Shawna set her free hand flat on the counter. “Say it, Aiden.”

I looked at the watch again. “That song was about Dante?”

Shawna answered before I could look away. “Yes.”

She gave me the truth without padding it. I wanted one little lie to lie down beside. Something that would let me postpone the work of sharing a room with a past I couldn't defeat.

I forced my eyes back to her face. “You look at me when you sing to him?”

Shawna stared at me. “I looked at you because you were there.”

“I'm always there,” I said. “His watch. My earrings. His chain on my fucking neck. I put you in a house and he already wanted the water.”

Shawna looked at the cross against my clean shirt. I wished I had stopped at the watch. Instead I spread my hands, inviting her to admire the case I'd made.

She reached toward the cross. “You want it off?”

I caught her wrist.

We both stopped.

I let go. Quickly, but the quickness didn't erase the fact that my hand had closed around her before I had decided what right I thought I possessed.

“Don't,” I said.

She rubbed the place my fingers had been.

“I'm sorry,” I said. I put my hands at my sides and left them there. She watched me for a moment before letting her hand fall.

“What do you want me to give you, Aiden?” Shawna asked.

I looked down at the floor I had bought and couldn't find a place on it that made me feel less lost.

I could barely make myself look up when I answered. “One thing that’s only mine.”

The tight line beside her mouth eased. She didn't apologize, and I didn't ask her to. She came close and laid her palm over my heartbeat, clear of the wound.

“This is you,” she said. “I know who this is.”

Then I asked why she had said his name in the hospital.

She closed her eyes. I wished I had kept the question inside. I also knew I had been carrying it around looking for somewhere to set it down, and the watch had only been a convenient table.

“I dreamed he was there,” she said. “I was trying to tell him you were hurt.”

I didn't know what to do with that. I had imagined his name taking me out of the bed. What she was telling me now put us both there: her dead husband in a dream, me bleeding in the world she had to wake up to. I'd spent days punishing myself with a story she hadn't told.

She put the watch on the counter and took my hand. Before leading me to bed, she picked it up again and carried it with us. She laid it on her bedside table and helped me lower myself onto the mattress. We had not solved the argument. The watch stayed within her reach. The cross stayed on my neck. She kissed me until I stopped asking questions that needed more of us than that exhausted night could give.

She switched off the bedside lamp and put her head against my good side.

I woke once before dawn. The lake beyond the curtains had begun to separate itself from the sky. I could hear Shawna breathing beside me, feel the weight of her hand against the sheet.

She was still there.

For that morning, that was enough.
