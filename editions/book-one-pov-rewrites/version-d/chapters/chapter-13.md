## 13. Everybody Bring Your Heart

At nine forty, I put my fist through the front door.

I owned the fucking door. Paid for the glass, the frame, the handsome handle I had apparently decided was beneath me. None of that stopped the glass from cutting my hand. Being rich had failed to make even my own house respect my authority.

Tank caught my wrist before I could hit it again.

“Aiden.”

I swung at him with the other hand. Dante's folder fell from under my arm onto the path. Tank's head went sideways, but his grip stayed closed.

“Let go,” I told him.

“No.”

“Had the money this whole time.”

“Yeah,” Tank said.

I hit him again. Something in my side pulled so hard my knees tried to leave. I wanted to drag him into the broken doorway. He turned and held me against the wall instead, putting all that solid weight between me and the glass.

Tank could've ended an argument by falling asleep on the other person. That night I wasn't even making him work at it.

“You let me walk in there thinking I knew what was mine,” I said. “Let him laugh at me.”

Tank looked past my shoulder into the house.

“Heard him,” he said. “Was standing right there.”

I shoved once more. He let me go when my hands stopped trying to reach anything.

The front room still looked like money had come home drunk. Champagne in silver buckets. A red heel beneath the piano. Four men spread between the couches, suddenly remembering appointments elsewhere.

On a side table, somebody had put cocaine on a photograph of me and Shawna. Her face was beneath it. All the fucking surfaces in that house, and a man had picked the only one I couldn't tolerate.

I swept the table clear.

“Everybody out.”

The four men got up. One had a woman's purse over his shoulder. He looked down, turned toward the hallway to find her, then looked back at my face and decided he could notify the owner from somewhere outdoors.

Tank picked up Dante's folder and brought it inside. He pushed the ruined door as far shut as it would go.

“Want me gone too?” he asked.

His jacket was torn. One cuff link was missing. He had blood on his hands from people I'd watched him carry, as well as the blood I'd put on his mouth. I didn't know how to fit all of him into one answer.

“Get the money,” I said.

He went upstairs.

I sat on the piano bench because it was close and my legs had begun shaking. Shawna had sat there that afternoon, missed a note, and blamed the piano. I'd offered another piano. She'd asked whether I planned to replace the instrument every time she made a mistake.

“Yes,” I'd told her.

Meant it too. We would've had a fucking orchestra by Christmas.

Her perfume was still near the bench. I put my head down and saw her dancing with Red. Her hand against his cheek. We'd agreed she had to make him believe. I'd said I could stand there and take it. Then I'd arrived in my king costume and discovered I couldn't take shit.

A heavy thump came from upstairs.

“Don't drop her money,” I shouted.

“Got it,” Tank called back.

“You keep saying that.”

He came down carrying two bags. No silver case. He'd left the broken case at the motel and brought the cash here, through the same door I'd believed belonged to the three of us.

He opened the bags on the dining table. Three million dollars lay between the candles Shawna had bought because the house smelled, according to her, like men lying to each other.

My girl had selected the correct fragrance.

“All of it,” Tank said.

“Counted it?”

“More than once.”

“Feel good doing that?”

He shook his head.

I took a bundle and threw it at his chest. The band broke. Money slid over his shoes.

“Your fucking tip.”

He watched it settle.

“Look at me,” I said. “What did you think was going to happen?”

Tank looked up. “Thought you were going to die.”

The refrigerator hummed from the kitchen. I remember that noise. Three million dollars open on a table, two men bleeding, and the refrigerator carrying on with its little job.

Tank rubbed his hands over his head.

“That morning,” he said. “Before she drove. You kept saying water, house, room for singing. She kept telling you to look at her. Half the time you weren't answering what she asked.”

I could remember her voice. Couldn't put every word back. I didn't want him doing it for me.

“So you went shopping,” I said.

“Went back,” Tank answered. “T was there. Money was there. Ten years I stood behind him. Every room. Every car. Somebody said his name wrong, I was the man who got up.”

“Loved him.”

“Yeah.”

I stared at him. “Say that again.”

“Loved him,” Tank said. “Want another lie?”

His mouth was bleeding down his chin. He let it.

“You knew him one night. Knew him ten years myself. Know what he was. Give you something with one hand, keep the other round your neck. Know all that. Still got to that pool and wanted him to stand up.”

I looked at the bills on the floor.

“When he didn't?”

Tank swallowed. “Took what was left.”

That didn't make it right. Didn't make the money his. But it was the first answer that let me see him standing there, alone in a place where his whole life had just stopped giving orders.

“Red called after?” I asked.

Tank nodded. “Said he knew. Money bought him whatever he asked.”

“Believed him?”

“For a while.”

“Until when?”

Tank looked at my chest. “Put his hand on her, and you stopped breathing.”

“Don't tell me what I looked like.”

“Like you did in the car.”

I picked up a candle and hurled it into the empty fireplace. Glass broke against brick. The flame went out.

“Not dying in another fucking car,” I told him.

Tank stayed beside the table.

“Then let me drive,” he said. “Let me get one thing right.”

***

Blue lights moved across the windows.

For one stupid second, I thought somebody had brought Shawna home. I was on my feet before the cars stopped.

Tank looked through the front window.

“Not ours,” he said.

Captain Wade Frost came through the broken door like knocking was a tax he had successfully avoided. Silver hair. Pink face. Two younger officers behind him. I recognized the woman, Bell, from the station when I'd brought in the robbers the day before.

They looked at the bags. Frost looked at my badge.

“Take it off,” he said.

“Selling shirts now?” I asked.

“The badge.”

Tank stepped between him and the table.

“Got an appointment?” Tank asked.

“Move.”

“House,” Tank told him, pointing at the floor. “Ask like you walked into one.”

Frost put his hand on his gun. Bell glanced at the other officer. Neither came closer.

I stepped around Tank.

“What you want?”

“Redmond filed a complaint,” Frost said. “Theft. Assault. Armed men at his property. A woman held against her will.”

My face went cold.

“Got that last part right.”

“We're recovering the money. Your authority is suspended pending investigation.”

“Who sent you?” I asked.

“You don't question me.”

“Doing it beautifully. Who?”

Frost held out a folded paper. I let his arm get tired.

“Knox sign it?” I asked.

“Chief has larger concerns.”

“Bought me a Lamborghini two days ago. Thinking about me plenty.”

Bell looked at the paper again. She'd eaten the lunch I'd bought the station. I could see her trying to figure out how that man had become the assignment standing in front of her.

“Bell,” Frost said. “Collect the bags.”

She stayed still.

“Order, Officer.”

I put my phone on the table and called Knox. Frost reached for it. Tank caught his wrist.

“Let the telephone ring,” Tank told him.

I picked it up when Knox answered.

“Tell me you have her,” she said.

Nigga, that nearly broke me. Not the money, not the fire, not a question about the badge she'd pinned on my chest. She wanted to know whether I had my girl.

“Not yet,” I managed. “Send Frost?”

Silence. Then Knox told me to put her on speaker.

I did and set the phone back on the table.

“Wade,” she said.

Frost stopped fighting Tank's grip.

“Chief, we're securing—”

“At his house?” Knox asked.

“Evidence of—”

“Wade. At his house?”

Frost looked down at the phone. “Yes.”

“Captain Aiden retains his command,” Knox said. “You have no order from me. Bring whatever Redmond gave you to my office. Bring your badge with it.”

Bell breathed out. Frost turned a shade redder than his friend had managed at the gala.

“Choosing this thug?” Frost asked.

“Say Captain while you cry,” I told him.

Knox didn't laugh.

“Choosing the officer who carried people out while Redmond locked the doors,” she said. “Get out of his house.”

Frost jerked toward the phone. Tank released his wrist just as I stepped forward. The captain stumbled against the table, knocking a candle over. One bag slid toward the edge.

He reached for the money with one hand and drew his pistol with the other.

I hit him before he brought it level.

He went backward through a chair. Money flew. His pistol struck the floor and skidded beneath the piano, which was an extremely expensive place to learn you weren't the police in charge.

Bell had her gun on him before he started getting up.

“Stay down, sir,” she said.

“His side too?” Frost demanded.

“Following my chief.”

“People keep explaining,” I told him. “Help us a little.”

He tried rising anyway. Tank put one hand on his shoulder and returned him to the carpet like a piece of furniture somebody had set in the wrong place.

I picked up the paper. Red's handwriting sat across the bottom: recover the money, disarm me, use Shawna's name to make me cooperate.

I gave it to Bell.

“Her rescue money,” she said after reading it. “That's what he sent you for.”

Frost didn't answer.

Bell cuffed him. The other officer retrieved the pistol from under the piano, then took the paper as evidence.

“Where do you need us after this, Captain?” he asked me.

Tank had knelt to gather the scattered bills. Shawna's future lay beside a chair leg and a police captain's shoe.

“With Knox,” I told the officer. “Keep anybody else from coming here in a uniform Red borrowed.”

He nodded. “Yes, Captain.”

They took Frost through the doorway he hadn't bothered knocking on. The lights crossed the ceiling and went away.

Knox was still on speaker.

“Aiden.”

“Here.”

“Officers are searching the Saint,” she said. “Haven't found her yet. Your car's safe. Can bring it.”

“Keep roads open. My people are going to the unfinished casino. Desert. Midnight. That's where he's taking her.”

“I'll hold the outside,” Knox said.

I looked at my badge beneath the blood.

“Won't forget this, Chief.”

Her voice softened a little. “Bring your wife home, Captain. Give me something worth remembering.”

She ended the call before I corrected a word I wanted so badly to make true.

Tank put the last bundle in its bag and stood the candle up.

“Really are the police,” he said.

“Discussed it at graduation.”

“Heard the words.”

“Seeing the picture?” I asked.

He nodded. Then my brother smiled at me, just a little, through his swollen mouth. I let him.

***

Rosa came in with Marcellus on her arm. A man's coat covered her gala dress. One earring was gone. She stopped at the blood on the wall, then looked straight at my hand.

Tank went out for the blue box I'd left in the Mercedes and brought it to Marcellus. The old man held it closed as though there were something dead inside.

My phone rang before Rosa reached the table.

Rex Crown.

I nearly ignored him. Then I remembered the man hitting a guard from behind to get us out and answered.

“Captain,” Rex said.

“Busy.”

“Know. Your woman.”

I shut my eyes. “Everybody knows?”

“Still outside saying your name,” he said. “Saw you bring those people out.”

“Need her. Not my name.”

“Give me somewhere to bring myself, then.”

I looked at Tank, at the cash, at my hand dripping onto the cloth Rosa had silently put beneath it.

“How bad you hurt?” I asked Rex.

“Pride's worse than my face.”

“Stay home with it.”

“Didn't call for a bedtime,” Rex said. “Could've let the same people who watched you beat me watch me die. Came back. Give me somewhere to put that.”

I looked toward the dark lake windows.

“Unfinished casino. Desert. Midnight.”

He repeated it, then said he would be there.

“Your friend with the gun—” I began.

“Not coming. Got better friends.”

I laughed once. It hurt enough to make Rosa look up.

“What?” she asked.

“Nigga making sense.”

Rex heard her. “Tell those women the check's good. Don't stop being a gentleman because somebody rearranged my evening.”

Rosa took the phone.

“Bring your gentleman ass ready to help,” she told him.

“Yes, ma'am.”

She ended the call and gave the phone back.

“Let them,” she said to me.

“Let who?”

“People who love you. Let them do something.”

She touched my good shoulder, then pointed toward the entrance.

“Which stupid motherfucker broke the door?”

Nobody answered. She looked at my hand.

“Of course.”

She took my wrist. Her fingers were shaking. Hadn't noticed that while she was walking around ordering us back into being people.

“Where is she?” Rosa asked.

“Red—”

“Know fucking Red. Where?”

“Bringing her to the casino. Midnight.”

“Then why you bleeding on furniture?”

I started answering. She tightened her grip.

“Get angry after she's here,” Rosa said. “Buy a mountain. Fight the whole mountain. Right now, stand up without falling down.”

“Don't talk to me like a kid.”

“Stop making it necessary.”

Tank turned his face away.

“Funny?” I asked him.

“No.”

“Good.”

Rosa sat me on the bench and went for a thicker towel. Marcellus stayed beside the money.

“Yours?” he asked.

“Hers.”

He nodded. Apparently that was all the accounting he needed.

***

My phone vibrated on the table as Rosa came back. She picked it up, saw the screen, and stopped.

Red had sent a photograph.

White chair. Shawna sitting in it. Blue dress torn at her shoulder. Hair over one side of her face. Red behind her with his hand beneath her chin and his ring turned toward the camera.

I stood so fast the bench fell over.

Rosa caught my shirt.

“Aiden.”

“Going to kill him.”

“Look at her,” Rosa said.

“Am looking.”

“Her.”

I forced my eyes off his hand. Shawna faced the lens. Her mouth was swollen on one side. Her eyes were open and furious. He'd gotten her into the chair. Hadn't gotten her to look away.

Below it, he'd written: *She remembers this room.*

I called him and turned on speaker so Rosa could hear.

“Touch her again—” I began.

“Sitting right here,” Red said.

“Put her on.”

“Ask nice.”

I couldn't get enough air. Rosa squeezed the back of my neck. I felt every finger.

“Please.”

Red laughed softly. I held the phone and listened to him enjoy the word.

Then Shawna was there.

“Aiden?”

“Baby.”

“Are you hurt?” she asked.

“No.”

“Don't lie to me.”

“Here,” I told her. “I'm here.”

I heard fabric move and a breath stop too early. Couldn't see why. Almost worse than the picture, having to invent what every sound meant.

“Tell Rosa I ruined her dress,” Shawna said.

Rosa pressed a fist to her mouth.

“Doesn't give a fuck about the dress, baby.”

“Does,” Shawna said. “Tell her I'll pay.”

Rosa took the phone.

“Owe me an earring too, bitch.”

Shawna laughed once.

That little laugh held the whole room still. For a second she was with us in a way Red's picture couldn't stop.

Then he took the line back.

“Midnight,” Red said. “Money, what you stole, and an apology.”

I took the phone from Rosa.

“Want me on my knees?” I asked.

“Now you're listening.”

“Keep her alive. See me any way you want.”

He hung up.

Tank righted the bench behind me. I stayed standing, holding a call that had ended.

Marcellus put out his hand.

“Photograph,” he said. “Let me see.”

I gave him the phone. I'd run out of things I could break that Red would feel.

The old man enlarged the picture. His thumb stopped over the ring.

“There you are,” he whispered.

I looked up. Marcellus had begun smiling, and there wasn't a happy thing in it.
