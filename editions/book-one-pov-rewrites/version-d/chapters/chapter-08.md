## 8. Let Him Think You Love Him

Jealousy discovered the seam.

I would like to offer a finer explanation. An eye for craftsmanship. Unusual powers of observation. But I had been staring at Dante's watch every time it touched Shawna's face, her pillow, the hand she laid on my knee. She had missed him for eight months. Since taking the watch from Valentino's glove compartment the night she killed him, she'd carried a piece of him everywhere she went. I had studied the motherfucker as if it might eventually apologize.

When its clasp shifted beneath her thumbnail, I saw a narrow line move where no line should have moved.

I caught her hand before she closed it. “Wait. Something there.”

Shawna held the watch beneath the bedside lamp. “Where?”

I pointed at the inner edge. “That bit. It moved.”

She let me turn it once between my fingers. “You know how to open it?”

I examined the seam from another angle. “No.”

Shawna held her palm out. “Then give it back before you become the next thing I hit with it.”

I returned the watch. “Make a discovery, museum throws me out.”

She called Rosa and put the phone on speaker between us. Rosa answered from elsewhere in the house, sounding as if we'd interrupted a much better conversation. Shawna asked for a jeweler she trusted.

“Trusted to do what?” Rosa asked through the phone. “Those are different men.”

Shawna turned the watch over in her palm. “Open this without breaking it.”

Rosa's voice brightened. “Got that nigga. Owes me money.”

I leaned toward the phone. “Makes him trustworthy?”

Rosa answered immediately. “Makes him findable. You want reliable or you want found?”

I looked at Shawna. “Send the findable one.”

An hour later we sat downstairs with the jeweler at our table. He had laid a cloth beneath the watch and opened a small case of tools. I watched him bring the clasp close to a loupe held against his eye. When I reached toward it, Shawna slid the cloth farther away from me without turning her head.

The jeweler lowered his hands. “Who built this?”

Shawna kept her hand at the edge of the cloth. “Marcellus Vale. Dante told me he worked on it.”

The jeweler took the loupe from his eye. “You know where he is?”

Shawna looked at me, then back at him. “Prison.”

The jeweler shook his head. “Got out nine days ago. Came asking about his old bench.”

Shawna grabbed my forearm. I put my hand over hers and leaned toward the man.

“Call him,” I said.

The jeweler reached into his pocket. “I'm not—”

I pointed at the phone emerging in his hand. “Then call him whatever you are. Call the fucking man.”

The jeweler paused with the screen lit. “Not certain he answers this number. What I was going to say.”

Shawna turned her head slowly toward me.

I sat back. “Your nigga's excited.”

Shawna returned her attention to the phone. “Your nigga's letting him dial.”

The jeweler made the call. I heard only his side at first: his name, a reminder of the visit to his shop, a question about a watch. Then Shawna held her hand out. He gave her the phone, and she told the man at the other end who she was.

Marcellus's answer came loudly enough for me to hear across the table.

“Dante's wife?” he asked through the phone.

Shawna covered her mouth and nodded. I touched her wrist. She blinked at me, then spoke so he could hear her too.

“Yes,” Shawna told him.

I kept quiet while she gave him the address and explained why we needed him. My part was to hold the hand she wasn't using. For once, I managed to leave it at that.

He arrived before sunset.

I saw him from the front steps: a tall, narrow man in a brown coat too heavy for the weather, with close gray hair and a paper bag tucked under his arm. Marcellus was sixty-two, though I learned the number after I learned how stubborn he could be about a shirt.

Our gate had been left partly open for him. He was turning through it when a car stopped outside and three men got out. The tallest caught the back of his coat. Marcellus turned, the paper bag slipped, and a folded shirt fell onto the dusty edge of the drive.

I put a hand against the porch rail. My dock was still broken. My car had come home tied shut with Tank's belt. Now somebody had decided my front gate needed a fucking incident too.

“Tank!” I shouted.

He came out through the side door with bread in his mouth. When he saw the men, he took it out and set it on the rail.

Tank looked at me. “Jeweler's friends?”

I started down the steps. “Holding him like enemies.”

Shawna came out behind me. Her footsteps stopped at the top of the stairs.

“Marcellus,” Shawna said.

I looked back at her face. Then I turned toward the gate and kept going.

The tall man had pulled Marcellus against the outside of the open gate leaf. He wore a purple suit, a pale scar along one cheek, and rings on nearly every finger. He looked about thirty-five. One unadorned finger stood out from the rest as if humility had been assigned there by mistake.

He watched me approach from inside the drive.

“Go back in,” the man in purple told me.

I stopped a few feet short of him. “Came out for you. Don't make the trip disappointing.”

His eyes moved toward Tank. He shifted his grip higher on Marcellus's coat.

“Private business,” the man said.

I rested a hand on the gate's inner edge. “My private gate. Keep finding me in your problem.”

One of the other men pulled his jacket open to show a pistol. I looked down at it, then up at his face.

“Buy a second one?” I asked him. “For when that one leaves you?”

He reached for the gun. Tank hit him before it cleared the jacket, driving him backward into the car. The rear window broke behind his shoulders. His pistol struck the drive, and Tank kicked it beneath the car. The third man swung at me. I caught his shoulder, turned with him, and put his face against the stone gatepost. He dropped to one knee, both hands rising to his nose.

Pain pulled through my side. I kept one hand on the gate to stay upright.

The man in purple dragged Marcellus in front of him. His own pistol appeared against the old man's coat.

I stopped moving.

“Don't,” Shawna called from behind me.

I didn't know which of us she meant. The gun lay flat against Marcellus's chest. Marcellus looked down, past the weapon, at the shirt in the dust.

“Pick it up,” Marcellus told the man holding him.

The man blinked. “What?”

Marcellus nodded toward the ground. “My shirt.”

The man in purple stared at the side of his head. “You out your fucking mind?”

Marcellus kept looking at the shirt. “I've been out nine days. That is the only shirt I have bought.”

The man looked at me. Marcellus kept staring at the shirt. He had apparently finished discussing the gun.

I held my free hand out. “Let him get it.”

The man tightened his arm across Marcellus. “Think I'm stupid?”

I kept my hand open. “Giving you a chance to surprise me.”

He pulled Marcellus backward, away from the gate's edge. The old man's heel caught the fallen paper bag. He stumbled, and the pistol moved a few inches off his chest as the man in purple tried to keep hold of both him and the gun.

I put both palms against the gate and shoved it outward.

The bars struck the man's forearm. His gun went off into the drive, taking a white chip from the paving beside my foot. Marcellus dropped clear. I caught the gunman's wrist against the iron before he could bring it around again.

His rings dug into my hand. The bones beneath them moved like any other man's.

The pistol fell. I drove him backward against the gate a second time and let him slide down the bars. Tank had forced his first man to the ground beside the car. He pulled the man I'd put into the pillar away from me and planted him beside the other.

Shawna ran past my shoulder and knelt with Marcellus. She got her hands beneath his arms.

“You hurt?” Shawna asked him.

Marcellus looked at the torn front of his coat. “My coat.”

Shawna lifted his chin. “Fuck the coat. You hurt?”

He shook his head. Mercedes came through the gate, bent to pick up the shirt, and shook the dust from it. I hadn't seen her arrive at the house, but there she was, taking charge of the one thing the old man had demanded from an armed stranger.

Mercedes brushed the sleeves, then held it by the shoulders against the sunlight. “Little wash. Handsome again.”

Marcellus looked at her, then down at the shirt. His mouth moved as if he had begun an answer and lost it. He took the cleanest corner of the cloth between his fingers.

Mercedes folded the shirt over her arm while I turned back to the man against the bars.

“Who are you?” I asked him.

He pressed his empty hand against his stomach. “Felix.”

I waited. “Felix what?”

The man swallowed. “Barrow.”

I pointed toward Marcellus. “What do you want with him?”

Felix looked at his fallen gun. “Owes people.”

I moved my foot beside the weapon so he would have to look at me again. “Which people?”

Felix shifted against the iron. “Inside. He made things. Promises got made.”

Marcellus had got to his feet with Shawna's help. He looked at Felix and shook his head once.

“I promised him nothing,” Marcellus said.

I looked back down at Felix. “There. He ain't got your promise. You ain't got your gun. Everybody learned something.”

Felix looked past me at the house, then toward Tank and the two men lying beside the car.

“You're that Aiden,” Felix said.

I bent just enough for him to hear me comfortably. “Which one you hoping for?”

Felix's eyes came back to mine. “Boat. Hearse.”

I shook my head. “Behind already. Two boats.”

Mercedes laughed with the shirt folded over her arm. Tank laughed too, keeping his eyes on the men at his feet. Marcellus looked from them to me. The corner of his mouth moved.

Felix leaned his head back against the bars. “Should've asked where he was going.”

I straightened slowly. “Should've stayed home.”

Tank called Knox's men. I left him by the gate and helped Marcellus toward the house. Shawna held the old man's other hand. Mercedes carried his shirt and the retrieved paper bag, holding the torn bottom closed with her palm.

At the front door, Shawna hurried ahead to fetch water. I stopped beside Marcellus and offered my hand properly.

“Aiden,” I said.

Marcellus took it. “I know.”

I glanced toward the drive. “People talking?”

Marcellus looked at Shawna crossing the living room. “Dante's widow said your name six times on the telephone.”

I liked him right then. He hadn't even asked for a favor yet and had already brought useful news.

His eyes dropped to the cross at my chest. He touched it with one fingertip, following the letters.

“I did those,” Marcellus said. “Dante sent the cross with the watch. Said his wife thought he was magnificent. He wasn't arguing.”

I looked down at the inscription, then at him. “Come inside.”

Marcellus paused on the threshold. “Dante would've liked you.”

I held the door wider. “Everybody gets there. Some men take the long way.”

He smiled, small but plain enough for me to see. For the first time, I could imagine him in the kitchen Shawna had described, holding a stone toward a patch of sunlight while a woman swore at him over chicken.

“Apparently,” Marcellus said, “I came to the right house.”

Cruisers pulled up at the gate while we stood on the threshold. I looked back and saw Tank pointing out the fallen pistol to an officer. Two others brought Felix and his men to their feet and began cuffing them. I waited until all three were in police hands, then took Marcellus inside.

Shawna set the water down when we reached the living room. She crossed toward him and stopped short. Marcellus opened his arms. She put her face against his coat, and he laid one hand between her shoulders.

His eyes closed. Hers were hidden. I stood close enough to hear her crying and gave them the space between the chair and the table. Until then I'd been the man listening to a story about both of them. Now they were in my living room, and I couldn't solve what they were feeling by buying another fucking thing for it.

Through the open front door, I saw Tank take a bag of food from a delivery driver before coming back from the gate. He saw Marcellus and Shawna together and stopped, letting the bag hang by his leg.

“Who this?” Tank asked me quietly.

I looked over at him. “Marcellus.”

Tank glanced toward the front door. Marcellus opened his eyes and looked at Tank's face for a long moment.

“Your friend?” Marcellus asked me.

I nodded. “Yeah.”

Marcellus looked back at Tank. “I see.”

He didn't add anything. Tank put the food down and went outside. I watched him leave, then turned toward the table where the jeweler had kept the watch waiting on its cloth.

Marcellus sat down and asked for the loupe.

The jeweler gave him room. I stood beside Shawna and watched Marcellus turn the clasp beneath the light. His hands hardly seemed to hurry at all. After the trouble at the gate, their steadiness held my attention almost as tightly as the gun had.

Marcellus held the clasp toward the light. “Dante asked where a man could keep a letter. Back when we were inside.”

He selected a narrow tool from the jeweler's case and turned it against his thumbnail to inspect the point.

“After he got out, an old client arranged a legal repair commission,” Marcellus told us. “Watch came in with the cross. I made the space and sent them back.”

I touched the letters at my chest. His hands had been on this too, in that cell, while Dante was still making plans that required him to remain alive.

Marcellus pressed a tool into the seam. A thin cover lifted. Folded paper lay beneath it.

He set the paper on the cloth, then laid both hands flat on the table.

Shawna picked it up. I moved beside her shoulder as she unfolded it. One edge had been torn from a longer note. The ink was red, the handwriting crowded and slanted exactly like the card I'd carried to breakfast.

I read the order with her.

*T — He has the withdrawal and Vale's story. If he gets the stone, he can name every one of us. Finish Dante tonight. No more brother shit. Valentino takes the watch. Keep her alive. I'll deal with her. R.*

Beneath it, in darker ink, Dante had added four words.

*Shawna. If I'm wrong.*

She went back to the beginning. I watched her eyes reach the bottom and begin again. The third time, her mouth opened but she couldn't get the sentence out.

“No,” Shawna said.

I reached toward her hand. She pulled away and stood so fast that her chair fell behind her.

“He knew?” Shawna asked, looking at Marcellus.

The old man kept his eyes on the paper. “He suspected.”

Shawna shook her head. “He fucking knew.”

I took the note before it could tear in her hands. Everything in me became very still.

Red had spoken about protection at breakfast. About the room he'd given Shawna. About buying her a dress. On this paper, he'd ordered her husband's death and reserved the widow for whatever he intended next. The same hand that offered me breakfast had decided who would get Dante's watch.

I looked at the signature.

“Nigga,” I said, so quietly that Marcellus turned toward me.

I still don't know who Dante got the note from. I didn't know why he hadn't run the moment he read it. He had written if beneath an instruction to kill him, as though a brother might explain the words until they meant something else.

I knew the kind of hope that could do that to a man. T had put his hand on my neck and called me son. I had wanted to believe him right up to the moment he ordered me back inside to fetch Shawna.

She stood by the living-room window with both hands on her head.

“He went to talk to his brother,” Shawna said. “Told me there was a mistake he needed to straighten out. I was mad he wouldn't stay for dinner.”

Marcellus closed his eyes.

Shawna turned toward us. “Told him I'd throw his plate away.”

Marcellus closed the empty compartment in the watch and set it beside the cloth. I laid the note on the table next to him and went toward Shawna.

“Shawna,” I said.

She held one hand out. “Don't say it.”

I stopped a step away. “Wasn't going to—”

Shawna's voice rose. “Don't say there was nothing I could do. Could've locked the door. Could've gone with him. Could've—”

I answered before she finished. “Died too.”

She slapped me across the mouth.

My head turned. The place that hadn't finished healing split again, and blood touched my tongue. I stayed where I was with my jaw clenched. When I looked back, her hand was still in the air.

“Aiden,” Shawna said.

Behind her, Mercedes had stopped beside the table. Marcellus's hands lay flat against the cloth. I could feel myself wanting to turn pain into something the room would have to deal with for me.

I took a breath through my nose. Then I caught Shawna's wrist, lowered her hand, and let it go.

“I'm going outside,” I told her.

I walked out through the patio doors.

Tank stood near the lake throwing pieces of bread to the birds. He turned when he heard me and looked at my mouth.

“What happened?” Tank asked.

I stopped beside him. “Don't.”

He put the rest of the roll on the low wall. I stood looking across the water until my breathing slowed enough that I didn't want to break something simply because it was available.

Tank kept his eyes on the lake. “She hit you?”

I turned toward him. “Said don't.”

Tank raised a hand. “All right.”

A bird walked between us, hopped onto the wall and took the entire roll. Tank watched it struggle to get the bread turned sideways in its beak before waddling away.

“That was mine,” Tank said.

I lowered myself onto the wall. “Go tell him.”

Tank stayed where he was. I almost laughed. It pulled at my mouth, so I stopped halfway through and wiped the blood with my thumb.

“Red ordered it,” I told Tank. “Dante. His own handwriting.”

Tank stared at the water.

I watched the side of his face. “You know?”

Tank shook his head. “No.”

I waited before asking the next thing. “Hear anything?”

Tank moved his jaw once. “Heard Dante took money.”

I looked down at the blood on my thumb. “All?”

Tank faced me. “All anybody said around me. Didn't know him like that. T had me moving things. Driving. Wasn't in every room.”

I wanted to believe him. I wanted somebody I loved to finish a story before another man arrived carrying the piece they'd left out.

I looked back at the house. “Taking Red's diamond.”

Tank followed my gaze. “Then?”

I touched the edge of my split lip. “Look at how poor he got.”

Tank folded the empty bread bag. “Forty million ain't everything he has.”

I kept watching the patio door. “Then I'll stay busy.”

Shawna came out carrying a wet cloth and the watch, closed again but loose in her other hand. Tank picked up the bread bag and walked toward the side of the house. She stopped in front of me.

“Shouldn't have done that,” Shawna said.

I looked at the cloth. “No shit.”

She held it a little higher. “Can I see?”

I let her touch my chin. She cleaned the blood from my mouth while I looked past her shoulder. I was still too angry to look directly at her without preparing something cruel to say.

“I love him,” Shawna told me.

I closed my eyes for a moment. “I know.”

She lifted the cloth away. “Love you too.”

I opened my eyes and looked at her. “That last night with Dante. Did you get to tell him you loved him before he left?”

Shawna shook her head. “Mad about dinner.”

Her answer took the next bitter sentence out of my mouth. She laid the cloth on the wall and touched my face with her empty hand.

“I love you,” Shawna said again.

I took that hand and pressed it against my chest, clear of the wound. I wanted her to feel me there, alive, without having to strike anything to find me.

“Don't hit me again,” I told her.

Shawna met my eyes. “I won't.”

I looked at the watch still in her other hand. “Don't leave that off to shut me up either. Put it on. When you tell me something, I want the whole thing.”

She lowered her eyes to the watch. Then she fastened it around her wrist.

We went back inside. Marcellus returned the loupe and tools to the jeweler, who packed his case and said goodbye. While the food was opened, Shawna went upstairs and returned with the blue invitations. Then we sat down together. Marcellus took a clear sleeve from inside his coat and laid it beside my plate. The folds in the paper inside had worn pale across the writing.

“Celeste's,” Marcellus told me. “What Dante heard.”

I put down my fork. Shawna rested her hand beside mine while I read the whole letter, including the lines she hadn't remembered upstairs. At one fold I held the sleeve closer to the lamp. Marcellus reached across, flattened the crease beneath two fingers, and withdrew his hand so I could finish.

When I returned it, he slid it inside his coat carefully, smoothing the pocket afterward with his palm.

I waited until he finished the movement. “Want your stone?”

Marcellus looked at me. “Yes.”

I kept my voice level. “Want Red dead?”

He looked at Shawna's face before answering. “Yes.”

I leaned back. “Think we can work together.”

Marcellus didn't smile. Shawna set the invitations on the table, and he picked one up.

“He's displaying it in three nights,” Shawna said.

Marcellus studied the printed necklace. “He's displaying something.”

I put my fork down. “Two of them.”

Marcellus nodded. “Yes.”

I tapped the edge of the card. “You tell which one's which?”

Marcellus looked up at me. “Of course.”

I spread my hand toward him. “Then you're going.”

He set the invitation down. “He knows my face.”

I looked at his coat. “Knows the man he put away eight years ago. Different suit. Different name.”

Rosa had joined us while we ate. She leaned forward to examine Marcellus with the same attention she'd given Shawna's dress.

“Know a woman can make you look like you have money,” Rosa told him.

Marcellus looked around my dining room, then at me. “Apparently people are easily confused.”

Tank covered his mouth with a napkin. I let the old man have it. He'd had a hard day and found a reliable use for mine.

Marcellus borrowed a pencil from Rosa and turned the invitation over. On its plain back he drew the blue outer setting, then a smaller outline inside it.

“This comes off,” Marcellus said, tapping the outer shape. “Stone can be worn another way.”

He put a tiny mark inside the second outline. “The true diamond has a flaw here. I remember exactly where.”

Shawna leaned close to the picture on the other invitation. “This necklace could be either?”

Marcellus nodded. “The mounting doesn't make the stone genuine.”

I looked at the card, then at the hands that had opened the watch. “Get you close enough to look, then.”

Red had made me stand beneath Shawna's photograph. He'd made sure I saw his white piano. At breakfast, he'd offered me my own streets in exchange for returning her to him. I believed there was something he wanted badly enough to bring us closer than he ought to. The trick would be persuading him it was already coming willingly.

Shawna sat straighter in her chair. “I can get him to invite us closer.”

I looked at her. “No.”

Shawna put a hand beside my plate. “Aiden.”

I shook my head. “No.”

She left her hand there. “Haven't heard what I'm saying.”

I looked at the way she'd squared her shoulders. “Heard how you sat up.”

Shawna took my hand. “I tell him I made a mistake. You scare me. I'm bringing you to the gala so he can handle it quietly.”

Tank leaned forward. “He believes that?”

Shawna looked at him. “He wants to.”

I tightened my fingers around hers. “What else?”

She returned to me. “Tell him I know where you're keeping the money.”

I looked toward the note on the table. “Don't have his money.”

Shawna squeezed my hand. “He doesn't know that.”

Tank set his glass down beside his plate. I heard the careful click and looked over for a moment. He was looking at the invitation. I went back to Shawna.

She continued. “I'm tired of running. I'll come back if he clears the debt and leaves you alive.”

I let out a short breath. “Why he leaving me alive?”

Shawna held my gaze. “Because I'll say you can give him the west side.”

I took my hand from hers.

Nobody spoke for a moment. Shawna leaned toward me across the table.

“Words he'll listen to,” she said. “You know that.”

I did. Knowing didn't stop the words from hurting. I had sat beneath his photograph of her that morning, and now we were discussing how convincingly she could promise to stand beside him again.

Marcellus folded the invitation closed. “If he thinks she can give you to him, he'll want you close enough to take.”

I looked at the old man. “He can try.”

Rosa put both palms on the table. “Near the jewel. He looks. We leave with what's his. Nobody gets mad halfway through and decides it's time to kill somebody.”

She looked at Shawna first. Shawna didn't answer. Then Rosa pointed at me.

“You either,” Rosa said.

I looked around the table. “Why everybody looking at me?”

Rosa pointed at my face. “Because you have it.”

Tank nodded. I looked at him until he lowered his eyes to his plate, still nodding.

Shawna made the call from our bedroom that night. I sat beside her while she brought Red's number up on the screen. She raised the volume enough for me to hear and held the phone between us.

Red answered on the second ring.

“There she is,” Red said through the receiver.

Shawna closed her eyes. I put a hand on her knee.

“Can you talk?” Shawna asked him.

Red's answer came softly. “For you?”

I wanted to take the phone and drive it through the wall. I kept my hand where it was.

“Think I made a mistake,” Shawna told him.

The line went quiet. Beyond the bedroom glass, the lake had gone black. Our reflection showed her holding the phone and me beside her, looking far less like a man helping with a plan than a man waiting to hear how the plan would hurt.

“He hurt you?” Red asked.

Shawna looked at the cut on my mouth. “Don't know what he'll do next.”

Red answered quickly. “Come home.”

Shawna lowered her eyes. “Can't just leave.”

Red drew a breath I could hear through the receiver. “You can.”

Shawna kept the phone steady. “You want the money. His streets. I can bring him to you, Red. Have to promise me something.”

Red didn't pause. “Anything.”

I stared at the wall. His written order to kill Dante had been shorter than the promises he was making to Dante's widow.

“Let him live,” Shawna said.

Red sighed into the phone. “Too good to these boys.”

My hand left her knee. Shawna caught it before I could stand and pressed it flat against the sheet.

“I know where he's keeping the money,” Shawna continued. “Can get him to bring what you need. At the gala I stay close to you. Everybody sees I'm back. After that, you talk to him.”

Red's voice came lower. “And you stay?”

Shawna looked straight at me. “Yes.”

He let out a long breath. I heard satisfaction in it, or what sounded like satisfaction to a man forced to sit there and listen. My fingers curled beneath hers. She kept holding them.

“Wear the blue dress,” Red said. “The one I liked.”

Shawna didn't look away from me. “I will.”

Red said her name again before she could end the call.

“What?” Shawna asked him.

Red answered slowly. “Knew you'd remember who loved you.”

She ended the call without replying. The room was silent except for the air coming through the vent.

I knew it was a lie. I had sat at the table and helped make it. I still felt every word as if she had tried it on against my skin.

Shawna put the phone facedown, took my face between her hands, and waited until my eyes met hers.

“I'm here,” Shawna told me.

I pulled her close. Too hard. My side burned, and I flinched against her. Shawna moved against my uninjured side, taking my hand with her, and held it at her shoulder until my breathing settled.

Downstairs, I could hear chairs moving and Tank's low voice on a call. I couldn't make out the words. Before we came up, Marcellus had spread the invitations beside his letter and Rosa had begun making arrangements for clothes. The house had filled with people preparing to take something back.

I held Shawna and pictured Red standing where I had stood that afternoon: looking at an empty hand, trying to understand who had left him with it.

Three nights.

I could wait three nights.

I told myself that until morning.
