Shawna told me later what Red had been saying on that floor. I asked for every word. Some I had to hear twice because the first time I was too busy getting angry to listen properly. The conversation I'm giving you now is hers. That night, I could see their faces and hands from beside the windows, but the music swallowed the words.

She said he began when they passed close to the piano. He bent toward her ear again, his arm drawing her around the next couple.

“You used to sing this upstairs,” Red said.

“I sang a lot of things,” Shawna answered.

His hand moved farther around her waist. “Before Dante.”

Shawna looked up at him. “I had a life before all of you.”

Red brought her through a turn. Her watch touched his cuff, and the blue stone on his hand passed beneath the light.

“You could have that life now,” Red told her.

“Could I?” Shawna asked.

He smiled. “Whatever you want.”

She looked down at the ring. Red rotated his hand inward and took another step, drawing her after him.

“I want him left alone,” Shawna said.

Red's mouth stopped moving with the song. “Already thinking about that nigga?”

“His name is Aiden,” she told him.

Red raised his chin. “I know what the nigga's name is.”

A couple passed close. He smiled at the man; Shawna made herself smile at the woman. She remembered the effort of doing that. I remembered seeing the result from the side of the floor and giving it a very different meaning.

“You said you were tired of running,” Red told her when the couple had passed.

Shawna kept moving. “I am.”

“You said you wanted to come home,” Red said.

She raised her face. “I said I wanted somewhere I could sleep.”

Red lowered his voice. “My bed was never the problem.”

Shawna drew her shoulder back. He kept hold of her. I had seen that small movement without knowing what had caused it; afterward she told me how hard she'd had to work to keep her voice level.

“Don't do that tonight, Shawna,” Red said.

“Then don't talk to me like that,” she answered.

For a moment they stopped, with the other couples turning around them. Red held their joined hands between their bodies.

“Do you know what it felt like?” he asked. “Standing there while he put that ring on you? Paying for a room where you were going to spend the night with him?”

Shawna looked him in the face. “Dante was your friend.”

“Nigga knew,” Red said.

“Knew what?” she asked.

Red lifted their hands a little higher. “That his nigga saw you first.”

When Shawna told me what she said next, she waited until I stopped moving around the room and looked at her.

“You all keep saying that like I was a fucking parking space,” she told Red.

I didn't need her to repeat that one.

She said his fingers tightened, then loosened. Another guest raised a glass toward them, and Red smiled back before he began dancing again.

“I could've given you everything,” he told Shawna.

She waited until they'd passed the next couple. “Then give me the diamond.”

Red slowed. Shawna kept moving, bringing his arm with her until he followed.

“That's what tonight is for,” she said. “You wanted me to know how serious you were. Show me.”

Red looked across the room. “And Aiden?”

“You let him walk out,” Shawna said.

Red kept looking at me. “Will he?”

She placed her palm against his cheek. “He will if I tell him to.”

Back at the gala, watching without any of those words, I saw her palm on his face and his head leaning toward it. I hadn't heard her ask for the stone or demand that he leave me alive. I'd seen her touch him and supplied the rest myself.

I retrieved the glass from the table, finished the drink Tank had brought me, and put it down again.

Then I went onto the floor.

Tank said my name. Rosa stepped toward me. I went around her before she could put a hand on my arm. I told myself I'd only take Shawna aside for a moment, check whether she was all right, make sure Red remembered I was still standing in the room.

“Dance is over,” I told Red when I reached them.

He kept moving, drawing Shawna through another step. “You hear the band? The band's still playing.”

I put my hand on his shoulder.

He stopped then.

Shawna looked at my hand, then at my face. She shook her head very slightly. I saw the warning and decided there must be some loving reason for me to ignore it.

Red's voice stayed low. “Take that off me.”

I held his shoulder. “Yours comes off her first.”

Three men in dark jackets moved from the edge of the room. Tank came after me. The singer missed a word. The band carried the tune beneath the little gap, and I felt every face around us beginning to turn.

Shawna stepped between me and Red.

“Go sit down,” Shawna told me.

I stared at her. “What?”

She kept her eyes on mine. “You're embarrassing me.”

We'd agreed she might need to hurt my feelings in front of him. Earlier that afternoon, at the bedroom mirror, we'd even discussed a slap if words weren't enough. She had refused twice. I had told her I could take one if it made the act convincing and got her close enough to finish the plan. I remembered her standing with her hand against the mirror's edge, asking if I was sure.

I'd said yes.

Now I stood in front of her, surprised to discover that the feelings I'd volunteered had arrived armed.

“Mean that?” I asked Shawna.

She gave me the one slap we'd agreed on.

The sound cracked across the music. My face turned with it. When I looked back, her hand shook beside her dress.

“I told you to listen,” Shawna said.

Her eyes were bright. I believed there was something real in the anger; I had just walked through her warning and put my hand on the man she needed to keep calm. Red stood beside her with his mouth softening into a smile.

I stepped back.

Shawna held my eyes for one more second, then turned away from me.

“Let's finish our dance,” she told Red.

He took her hand, and they moved off together. I stood in the space they'd left with people watching from every side. I had never had less need of my gun or more desire to touch it.

Tank caught my sleeve.

“Come on,” Tank said.

I looked at his hand. “Don't.”

Tank stepped between me and the dance floor. “Come on.”

I let him lead me to the side of the room. I could see the carpet beneath my feet; I couldn't seem to choose where on it I ought to stand. Rosa came up beside us.

“She just kept you alive,” Rosa told me.

I looked down. “I know.”

Rosa waited until I raised my head. “Then act like you know.”

My cheek burned. My side burned. The people I loved had found different places on me to leave the cost of being near them. I knew that wasn't the whole truth. It was the part I could feel while I stood beside Tank and watched Red kiss Shawna's hand again.

This time I stayed where I was.

At nine, Red stopped the music.

He went onto a low stage with Shawna beside him. Attendants opened a curtain behind them. A glass case stood under a white light, and inside it lay the blue necklace he was presenting as Blue Mercy.

Three days of stories had brought me to that little patch of blue. Forty million dollars. A marriage. A prison. Dante dying in the desert. I had expected the thing to stand up and introduce itself.

It was smaller than I expected.

That made it worse. A small bright object in a velvet cradle, with all those lives wrapped around it. I looked at Marcellus. His mouth had opened slightly. One hand rested against the front of his coat. His eyes stayed on the case.

Red lifted the microphone.

“Some things wait a long time to come home,” Red told the room.

He looked at Shawna. People clapped at the pause he'd left for them. I gripped my empty glass, realized I had picked it up again without thinking, and put it down before Tank could do it for me.

“Tonight isn't an auction,” Red continued. “Nobody here can afford what this means to me.”

A man near the stage laughed loudly. Red kept his eyes on Shawna.

“Tonight is a promise,” Red said.

He raised her hand. Dante's watch slid up her forearm. Tank said something under his breath beside me, but I couldn't make out the words and didn't ask.

Marcellus took the small jeweler's case from Rosa and moved toward the stage. Red's men let him approach. An attendant introduced him under the name we had arranged, describing him as a jeweler. Marcellus inclined his head and waited while Red looked him over.

“You approve?” Red asked him.

Marcellus kept his eyes on the glass. “I would like to see it closer.”

Red lowered the microphone from his mouth, though I could still hear him from where we stood. “Later.”

Marcellus set his case beside his leg. “A piece this old deserves—”

Red cut him off. “Later.”

The ring on Red's hand flashed blue as he lowered that hand. Marcellus looked at it, then returned his eyes to the case.

Shawna turned toward the crowd and found me. She held my gaze. I nodded once, trying to give her something useful after the trouble I'd made on the dance floor.

It felt like swallowing a knife.

After the presentation, an attendant came to find me.

“Mr. Redmond would like you upstairs,” the attendant said.

I looked toward the stage. “Can come get me.”

Tank touched my arm. The attendant waited.

Shawna stood beside Rosa near the steps. Rosa had a hand against her back. They both looked over when the attendant repeated the invitation, and Shawna nodded at me.

Fine. Red wanted privacy. I was tired of watching him perform ownership under lights somebody had chosen to flatter it.

Tank came with me. Marcellus picked up his case and joined us. The attendant began to object to the old man, so I stopped at the foot of the stairs.

“If he's giving away forty million,” I told the attendant, “he can let a jeweler look.”

The man looked toward Marcellus's case, then led us up without another argument.

Red's office stood above the ballroom. I could hear the party through the floor. The door opened onto dark wood, tall windows, and a desk wide enough to lay a king out on without troubling his shoes.

Red waited behind it. A second door at the far side of the room was just closing. Shawna moved away from that doorway toward him, carrying a small blue box. They had brought her up ahead of us.

“I thought you'd prefer privacy,” Red said.

I stopped short of the desk. “For what?”

Red rested both hands on the polished wood. “The end.”

Shawna took a step toward me. Red put one hand on her shoulder, and she stopped.

“Tell him,” Red said to her.

Her face was pale. Her fingers tightened beneath the little box.

“Take it, Aiden,” Shawna told me.

I held my hand toward her instead. “Come here.”

She extended the box. “Take it.”

I took it because her hands shook and, for that moment, stopping the shake mattered more to me than asking the right question.

Inside lay the blue jewel in the mounting I'd seen beneath the ballroom's light.

Red watched me open it.

“Forty million,” Red said. “Enough to start over anywhere.”

I looked up from the velvet. “You think I'm selling her?”

Red smiled. “I think she just sold you.”

Marcellus stepped toward the box. Red raised one finger, and two men appeared at the office door behind us. I heard their shoes before I looked back and saw the dark jackets. Marcellus stopped with his hand beside his case.

Shawna looked at the men, at Red, then back at me. Something about the plan had changed. We had meant to get close enough to take his diamond, and here he was placing it into my hands without asking another question. I couldn't make the ease of it fit the man I'd met at breakfast.

Red opened a desk drawer.

“Since your friends enjoy history,” Red said.

He took out a flat leather folder and laid it in front of me. A white label on its cover read DANTE.

Shawna made a small sound. I looked at her, then set the blue box against my left palm so I could reach for the folder with my right.

Red let me take it.

That was when I knew we were in serious fucking trouble.
