Then the lights flickered.

I looked up. A man in a gray suit stood in the upper aisle. He was watching the exits instead of the stage. Below him, another man waited by the door under the balcony. Both jackets hung around shapes I recognized more readily than my own curriculum.

I caught Knox's eye and looked toward the upper aisle. She followed my gaze.

The man below the balcony drew his pistol. At the same moment, orange flame appeared at the lower edge of a curtain behind the choir.

It ran upward faster than I expected. Blue cloth darkened, curled, and became a strip of fire beside the white robes. A woman in the choir screamed. The gunman below the balcony fired into the ceiling, and people all over the auditorium tried to rise at once.

I stepped out from the curtain and grabbed the microphone.

“Off the stairs!” I shouted. “Heads down! Clear the aisle!”

My voice came through the speakers loud enough to turn faces toward me. Knox fired from beside the stage. The lower gunman fell backward behind a row of seats. Her officers moved toward him while Tank got our front row down. Shawna pulled Rosa away from the open aisle.

Above them, the other man raised his weapon over the balcony rail.

I drew my pistol from beneath the robe and fired. He struck the rail and dropped behind it. His gun fell into the empty strip beside the lower seats. An officer ran toward the balcony stairs while I turned toward the choir.

People were trying to descend from the risers through the smoke. A woman with a cane had stopped halfway down. I put the microphone back on its stand, secured my pistol, and ran to her. My cap fell when I bent beneath her arm. I left it.

“This way,” I told the woman with the cane.

A man behind her took her other side. We brought her down to the stage, keeping her feet clear of the next step, and got her to the wing away from the burning cloth. I looked back long enough to see other singers following the route we'd used.

Mercer stood near the lectern with my diploma pressed against his chest.

I caught his sleeve. “Principal. Move.”

Mercer held the folder up. “Your degree—”

I pulled him toward the clearer side of the stage. “Need somebody alive to sign it.”

He came with me. Shawna had got onto the stage and took his arm. My cap was in her other hand.

A burning crossbeam from the hanging decorations fell across the side exit before we reached it. Sparks rose from the broken timber, and Shawna pulled Mercer back from the falling edge. Behind us, fire had spread across the passage to the loading doors. People near the back of the auditorium were escaping through the main doors, but overturned seats and benches jammed the central aisle between them and the front rows. We could see daylight. We couldn't get our people to it.

I looked at the covered car.

Knox was already pulling the cloth away.

Dark blue. A gold shield on the door. Black wheels. A light bar above the windshield. It was the most beautiful fucking police Lamborghini I'd ever seen waiting to become evidence.

Knox threw me the keys.

“Don't scratch it,” she called.

I caught them against my chest. “Don't limit me.”

Tank reached the stage as I opened the driver's door. The woman with the cane stood beside the wall between two singers; he checked that they had her before turning toward me.

“Driving in the school?” Tank asked.

I lifted the robe clear of my feet. “Improving the exit.”

Tank looked toward the aisle. “Went to the wrong fucking college.”

Tank went down the shallow center steps and pulled the nearest overturned seats out of the way. Knox took the microphone and sent her officers ahead, telling everybody to get clear of the central aisle. I waited with the engine running while a graduate crawled beneath a row and an officer lifted a fallen chair off a woman's dress. Then Tank looked back and raised his hand. He moved aside, keeping the last two graduates behind him. Only furniture remained between my bumper and the doors.

Through the windshield, I could see Shawna at the stage's edge with Mercer's arm in her grip. She looked at the car, the fire, then me. Her hand shook against the principal's sleeve. She smiled anyway.

I held that smile for one breath, then drove.

The Lamborghini crossed the stage and dropped down the shallow steps. The impact drove pain through my side. I kept both hands on the wheel. Empty seats struck the bumper and went skidding aside. A broken bench turned beneath us with a crack that I felt through the floor.

Smoke thickened against the windshield. I stayed pointed along the aisle Tank had cleared. A burning strip of curtain fell over the hood, washing the glass orange. For a moment I couldn't see the doors. I kept the wheel straight until the cloth slid off and the opening appeared beyond it.

I pressed the accelerator.

We went through the far doors together, me and the car. Both doors tore from their hinges. Daylight filled the windshield. The Lamborghini dropped down the broad front steps and struck the courtyard paving with its siren screaming.

I braked beyond the foot of the stairs, out of the opening I'd made.

In the mirror, people appeared through the smoke: white choir robes, graduates supporting one another, Tank carrying the woman with the cane. Rosa and Mercedes dragged a loose bench away from the opening. Porsche came out with an extinguisher in each hand, set one at her feet and sent a white cloud into the burning frame with the other.

“Keep moving!” Porsche shouted. “Niggas, the air is outside!”

She sounded like she'd personally purchased the oxygen for the event. People obeyed her all the way down the steps.

I turned off the engine, took the keys, and climbed out. My legs shook when I stood, but I went back up the steps as fast as I could make them work.

Knox met me at the broken opening with Mercer beside her. I dropped the keys into her hand. Shawna came behind them, one hand over her mouth, the other still holding my cap.

“Baby,” I said, reaching for her.

She came into my arms, hit my good shoulder with the cap, then settled it on my head.

“Almost forgot something,” Shawna told me.

I straightened it. “Making an entrance.”

She brushed soot from my cheek. “Made an exit.”

I put a hand against her back. “Great man does both.”

She kissed me while people in the courtyard shouted. I kept my arms around her until she was breathing more easily.

Fire crews came through the gates. Knox's officers brought both attackers outside alive and cuffed. One leaned between two officers; the other walked with his jacket hanging open while a medic held a dressing to his shoulder. They stopped close enough for me to hear them arguing.

“We burned the building,” the lower-floor gunman said, staring at the Lamborghini.

The man who'd been in the balcony looked up at the broken doors. “Still came through it.”

His partner pointed his chin at the shield on the car. “Police car.”

The balcony gunman turned toward him. “I saw.”

They had come to ruin my graduation and ended up reviewing the best part together. I adjusted the tassel until it hung properly, then looked for Mercer.

“Principal,” I called. “Got a microphone out here?”

They put the lectern on a low platform beside the fountain. Halcyon brought a portable speaker. Mercer stayed close to my diploma while the graduates gathered in front of us, some with soot on their robes, some holding shoes they'd lost on the stairs. People kept finding one another and staying together once they did.

Lorne stood near the edge of the crowd, looking from the damaged auditorium to the roof still above it. I hoped he was getting some satisfaction out of having the right drawing at last.

I climbed the two steps to the lectern. Tank stood behind Shawna, arms folded. Marcellus rested a hand on Rosa's shoulder. Mercedes raised her camera.

I leaned toward the microphone. “Aight. Where we at?”

Laughter spread across the courtyard, loud and uneven. I waited until it let me back in.

“Ain't gonna lie,” I told the graduates. “Wasn't here much.”

A man near the fountain called out that I hadn't been there at all.

I pointed toward the smoke. “Look what happened when I came. You're welcome.”

They cheered. A man in the third row took off his cap and waved it. I looked past him at people still catching their breath, still holding on to the person beside them. They had come for their names. I could say something about that.

I put both hands on the lectern. “Some of you worked all night and came anyway. Some had people telling you this wasn't your kind of room. Some got somebody missing today who should've been here making too much fucking noise.”

Shawna looked down at her watch, then back at me.

“But you're here,” I told them. “When the doors caught fire, you started carrying each other. Saw it. That belongs to you. Keep it. Don't let anybody make you little enough to forget.”

I took the cross out from beneath my robe and held it up.

“Three letters,” I said. “D. B. M.”

Tank's eyebrows went up. Shawna began smiling before I gave the words to the crowd.

“Damn, boy,” I said. “Magnificent.”

The courtyard erupted. I let the noise rise around the fountain, then lifted my hand for another moment.

“That's you too,” I told them. “Men, women, every grown motherfucker here with a cap and a dream. Look in your mirror tomorrow and say it properly. Don't mumble greatness in your own house.”

Mercedes lowered her camera long enough to shout. “Say it again!”

I leaned into the microphone. “Damn!”

The crowd answered me. “Boy!”

I spread my arms. “Magnificent!”

They shouted the last word back so loudly that pigeons lifted off the roof of Aiden Hall together. I watched them go while Mercer put the diploma into my hand. He was still crying. I had driven through a burning auditorium and finished the speech before that man finished his first breakdown.

Education was going beautifully.

Knox came onto the platform before somebody could schedule the next thing for me to survive. Two officers followed with a blue velvet box. Another carried a dark uniform jacket with gold braid at the shoulders.

People went quiet as they noticed the box. I looked at Knox.

“You're making this difficult to postpone,” Knox told me.

I held up the diploma. “Busy graduating.”

Knox turned toward the crowd. “You've saved my officers and protected this city's people. You've also demonstrated a relationship with automobiles I've never seen in a man who still has all his limbs.”

Porsche began clapping. A few people joined her before Knox opened the box.

Inside lay a gold captain's badge.

“Captain Aiden,” Knox announced. “Las Vegas Police Department. Actual command. Actual badge. Actual trouble for anybody who thinks otherwise.”

An officer beside the platform turned toward his partner. His partner nodded, and they both looked back at me. I looked down at the gold. Nobody appeared to be bringing out the joke badge afterward.

Tank bent forward with his hands on his knees.

“Nigga,” Tank said.

Rosa caught his arm. “You all right?”

Tank looked up at her. “He's a cop.”

Rosa nodded toward the badge. “Heard.”

Tank turned toward me. “My nigga's the police.”

Rosa pulled at his sleeve. “Stand up before he arrests your posture.”

Knox held the badge in one hand and faced me.

“Right hand,” she said.

I gave Shawna the diploma at the edge of the platform, then raised my hand. Knox spoke the oath in short phrases, and I repeated each one. Keep faith with my people. Stand when somebody needed standing for. Put my whole name behind the authority I used.

I heard my own voice through the speaker. The chief, the officers, the graduates, Shawna: all of them stood in front of me while I said it. I had accepted a lot of things before understanding what they might ask of me. This time I listened to every word on its way out of my mouth.

Knox opened my robe and pinned the badge inside my jacket.

“Captain,” she said.

I looked down at the gold. My mother would have needed to sit down twice.

Shawna came up the steps with my diploma in one hand. She put it carefully on the lectern, took my face between her palms, and kissed me slowly. The officers began applauding before the students did.

“My man,” Shawna whispered.

I kept my forehead close to hers. “Your captain.”

She held my face where she wanted it. “Still my man.”

I could have stayed there letting her settle the rank. Knox waited until Shawna stepped back, then put the Lamborghini's keys in my hand again. An officer passed the new uniform jacket to Tank, who held it carefully over his arm, looking at the braid as though it might start ordering him around.

“Car's yours,” Knox said.

I looked past her at the smoke-darkened hood. “Noticed.”

She moved close enough that I could hear her beneath the crowd.

“Red's people thought they owned every uniform that mattered,” Knox told me. “Let him see yours.”

I looked at her properly then. She smiled and closed my fingers over the keys.

“I know who I recruited,” Knox said. “Captain.”

Shawna collected the diploma from the lectern before we left. She sat beside me in the Lamborghini with it resting on her lap and the badge inside my jacket. I had taken off the graduation robe and folded it behind the seat. The hood smelled of smoke. One door wore a long scratch. The siren still worked, because apparently the day had decided to leave me something perfect.

Tank followed in the original Mercedes. The rest of the convoy came behind him, horns sounding as we crossed the campus. Graduates turned to watch. At the fountain, Marcus lifted his cap.

“That's our nigga!” Marcus shouted.

His friend raised the program he'd hit him with earlier. “Our captain!”

I sounded the siren once for each of them.

Shawna put a hand on my leg as the college disappeared behind us.

“What you doing tomorrow?” Shawna asked.

I looked at her, then returned my eyes to the road and let the car stretch out beneath us.

“Baby,” I told her, “they better not schedule anything they can't afford.”
