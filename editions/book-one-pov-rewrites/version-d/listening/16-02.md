The room opened straight onto the balcony. No windows, no doors along that side, just a thin rail between the floor and concrete far below.

Red held Shawna against it. As he hauled her backward, her little silver pistol fell out of his jacket and struck the floor beside his shoe.

I stopped with mine raised.

“Down,” Red ordered.

“Let her go.”

He pulled her higher. One heel scraped for the floor. She gripped his arm.

“Gun down or she goes over.”

She looked at me. Eyes wide, mouth working around a breath she couldn't finish. She was scared. I could see it plainly, and all I wanted was to make the space beneath her feet become solid again.

“Put her down,” I said.

“Gun first.”

I crouched and set my pistol on the concrete beside the doorway. Opened my hand. Stood up.

Red watched it settle, then smiled.

“How your husband died,” he told Shawna. “Hands open. Begging. T couldn't even look at him.”

She turned her head just enough to speak.

“Not for himself,” she said.

Red's arm tightened. “What?”

“Said he wanted somebody to look after me. Wasn't begging for himself.”

“Shut the fuck up.”

She drove her head backward into his face.

Red staggered. His gun fired into the empty sky. Shawna dropped from his arm, reached across the floor for her silver pistol, and came up with it in both hands.

The first shot hit his leg.

The second hit his chest.

He fell against the railing, his free hand still reaching for her.

“Dante,” she said.

Just his name.

She tried raising the gun again. Her hands shook so badly the barrel sank beneath his chest.

Red was still upright against the rail. His pistol started coming up.

I'd dropped back for my own the moment she got free. I took it off the concrete and fired before he could bring his muzzle level with her.

His gun fell. I fired again. His knees hit the balcony. He toppled along the base of the railing and lay looking up at a sky he was never going to own.

Then I went to her.

She knelt with the little gun in front of her, her mouth moving around her husband's name.

“Baby.”

She didn't look up.

“Shawna.”

Her fingers wouldn't open. I knelt and put my free hand around them.

“Done,” I told her. “It's done.”

She shook her head.

I wanted her to say Aiden. Wanted my name so badly I could barely breathe. She kept saying Dante's.

I set my pistol on the floor, then eased the silver one out of her hand and laid it beside mine. Put both arms around her. She folded against me and cried.

I held her on the floor while she cried for Dante.

Held her tighter because I was the man there.

I hadn't stopped being jealous. Didn't become holy on an unfinished balcony. I had her shaking against my chest, and comforting her mattered more than winning an argument she'd never agreed to have.

Somebody came to the doorway, saw Red, and shouted that he was dead. The words went down the stairs. My name followed them.

Through the hall. Across the casino. Out toward our headlights.

Blood in both shoes, her face against my neck, and they were calling me king.

I believed them.

***

When Shawna could stand, I picked up both guns. Put mine in its holster, hers safely inside my jacket. Then I went to Red's body.

His right hand lay open. I pulled the blue ring from it.

Didn't know for certain what I held yet. Knew whose hands it belonged in. The setting pressed into my palm when I closed my fist.

Shawna waited beside the doorway. I went back, put my other arm around her, and helped her downstairs.

The shooting had stopped. Men stood with hands raised among the tables. Guns lay on the floor. Malik was speaking to an officer near the landing, holding his own weapon by its barrel.

Tank was alive beside the roulette table.

Rosa knelt over him, pressing a folded tablecloth below his shoulder. Dr. Vega was working on another wounded man near the entrance. I saw them and nearly stopped walking.

Rosa looked up.

“Where the fuck you been?”

“With her.”

She saw Shawna behind me. Her face broke open. She pressed her lips together until she could speak.

“Come here, baby.”

Shawna knelt beside her. Rosa kept one hand on the cloth and put the other around my girl. They held their foreheads together.

“Couldn't sit in that house,” Rosa told me. “Got Vega. Followed you. Came in when it stopped.”

I nodded. Couldn't imagine the room without her anymore.

Tank opened his eyes when I knelt. I put the ring into my pocket and took his hand.

“Got her?” he asked.

“Yeah.”

“Red?”

“Dead.”

He tried smiling. Hurt him halfway through.

“Pay my hospital bill,” Tank said.

“Every penny.”

“Car too.”

“What car?”

“Want one.”

“Hundred,” I told him. “Buy you a hundred fucking cars.”

He shut his eyes.

“Black ones.”

I held on until Vega reached us. The last man who'd called me family had put a bullet in me. Tank had taken one instead. I watched the doctor work and listened for every breath.

Wasn't letting him wake up alone.

***

Marcellus came in after the weapons were down. He stepped over the glass with the box in one hand, his coat closed over the papers. Looked at the marble, the naked walls, the staircase Red had built out of other people's lives.

Then he found me beside Tank. He set the blue box on a covered roulette table beneath a work light, freeing both hands.

Vega had attendants getting my brother ready to move. I stood slowly and took the ring from my pocket.

Put it in the old man's hand.

Marcellus didn't speak. He brought out his jeweler's lens, held the ring beneath a work light, and turned it a fraction at a time.

We waited.

He drew a long breath.

“There,” he said. “Inside. The flaw. That's her.”

He lowered the lens. His eyes were full. Then he took Celeste's letter from his coat, laid the ring against it, and bent over both.

Now I knew. Forty million dollars. Standing right there in an old man's palm.

“What you going to do?” I asked.

He closed his hand around the ring and paper.

“Take my wife home.”

I nodded. Let him have the silence after it.

I'd put forty million in somebody else's hand while the woman I loved was still grieving somebody else. For the first time that night, I understood a little better why there were things I couldn't buy back.

Shawna came beside me and put an arm around my waist, careful of the wound. I kissed her hair.

Knox entered with Bell after the last men had lowered their guns. She stopped in front of us, looking from Shawna to the badge beneath the blood.

“Brought her home,” Knox said.

“Told you I don't forget.”

She saluted. Bell followed. The officers behind them straightened while the men among the tables started saying my name again.

Captain. King. Aiden.

Shawna held me tighter when she heard them.

I pulled her close and stood where every motherfucker in that room could see.
