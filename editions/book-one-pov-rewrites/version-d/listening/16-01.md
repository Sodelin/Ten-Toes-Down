## 16. The Last King of Vegas

Red stood with my girl beneath a chandelier that had never been turned on.

Motherfucker hadn't finished building the place where he wanted to die.

Tank came around the Mercedes and stood beside me. Behind us, our headlights shone through the dust. Men got out wearing blue at their wrists, blue at their throats, guns where nobody had to guess at them. Some I'd known for years. Some had been calling me boss for eleven days. None had come to make peace.

The building was marble on one wall, naked concrete on the next. Empty windows. Plastic moving in the night wind. A staircase wide enough for a king led down into construction dust, and Red had put Shawna above it like she was the last expensive thing the place needed.

I raised my good hand. She found me between the headlights and held my eyes.

That was the world. Everything else was stuff I had paid for or planned to. Could've burned all of it while I watched if they let her come down.

Red wore white from his shoes to his collar. The ring on his right hand caught the floodlights when he put that hand on her shoulder.

“Brought everybody,” he called.

“Told them there was a dead man here,” I said.

Tank looked at me. I knew the look. Don't let this man move you before you decide to move. Don't give him anything else just because you're angry.

I was already leaning toward the stairs.

“Inside,” Red said. “You and Tank. Nobody else.”

I started walking. Tank caught my sleeve.

“We get her and leave,” he said.

“We get her.”

He looked at me another second, then let go.

***

Thirty men waited among roulette tables covered in plastic. I counted because a man should know how many people he's being stupid in front of. Some hid their guns under jackets. Others had decided there was no point preserving the surprise.

My badge showed inside the ruined white coat. A few men studied it longer than they studied my pistol. Outside, a siren sounded once. Through the entrance I could see Knox and Bell putting officers around the parked cars.

No uniform was coming to make Red feel better this time.

He brought Shawna down by the elbow. Every time his fingers tightened, I wanted to tear his arm out of its sleeve.

“Let her walk,” I told him.

“Walking.”

“To me. Let my woman walk to me.”

Red stopped halfway down.

“Haven't paid for anything yet.”

Tank stepped beside me.

“Brought your money,” Tank said.

Red laughed. “Still think this is three million?”

“Kept asking for it.”

“Asked for respect,” Red said. “Money was the easy part.”

He turned back to me.

“How much did she tell you about that first night?”

Shawna closed her eyes.

“Enough,” I said.

“Was supposed to get into a car with whoever T sent. You. Tank. Anybody with a pulse and somewhere to hide the money.”

“Don't,” Shawna told him.

Red leaned near her.

“Being honest,” he said. “What lovers want, right?”

I could hear Tank breathing beside me.

Red lifted her wrist. Dante's watch slid down against her hand.

“Got the house, cars, army,” Red told me. “Whole parking lot thinks you're the next big thing. Still wearing his watch.”

I had looked at that watch every morning. At breakfast. In bed. When she touched my cheek. I'd acted as if it didn't bother me because apparently a man who could afford a lake wasn't allowed to admit a wristwatch hurt his feelings.

It did. I was jealous of somebody in the ground, and ashamed enough to hide it badly.

Red saw my face. He held her wrist up a little higher.

“Tell him whose chain brought you over.”

Shawna looked at me.

“Dante's,” she said.

Tank turned toward me. I didn't look away from her.

“First time I kissed you, I was looking at his cross,” Shawna said. “Wanted something of him back. Didn't know what I was doing.”

Red smiled. Watched my hand beside the holster, waiting for it to fall away. Wanted me to turn against her while he stood there holding the evidence of another man's life.

My throat hurt.

“Now?” I asked.

Shawna swallowed. Blood marked the corner of her mouth.

“Looking at you,” she said.

Red's smile went away.

“Don't give him that shit.”

She kept her eyes on mine.

“You, Aiden.”

I nodded once.

“Keep looking, baby.”

Red hit her.

I moved before I found anything in my head that could stop me. Tank caught my arm, and I pulled free. Guns rose around the room. Men shouted. Red shouted louder, telling them not to fire yet.

Shawna caught the banister. Didn't fall. She brought her head up slowly and looked at him with blood at her mouth and something settled behind her eyes.

I hadn't been in Valentino's car. But standing there, I understood how badly that man's last evening must've gone.

Red drew his pistol and put it beneath her jaw.

I stopped at the foot of the stairs.

“One more step,” he warned me.

My heartbeat struck T's bullet with every beat. Felt like my body was trying to shoot itself from the inside.

“You win,” I said.

Tank stared at me. “Aiden.”

“Let her come down,” I told Red. “West side, house, money. Take it. Want my name? Have that too.”

He watched my face. I meant it. We could sleep in the fucking car. Find her another piano when we had a floor to put it on. All those things I'd been buying were supposed to be the life around her. I could rebuild the things.

“Hear that?” Red asked Shawna. “Everything.”

“Take it,” she said. “Let me go.”

He shook his head.

“Dante offered everything too.”

The room went quiet around that name.

Red looked at the ring, then at Tank.

“Her husband thought a brother made him untouchable,” he said to me. “You brought a parking lot. Everybody thinks somebody won't sell.”

He smiled at Tank.

“How much did you take out of that pool?”

“Gave it back,” Tank said.

“After I told him.”

“Gave it back.”

Red beckoned him closer.

“Come here, Tank. Don't die for somebody you met eleven days ago.”

My nigga looked at me.

I said nothing. Had asked for the money, the car, the drive, his whole body between me and the world. Wasn't going to beg for this with Red watching.

Tank put his hand on my shoulder, right where T had put his on that first night.

“That's my brother,” he said.

Red shot him.

***

Tank hit me as he fell. His weight knocked me sideways, below the first shots that tore across the room.

I drew my pistol and fired toward the stairs. Red pulled Shawna across himself and backed up. I stopped shooting when he put her between us. Too much of my whole life in the way of the man I wanted dead.

He dragged her through the doorway at the top.

Tank caught my ankle.

“Go,” he said.

Blood spread through his shirt below the shoulder. I went down beside him for half a second. Couldn't make my body choose between the people who had chosen me.

“Get her,” Tank told me. “Go.”

Our men hit the front doors.

Glass fell across the entrance. I heard their voices and saw blue moving between tables. A man went down over a roulette wheel. Another disappeared through plastic behind the bar. The casino filled with gunfire and people trying to decide which side of a table would keep them breathing.

I went up the stairs with Tank's voice still in my ears.

A man in a brown jacket rounded the first turn and fired too close. The shot broke the rail beside my hand. I drove my left shoulder into him and slammed him against the wall, keeping my own pistol turned away from the people below. His gun hit the steps and bounced away.

I kept climbing.

Through the open stairwell, I saw Rex come in with a man by the jacket and another by the belt. He threw one into the other. Both hit the nearest table. My heavyweight had finally found a room where everybody needed a rematch.

“Captain!” Rex shouted.

“Stairs!” I called back.

He came toward them.

Red was above me with Shawna, hauling her toward the upper corridor. She stumbled, caught herself, and turned long enough to find me.

Half a second. Got me up another flight.

A young man stood between two empty light fixtures at the top, his gun aimed at my chest.

“Move,” I told him.

He looked at the badge.

“Captain Aiden?”

“Terrible place to introduce yourself. Move.”

“Red said you weren't really—”

A shot came from deeper in the corridor.

The man flinched. I caught his collar and dragged him onto the step beside me. The second bullet struck the wall where his head had been. He stared at the white dust falling over his shoes.

“Your boss is generous,” I told him.

I fired down the corridor, forcing the other shooter behind a pillar. The young man turned his own pistol away from me and toward that pillar instead.

“Go,” he said.

I looked at him. “Decide properly.”

He raised his voice.

“Go get her, Captain.”

“Name?”

“Malik.”

Twenty-seven, I learned afterward. Three people depended on the money Red paid him to stand in that spot. He'd almost died working for a man who couldn't wait for him to step aside before shooting through him.

I nodded and went past. Behind me Malik shouted at the others to put their guns down. Heard somebody argue. Heard another pistol hit the floor. Couldn't keep track of every decision. Had one woman still up ahead.

Before I turned into the corridor, I looked down through the stairwell opening. Porsche was dragging a wounded man behind the roulette table beside Tank. Mercedes stood over them with her pistol pointed toward the dark bar.

“Rosa's coming!” Porsche shouted up.

I lifted my hand. Didn't know how she knew. Was glad my people had started finding each other without waiting for me to hold every name in my head.

Little Saint came through a side entrance with three more men.

“Our man up there!” he shouted. “Clear him a road!”

I was already on it.

***

The corridor split around an unfinished fountain. Dirty water lay in its bottom with scraps of tile. Red's white jacket disappeared through the far doors, Shawna pulled against him.

I followed with a hand against the wall. Blood warmed the bandages beneath my shirt.

A man stepped beside the fountain carrying a shotgun.

I dropped when he fired. Stone burst from the rim. Water and grit hit my face. I shoved my pistol into its holster as I reached his legs, getting beneath the barrel before he could bring it down toward me.

We hit the floor together. The shotgun slid away.

He drove an elbow into my wound.

Everything disappeared. Doors, floor, gunfire. Just a pain so large I couldn't find where the rest of me had gone.

Then Shawna shouted my name from the room beyond us.

I hit him.

My wrapped knuckles hurt. I used the other hand. Hit him again until his grip opened, until his head struck concrete and he stopped holding the part of me that needed to move.

I got one foot under myself and stood.

He caught my ankle.

I looked down at him.

“Don't make this your whole life.”

His fingers opened.

Another man came through the far doorway shooting. I crouched, picked up the shotgun, and fired toward the marble stacked beside him. He dropped behind the slabs. I left the shotgun and drew my own pistol again as I passed the fountain.

Ahead, desert light showed through a room without glass. Headlights below. A railing along the edge. I heard Red breathing like the building had finally presented him with its bill.

“Don't get tired?” he called.

I reached the doorway.

“Keep giving me somewhere to be.”
