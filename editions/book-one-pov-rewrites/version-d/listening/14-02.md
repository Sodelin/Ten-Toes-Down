At the lake house that night, I hadn't heard any of this yet. Marcellus was still holding my phone under the dining-room lamp, enlarging Red's ring until Shawna's face moved out of the picture.

I wanted him to put her back.

“The mounting,” he said. “There.”

“Tell me you're sure.”

“About my work, yes.”

“Were sure at the party,” I told him.

“You were,” Marcellus said.

I wanted to shake him. Wanted him right almost as badly as I wanted to hear her voice.

“Show me.”

He opened the decoy's box on the table. It still looked expensive enough to ruin several sensible lives. Its best contribution so far had been hitting a gunman in the head.

Marcellus laid the phone beside it.

“Changed what surrounds the other stone,” he explained. “Dark stones at the edges. Makes the blue look smaller. Kept this piece underneath.”

He pointed to a curved strip of metal in the photograph.

“Saw that shoulder at the gala. Only from the side. This gives me the underside.”

Tank leaned over him.

“Kept it because it's gold?” Tank asked.

“Kept it because he's an idiot,” Marcellus said.

Rosa exhaled. “Finally. Good fucking news.”

Marcellus showed us the curve beneath the stone. No map, no secret number. A shape he'd made with his hands. He recognized it the way I recognized Shawna's steps coming down our stairs.

“Both stones fit the mounting,” he reminded us. “This one here is the copy. Ring's where I'd look for the original.”

“Can read forty million through my phone?” I asked.

“Can recognize work he hid badly. Need the diamond in my hand to swear to it. Need its flaw. Picture won't give me that.”

I looked at Red's fingers under Shawna's chin. He might have been touching her with the thing Dante died trying to find.

“We'll get it,” I said.

Marcellus looked up.

“Say the whole promise.”

“What?”

“Says her or the diamond?”

“Her.”

Didn't wait to find out how much that hurt him.

“Wants the letter burned?” he asked.

“Burn it.”

“Proof of what he did?”

“Gets it. Want my promise? Bringing her home. Everything else finds a way to come with us.”

Marcellus nodded. His mouth tightened once.

“Dante said something very much like that.”

I caught the table's edge.

“Not Dante.”

“Know,” he said.

“Stop putting him between me and every fucking thing I do.”

Rosa said my name. I shook my head.

“Love her now. Me. Standing here bleeding. Going out there. Remember him all you want. When her phone rings, want her to know who's calling.”

My voice shook. I hated letting the three of them hear how much of me was still fighting a man who couldn't answer.

Marcellus took the gala folder from the end of the table. He set the records beside Celeste's letter. Rosa handed him the envelope containing the original order from Dante's watch. He kept them separate: a dead woman's words, a dead man's warning, the books Red had kept on people he could dispose of.

I'd have burned every page to get her through the door. I meant that. Being ashamed of a thing didn't always make me stop meaning it.

Marcellus slid my phone back.

“Give her something to answer,” he said.

I opened Shawna's messages.

My last one before the gala read: *You look good as fuck.*

She had replied with one eye, half a forehead, and a middle finger. I had expected a photograph of the dress. Got a report on the phone's performance instead.

I stared until it blurred.

Rosa stood beside me.

“Sent me six,” she said quietly. “Bathroom ceiling in one. Whole gorgeous ceiling, bitch nowhere in sight.”

I wiped my face with my good hand. Started typing.

*I'm coming. Wear whatever you want.*

Then I deleted the second sentence. She couldn't pick the dress or the chair or the room. Red might read it first. I wanted his head for standing between us even inside a fucking telephone.

I sent the two words that remained.

*I'm coming.*

***

Shawna told me later how he showed her that message. This is the last part of her account before he brought her out to the desert.

Red held the screen where she could see it.

“Thinks he's in a love song,” he said.

“Don't stand in the chorus, then,” Shawna told him.

He took the phone away.

She asked for her purse. He laughed. She asked for water and another cigarette instead. Those he brought, apparently still willing to audition for the part of a man who brought women things. He set the silver box back on the table, lit the cigarette, and gave it to her. She rested it across the box's open lid while she drank.

“Tell me you were lying to him,” Red said.

Shawna drank.

“About what?”

“All of it.”

“Lot of lying,” she said.

“Lied to Valentino.”

She put the glass down. “Yeah.”

“Lied to me.”

“Yeah.”

“Tell me you lied to Aiden.”

He waited. She told me that was the ugliest part: after hurting and trapping her, he still wanted her to make his heart feel better. She knew how. Knew the softness to put into her voice. Could have called him baby and watched his shoulders loosen.

For a moment, she nearly did it to buy herself room.

Then she looked at him.

“Told Aiden I wasn't scared,” Shawna said.

Red leaned nearer. “There.”

“Told him I didn't think about Dante when we were together.”

Red smiled. “And?”

“Told him I didn't like that stupid fucking cross.”

“See?” Red said. “See what I'm saying?”

She picked up the water again. Looked straight at him over it.

“Told him I could live without him.”

Red's smile went away.

She used his name next. Wanted him certain he was the one hearing her.

“Red. That was a lie.”

He knocked the glass out of her hand. Water struck the front of her dress. For a second she thought he would hit her again. Instead he stood there breathing, staring at the wet silk, then picked up his jacket.

“Get up,” Red told her.

“Where?”

“Want to see him so badly, let's go.”

He opened her purse and took out the little silver pistol. Turned it once in his hand, looked at her, and put it inside his jacket.

“This comes too,” he said.

Before he pulled her into the hallway, she left the cigarette burning in the silver box he'd put back on the table. She insisted I remember that. Everything he'd taken from her, and he had forgotten to make her put the cigarette out.

I remember, baby.

***

Back at our house, Rosa was finishing the wrap around my cut hand when Red's next message arrived.

Ten thirty-seven.

*On our way. Don't disappoint her.*

I looked around the dining room. Tank. Rosa. Marcellus. Three million dollars that had already cost too much.

“Call everybody,” I told Tank.

He picked up his phone.

“Everybody?”

“Everybody who ever called me brother.”
