At eleven fifty-four, Red called.

I put him on speaker.

“Coming?” he asked.

“Hear the cars?”

“Told you to come ready to apologize.”

“I am.”

Tank glanced over.

“Told her I'd never let you touch her again,” I said. “Got plenty to apologize for.”

Red breathed into the phone.

“Think all those men love you?”

“Enough to leave the house.”

“That's money.”

“Should've paid better.”

He went quiet. I heard Shawna behind him. Not the words, just her voice before he moved the phone away.

“Let me talk to her,” I said.

“See her soon.”

“Let me—”

The call ended. I gripped the phone so hard Tank took it before I could break that too. He set it in the console.

Past the last gas station, headlights shone across the highway instead of down it.

Two trucks. Men beside them, no uniforms. Burning barrels between the trucks. Red had arranged the road the way he arranged a dining room: everybody would stop where he told them and appreciate the view.

Tank slowed. Thirty engines behind us changed their voices.

Knox called. I answered on speaker.

“See them,” she said. “Stay in the convoy.”

“Convoy is me.”

“Then don't make me explain a road accident to your wife before you get there.”

A shot hit our hood. Tank pulled left. Another made a white star in the windshield directly in front of him.

Marcellus dropped below the rear windows, pulling his coat over the papers and taking the box down with him. I ducked as Bell brought the Lamborghini alongside us. Knox leaned forward in the passenger seat, staring at the trucks.

The siren rose.

One man stepped backward. Another raised his gun anyway.

“Hardheaded motherfucker,” I said.

Tank put his foot down.

The Mercedes left the road in a sheet of dust. We dropped into a shallow ditch and came out hard enough to throw my head against the roof. My seat belt caught the rest of me. I tasted blood.

Behind us, the convoy spread to both sides of the trucks. Headlights bounced over scrub and stone. Red's men had expected a line.

Got a fucking horizon.

Rex's coupe came through on our right. Little Saint rode beside it, up off his seat over the rough ground. The green Bentley emerged from the dust with its roof down. Mercedes held her hair in one hand and a pistol in the other.

“Keep the old man down!” she shouted as they passed.

“Old man is down,” Marcellus called from the back. “Thoroughly fucking down.”

I laughed. Bullet hole in the glass, girl waiting inside a nightmare, and that old man was still correcting the way my people talked to him. I loved him for giving me a sound Red hadn't put in my mouth.

Then the nearer truck moved.

Its driver turned toward the cars passing through the desert. Rex braked. Saint swerved. One of the truck's front wheels climbed a ditch, bringing its nose across the coupe's escape route.

I saw Rex look up through the windshield. Saw Little Saint turn his bike back.

“Tank.”

“See it.”

“Believe you this time.”

Tank drove straight into the truck's rear corner.

Metal struck metal. Our car jumped sideways. My shoulder hit the door, and every stitch in my side tried leaving without me. The truck's rear swung through the dirt, pulling its nose away long enough for Rex to get clear.

Little Saint shot through the gap and raised a fist.

“Aiden!” he shouted.

“Tank!” I shouted back.

Tank looked at me. “What?”

“Giving you your fucking credit. Enjoy it.”

He grinned through the blood on his lip.

The truck stopped against a low rise. Its driver jumped out with a gun, took one look at the cars surrounding him, and dropped it.

Bell braked beside him. Knox got out with her weapon raised. Tank had slowed close enough that I heard her through my open window.

“Blocked my captain's road,” Knox told him.

The man stared at her uniform.

“Your captain?”

She pointed at me. “That one.”

He looked over. His face fell so completely I almost respected the honesty.

“Didn't say it was him,” he told Knox.

“What part of thirty cars looked like a small mistake?” I shouted.

Rex pulled up beside us with his window down. The cut on his eyebrow had opened again.

“Three times,” he told me.

“That was Tank.”

Rex looked at my driver. “Owe the family, then.”

Tank raised two fingers from the wheel. Didn't bother trying to look modest. Would've annoyed me if he had.

Knox's officers moved around the trucks. The second crew had run before the first finished moving. I could see four flashlights bouncing through the desert, trying to become somebody else's problem.

Bell helped secure the driver while Knox directed the others. Then they returned to the Lamborghini as our cars found the highway. Tank counted headlights coming through the dust: coupe, Bentley, borrowed bike, everybody else, scraped and louder but still moving.

“All back,” he said.

The police car took the lead. Cruisers stayed to hold the roadblock. Bell gave the siren one short burst, and horns answered along the line.

Marcellus sat up carefully.

“Finished doing that?” he asked.

“For now.”

He looked down at his collar.

“Tie gave up.”

At the next slow bend, I turned and straightened it with my good hand.

“Meeting forty million,” I told him. “Look ready.”

He looked at the cross on my chest.

“Damn, boy,” Marcellus said.

I smiled. “Magnificent.”

He nodded like the last repair of the evening had finally been completed.

***

At eleven fifty-nine, the unfinished casino rose out of the dark.

White columns beneath floodlights. Empty windows. Plastic moving behind them. A dry fountain beyond the cars parked across the entrance. Men standing between those cars.

Above it all, a balcony ran beneath an unfinished crown of stone.

I saw blue at the railing.

Shawna.

My whole body knew before my eyes finished crossing the distance. Her hair blew across her face. Red stood beside her and lifted the hand with the ring, waving like he'd invited us to dinner.

Tank stopped. Behind us, one engine after another went quiet.

I opened the door. My side hurt. My hand hurt. Everything in me that knew how to be scared was awake and screaming.

I put both feet on the ground anyway.

Shawna stepped toward the railing. Red caught her arm.

“Aiden!” she called.

I looked straight up at her.

“Here, baby,” I said. “I'm here.”
