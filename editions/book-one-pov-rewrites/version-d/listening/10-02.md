At home, Rosa had turned our bedroom into a place where my belongings existed only until a woman needed the space. Shoes occupied the chairs. Perfume hung in the air. A champagne bottle stood in the bathroom sink beside my toothbrush. My pistol had moved from the dresser to a folded hand towel, making room for a heated hairbrush.

I stopped in the doorway. Rosa looked up from a pair of earrings and asked why I was there.

“Sleep here,” I told her.

Rosa pointed toward the hall. “Not while we're busy.”

She wore red with diamonds at her throat. Shawna sat farther inside with her hair being pinned up. I could see one eye in the mirror and the beginning of a smile when Rosa tried to send me away.

I put a hand against the doorframe. “Need Shawna.”

Rosa addressed her reflection. “Your nigga needs patience.”

I lifted my free hand. “Bought some. Arrives tomorrow.”

Rosa picked up a shoe. I retrieved my pistol, secured it beneath the white jacket, and clipped the captain's badge inside its lining. Then I went downstairs before she could explain the shipping options.

Marcellus sat by the windows in a gray suit. His shoes had been polished until they looked wet. We meant to pass him off as a wealthy jeweler under another name. The suit helped. The set of his mouth suggested the tailor had been unable to remove the part of him that wanted Red dragged behind a car.

Celeste's letter lay in its sleeve on the table. He had one palm across it and wasn't reading. I stopped beside the opposite chair.

“Ready?” I asked Marcellus.

He looked out toward the water. “For years.”

I touched the chair back. “Not what I asked.”

He turned toward me. A small smile returned.

“Yes, Aiden,” Marcellus said.

I lowered myself into the chair. My side complained before I finished sitting. That bullet had settled into my life like it intended to pay rent. Marcellus watched me pause until the worst of the pain passed.

“Does she know how much it hurts?” Marcellus asked.

I looked toward the stairs. “Knows everything.”

Marcellus kept his eyes on me. “Does she?”

I turned back. “Enough.”

He folded the letter inside its sleeve and put it into his inner pocket.

“The stone cannot love you back,” Marcellus said.

I rested my hand against my bandage. “Got somebody for that.”

Marcellus smoothed the pocket. “Remember which one you came for.”

Tank entered before I could answer. He held two phones. When he saw us, he turned one facedown against his palm.

“Cars ready,” Tank said.

I looked at the hidden screen. “Who's calling?”

Tank stopped beside the doorway. “Nobody.”

I pointed at his hands. “You need two phones for nobody?”

Tank slid them into his pockets. “In case somebody does.”

On another night I might have laughed. I watched his hands come out empty and thought about asking again.

Then I heard Shawna on the stairs.

I turned.

She wore dark blue, close against her body, with a train following down the steps. Diamonds touched her ears. Dante's watch lay on her wrist. Her hair was up, leaving the little scar above her eyebrow plain to see. I knew that small break in her skin as well as I knew my own hands. I wanted to kiss it before we went anywhere.

Shawna looked down at the dress as she reached the last step.

“It's Rosa's,” Shawna told me. “She never wore it.”

I got to my feet. “Said I'd buy anything.”

She smoothed the skirt. “Wanted this one.”

Rosa lifted the train off the step behind her.

“Want it back,” Rosa said. “Both of you hear?”

Shawna looked over her shoulder. “Yes.”

Her voice was soft. I heard it without giving it the attention I wish I'd given it then.

Rosa came down carrying a small envelope. She showed it to me before putting it into her purse.

“Watch paper,” Rosa said. “Original. Nobody gets it off me.”

Red's order was inside. Shawna had made a copy for the night and put that in her own purse. I watched Rosa close hers over the original and thought about how little paper it took to carry what had happened to Dante. If something went wrong with us, the truth still needed somewhere to be.

I looked back at Shawna because I didn't want to stay with that thought.

“Turn around,” I told her.

She made a slow turn, lifting the train clear of her heel.

I put my hand over my heart. “Again.”

Shawna smiled at me. “Aiden.”

I watched the blue cloth settle. “Trying to remember who I need to thank.”

She put the little silver pistol into her purse beside the copy of the note, then came close and rested both hands against my chest.

“Tear this dress,” Shawna told me, “I'm killing you before Red gets to.”

I touched her waist. “Buy the factory.”

Shawna brought her face closer. “Kill you in the factory.”

I kissed her. Tank cleared his throat behind us. I kissed her again, because that man did not determine how long I was allowed to be happy.

Outside, I counted the cars with one finger.

“I wanted six,” I told Tank.

He pointed to the Lamborghini, the Mercedes, Rosa's car and the green Bentley. “Four drivers.”

“Put somebody in the other two,” I said.

Tank kept his hand raised. “Which somebody?”

I looked toward our people. Shawna held her purse out to me.

“You can drive this,” she said.

I took it. “Four cars. Everybody got a fucking opinion.”

Apparently Tank had chosen the evening of a robbery to begin requiring arithmetic. I carried Shawna's purse to the Lamborghini, opened her door, and returned it when she sat down. Tank took the original black Mercedes. Rosa drove Marcellus, and Porsche brought Mercedes in the green Bentley.

Four cars reached the canopy together. Gold pillars. Flowers taller than the men guarding them. A fountain glittering with coins beneath lights that probably cost more to run than the wishes dropped into the water. I slowed beneath the entrance and looked at the building Red had raised against Marcellus's diamond.

White tie, the invitation said. I stepped out in my white suit with the cross at my chest and the badge inside my jacket. Shawna took my arm.

Cameras flashed.

That surprised me. I'd been preparing for guns.

“Keep walking,” Shawna said close to my ear.

I did, as if I'd bought the pavement and was pleased with the delivery.

A photographer asked us to stop beside a wall of white roses. Shawna put one hand on my shoulder. I settled mine at her waist. Before the flashes ended, a man with a microphone came alongside us and asked whether I was taking over T's interests.

I looked toward the lens. “Anybody owes them, owes me.”

The reporter moved the microphone closer. “And your relationship with Mr. Redmond?”

Redmond. He was using the whole government name for the evening. I respected the effort of dressing that much information for a party.

“Ask at the end,” I told the reporter.

Rosa stepped between him and us before I could improve the answer. Tank came close enough for his jacket to brush mine. He touched my arm.

“Anything happens,” Tank said quietly, “stay with me.”

I looked up at him. “Was the idea.”

He didn't answer. He took his hand away and followed us inside.

A staircase curved around a statue of a naked woman holding a bowl of real fire. I paused long enough to appreciate that. If you intended to waste money, you might as well make the building nervous.

I recognized people all over the room. Women wearing jewels I had seen being offered as security for drugs. Men shaking hands with people I'd heard them threaten across tables. A preacher from television stood beside a man I knew dealt in stolen cars. The preacher wore the larger watch.

Through the open door of a private lounge, I saw champagne buckets and cocaine laid out on a mirror. A man sitting between two women lifted his glass when he noticed me. I had spent a reckless, filthy weekend with all three before I knew Shawna. He looked entirely too pleased to find me dressed for public life.

“Disappear when you fall in love?” the man called to me.

I kept Shawna's hand in mine. “Got promoted.”

The women laughed. Shawna looked from one face to another, then up at me.

“All three?” Shawna asked.

I considered the possibility of improving the truth, then left it alone. “Long weekend.”

Shawna shook her head. “Nasty motherfucker.”

I smiled at her. “Love me.”

Her grip tightened. “Don't get confident.”

I got confident. Enough that when Red appeared on the far staircase in a dark green jacket, I smiled at him as though he'd arrived at a party I was considering letting him enjoy.

His eyes stayed on Shawna.

“There she is,” Red said when he reached us.

He held out both hands. Shawna gave him one. He lifted it and kissed her skin above Dante's watch.

I pictured his head going through the nearest arrangement. His green jacket catching fire in the statue's bowl. The preacher beside the stolen-car man explaining to God why everybody ought to let me finish.

I smiled instead.

“Good party,” I told Red. “Shame about your future.”

He looked at me at last.

“Captain,” Red said. The word came slowly, with his jaw tightening around it. “You clean up well.”

I met his eyes. “You still talking.”

Shawna's fingers tightened against my arm. Red laughed softly and turned toward a photographer.

“All three of us,” Red called. “Get a photograph.”

He guided Shawna into the middle before I could answer and placed a hand against her upper arm. A broad, dark ring sat on that hand, a blue stone set almost level with the surface. For a moment the green sleeve pulled back enough to show it clearly.

Marcellus had been approaching a few feet behind us with Rosa. When Red put his hand on Shawna, the old man stopped. I couldn't tell what he was looking at from where I stood.

The flash went off.

Red asked for another. I looked straight into the lens. If that picture ever reached a museum, I wanted visitors to have no trouble identifying which man had come with a plan to ruin the evening.

An attendant returned with small glossy copies while Red greeted Rosa. She took two and put them into her purse. Her smile stayed in place while the papers went inside. I watched her fingers close around the clasp before she let go.

Red turned back to Shawna and offered his arm.

“Promised this woman a dance,” Red said.

Shawna looked at me.

I remembered the plan. I remembered helping make it. She needed him close, distracted, willing to believe he'd already taken her back from me.

I also remembered having hands.

“One dance,” I told Red.

He smiled at Shawna. “See what she wants.”

She took his arm. I let her hand leave mine.

Then he led her onto the floor, and I followed with my eyes as if somebody had carried my heart into traffic and asked me to admire how carefully he was driving.
