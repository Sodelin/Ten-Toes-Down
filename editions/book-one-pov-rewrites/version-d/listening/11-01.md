## 11. Blue Mercy

Shawna knew the song. She told me that later. What I saw that night was the change in her face when it began.

The band played its first few notes. Her head lifted slightly, then her eyes lowered. Red leaned close to her ear and said something I couldn't hear. She looked down at their joined hands.

I wanted to tear the music out of the speakers.

They moved beneath the chandelier, his green sleeve beside her blue dress. He held her at a distance nobody running a ballroom could have objected to. I objected to every inch of it.

Tank brought me a drink.

“This one's for drinking,” Tank told me. “Don't throw it.”

I took the glass. “Bring it where I got hands, what you expect?”

Tank kept his own glass close. “You might surprise me once. I keep hoping.”

I swallowed half. Across the room, Marcellus spoke with an older woman whose necklace looked heavy enough to place her under arrest gradually. He nodded at something she said and touched the inner pocket of his gray jacket. I remembered Celeste's letter inside it. I knew what he'd said about wanting Red dead.

At least I wasn't the only man in the room finding the evening difficult to admire.

Red turned Shawna. Her skirt swept around his legs. She smiled at something he said.

“Nigga,” I told Tank.

Tank followed my gaze. “I see.”

I kept looking. “Nigga.”

Tank nodded once. “I know.”

I turned toward him. “No, you don't.”

He drank his entire glass.

The man who interrupted wore six championship rings and a gold mouthguard hanging from a chain. I heard him say my name before I turned and saw his chest occupying the space a conversation should have needed.

“Captain Aiden,” he said.

I looked up. “Depends who's bleeding.”

He held his ringed hand near his waist. “Rex Crown.”

I knew the face from television. Forty-five, heavyweight champion twice, ex-champion three times. He had made a respectable living losing something and arranging to be paid while he tried to get it back.

Tank inspected the rings. “Bring the whole display?”

Rex kept his eyes on me. “Heard you think you can fight.”

I lifted my glass toward him. “Heard you get paid. Congratulations. Have a drink.”

Rex stepped closer. “I'm asking you.”

His friends spread behind him in a loose half-circle. Mercedes and Porsche had joined the people watching. Mercedes was already smiling. If I had been Rex, I would have spent a moment wondering why she looked so pleased that I'd come over.

One of his friends pointed at me. “Champ asked you something.”

Mercedes looked at the pointing hand. “My nigga answered. Want him to sing it? He plays piano too.”

Tank nodded. “Ruined a breakfast.”

Rex moved his head from side to side, loosening his neck. “Five thousand says I put you down before your woman finishes dancing.”

I looked past him. Red had turned Shawna again. His hand rested at her back. I watched it long enough for Tank to say my name quietly.

I set the drink down on a table beside us.

“Five?” I asked Rex. “You're famous. Don't give me the introductory offer.”

Rex lifted his chin. “Twenty.”

I turned toward Mercedes. “What did a front-row seat cost when this nigga used to win?”

Mercedes looked at Rex's rings. “More than he can lose, apparently.”

Rex's voice rose. “A hundred thousand.”

A friend caught his sleeve. Rex shook the hand off. I watched the man try once more before surrendering the suit to its owner's decisions.

Porsche put her drink on the table beside mine. “Anybody hear that besides me?”

Rex looked around the circle. “Everybody heard.”

Porsche nodded toward Mercedes. “Find a pen.”

I began taking off my jacket.

“Hold this,” I told Tank.

He looked at the bandage showing beneath my shirt. “Your side.”

I gave him the sleeve. “Hold the good side.”

Tank let the coat hang between us. “Your jacket wasn't shot. You were.”

I pulled my arm free carefully. “Then it's had an easier week. Let it help.”

The lining turned outward, exposing the badge. Rex looked at it and paused.

“You're police?” Rex asked.

Tank folded the jacket over his arm. “You called him Captain when you walked up. What you think he captains? Breakfast?”

The men behind Rex cheered him on. He looked back at them, then faced me again and rolled his shoulders.

“Said a hundred,” Rex reminded me.

Mercedes held out a pen she'd found. “Heard you. Save some breath for after counting.”

I smiled at him. He had bought the room's attention. I was willing to let him use it.

People made space beside the tall windows. A waiter rolled the champagne table back, leaving a chair against the wall. Rex removed his rings one by one and put them in his jacket before handing it to a friend. The band kept playing. Across the floor, Red turned toward us and smiled. Shawna looked over his shoulder and shook her head once.

I held up one finger.

She closed her eyes. When she opened them, I was still there, providing exactly the problem she'd warned me about.

Rex took the mouthguard off its chain and put it between his teeth.

“Jewelry he can chew,” Tank murmured beside me.

Mercedes bent forward laughing.

Rex swung.

It was a proper punch. His shoulder moved behind it, his weight came forward, and the fist traveled straight toward my face without any of the warning a drunk man's arm gives you. I stepped close enough for it to pass my cheek. Air touched the split in my lip.

Then I hit him beneath the ribs.

The blow made less noise than I expected. Rex made more.

He folded forward with a broken breath. I caught the back of his collar before his head reached the champagne table. His hands came toward my arm, then fell away while he tried to straighten. I felt his weight pull against my side and held on just long enough to turn him toward a chair.

The crowd had gone quiet.

“Nigga,” Porsche whispered.

Mercedes answered beside her. “Nigga.”

Tank looked down at Rex. “That's about the whole sentence.”

I lowered the champion into the chair. He braced his hands on his knees. One friend checked his watch, looked toward the band, then lowered his wrist as if neither one had explained anything.

I leaned close enough for Rex to hear. “Before the dance, you said. Still got two verses. Enjoy them.”

The circle erupted. People came over from the far tables. A man pushed his phone toward me. A woman touched my shoulder and said she'd watched every one of Rex's title fights and had never seen him sit so politely.

Rex drew a full breath at last.

“Again,” Rex said.

I watched him struggle to lift his head. “Breathe first.”

Rex looked up at me. “Again.”

I put a hand on the back of the empty chair beside him. “Rematch or hospital bracelet?”

His jaw worked around the mouthguard. He took it out, held it in his palm, and stared at it before looking at me again.

“What the fuck are you?” Rex asked.

I took my jacket from Tank. “Her man.”

I looked toward Shawna as I said it. Several heads turned with mine. She and Red had stopped at the edge of the floor. Her hand remained in his, but she was looking straight at me and smiling. Red turned to see what she was watching. His own smile disappeared.

I put the jacket back on. The movement hurt. I did it slowly enough that only Tank, standing beside me, reached to help the sleeve find my hand.

Rex took out a checkbook. Mercedes gave him the pen. His writing hand trembled, but he filled the line, signed his name, and held it up.

“I don't welch,” Rex said.

I took the check and passed it straight to Mercedes.

“You and Porsche,” I told her. “Got our car out that first night.”

Mercedes looked at the amount. “That's a hundred thousand dollars.”

I reached halfway toward it. “Want it smaller?”

She pulled it against her chest. “Don't you fucking dare.”

Mercedes kissed one cheek. Porsche kissed the other. Rex watched from his chair, breathing carefully while people laughed around him.

Then a thin man behind him drew a gun.

Silver jacket. Tight mouth. I had seen him watching while Rex wrote the check. Now he pointed the pistol toward Mercedes's hand.

“Give it back,” the man in silver said.

Mercedes stepped behind my shoulder. I looked at the gun, then at Rex.

“Yours?” I asked Rex.

Rex pushed himself out of the chair. “Put it away, Silas.”

Silas kept the muzzle raised. “He made you look—”

Rex stepped toward him. “He beat me. Put it away.”

Silas didn't lower it. I caught his wrist while Tank caught his shoulder. The gun went off above us. Crystal burst from a small chandelier and fell across the table. People ducked. Tank drove Silas downward while I kept his wrist turned away from the crowd.

His knees struck the carpet. Then his shoulder. The gun slid from his hand. Porsche kicked it beneath the edge of an empty table and put her shoe on it.

Mercedes crouched, put the check safely inside her purse, and hit Silas with the purse before standing again.

“You made me bend down,” Mercedes told him.

Rex stood over him with the mouthguard still in his hand.

“Pull a gun because I lost?” Rex asked.

Silas looked up from the floor. “I was helping.”

Rex pointed at the broken chandelier. “You was ruining my fucking reputation twice.”

Two of Knox's officers came in from the lobby. I recognized them from the cars outside. One saw the badge inside my open jacket and looked from it to the man Tank was holding down.

“Captain?” the officer asked.

I released Silas's wrist as the officer took it. “His evening's over.”

Porsche lifted her foot when the second officer reached for the pistol. Silas was cuffed and led away while the band found its place after one frightened pause. Glass still glittered across an empty table beside Rex's chair.

Rex offered me his hand.

“Ever need somebody in your corner,” Rex said.

I shook it. “Stay out of mine first.”

He laughed once, stopped because it hurt, then bowed his head. I watched him go toward the bar holding his body carefully, the way I'd been holding mine for days.

When I turned back, Shawna was dancing with Red again.

I'd beaten a heavyweight, given my friends a hundred thousand dollars and sent one fool away in handcuffs. The band was still working through the same song. None of it had changed the thing I'd been watching before Rex arrived.
