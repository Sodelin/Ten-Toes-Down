By the time we reached the ballroom stairs, people were pressing toward the exit below us. Chairs lay in their path. A waiter tried to move one and nearly disappeared beneath the crowd pushing around him.

One of Red's men pulled the ballroom doors shut and put his back against them.

I saw him do it. A room full of people trying to get away from gunfire, and that motherfucker decided the most urgent thing in the building was the fact that nobody had given them permission.

“Open them!” Rosa shouted from the stairs.

He raised his gun toward her.

Tank pulled me down behind the banister. The shot struck the wall over our heads and showered pale dust onto Rosa's hair. Marcellus drew her against the stairwell wall.

I could still hear Shawna telling me to go. I'd brought the box and the papers. I'd left the only person in that office I wanted, and now a fool downstairs was aiming at Rosa as if the evening still had a seating plan.

I shoved the blue box into the deep inner pocket of my jacket. The folder went inside my waistband, flat against my stomach. It hurt to bend far enough to put it there. Everything hurt. I was beginning to resent the variety.

“Put me down,” I told Tank.

His grip tightened. “Aiden.”

I caught his wrist. “Ain't going back upstairs. Getting these people out. Put me on my fucking feet.”

Tank lowered me until my shoes touched the step. I held the rail while the room finished tilting. Then I took my pistol from its holster and looked over the banister.

“Police!” I shouted toward the doors.

The guard laughed.

I opened my jacket with my free hand. The badge caught the ballroom light.

“Captain, nigga,” I told him. “Try your joke with the right title.”

His eyes went from the badge to the pistol. For half a second I thought intelligence might have reached him in time.

Then he fired again.

I went over the low banister beside the last few steps. My shoes hit the carpet, my knee followed, and I dropped behind a table while the shot struck wood above me. Tank came down the remaining steps with his own gun drawn. He fired toward the doorway, forcing the guard behind a planter beside it.

People crawled away from the line between them. A woman in diamonds caught my sleeve.

“Are we going to die?” she asked me.

I looked past her shoulder to make sure her husband was down. “Not with that nigga shooting. Behind the table. Both of you.”

She crawled. Her husband followed so closely that he lost a shoe and didn't even pause to negotiate with it.

A second guard came through a side corridor near the bar. I saw his arm before the rest of him. He was bringing his gun around the corner toward me when Rex Crown hit him from behind.

They crashed into a champagne tower.

Glass fell in a bright waterfall around their shoulders. Rex drove the guard across the table and into the wall, holding on with both enormous arms. The champagne ran down his back. If the man wanted a rematch with anybody, he had chosen a much better opponent.

“Captain!” Rex shouted.

The first guard rose behind his planter. I fired toward him, well clear of Rex on my other side, and he dropped out of sight again. Tank reached the door and hauled it open. Guests began running through to the lobby.

Rex pulled the second guard to the floor. The guard twisted beneath him and struck him across the face with the pistol. Rex's head snapped sideways. His grip opened, and the man rolled free onto one knee.

I crossed the gap between our table and the bar before he could stand.

He raised the gun toward Rex. I pushed his wrist aside with my right forearm, keeping my own muzzle pointed at the floor, and pulled the blue box out with my left hand.

Then I hit him beside the temple with it.

Forty million dollars made a solid fucking sound. I would learn soon enough that it wasn't worth forty million. At that moment it was worth exactly one living boxer.

The guard fell onto his side. I set my foot on his gun hand until his fingers opened, then kicked the pistol deep beneath the bar. I put the box back inside my jacket and offered Rex my free hand.

Blood ran from his eyebrow.

“Twice,” Rex said, looking up at me.

I braced myself while he got his feet beneath him. “Keeping score now?”

Rex put a hand on the bar. “Beat me. Then saved me.”

I glanced toward the open doors. “Keep up. Night ain't over.”

He laughed with blood on his teeth, put his shoulder beneath my good arm, and helped me toward the exit. Tank held the ballroom door. Rosa pushed Marcellus into the passage ahead of her. I could see Porsche beyond the lobby, waving people toward the street.

Then metal ground against stone above the staircase.

I looked up.

The bronze woman holding the bowl of fire had tilted forward on her base. Something had struck it; I hadn't seen what. Burning liquid spilled down her arms and fell onto the flowers below. Flame climbed through the dry arrangement in a sudden sheet.

Mercedes stood beneath her, trying to pull a fallen man to his feet.

“Mercedes, move!” I shouted.

She looked up. The statue began to fall.

I shoved my pistol into its holster, pulled free of Rex, and ran the few steps back toward her. The bronze woman's arm struck the banister. Wood snapped. Mercedes had one hand beneath the man's shoulder, his other arm still dragging behind him.

I drove into both of them from the side, catching Mercedes around the waist and shoving the man ahead of us through the open passage. His shoes slid. We fell together beyond the doorway as the statue struck the lower stairs behind us.

The bowl flew off. Burning liquid splashed across the carpet we'd just left. Part of the broken banister crashed down after it.

Heat rolled over my back. Pain spread through my side so completely that, for a moment, I couldn't tell where the bullet was anymore. It felt like the whole fucking thing had gone into business across my body.

Mercedes rolled toward me and caught my face in both hands.

“Nigga,” Mercedes said. “Nigga, you got me?”

I tried to draw breath. “Got you.”

She gripped my cheeks. “You sure?”

I looked down at her. “Baby, count your fucking legs.”

She did. Two legs. One shoe. A dress she was going to mention for the rest of my natural life.

Then she kissed my forehead hard enough that I thought the bullet might come out from gratitude.

“You beautiful, stupid, magnificent motherfucker,” Mercedes told me.

The man we'd pushed clear sat against the wall, coughing and looking at the burning doorway. I saw him move both arms. Mercedes reached for him again while I tried to stand.

I couldn't do it the first time.

Rex and Tank reached me together. Each took an arm, and between them they got me up. I checked that my pistol still sat in its holster before they began moving me. My free hand couldn't have managed another job.

People had found their way through the passage. Porsche held the outer lobby door open with one leg and shouted directions at a frightened crowd that, for once in its expensive fucking life, followed instructions.

The woman in diamonds stopped beside us. Her husband stood behind her with one bare foot. She took my hand and pressed her wedding ring into it.

“For saving us,” she told me.

I pushed it back into her palm. “Keep your husband nervous. I got a woman waiting.”

She laughed through her tears. Her husband looked at the ring returning to her hand and nodded at me with an intensity I understood perfectly.

Then the cold air touched my face.

My own woman wasn't waiting outside.

The people around us kept moving, calling for friends, touching one another to make sure they had come out whole. Somebody called me Captain. Somebody else tried to shake the hand Tank was using to keep me upright.

I turned toward the building.

Tank caught my shoulder. “Not that way.”

I looked at the lit windows above the ballroom. “She's in there.”

Tank stepped in front of me. His mouth had swollen where I'd hit it. “Told me to get you out. You alive enough to go get her somewhere else. That's what we got left.”

I didn't forgive him beside that car. I didn't decide the money didn't matter. I let him take my weight because I couldn't stand without help, and I needed to stay alive long enough to find her.

Rex helped him lower me into our Mercedes. Tank went around to drive. The police Lamborghini stayed at the Saint with Knox's officers; one of them told Tank they'd bring it back when the way was clear.

Rosa came to my open window before getting into another car with Marcellus. She pushed one of the gala photographs into my hand.

“She gave me this,” Rosa said.

Shawna stood between me and Red in the picture. His ring caught the light beside her arm. My hand rested at her waist. I looked at my own face, at how sure I'd been that standing close was the same as keeping her.

Rosa touched the edge of the picture.

“Told me to keep one for when you two got married,” she said.

I couldn't look up at her.

Tank started the car.

We hadn't gone far when Marcellus called. I balanced the photograph against my thigh, took out the phone, and put him on speaker.

“The stone,” Marcellus said. “Aiden, listen carefully. The one you have is the duplicate.”

Tank glanced toward me before looking back at the road.

I took out the blue box and opened it beneath the dashboard light. My fingertips left a red mark on the lid.

“You sure?” I asked Marcellus.

His voice sharpened through the speaker. “Nigga, I made it.”

I shut the box.

We'd gone in with Shawna and come out with a fucking lie in velvet.

“Red know?” Tank asked toward the phone.

Marcellus paused. “He expected you to take it.”

Streetlights moved across the windshield. I counted them until I realized what I was doing. Every time I stopped looking at something in front of me, I saw Red's arm across Shawna's chest.

Rosa's voice came through next.

“Still have the original order,” Rosa said. “And you have his papers.”

I pulled the folder from my waistband and laid it across my lap. Its corners were bent. A page had torn where my fist had held it. I saw Valentino's name again, then a list of payments tied to the jewel transaction, figures arranged so neatly that they almost concealed the bodies underneath.

“Don't want his fucking papers,” I told Rosa.

Her voice softened. “I know.”

I put my hand over the page. “Want her.”

Rosa answered me by name. “I know, Aiden.”

Another call lit the screen. Unknown number.

I ended the first call and answered before the next ring could finish.

“Shawna?” I said.

Red breathed into my ear.

“Wondered how long you'd take to notice the gift,” Red said.

I pressed the phone so hard against my head that its edge hurt. “Let me hear her.”

Red kept speaking. “Your friend has my three million. You have papers that belong in my desk. Somewhere, somebody has a very old note.”

I held the box against my leg. “Let me hear her.”

Movement came through the line. A door, footsteps, the brush of something against the microphone.

Then Shawna spoke.

“Baby,” she said.

I closed my eyes. “I'm here.”

Her breath caught. “I know.”

I leaned forward against the phone. “I'm coming.”

Shawna answered softly. “I know.”

Red came back on the line before I could say anything else.

“Unfinished casino in the desert,” Red told me. “Midnight. Money. Papers. Original note.”

I looked at the dashboard clock. Less than three hours.

“If you hurt her,” I began.

Red cut across me. “Come before I get bored.”

The call ended.

For a while neither Tank nor I spoke. He drove with both hands on the wheel. I laid the phone on the folder and held the photograph in one hand, the closed blue box in the other. The suit we'd chosen that afternoon had blood on the sleeve and the side. My cheek still held the heat of Shawna's slap.

Every place she'd touched me that night seemed like the only part of my body still alive.

“Take me to the money,” I told Tank.

He nodded at the road. “Yeah.”

I turned toward him. “All of it.”

Tank swallowed. “Yeah.”

I put the photograph inside my jacket, over the cross. I'd gone into the Saint believing I could steal a fortune and a future in one night. Now he had my future locked in a room somewhere, and all the money in Vegas couldn't make the next red light change.

“Tank,” I said.

He glanced at me. “What?”

I kept my hand against the picture. “After we get her.”

Tank faced the road again. “Nigga, we are getting her.”

I looked out at the city.

“After we get her,” I told Tank, “there isn't going to be enough desert left to hide what we do to him.”
