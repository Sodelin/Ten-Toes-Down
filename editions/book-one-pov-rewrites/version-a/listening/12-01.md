## 12. The Money at the Bottom

The folder opened stiffly, as if somebody had spent more on keeping the leather handsome than he'd spent on the lives filed inside it.

I stood on the visitors' side of Red's desk with the blue box open in my left hand. The folder rested across my right forearm. Shawna leaned close enough to read beside me, and for a second her shoulder touched mine. I remembered that touch afterward. I remembered it much more clearly than the numbers on the first two pages.

On the third page, beside Valentino's name, somebody had written *settled* in red ink.

Underneath was a copy of the order from Dante's watch.

Red had kept his own.

I looked across the desk. “Nigga, you sick motherfucker.”

Red's hands rested flat on the wood. “Careful where you point that mouth.”

Shawna took the loose pages off my arm. She found the order, read it, and turned it over. The blank back faced me for a moment. She turned it again and held it closer.

I had watched her read the original at our table. Knowing the words hadn't prepared me for finding a clean copy here, in the same drawer a man might keep stamps. I couldn't imagine what it did to her. Her hand began shaking hard enough that the edge of the paper tapped against the leather folder.

“You kept this?” Shawna asked Red.

He looked toward Tank. “T believed in records. Nigga kept everything.”

Shawna moved the paper into his line of sight. “You're sitting behind the desk.”

Red turned back to her. “Shawna.”

She raised her voice. “You're sitting behind the fucking desk.”

She threw the page at his chest. It caught his jacket and slid down onto the blotter. He looked at it without moving his hands, then raised his eyes to me.

“Was going to make this generous,” Red said.

I put the folder on the near edge of his desk. “Shut your mouth.”

Tank moved up beside my shoulder. Both men at the outer door shifted forward. I heard a shoe scrape behind me and looked back long enough to see one of them watching Tank's hands.

When I faced Red again, he was smiling.

“Ask your man what generosity looks like,” Red told me.

Tank stopped moving.

I turned toward him. “What the fuck is he talking about?”

Tank said my name.

I waited. He looked past me toward the desk. That was all he did, and I felt something cold open beneath my ribs before I knew what belonged there.

“Nigga,” I said to Tank. “What?”

Red picked up a remote. A screen on the wall beside his desk lit up with a wide, grainy view of an empty swimming pool.

I knew the pale motel wall behind it. I knew the balcony rail and the blue tiles at the deep end. My body recognized the place before I saw the money: the ache in my side sharpened, and I caught myself taking the short breaths I'd taken against that fence.

Then Tank came into the picture.

Our Mercedes was already gone. T lay near the fence. The recording had no sound I could hear, just the quiet room around me and Tank on the screen, stepping down into the pool with a bag.

He bent over a bundle of money.

He picked it up.

I watched him put it inside the bag, turn, and reach for another.

The terrible thing about a recording is that it doesn't give a fuck when you've understood. It keeps going. The man I'd asked to help me was still bending and gathering and stepping carefully around the place where I'd nearly died.

“Turn it off,” I told Red.

On the screen, Tank climbed out with a full bag. He came back with another. One loose bundle lay beside T's shoe. Tank leaned over and took that too.

“You heard enough breathing in this room,” I said to Red. “Turn it the fuck off.”

Red pressed the remote. The screen went dark, leaving a reflection of the desk lamp and the people standing beneath it.

“Three million,” Red said. “All of it. Your friend is thorough.”

I turned toward Tank. He looked back at me. His arms hung loose beside his jacket.

“You took her money?” I asked him.

Tank swallowed. “Took it out the pool. I'm standing here saying that.”

I stepped closer. “Don't play with me.”

“I'm not,” Tank said.

I could smell the peppermint he'd been eating downstairs. I hated that I could smell it. A minute earlier I'd known the man who carried it in his pocket. Now I was standing close enough to hit a stranger who had his face.

“You watched her hold me together,” I told Tank. “You saw her drive off with me bleeding. Then you went back for the fucking money?”

His jaw moved before the word came out.

“Yes,” Tank said.

I hit him with my right hand.

His head turned. Pain went through my side so hard my knees bent with it. I caught my balance and hit him again. He rocked back half a step, then came upright with blood on his lip.

I still held the blue box in my left hand. It seemed impossible that I'd remembered how to keep something so useless from falling.

“Look at me and hit me back,” I told Tank.

Tank looked at me. “No.”

I raised my fist again. “Hit me the fuck back.”

Shawna caught my wrist with both hands. I felt her weight against my arm before I looked down and saw her there.

Tank wiped his mouth. “I'm not hitting you.”

Red leaned against the far edge of his desk. “Touching.”

I turned toward him. Shawna's grip tightened.

“You say one more word,” I told Red.

He glanced at the box. “And what? You'll die rich?”

I looked down at the jewel. It lay where it had been, blue and bright in a velvet bed, having contributed absolutely fucking nothing to the evening except reasons for everybody to behave worse.

I wanted to throw it through his face.

Marcellus stepped close enough that his shoulder brushed mine. He bent toward the open box under the lamp. His eyes narrowed, then lifted quickly toward Red.

“Aiden,” Marcellus said.

I looked at him.

“Close it,” he told me.

I kept the lid open. “What?”

Marcellus put two fingers against its edge. “Close the box.”

I let the lid meet his fingers, and he withdrew them. He looked into my face. Whatever he'd seen, I took that look to mean he wanted to discuss it somewhere else.

Behind me, Tank spoke again.

“At your house,” Tank said. “Back of my closet. Moved it when we moved in.”

Shawna let go of my wrist and turned toward him.

“All of it?” she asked.

Tank nodded. “Every fucking dollar.”

Shawna stood very still. “Why didn't you tell us?”

Tank looked at the floor. I stepped toward him, and he raised his head before I reached him.

“Thought you was going to leave,” Tank said.

I stopped. “What?”

He rubbed his wet mouth with the back of his hand. “You got her. T was dead. Had the car. Talking about water and houses, taking her anywhere. Thought you was gone.”

“I was fucking dying,” I told him.

Tank's eyes shone. “Know.”

I leaned toward him. “Do you?”

I'd seen Tank angry, drunk, half asleep in a doorway, laughing with food in his mouth. I hadn't seen him stand with tears gathering while he waited for me to decide whether to hit him again.

It made me angrier. I needed him solid. I needed something I could strike until the room stopped changing shape.

“Thought if you left, I'd have something,” Tank told me. “That's it. Whole thing.”

Red made a little impatient sound behind us.

“Tell him the rest,” Red said.

Tank looked past me. “You called me.”

Red nodded. “You answered.”

Tank took a breath through his nose. “Sent me that video.”

Red's smile came back. “Answered again.”

Shawna moved beside me. “You told him where we were?”

Tank turned to her immediately. “No.”

She said his name and waited.

“No, Shawna,” Tank told her. “Didn't tell him about the house by the lake. Didn't tell him what you was doing. Knew you was coming here because you fucking told him.”

Red straightened. “Offered him his place back.”

I kept my eyes on Tank. “What place?”

Red answered from behind me. “Yours.”

I heard the band through the floor. It had begun another song. Somebody downstairs laughed, and the ordinary sound reached the office like proof of a world none of us could get back into.

I thought about Tank sitting outside my bedroom. The spare phone. The way he'd watched me call men who used to answer to T, men who now waited for me to finish speaking before they asked what I wanted done.

“You want it?” I asked Tank.

He said nothing.

I stood closer. “I'm right here. You want it?”

His eyes dropped toward my waist. I followed them and saw a red mark spreading through the side of my white suit. I hadn't felt the wetness. Now that I could see it, the cloth seemed to cling to the wound.

Tank shook his head.

“Wanted you to ask me to stay,” he said. “Me. Not whoever drove the car.”

I had answers ready for greed. For orders taken, names sold, a man waiting for me to die so he could move into the warm place I'd left. I had been preparing answers with my fists.

I didn't have one for that.

Red opened a drawer and tossed a car key onto the desk. It skidded over the corner of the murder order before stopping.

“Still time for an intelligent decision,” Red told Tank. “Bring me my money. Walk out with a future.”

Tank looked at the key.

He reached past me and picked it up.

My hands went cold.

Then he turned and threw it at Red's window.

The key struck with a sharp crack. A white star spread through the glass, but the pane held. The key bounced back and landed on the carpet beside the desk.

Tank watched it settle, then looked at Red.

“Fuck your car too,” Tank said.

The nearest guard grabbed Tank's shoulder. Tank spun and drove him into the wall. A framed photograph jumped sideways on its hook. The second guard reached inside his jacket, and Shawna snatched the lamp off the desk with both hands.

She brought it down across his forearm.

The bulb burst. For an instant the shade lit from within; then the office lost its warm pool of light and the windows reflected only the ceiling lamps. The guard shouted and bent his arm against his chest.

Red came around the desk while Shawna still held the broken lamp. He caught her from behind and pulled her against him. The lamp fell between their feet. I moved toward them.

The guard Tank had thrown into the wall had got a hand free. He struck my wounded side with the heavy end of his pistol as I passed.

The room left my lungs.

I folded against the desk. Loose pages slid under my right palm. I saw Shawna's shoes scrape the floor while Red hauled her toward the inner door. One heel came off. She kicked backward with the bare foot and twisted hard enough to knock a photograph from a shelf.

“Let her go,” I said.

There wasn't enough breath behind it to reach the doorway.

I pushed against the desk. “Let her fucking go.”

Tank tore the guard away from me. Marcellus caught my sleeve and helped me straighten. I closed my right hand around the folder and the pages under it. The box stayed clenched in my left. I wasn't making sensible decisions about evidence. I was holding things because I couldn't stand the thought of losing anything else.

A gun went off.

Wood jumped from the desk beside my hand. I jerked back and saw Red at the inner doorway with his pistol pressed against Shawna. Smoke hung briefly in the light between us.

Downstairs, a scream cut through the band. More voices followed. The music stopped.

Tank stopped with one guard held against the wall. The man Shawna had hit cradled his injured arm and stayed where he was.

“There,” Red said. “You can learn.”

Shawna's hair had come loose. One earring was missing. Red's forearm lay across the upper part of her chest; his other hand held the gun against her body. She looked at me over his arm. Her eyes were wet, but she kept her mouth shut tight.

I didn't know what she was trying to tell me. I knew only that I could see her breathing and that he had a hand on the thing that could stop it.

“Take me,” I told Red.

His eyebrows rose. “Nigga, I don't want you.”

I moved one foot forward. “Let her walk. I'll stay.”

Red drew her tighter. “Heard you.”

Shawna shook her head once, hard.

“Aiden,” she said.

I couldn't look away from the gun.

“Take it and go,” Shawna told me.

I looked into her face. “I'm not leaving you.”

Her voice broke on the next word. “Go.”

The inner door opened wider. A third man waited beyond it. Shawna looked over her shoulder, then back past me toward Tank.

“Get him out,” she told Tank.

Red pulled her through the doorway.

I went after them.

Tank caught me around the chest before I reached the desk's far corner. I struck him with my elbow, then with the fist holding the folder. Paper bent against his face. I told him I'd kill him. I meant it with everything I had left, and he kept dragging me toward the outer door.

Behind us, a man shouted. A shot struck the wall near the inner doorway. I couldn't see whose gun had fired. Tank turned his body between me and the sound and pulled harder.

Marcellus reached the hall first. Rosa appeared there with her red gown gathered in one hand so she could climb the last steps. She looked at us, then into the room behind us.

“Where is she?” Rosa asked.

Marcellus caught her wrist. “He has her.”

Rosa tried to pull free and go past him.

“Not this way,” Marcellus told her.

Tank hauled me into the hall. I could still hear Shawna beyond the inner door. Then another door shut somewhere farther inside, and I couldn't hear her at all.
