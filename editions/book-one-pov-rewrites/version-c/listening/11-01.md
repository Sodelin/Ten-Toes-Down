## 11. Blue Mercy

The singer took a breath over the first piano chord, and Shawna lifted her head. Beside the dance floor, Aiden stopped with his glass halfway to his mouth. Red leaned down to Shawna's ear. Whatever he said disappeared beneath the first line of the song; she looked at their joined hands, then let him guide her around a couple turning beneath the chandelier.

Tank arrived at Aiden's shoulder with another drink. Aiden still had half of the first one. Tank took that glass away and put the fresh one in his hand.

“This one's for drinking,” Tank told him. “Don't throw it.”

“Then why bring it where I got hands?” Aiden asked.

Tank set the unfinished drink on a passing tray. “A nigga might surprise me once. I keep hoping.”

Aiden drank until the ice touched his upper lip. His free hand pressed against his side beneath the white jacket. Across the room, Marcellus spoke with an older woman whose necklace covered most of her bare chest. Every time she nodded, its lower edge struck a button on her dress. The jeweler inclined his head toward it, listened to her answer, and glanced past her at Red's hand.

On the dance floor, Shawna's skirt passed across Red's trousers in a blue sweep. Red bent toward her again. Aiden lowered his glass so hard that liquid jumped over its rim.

“Nigga,” Aiden said.

Tank followed his gaze. “I see.”

Aiden pointed at the dance floor with the bottom of his glass. “Nigga.”

“I know,” Tank said.

“No, you don't,” Aiden told him.

Tank retrieved the unfinished drink from another waiter's tray and swallowed the rest of it.

The man who approached them wore six championship rings and a gold mouthguard on a chain. His jacket stretched tightly across his shoulders. Behind him came three men with open collars, two women carrying their shoes, and a waiter who had been trying to deliver the same plate of scallops through the group for several minutes.

“Captain Aiden,” the man said.

Aiden looked from the mouthguard to his face. “Depends who's bleeding.”

The man held out his right hand, showing three of the rings. “Rex Crown.”

Tank leaned around Aiden to inspect the other three. “You bring the whole display?”

Rex kept his hand extended until Aiden shook it. “Heard you think you can fight.”

“Heard you get paid to,” Aiden replied. “Congratulations. Have a drink.”

Rex did not take the glass Aiden offered. “I'm asking you.”

Mercedes appeared between two of Rex's companions, with Porsche close behind her. Mercedes looked at Rex's rings and then at Tank. Tank put a hand over his mouth.

One of the men behind Rex stepped forward. “Champ asked you something.”

Mercedes held up a finger between them. “My nigga answered. You want him to sing it? He plays piano too.”

“Ruined a breakfast,” Tank said from behind his hand.

Rex stepped around Mercedes. “Five thousand says I put you down before your woman finishes dancing.”

Aiden turned his head. Across the floor, Red raised Shawna's arm and passed her beneath it. Her skirt brushed his green jacket. Aiden watched until Tank touched his elbow.

Aiden set the drink on the nearest table. “Five thousand? Rex, you famous. Don't come over here with the introductory offer.”

“Twenty,” Rex said.

“Mercedes,” Aiden asked, “what did a front-row ticket cost when this nigga used to win?”

Mercedes rested her elbow on Porsche's shoulder. “More than he can afford to lose, apparently.”

Rex's jaw moved above the gold chain. “A hundred thousand.”

One of his friends caught his sleeve. Rex shook the hand away so sharply that the man nearly spilled his champagne down a woman's back. Porsche put her own drink on the table and straightened.

“Somebody heard that besides me?” Porsche asked.

Rex looked around the growing circle. “Everybody heard.”

Porsche turned to Mercedes. “Find a pen.”

Aiden unbuttoned his jacket. Tank reached for it, then stopped when Aiden's hand went briefly to his ribs.

“Your side,” Tank said.

“Hold it on the good side,” Aiden told him.

“Your jacket hasn't been shot,” Tank said. “You have.”

Aiden slipped his arms free. “Then it's had an easier week. Let it help.”

Tank folded the jacket across his arm. The badge clipped inside flashed under the chandelier, and Rex's gaze dropped to it.

“You a cop?” Rex asked.

Tank spread the lining wider. “You walked over calling him Captain. What you think he captains? Breakfast?”

Rex's friends began cheering. One of the barefoot women shouted his name and slapped the sole of a high heel against her palm. The waiter finally pushed the scallops through the gap and escaped.

Rex pulled off his jacket. “I said a hundred.”

Mercedes uncapped a pen. “We heard you. Keep some breath for whatever happens after counting.”

They made space beside the tall windows. A waiter wheeled the champagne table back several feet, leaving a chair against the wall. The band continued playing, although the pianist looked away from his keys long enough to miss a chord. Red turned toward the gathering. Shawna looked over his shoulder and shook her head once at Aiden.

Aiden lifted one finger.

Shawna closed her eyes, then opened them and watched.

Rex removed the mouthguard from its chain and fitted it between his teeth. He rolled his shoulders, shifted his weight, and brought his fists up. His rings had gone into his jacket pocket. The gold in his mouth remained.

Tank leaned toward Mercedes. “Nigga brought jewelry he can chew.”

Mercedes bent over with both hands on her knees.

Rex's first punch crossed the space between the men before Mercedes straightened. His shoulder drove behind it, his other fist tucked close to his face. Aiden stepped inward and to the side. Rex's knuckles passed his cheek. Aiden's left foot planted beside Rex's forward shoe; his fist drove up beneath the champion's ribs.

There was a short, heavy sound through the shirt. Rex's mouth opened around the gold guard. His knees bent and his upper body folded over Aiden's arm.

Aiden caught the back of Rex's collar before the champion's forehead reached the champagne table. He held him there while Rex dragged air through his nose. A glass at the table's edge trembled, settled, and remained standing.

Porsche pressed her lips together. “Nigga.”

“Nigga,” Mercedes answered.

Tank looked down at the bent champion. “That's about the whole sentence.”

Aiden backed Rex toward the chair and lowered him into it. Rex braced his palms on his knees. One friend crouched beside him; another looked from the chair to his watch and back again.

“You said before the dance,” Aiden told Rex. “You still got two verses. Enjoy them.”

The circle broke into noise. A woman grabbed her husband's sleeve and dragged him nearer. Phones rose. A man with a cigar behind his ear tried to ask Aiden three questions while taking a photograph and received a picture of Tank's elbow. The woman carrying her shoes watched Rex's chest move, slowly, then with a deeper breath.

“I've seen every one of your title fights,” she told Rex. “I've never seen you sit down so respectfully.”

Rex held up a hand until he could speak. “Again.”

“Breathe first,” Aiden said.

Rex lifted his head. “Again.”

Aiden crouched far enough to meet his eyes. “You want a rematch or a hospital bracelet?”

Rex looked at him for several seconds. His hands loosened on his knees. He drew another breath and let it out through his open mouth.

“What the fuck are you?” Rex asked.

Aiden stood and took his jacket from Tank. “Her man.”

He looked toward Shawna while pulling the jacket over his shoulders. The people nearest him turned too. Shawna had stopped beside Red at the edge of the floor. Her mouth widened into a smile. Red looked down at her, then back at Aiden. His own smile disappeared.

Porsche approached Rex with Mercedes's pen. Rex took a checkbook from his jacket, rested it on his thigh, and wrote with the paper jumping slightly under his hand. He tore out the check and held it toward Aiden.

“I don't welch,” Rex said.

Aiden read the amount, then gave the check to Mercedes. “For you and Porsche. You got my car out of that first night.”

Mercedes held the paper by both edges. “That's a hundred thousand dollars.”

“Want it smaller?” Aiden asked.

Mercedes pulled it against her chest. “Don't you fucking dare.”

She kissed one of Aiden's cheeks. Porsche kissed the other, leaving a red mark beneath his eye. Rex sat between his friends and watched Porsche point at the amount again. She and Mercedes were still bent over the check when a thin man in a silver jacket reached beneath his lapel.

“Give it back,” the man said.

The barrel of his pistol pointed at Mercedes's chest. She moved behind Aiden, taking the check with her. The circle of guests opened abruptly. A woman stumbled over the shoes she had put on the floor.

Aiden kept his hands low. He looked at Rex. “Your nigga?”

Rex pushed himself out of the chair with both arms. “Put it away, Silas.”

Silas kept the pistol level. “He made you look—”

“He beat me,” Rex said. “Put it away.”

Silas turned his face toward Rex without lowering the weapon. Aiden caught his gun wrist and drove it upward. Tank hit Silas's shoulder from the side. The shot went into the ceiling; a small chandelier spat crystal across the tablecloth and floor.

Silas landed on his stomach with Tank's knee between his shoulder blades. Aiden twisted the gun free. It fell beside the table and slid across the polished floor. Porsche kicked it beneath the empty table, followed it in with one foot, and pinned it under her heel.

Mercedes put the check inside her purse. Then she struck Silas across the back of the head with the purse.

“You made me bend down,” Mercedes told him.

The band had stopped. A cymbal continued quivering beside the drummer's raised stick. Rex stood over Silas with both hands spread at his sides.

“You pull a gun because I lost?” Rex demanded.

Silas turned his face far enough to speak against the floor. “I was helping.”

“You was ruining my fucking reputation twice,” Rex said.

Two uniformed officers came through the lobby doors. Aiden pulled his jacket open to show his badge while keeping Silas's wrist bent behind him. Tank raised his free hand. Porsche called the officers toward the table and lifted her heel only when one of them crouched to collect the pistol.

The other officer drew out handcuffs. “Captain?”

Aiden stepped back as the cuffs closed. “This nigga's evening is over.”

The officers brought Silas upright and led him between the parted guests. His silver jacket had collected dark marks from the floor. A waiter looked at the broken crystal, picked up the champagne bucket, and moved it to another table without spilling a drop.

The drummer lowered his stick. The pianist found the opening notes. Music spread back across the room, first thinly, then under the chatter of people describing the gunshot to people who had also heard it.

Rex offered Aiden his hand. “You ever need somebody in your corner.”

Aiden shook it. “Stay out of mine first.”

Rex laughed, caught his ribs, and laughed more carefully. He gave Aiden a short bow of the head before a friend helped him toward the bar.

Aiden wiped Porsche's lipstick from his cheek with his thumb. He turned toward the dance floor. Red and Shawna had begun moving again.
