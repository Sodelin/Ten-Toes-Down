Below the gallery, the coffin lay partly inside the shallow fountain. Its wheeled stand had tipped against the basin's edge. One deliveryman crouched behind the black lid. The other stood near the reception desk with his gun raised toward the upper floor.

Aiden fired from the chair. The standing man's arm jerked back. He dropped behind the desk, and his pistol struck the floor beside it. The man at the fountain fired upward. Glass burst from a panel beneath the gallery's handrail, scattering across the tiles.

Tank pulled the wheelchair away from the rail. One wheel caught the broken panel's bottom bracket. The chair tipped. Aiden struck the floor on his side, rolled with a hoarse cry, and got one knee beneath him.

Porsche put down the chair and crouched beside him. Behind her, the nurse held the bed against the wall in the lift recess. The old man's fingers had whitened against his gown.

Across the atrium below, the broad stair ended beside the reception desk. The service lift opened on the same stretch of floor. The gunman behind the coffin turned his head between that landing and the broken gallery panel, his pistol following each movement.

Aiden looked through the opening beneath the handrail.

“Nigga, who sent it?” he called down.

The gunman raised his face above the coffin lid. “Who you think?”

“I think your taste is fucking awful,” Aiden told him.

He pushed himself upright against the wall. Tank reached toward him. Aiden caught the overturned wheelchair with his foot and shoved it toward the broken panel. Its first wheel passed through the opening, then the rest tipped outward beneath the rail.

The chair struck the fountain in a crash of metal and water. It hit the coffin and drove it across the shallow basin. The gunman jumped clear as the black lid swung toward his knees. Tank fired, and the man ducked behind a marble column.

Aiden pulled himself to the rail.

Tank turned toward him. “Don't you dare.”

“Catch up,” Aiden said.

He went over.

His shoes struck the middle of the coffin lid. Black wood split beneath him, and both knees bent into the opening. Water leaped around the box as it struck the bottom of the fountain. Aiden caught a silver handle with his free hand and hauled one foot clear.

For a moment he stood above his own engraved name, bent at the waist, his pistol held low beside him. People crouched along the walls of the atrium. A woman behind an overturned chair had both hands over her mouth.

The gunman came from behind the column. His pistol clicked without firing. He threw it at Aiden and lunged into the fountain.

Aiden tore a broken section of lid free and swung it. The lacquered edge struck the man's shoulder. They fell together into the water, scattering floating flowers from a nearby arrangement. The man caught Aiden at the throat and drove him against the coffin's jagged edge.

Aiden's mouth opened against the water. He planted a foot against the basin, rolled the man beneath him, and raised his pistol beside the man's face. The hands at his throat opened.

“You finished?” Aiden asked.

The man nodded.

Aiden pressed the muzzle closer without firing. “Nigga, use your words.”

“Finished,” the gunman said.

“Then stay fucking finished,” Aiden told him.

Tank came down the broad stair with his pistol trained across the lobby. He kicked the other deliveryman's dropped gun beneath the reception desk and went to the fountain. The man behind the desk remained on the floor with one hand pressed against his bleeding arm.

At the upper gallery, Porsche and the nurse pushed the bed into the service lift. The nurse held the control while Porsche steadied the patient's cast. When the ground-floor doors opened, Tank stood between them and the fountain with his gun pointed at the captive.

Porsche brought the bed out. The patient raised a fist from his gown toward Aiden.

“That's my boy!” the older man called.

Aiden tried to climb over the fountain's edge. His knee slid in the water, and Tank caught him beneath one arm. The bed passed them toward the rear treatment corridor while Porsche held its front corner clear of the broken entrance glass.

Aiden put his pistol back in his waistband while Tank held him upright.

Outside, the two drivers had got back into the white sedans. The first accelerated toward the gate. A police cruiser struck its side and pushed it across the exit. The second braked behind it with no room to pass.

A woman stepped from the cruiser carrying a long gun. Her dark glasses stayed in place as she lifted the weapon toward the first sedan.

“Hands where I can fucking see them!” she shouted.

Hands rose behind both windshields. Officers ran in from another car and opened the sedan doors.

Two minutes later, Chief Gloria Knox entered the atrium through the broken entrance. Silver showed at her temples above the arms of her glasses. She looked at the coffin, then up at the shattered gallery panel, and finally at Aiden leaning against Tank beside the fountain.

“You jumped from there?” Knox asked.

Aiden pointed toward the man still sitting in the water. “Nigga was in my way.”

Knox tilted her head. “And that solved it?”

The captive groaned. A broken strip of lacquer floated against his chest.

Knox looked down at him. “Apparently.”

Vega came from the treatment corridor with another nurse. She stopped at the sight of Aiden's wet shirt. Blood had begun spreading through it below the ribs.

“Aiden,” Vega said.

He opened his free hand toward the patient, the nurse, and Porsche. “Everybody's alive.”

Vega's eyes stayed on his side. “You were in bed.”

“Didn't have wheels,” Aiden said.

Porsche raised a hand. “I tried a chair.”

Vega shut her eyes, opened them again, and held out an arm for the fresh stretcher the nurse brought beside them.

“You saved my nurse,” Vega told Aiden.

“She saved my ass first,” Aiden said.

The nurse who had moved the old man's bed covered her face with one hand. The patient insisted on being rolled close enough to shake Aiden's. Aiden gave him his unhurt hand, and the older man gripped it with both of his.

Knox took a card from her pocket and held it in front of Aiden. He released the patient's hand to accept it.

“When you're tired of people sending coffins, come see me,” Knox said. “I'd rather give you a badge.”

Tank looked at her. “This nigga?”

“Especially this nigga,” Knox told him.

Upstairs, an officer knocked on the locked bathroom door and announced himself. Shawna opened it with Mercedes beside her, still holding the vase. Rosa came out behind them. The officer pointed toward the gallery, and the three women went down the broad stair together.

Shawna reached Aiden as Vega began lowering him onto the stretcher. She got both arms around his shoulders, kissed his face, and drew away to look at the fresh blood on his shirt.

“Impossible,” Shawna said. She kissed him again. “You are fucking impossible.”

Aiden's hand rested against her back. He kept the other pressed to his side while Vega waited beside them with a clean dressing.

“You can continue on the stretcher,” Vega told Shawna.

Shawna moved with him when the attendants laid him down. This time her hands stayed clear of the wound. Mercedes set the vase on the reception desk. It stood upright beside the hole a bullet had made in the wood.

That evening, workers carried the broken coffin outside. Knox stood beside a truck while they loaded it. Two of the captured attackers sat handcuffed near its rear wheel, including the man Aiden had fought in the fountain. He looked up toward the hospital windows as officers brought him to his feet.

Aiden raised two fingers from the edge of his bed. The man looked away.

Vega took the hand and placed it back on the sheet. Shawna drew the curtain before the doctor began checking the new dressing.

By Aiden's fifth day in the hospital, he could cross the room slowly with somebody beside him. He stopped halfway to the window and put a hand on the visitor's chair. Shawna stopped too. When he started again, she moved with him without taking her hand off his arm.

Vega stood by the door with the discharge papers.

“Leaving doesn't mean healed,” she told him. “Walk a little. Rest. Sit down when you're tired. No heroics.”

Tank waited behind the wheelchair with two duffel bags at his feet and Rosa's red coat over one arm. He looked at the papers, then at the fresh bandage beneath Aiden's shirt.

“He won't,” Shawna told Vega.

The doctor turned toward her. “You either.”

Shawna nodded. Aiden looked from one woman to the other and lowered himself into the wheelchair.

Before Tank pushed it toward the door, Shawna lifted the cross from around her neck. She held it in front of Aiden. He opened his collar, and she lowered the chain over his head, drawing it clear of the healing cut at his hairline.

The cross settled against his chest. Her hand stayed over it for a moment. Dante's watch slid toward her knuckles when she withdrew it.

Downstairs, six black cars waited beneath the hospital canopy. Men stood beside them as Tank brought the wheelchair out. One straightened his jacket. Another opened the rear door of a Mercedes and stepped back, holding it wide.

“Welcome back,” the man told Aiden.

Aiden set both feet on the pavement. Shawna took his arm while he pushed himself up from the chair. His shoulders lifted with the effort, then lowered when he stood straight.

She kept her hand beneath his elbow.

“Where we going?” Shawna asked him.

Aiden looked at the open car door, then down at her.

“Home, baby,” he said. “We just gotta get one.”
