Back at the chapel, the second SUV had reversed behind a line of parked cars. Men from the funeral held it there with gunfire whenever its driver tried to pull forward. Tank stood at the chapel entrance, shouting for people to stay away from the windows.

Shawna took off her white coat and put it into Rosa's hands. Porsche opened the Bentley's driver's door and held out the keys. Shawna took them, pulled on her gloves, and got behind the wheel. Porsche ran around to the passenger side with her purse against her chest.

They left by the clear end of the drive. Tank saw them turn toward the service road, called two men to the chapel doors, and went to his Mercedes.

At the intersection ahead of the hearse, the Bentley appeared broadside. Shawna turned it toward the approaching SUV. Porsche braced her purse against the dashboard and caught the door handle with her free hand.

Aiden leaned forward in the hearse.

“Baby, don't—” he said.

The Bentley hit the SUV's front corner. The larger vehicle swung sideways across the road. Shawna's car spun behind it, tires smoking. Porsche's door flew open, struck its stop, and slammed shut as the car changed direction.

The SUV climbed the curb and hit a stone wall. Its hood flew upward. One front wheel hung crookedly beneath it, still turning.

Aiden braked the hearse beside the Bentley. Shawna looked across at him through her broken window. White glove fabric had torn across one hand.

“Nigga, your wife drives too,” Shawna told him.

Aiden grinned. He started to open his door, then reached for his pistol as a man came out of the wrecked SUV holding a gun.

Aiden fired from behind the hearse door. The man dropped behind the engine, and his weapon fell into the gutter. Another man reached out to pull him back. Tank's Mercedes arrived from the far end of the road and stopped across their path.

Tank got out with his gun raised. The man pulling his companion lifted both hands above the SUV's roof.

“Who sent you?” Tank shouted.

The man kept his hands up but said nothing. Tank moved closer, his shoes crunching over the Bentley's broken glass.

“Nigga, this is the easy question,” Tank told him.

The man looked from Tank to the hearse. “T had friends!”

Aiden stepped out, holding the open door to steady himself.

“Then where the fuck were you when they passed the collection plate?” Aiden asked him.

The man's eyes moved to the coffin visible through the broken rear window.

“You killed him,” the man said.

“And I'm burying him,” Aiden told him. “You coming between those things again, you can ask him how he feels about it yourself.”

The man looked at Aiden's pistol and the dark patch beginning to spread through his shirt. Shawna stepped from the Bentley in her white dress, glass falling from the door onto the road around her shoes.

“You don't stop,” the man said to Aiden.

Aiden kept his gun pointed toward the ground between them. “Nigga, you should've asked somebody before you came.”

Police cruisers arrived behind Tank. Officers moved the armed men onto the ground, kicked their guns clear, and opened the SUV's remaining doors. Knox stepped from her car and walked toward Aiden with her eyes on the hearse.

“Please tell me that's an empty hearse,” Knox said.

Aiden pointed behind himself. “Family in the back.”

She looked through the broken window. “You brought the deceased into a pursuit?”

“Nigga wasn't walking,” Aiden told her.

Knox took off her glasses. She looked at the coffin, then at the crushed Bentley, then at Aiden. Her mouth opened. She laughed with both hands on her hips, her face tilted toward the sky. One of the men kneeling beside the SUV turned to stare at her until an officer put a hand on the back of his collar.

“Aiden,” Knox said, putting the glasses back on, “you are wasting your fucking talents in civilian clothes.”

“You keep saying that,” Aiden told her.

“I'm going to keep saying it until somebody gives me what I want,” Knox said.

Shawna reached him. He tucked his pistol away, took her torn glove, and eased it over the shallow cut on her hand. A little blood marked the white fabric. He kissed the skin beside the cut.

“I asked you to stay with the car,” Aiden said.

Shawna looked at the Bentley. “I did. I moved the car.”

Porsche came around its crushed front corner and held the keys out to Tank.

“Tell Leon this one had a meaningful life too,” she said.

Tank looked at the folded fender. “Nigga gonna start sending bicycles.”

***

A police cruiser led the hearse back to the chapel. Aiden drove with Shawna beside him. The rear window was gone, and small pieces of glass shifted in the back whenever they turned. T's coffin remained secured beneath its scratched blue lid.

At the chapel, officers had emptied the second SUV. Its occupants sat along the curb under guard. Other officers stood at the entrance while medical workers moved among the guests.

People came out when they heard the engines. Someone began applauding. A woman on the steps raised both hands and shouted Aiden's name, her hat slipping sideways as she waved.

Aiden stopped the hearse and sat for a moment with his eyes closed. Shawna put a hand on his shoulder. He opened his door, lowered one foot to the pavement, and used the frame to draw himself upright.

The older couple Tank had rescued came down the steps. The woman put both hands against Aiden's face.

“You brought everybody home,” she told him.

Aiden looked past her at a white sheet beside the curb. One gold cuff link remained visible beneath its edge. A funeral worker bent and drew the sheet down over Rico's hand.

Aiden took the woman's hands gently away from his face. He held them for a moment, then released them. Tank came beside him.

“Vernon too,” Tank said quietly.

Aiden turned toward him.

“Carried him inside,” Tank continued. “He didn't make it.”

For a while Aiden stood without moving. The applause had stopped. From inside the chapel came a woman's voice repeating a name, interrupted each time by her own breathing.

Aiden looked toward the hearse.

“Help me finish this,” he told Tank.

They went to the rear doors. Four other men joined them. The funeral workers released the restraints and guided the coffin toward their hands. Aiden closed his fingers around a gold handle. Tank shifted nearer, taking more of the weight beside him.

The musicians began the hymn again. Its first phrase shook; on the next, the voices came together. A replacement hearse waited beside the shot-up car, its rear door held open by a funeral worker. People moved aside as the men carried T between the two vehicles. Nobody stepped into their path.

***

T was buried later that afternoon. At the house, Tank came upstairs with the cemetery receipt and a program folded into his palm. Aiden remained on the bed while Vega examined him behind the closed bedroom door. Shawna sat beside him in the dirty white dress, one hand wrapped in a small dressing.

“You were told to rest,” Vega said.

“I was sitting at a funeral,” Aiden told her.

The doctor removed the bloodied cloth from his side. “You are bleeding in a house full of armed men. I am interested in what happened after the sitting.”

Aiden turned his face toward the window. Shawna kept her hand on the edge of the mattress while Vega checked the wound.

The doctor gave her instructions slowly. She wrote them down, placed the paper on the nightstand, and tapped it with her pen.

“If this worsens, he comes back under my care,” Vega told Shawna. “Not after another event. Not when he finishes a telephone call.”

Shawna nodded. Aiden opened his mouth. Vega held up one finger without turning toward him, and he closed it again.

After she left, Tank came in. He set the receipt and program on the dresser. Then he pulled a chair near the bed and laid two names in front of Aiden.

“Rico,” Tank said. “Thirty-two. Vernon. Forty-one.”

Aiden repeated both names. Tank gave him the names of the injured men as well, pausing after each one until Aiden said it back.

“I'll pay for everything,” Aiden told him.

Tank put two telephone numbers beside the names. “Their mothers need to hear a human being first.”

Aiden picked up his phone.

On the first call, a woman answered after several rings. Aiden gave his name and sat straighter against the pillows.

“Rico was at the funeral with me,” Aiden told her. “I'm sorry.”

“They told me,” Rico's mother said.

He lowered his face. Her breathing moved against the receiver. In the hall outside, somebody laughed before a door closed over the sound.

“Was he by himself?” Rico's mother asked.

Aiden's free hand closed against the blanket. He looked at Tank, then down at the two names beside his knee.

“I saw somebody reach him,” Aiden said. “One of the men carrying the coffin. He went right to him.”

The woman drew a breath. Aiden held the phone against his ear and waited while she cried. Shawna's hand moved toward his on the blanket and stopped beside it.

“He showed me his cuff links,” Aiden said when the woman grew quieter. “The ones his daughter sent. He was wearing them.”

“He called me about those,” Rico's mother said.

Aiden shut his eyes. His mouth moved before any words came.

“He looked good,” he told her.

She began speaking about Rico. Aiden stayed on the line. He did not reach for the papers about costs or interrupt when she returned to something she had already said. By the time the call ended, his hand lay open beside Shawna's.

On the second call, he had to give his name twice. Vernon's mother asked him to repeat it, then remained quiet for so long that he lowered the phone and checked the screen before putting it back to his ear.

“I'm here,” Aiden told her.

The woman asked what had happened. He answered in short sentences, stopping when she spoke. His face remained lowered toward the sheet. Shawna sat beside him without touching the phone.

When both calls were over, Aiden threw it across the room. It struck the wall and landed on the rug beneath the dresser. Shawna followed it with her eyes. Tank got up and left them alone, closing the door behind himself.

“I want Red,” Aiden said.

Shawna turned toward him. “You don't know it was him.”

“Who the fuck else, Shawna?” Aiden asked.

“People loved T,” she said.

Aiden struck the blanket with his palm. “Nigga, I paid for the funeral.”

“You also killed him,” Shawna told him.

Aiden looked at her. His mouth tightened, then opened.

“You defending Red now?” he asked.

Shawna stood. “Say that again.”

He did not answer. She opened Dante's watch clasp and put the watch faceup on the bed between them.

“You think I forgot what those men did?” Shawna asked.

Aiden looked down at the watch.

“You think because I got a house and some fucking earrings I forgot where they left him?” she continued.

“No,” Aiden said.

Shawna kept her hands at her sides. “Then don't talk to me like I'm sleeping through this.”

She picked the watch up. Her fingers shook as she tried to close its clasp. Aiden reached for her hand and stopped just short of touching it. She held it toward him. He steadied the watch and fastened it around her wrist.

“I'm scared,” Aiden said.

Shawna looked at his face. He kept her hand between his.

“I'm scared they get you while I'm laying here,” he told her.

She sat down again, closer this time.

“Then stop trying to stand up every time somebody says your name,” Shawna said.

He bent toward her until his forehead rested against hers. They stayed that way while voices passed along the corridor outside.

Rosa stopped at the far end of the hall with a plate in her hand.

“Eat something,” she told Tank.

“I ain't hungry,” Tank said.

“Nigga, I didn't ask for your fucking feelings about the sandwich,” Rosa told him.

Inside the bedroom, Shawna laughed once through her nose. Aiden's hand stayed around hers.

***

At sunset, a man from the Saint left a black envelope at the gate. A guard brought it to Tank, who placed it on a silver kitchen tray and carried it upstairs.

Aiden looked at the tray when Tank stopped beside the bed. Shawna sat near the window with a clean dress laid across her lap.

“Nigga, why you serving me a threat?” Aiden asked.

Tank held the tray farther out. “Didn't wanna touch it more than I had to.”

Aiden took the envelope. Inside, gold printing announced breakfast at the Saint the next morning. Penthouse. His name and Shawna's appeared together beneath the invitation.

A smaller card lay behind it. He read the handwriting once, then held it toward Shawna. She set down the dress, took the card, and sat on the edge of the bed.

“What?” Tank asked.

Shawna did not answer. Her thumb moved to Dante's watch and stayed against its face. Aiden put the gold invitation on the sheet between them.

Red's handwritten card contained one sentence:

*Dante didn't die for three million.*
