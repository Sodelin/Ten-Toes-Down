At the house, shoes covered the bedroom chairs. Perfume hung in the air. A champagne bottle stood in the bathroom sink beside Aiden's toothbrush, and a heated hairbrush occupied the space where he had left his gun. The pistol now lay on a folded hand towel atop the dresser.

He stopped in the doorway. Rosa stood in red with diamonds at her throat. She held a comb in one hand and pointed it toward the hall.

“Why are you in here?” Rosa asked him.

Aiden spread both hands. “Nigga, because I sleep here.”

“Not while we're busy,” Rosa told him.

He looked past her toward Shawna. “I need Shawna.”

Rosa turned toward her friend. “Your nigga needs patience.”

“Nigga, I bought patience,” Aiden said. “They said it'll be here tomorrow.”

Rosa picked up a shoe. Aiden took his pistol from the dresser, tucked it inside his jacket at the waist, and stepped back into the hall before she raised it.

Downstairs, Marcellus sat by the windows in a gray suit. His shoes had been polished until they reflected the table legs. Celeste's letter lay before him inside its protective sleeve. He rested a hand across it without covering the signature.

“My nigga, tell me you ready,” Aiden said.

Marcellus lifted his face. “I have been ready for years.”

“Nigga, that's not what I asked,” Aiden told him.

Marcellus's mouth moved toward a smile. “Yes, Aiden.”

Aiden lowered himself into the opposite chair. He stopped halfway, braced a hand against the table, and sat the rest of the way down. Marcellus watched the movement.

“Does she know how much that hurts?” Marcellus asked.

“She knows everything,” Aiden said.

Marcellus kept his hand on the letter. “Does she?”

Aiden looked toward the stairs. “Nigga, she knows enough.”

Marcellus folded the protective sleeve around the letter and put it inside his coat.

“The stone cannot love you back,” Marcellus told him.

Aiden faced him again. “I got somebody for that.”

“Then remember which one you came for,” Marcellus said.

Tank came in holding two phones. He turned one facedown when he saw them.

“Niggas, the cars ready,” Tank said.

Aiden looked at the phones. “What nigga calling you?”

“Nobody,” Tank said.

Aiden pointed at his hands. “Nigga, then why you got two phones?”

Tank put both away. “In case somebody does.”

Aiden watched his hands disappear into the pockets. Then Shawna's footsteps sounded on the stairs, and he turned toward her.

She wore dark blue, fitted close to her body with a train trailing behind her. Diamonds hung from her ears. Her hair was pinned up, leaving the little scar above her eyebrow visible. Dante's watch remained on her wrist.

“Rosa's dress,” Shawna said when she reached the lower steps. “She never wore it.”

Aiden rose from his chair. “Nigga, I said I'd buy you anything.”

“I wanted this one,” Shawna told him.

Rosa followed, carrying the train clear of the stair.

“And I want it back,” Rosa said. “Both of you hear me?”

“Yes,” Shawna answered.

Rosa let the train settle onto the floor and took a small envelope from her purse.

“I've got the watch paper,” Rosa said. “The original. Nobody gets it off me.”

Shawna opened her own purse and showed Aiden a folded copy of Red's order. Rosa returned the original to the small envelope and put it back inside her purse. Marcellus touched his coat above Celeste's separate letter, then stood.

Aiden held out his hands toward Shawna.

“Turn around for your nigga,” he told her.

She turned. The train moved across the floor behind her.

“Again,” Aiden said.

Shawna faced him. “Aiden.”

“Nigga, I'm trying to remember whether there's anybody left alive I need to thank,” he told her.

She took her silver pistol from the table, slipped it into her purse beside the copy, and closed the catch. Then she came to Aiden and put both hands against his chest.

“If you tear this dress, I'm killing you before Red gets the chance,” Shawna said.

“Your nigga will buy the factory,” Aiden told her.

She leaned closer. “I'll kill you in the factory.”

Aiden kissed her. Tank cleared his throat. Aiden drew her back for another kiss while Rosa gathered the train away from their feet.

Before they left, Aiden stood in the drive and counted the cars with one finger.

“Wanted six,” Aiden told Tank.

Tank pointed to the Lamborghini, the Mercedes, Rosa's car, and the Bentley. “Four drivers.”

“Put somebody in the other two,” Aiden said.

Tank waited with his hand still raised. “Which somebody?”

Aiden looked toward the people gathering by the cars. Shawna held out her purse to him.

“Can drive this,” she said.

He took it. “Four cars. Everybody got a fucking opinion.” He carried the purse to the Lamborghini, opened the passenger door for her, and returned it when she sat down.

They went to the Saint in four cars. Aiden drove Shawna in the dark-blue police Lamborghini. Tank followed in the original black Mercedes. Rosa drove Marcellus, and Porsche brought Mercedes in the green Bentley.

The cars pulled beneath the canopy one after another. Gold light shone across the entrance and the tall flowers beside it. Guests in white tie crossed the drive between photographers and guards. Coins glittered at the bottom of the fountain.

Aiden came out in his white suit with the cross against his chest and the captain's badge inside his jacket. Shawna took his arm. Cameras flashed as they approached the doors.

“Keep walking,” she told him softly.

He raised his chin and continued beside her. At a wall of white roses, a photographer asked them to stop. Shawna put her hand on Aiden's shoulder. He rested his at her waist while the man took the picture.

A reporter approached with a microphone.

“Are you taking over Big T's interests?” the reporter asked Aiden.

“Every nigga that owes them anything owes me,” Aiden said.

The reporter brought the microphone closer. “And your relationship with Mr. Redmond?”

Aiden's mouth curved. “Ask me at the end.”

Rosa stepped between them and the microphone. Tank came close behind Aiden, his jacket brushing the white sleeve. He put a hand against Aiden's arm before they went through the entrance.

“Anything happen tonight, you stay with me,” Tank told him.

“Nigga, that was already the idea,” Aiden said.

Tank let go and followed him inside.

A staircase curved around a statue of a naked woman holding a bowl of fire. Real flames lifted above the rim. Guests paused beside it for photographs, then moved toward the ballroom past white flowers and waiters carrying champagne.

A television preacher stood near the stairs displaying a broad watch to another guest. The other man brought out his own wrist. The preacher held both side by side and laughed.

Beyond them, the door of a private lounge stood open. Champagne bottles crowded mirrored tables beside cocaine and rolled notes. A man sitting between two women lifted his glass when Aiden passed.

“Aiden!” the man called. “You disappear when you fall in love?”

Aiden stopped beside the doorway. “Nigga, I got promoted.”

The women laughed. One blew him a kiss. Shawna looked from her to the man and the other woman, then turned toward Aiden.

“All of them?” Shawna asked.

“Your nigga had a long weekend,” Aiden told her.

She tightened her hand around his. “You nasty motherfucker.”

He smiled at her. “You love me.”

Shawna pulled him back into the hall. “Don't get confident.”

At the far end of the room, Red came down the staircase in a dark green jacket. Aiden faced him with his hand still around Shawna's. Red's eyes remained on her as he crossed the floor.

***

“There she is,” Red said.

He held out both hands. Shawna gave him one. He bent and kissed it just above Dante's watch. Aiden's fingers curled against his own palm, then opened at his side.

“Nigga, you throw a good party,” Aiden told Red. “Shame about your future.”

Red raised his face toward him.

“Captain,” Red said. He paused before continuing. “You clean up well.”

Aiden looked at the hand still holding Shawna's. “You still talking.”

Shawna's free fingers tightened against Aiden's arm. Red laughed softly and turned toward a photographer crossing the room.

“We should get a photograph,” Red said. “All three of us.”

He beckoned the photographer closer and moved beside Shawna, leaving her between the two men. One of Red's hands rested against her upper arm. A broad dark ring sat on that hand, a blue stone set almost flush into it. The green sleeve partly covered its edge.

A few feet behind them, Marcellus stopped moving. His eyes stayed on Red's hand against Shawna's arm. Rosa stood beside him with her purse against her body.

The flash went off.

“Another,” Red told the photographer.

Aiden looked directly into the lens. His jaw remained tight above the open collar. Shawna held her position between them until the second flash faded.

An attendant brought glossy copies while Red greeted Rosa. She took two photographs, looked at them briefly, and put them into her purse in front of the envelope containing the original order.

Red turned back toward Shawna and held out his arm.

“Now,” Red said, “I promised this woman a dance.”

Shawna looked at Aiden. His hand remained around hers for another moment before he released it.

“One dance,” Aiden said.

Red smiled. “Let's see what she wants.”

He led Shawna onto the floor. Aiden stayed at its edge, turning as they moved among the other couples. When Red's green sleeve passed behind another dancer, Aiden took one step sideways to keep them in view.
