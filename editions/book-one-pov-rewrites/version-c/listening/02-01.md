## 2. First Night in Vegas

On the night Aiden met Shawna, white powder dusted the front of his shirt, and blood had dried inside the bezel of his watch. He rubbed at the watch with his thumb while Big T held the nightclub's back door open. The second hand passed beneath a thin red smear and emerged on the other side.

“You coming?” T asked.

Aiden showed him the watch. “Nigga, somebody got inside it.”

T took Aiden by the shoulder and steered him through the doorway. The bass shook the metal door against its stop. Beyond the loading light, cars stood crookedly across the lot, blue cloth hanging from pockets and mirrors. Eleven men waited among them. One bent his knee and pulled his heel up behind him. Another removed his rings and laid them on the hood of a Cadillac.

Aiden lowered his wrist.

T shut the door. With him, there were twelve.

“Once a nigga in, that nigga in,” T said. “Understand?”

“Nigga, I understand,” Aiden told him.

T kept his hand on Aiden's shoulder. “Summer ends, a body drops, suddenly a nigga got somewhere else to be.”

“I got nowhere else to be,” Aiden said. “That's why I'm standing here instead of enjoying the fucking music.”

The man stretching his leg laughed. T looked at him. He put his foot down and rubbed his palms together.

“You think this funny?” T asked Aiden.

Aiden looked around the circle. “Nigga, I think twelve is a committee. Which one of you takes the minutes?”

T drove a fist into his face.

Aiden struck the Cadillac with one shoulder. The man who had left his rings on its hood snatched them away before Aiden's hand came down. A second fist split Aiden's lip against his teeth. He turned into another man's elbow, staggered sideways, and lost his footing when a shoe struck behind his knee.

His palms hit the concrete. Somebody kicked him under the arm. His breath broke into a dry cough against the ground.

“Stay down,” a man in a blue cap told him.

Aiden caught the man's trouser leg and pulled himself up far enough to hit him in the stomach.

“Nigga, y'all just told me to stay IN,” Aiden said. “Pick an instruction.”

The man bent over him. Aiden shoved him into the others and got to his feet with one eye running blood. He wiped it with the back of his hand. The next punch caught his raised forearm; his answer struck a jaw with an audible click.

Tank came through the gap. His shirt pulled tight across his shoulders, and its top button had already disappeared. He ducked beneath Aiden's arm and landed a short punch in his ribs. Aiden folded around it, hooked both hands into Tank's shirt, and kept going forward.

They hit another car. The alarm began screaming. Tank's elbow dented the door as he tried to turn Aiden against it.

“Not the paint!” the car's owner shouted.

Aiden drove a fist into Tank's face. “Nigga, you should've thought about the paint before you started throwing people at it!”

Tank caught one of his wrists. Aiden hit him with the other hand, short blows with no room to draw back. The cloth tore under his knuckles. Tank planted a foot against the tire and shoved, but Aiden's grip pulled him back against the dent.

“Get this nigga off me!” Tank called.

T stood several feet away, laughing. The others had stopped advancing. One wiped his mouth; another held a hand against his stomach.

“T!” Tank shouted.

“You said he was little,” T told him.

Tank ducked another blow. “Nigga, I have new information!”

T caught Aiden around the chest from behind and dragged him clear. Tank came off the car with both fists raised. T put an arm between them.

“Enough,” T said. “Twelve niggas. Look at him. Still shopping.”

Aiden stood with his fists beside his face. Blood dripped from his chin onto the powder on his shirt. His hands shook.

“Nigga, I'm done,” he said.

T looked at the fists. “You sure?”

“I'm waiting for my hands to hear it,” Aiden told him.

T laughed once, then reached into his pocket. He took out a gold chain and opened it across both hands. The little cross at its center swung beneath his fingers. He lowered the chain over Aiden's head, holding it clear of the cut above his eye, and let the cross settle against his chest.

The car alarm stopped. Nobody spoke while Aiden lifted the cross away from his bloody shirt.

“My brother wore that,” T said. “Real nigga.”

Aiden raised his face. “Where my nigga at, then? I should thank him.”

“Dead,” T said.

A car passed on the street beyond the lot. Its light traveled over the Cadillac and disappeared behind the club. Aiden closed his hand around the cross. T took the back of his neck, drew him forward, and kissed his forehead.

“Nigga, don't make me bury you too,” T said.

Aiden swallowed. “Not this nigga.”

T kept hold of his neck as they turned toward the door.

“Come get your money,” T said. He raised his voice to the men ahead of them. “Niggas, make room for my son.”

Tank opened the door. Aiden reached for it, and Tank moved his hand away.

“Nigga, walk through,” Tank told him. “I can still defeat a door.”

***

Blue light filled the back room. It struck the smoke above the tables and lay in bright bars along bottles, gun barrels, and the edges of bundled cash. Bricks of cocaine covered a glass tabletop beside an ashtray too full to contain its ashes. A woman in a diamond necklace leaned against a man with two pistols tucked into the same belt. When he shifted in his chair, both grips knocked against the glass.

The woman raised her drink toward Aiden.

“Damn, baby. You survive a war?” she asked.

Aiden wiped his mouth. “Yeah. And I won.”

Several people cheered. T pushed him toward a chair beside the money. A man handed him an open bottle; another held out a burning blunt. Aiden took the bottle first. The liquor touched his split lip, and he lowered it with one eye screwed shut before taking a second drink.

“This the nigga beat Tank?” the woman asked T.

“That's the nigga,” T said.

She folded a thousand dollars into Aiden's shirt pocket, pressing the bills down with two fingers so they sat clear of the blood. Aiden looked at the pocket and then at her.

“Keep looking like that,” she told him. “I'll need change.”

Tank came in holding a plastic bag of ice against his jaw. He took the bottle from Aiden, drank, and returned it before pulling out the next chair.

“Nigga, who taught you to fight dirty like that?” Tank asked.

“Same nigga taught you to fight unconscious,” Aiden said.

Tank stayed upright beside the chair. Laughter broke around the table. Aiden lifted the bottle a little but did not drink. Then Tank's mouth opened, showing blood on his teeth, and his laugh joined the others. He dropped into the chair hard enough to send an ash down from the edge of the tray.

“Twenty-eight years old,” Tank said, moving the ice to the other side of his jaw. “Nigga, I'm too old to learn shit like this.”

Aiden leaned toward him. “I'm twenty-four. You got four years to catch up.”

Tank reached for the bottle again. This time Aiden passed it to him before he asked.

T threw a set of keys onto the table. They slid between two stacks of money and stopped against Aiden's forearm.

“Black Mercedes outside,” T said. “That's yours.”

Aiden lifted the keys and pressed the emblem with his thumb.

“Nigga, these keys real?” he asked. “You serious?”

“Where's that car you had?” T asked him.

“Burned outside my ex-boyfriend's apartment,” Aiden said. “Nigga still thinks that means I want him back.”

T pointed at the keys. “Then drive this one somewhere else. West side need a nigga. West side got you.”

Aiden's thumb stopped on the emblem. Around him, cash continued passing from one pile to another. Tank knocked the neck of the bottle against his wrist. Aiden took it and drank without looking away from T.

T opened a photograph on his phone and put the screen in front of him.

A woman stood beside a swimming pool. A black dress followed the line of her body, and diamond earrings caught the pool lights beneath her dark hair. Behind her, the Strip rose in rows of light against the night. Aiden brought the screen closer. Tank lowered his ice bag and watched him.

“Shawna,” T said. “Twenty-six. Works for Red.”

“Nigga, working for Red doing what?” Aiden asked.

T took the phone back. “Whatever Red wants.”

Aiden set the bottle down.

“She's at the Saint tonight,” T continued. “Penthouse. She's bringing a case down at eleven. You take her and the money to my house in Henderson. That's all you gotta do.”

“Why she leaving him?” Aiden asked.

“Because she finally got smart,” T said.

Aiden held out his hand for another look at the picture. T showed it to him without releasing the phone.

“Does Red know about this little family pickup?” Aiden asked.

“If he knew, we wouldn't be having this conversation,” T said.

He bent over Aiden's chair and gripped the back of his neck. The people at the next table were shouting over a count of money. T brought his mouth close to Aiden's ear.

“Don't fall in love with her,” T said.

Aiden laughed. T's hand stayed where it was.

“I'm serious,” T told him. “Look at me.”

Aiden turned his face. “Nigga, look at me. Do I look like the nigga falls in love?”

T studied him, then released his neck. Aiden stood, pushed the keys into his pocket, and adjusted the pistol already tucked at the back of his waistband. Tank tipped the bottle toward him. Aiden touched its neck with two fingers on his way to the door.

***

Water fell through a gold fountain outside the Saint. The valet lane curved around it beneath a canopy lit so brightly that the blood on Aiden's collar showed as soon as he stepped out of the Mercedes. A valet approached holding a ticket and slowed beside the open door.

“You need somebody to call a doctor?” the valet asked.

“Nigga, my car needs you first,” Aiden told him. He placed a hundred-dollar bill against the ticket. “Nothing happens to it.”

The valet took the money. Aiden looked back once at the car before going through the revolving door.

Upstairs, the penthouse windows reflected a room full of people. Dice struck a marble table between bank-wrapped stacks of bills. Dresses brushed against holstered pistols. A man in a white fur coat bent over a trophy with a rolled note at his nose, then carried the trophy with him when he went to get a drink. Someone reached toward it. He turned his whole body away.

Near the bar, Mercedes saw Aiden and held her glass against her chest. She looked him over from the cut on his face to the new keys swinging in his hand.

“You finally got a Mercedes you can afford, baby?” she asked.

“Come see it,” Aiden said.

Mercedes lifted an eyebrow. “Nigga, you still owe me flowers.”

Aiden reached into the pocket the woman at T's club had filled. “You took my shoes.”

“You called me my sister's name,” Mercedes said. “In a hotel room. While I was in it.”

“I noticed when I had to leave barefoot,” Aiden told her. He counted five hundred dollars into her hand. “Buy the whole fucking garden.”

Mercedes folded the money and kissed his cheek. Her lipstick touched the edge of a dry streak of blood.

“You bleeding on the good side of your face,” she said.

“Both sides good, baby,” Aiden told her.

She laughed. Aiden smiled back, then looked past her toward the windows. His hand stopped turning the keys.

Shawna stood near the glass with a cigarette between her fingers and a silver case beside her ankle. A man in a dark suit spoke close to her shoulder. She had turned her face away from him. Her gaze followed Aiden as he crossed the room.

He stopped in front of her. At this distance the little scar above her eyebrow showed beneath the light. She lowered her cigarette. The man beside her looked at Aiden's cut mouth and stepped aside with his glass.

“T sent me,” Aiden said.

“I know,” Shawna told him.

“How?” Aiden asked.

Her cigarette rose toward her mouth and stopped. With her other hand, she touched the gold cross against his chest.

“Where'd you get that?” she asked.

“T,” Aiden said.

“Tonight?” Shawna asked.

Aiden nodded. “Yeah.”

She turned the cross over. Three small letters had been cut into its back: D. B. M. Her thumb moved across them. Aiden looked down at her hand and let the keys fall still beside his leg.

“You know what those mean?” Shawna asked him.

“No,” Aiden said.

She let the cross drop against his shirt. “Drive before you find out.”

She crushed the cigarette into a nearby ashtray and picked up her purse. Aiden took the case. Shawna caught his free hand, and they crossed the room together. A man by the door put down his drink and took out a phone. At the marble table, another man stopped counting.

The elevator stood beyond a short corridor. A guard stepped into their path with one hand against the front of his jacket.

“Nigga, where the fuck you taking her?” the guard asked Aiden.

Aiden lifted their joined hands. “With this nigga right here. You need my picture?”

“Nigga, I'm asking her,” the guard said.

Aiden released Shawna's hand and struck him in the throat. The guard hit the wall with both shoulders. His mouth opened without a word coming out. Aiden shoved him down as his fingers went under his jacket. The guard's heels slid on the polished floor, and the back of his head struck the paneling.

Shawna pressed the elevator button. The doors opened with a soft chime. Aiden stepped over the fallen man's shoes, carrying the case close against his leg.

Another man came around the corner. Shawna took a small silver pistol from her purse and aimed it at his chest.

“You get one chance,” she told him.

The man stopped. His hands remained at his sides. Shawna backed into the elevator beside Aiden and kept the muzzle level until the doors met between them and the corridor.

For several floors neither spoke. The numbers descended above the doors. Shawna held the pistol in both hands, low now, with its barrel pointed at the floor in front of her shoes.

“Niggas chasing you out of every party,” Aiden asked, “or I got the special one?”

Shawna looked at him. “Only the ones I survive.”

The elevator slowed. She put the pistol into her purse before the lobby doors opened. Aiden held out his hand. She took it again.
