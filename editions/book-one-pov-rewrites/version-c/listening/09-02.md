Blue cloth turned orange along the curtain's edge. A singer stepped backward into the row behind her. Someone shouted. The gunman under the balcony fired into the ceiling, and the audience rose almost together, chairs scraping and caps falling into the aisles.

Aiden took the microphone from its stand.

“Stay off the stairs!” he shouted. “Niggas on the floor, heads down!”

His voice came from every speaker in the room. Knox fired from beside the stage. The gunman fell behind a row of seats, and her officers moved toward him along the wall. Tank brought the people nearest him down into their row while Shawna pulled Rosa clear of the aisle.

The balcony gunman raised his pistol. Aiden drew his own and fired. The man struck the rail, dropped behind it, and lost his gun into the empty aisle below.

The choir began moving down from the risers. Smoke spread across their white robes. Aiden put his pistol away, left the microphone on the stage edge, and ran toward them.

“This way!” Aiden called.

A woman with a cane had stopped halfway down. He caught her beneath one arm. A man from the choir took her other side, and together they brought her onto the stage while flames spread above the risers.

Mercer stood near the lectern holding Aiden's diploma against his chest.

“Principal! Move!” Aiden called.

Mercer looked through the smoke. “Your degree—”

“Nigga, I need somebody alive to sign it!” Aiden told him.

Mercer came away from the lectern. Shawna reached the stage and caught his arm. Aiden helped the woman with the cane toward Tank, who lifted her clear of the press of people.

A beam fell across the side exit. Sparks leaped over the floor, and the doorway disappeared behind burning wood. At the rear of the auditorium, people continued getting out, but overturned seats had jammed the center aisle. Smoke spread between the front rows and the far doors. Flames had reached the loading entrance behind the choir.

Aiden looked at the covered car. Knox was already pulling the cloth away.

Dark blue bodywork showed beneath it. Gold shields marked the doors. Black wheels stood low against the stage, and a light bar gleamed across the windshield. Knox held the keys above the open driver's side.

“Don't scratch it,” she told Aiden, throwing them to him.

He caught them. “Nigga, don't limit me.”

Tank handed the woman with the cane to two choir members beside the wall and came to the car.

“You driving in the school?” Tank asked.

Aiden opened the door. “I'm improving the exit.”

Tank looked toward the blocked aisle. “Nigga, I went to the wrong fucking college.”

He pulled the nearest overturned seats clear of the car's path. Knox took up the microphone from the stage edge.

“Away from the center aisle,” Knox ordered. “Stay between the rows. Officers, clear his path.”

People moved toward the walls and deeper into the seats. An officer lifted a fallen chair over the end of a row. Tank waved both arms toward Aiden when the strip between the stage and the jammed seats was clear of people.

Aiden started the Lamborghini. The engine's roar rose above the fire. Shawna stood beside Mercer at the edge of the stage with Aiden's cap clutched against her dress. She looked at the hood, then through its windshield. Her mouth opened into a smile. Her hand trembled around the cap's brim.

The car crossed the stage and dropped down the shallow steps into the center aisle. Its nose struck the piled seats. Wood broke. Metal frames slid toward the empty spaces on either side. Aiden kept the wheel straight as the car drove the obstruction ahead of it.

A burning curtain fell across the hood. Flame washed over the windshield. The cloth caught on the mirror, tore, and slid away as the Lamborghini reached the far doors.

Aiden pressed the accelerator.

Both doors came off their hinges. The car burst into daylight, went down the broad front steps, and landed in the courtyard with its siren sounding. He braked beyond the foot of the steps and got out.

People came through the opening behind him. Choir members led graduates by the hands. Tank carried the woman with the cane. Rosa and Mercedes pushed two benches clear of the doorway while Porsche stood nearby with a fire extinguisher in each hand, taken from the cabinets beside the exits. She set one down, pulled the other against her hip, and drove a white cloud toward flames at the side of the opening.

“Keep moving!” Porsche shouted. “Niggas, the air is outside!”

Aiden ran back up the steps. Knox met him at the broken doorway with Mercer beside her. Shawna came through behind them, one hand over her mouth and the other holding his cap.

“Baby!” Shawna called.

He caught her. She struck his good shoulder with the cap, then set it on his head and straightened it above his forehead.

“You almost forgot something,” Shawna told him.

Aiden looked back at the broken doors. “Nigga, I was making an entrance.”

“You made an exit,” Shawna said.

He turned toward her again. “A great man does both.”

She kissed him while the crowd below the steps applauded. Fire engines arrived at the edge of the courtyard. Knox's officers brought the two attackers out in handcuffs. One officer kept a bloodied dressing pressed against a prisoner's shoulder as they descended the steps.

The first man looked at the Lamborghini.

“We burned the building,” he said to the other attacker.

“And the nigga still came through it,” his partner answered.

The first man kept looking at the car. “With a police car.”

“Nigga, I saw,” the second man said.

Aiden turned toward them and straightened his cap. An officer led the men away. Aiden looked at Mercer, who still had the diploma pressed against his robe.

“Principal,” Aiden said. “You got a microphone out here?”

He handed the car keys back to Knox before following Mercer toward the courtyard. She put them into her pocket and called the officers beside the stage.

***

Workers brought a lectern into the courtyard and ran a cable from the portable sound equipment. Mercer stood beside it while Halcyon kept a hand on his back. Lorne looked at the smoke above the auditorium, then down at the folded roof drawing he still carried.

Graduates gathered in front of the steps. Soot marked some gowns. A woman held her shoes in one hand and another graduate's wrist in the other. Marcellus stood beside Rosa with one hand on her shoulder. Tank took a place behind Shawna while Mercedes lifted her camera.

Aiden stepped to the microphone.

“Aight,” he said. “Where we at?”

Laughter spread across the courtyard. People at the back moved closer. A man who had been bent over coughing lifted his face and grinned at the stage.

“Niggas, I ain't gonna lie,” Aiden continued. “I wasn't here much.”

“You were not here at all!” a graduate called from the crowd.

Aiden pointed toward the damaged auditorium. “And look what happened the morning I came. You welcome.”

Cheers rose around the fountain. A man in the third row took off his cap and waved it above his head. Aiden waited for the sound to settle before speaking again.

“You came for your name,” Aiden told them. “Some of you worked all night and came anyway. Some of you had people telling you this wasn't your kind of room. Some of you got somebody missing today who should've been here making too much fucking noise.”

Shawna looked down at Dante's watch. Then she raised her face toward Aiden.

“But you here,” he continued. “And when the doors caught fire, niggas started carrying each other. I saw that. That part belongs to you. Keep it. Don't let anybody make you little enough to forget it.”

Aiden lifted the cross from his open collar.

“Three letters on this,” he said. “D. B. M.”

Tank raised his eyebrows. Shawna's mouth widened into a smile.

“Damn, boy. Magnificent,” Aiden told the courtyard.

The crowd shouted back at him. Aiden kept the cross raised until the first wave of noise subsided.

“That's you too,” he said. “Men, women, every grown motherfucker standing here with a cap and a dream. You look in the mirror tomorrow, say it properly. Don't mumble greatness in your own house.”

Mercedes called from beside her camera. “Say it again!”

Aiden raised his free hand.

“Damn!” he shouted.

“Boy!” the crowd answered.

Aiden spread both arms. “Magnificent!”

The graduates shouted the word back. Pigeons lifted from the roof of Aiden Hall, circling above the fountain before moving toward the other buildings.

Mercer put the diploma into Aiden's hand. His eyes were still wet. Aiden shook his hand and held the diploma high enough for Mercedes to photograph it.

***

Knox stepped beside him before the next name could be called. Two officers brought a blue velvet box. Another carried a dark uniform jacket with gold braid at the shoulders. The graduates near the front stopped talking; the people behind them quieted as the officers reached the lectern.

“Aiden,” Knox said, “you've been making this offer difficult to postpone.”

He lowered his diploma. “Chief, I been busy graduating.”

“You've also saved my officers' lives, protected this city's people, and demonstrated a relationship with automobiles I have never seen in any man who still has all his limbs,” Knox told him.

Porsche began applauding. Others joined her while Knox opened the box.

A gold captain's badge lay against the blue lining. Knox lifted it and turned toward the microphone.

“Captain Aiden,” Knox announced. “Las Vegas Police Department. Actual command. Actual badge. Actual trouble for anyone who thinks otherwise.”

Tank bent forward with both hands on his knees.

“Nigga,” Tank said.

Rosa caught his arm. “You all right?”

Tank looked toward the badge. “This nigga's a cop.”

“I heard,” Rosa told him.

Tank stayed bent over. “My nigga's the police.”

Rosa pulled at his sleeve. “Stand up before he arrests your posture.”

Knox held the badge in front of Aiden.

“Raise your right hand,” she told him.

He passed the diploma to Shawna, who had come to the side of the lectern, and raised his hand.

Knox gave him the oath a phrase at a time. Aiden repeated each promise into the microphone: to keep faith with his people, to stand when someone needed him to stand, to use the authority of the badge with his whole name behind it.

When he finished, Knox pinned the badge inside his jacket. He looked down at it while she straightened the cloth around the fastening.

“Captain,” Knox said.

Shawna stepped in front of him, holding the diploma against her side. She took his face in her free hand and kissed him. Officers began applauding before the graduates joined them.

“My man,” Shawna said softly.

Aiden touched the edge of the badge. “Your captain.”

She looked at his face. “Still my man.”

An officer passed the uniform jacket to Tank, who spread its gold-trimmed shoulders carefully over his arm. Knox held out the Lamborghini's keys. One door bore a long scratch from the auditorium, and a black mark spread across the blue hood where the curtain had burned. The lights continued flashing above the windshield.

“It's yours,” Knox told Aiden.

He took the keys. “I noticed.”

She leaned closer and lowered her voice.

“Red's people used to believe they owned every uniform that mattered in this city,” Knox said. “Let him see yours.”

Aiden turned toward her. Knox held his gaze.

“I know exactly who I recruited, Captain,” she told him.

They left the college with the diploma on Shawna's lap and the badge inside Aiden's jacket. Tank followed in the original Mercedes. The others came behind him in a line of cars, horns sounding as they approached the gates.

At the fountain, Marcus raised his cap.

“That's our nigga!” Marcus shouted.

His friend waved the program toward the Lamborghini. “That's our captain!”

Aiden sounded the siren once. Shawna put a hand on his leg as the college gates passed behind them.

“What you doing tomorrow?” she asked.

Aiden looked at her, then back at the road. The Lamborghini accelerated smoothly beneath them.

“Baby,” he said, “niggas better not schedule anything they can't afford.”
