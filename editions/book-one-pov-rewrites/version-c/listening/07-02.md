Two weeks after the lounge performance, Red came to Marcellus's workshop with T and a bottle of wine. He put the wine on the table and named a price for Blue Mercy.

“It isn't for sale,” Marcellus said.

Red gave another figure. T leaned against the doorframe, looking from the lamps to the tools arranged along the wall.

“It belongs to my wife,” Marcellus told Red.

“Then ask your wife,” Red said.

Marcellus stayed behind his bench. “She isn't for sale either.”

T laughed. Red looked toward him, then back at Marcellus. When they left, the bottle remained on the table.

Celeste came into the workshop afterward. She read the label and carried the bottle to the kitchen sink. Marcellus followed and caught her wrist as she tilted it.

“Do you know what that's worth?” Marcellus asked.

“Yes,” Celeste said.

She pulled her wrist free and poured the wine away. The red stream ran against the steel, circled the drain, and disappeared.

“That's why I don't want to find out what we owe him if we drink it,” Celeste told him.

Bills continued arriving. Celeste set them beside his plate at supper. Marcellus laid a letter from Valentino on top: a private showing, a prospective client, commissions for jewelry using the same blue setting. There were other names beneath the invitation, men asking for valuations of stones they already owned.

“It would give me enough work to stop worrying,” Marcellus told her.

Celeste looked at the letter. “Will you ever have enough work to stop?”

Before the showing, he made a second necklace. He placed the copy beside the original beneath a warm lamp. Both caught blue light at the same angle. He turned the lamp aside, then brought it back.

“A lesser stone,” Shawna explained to Aiden. “Dante said Marcellus could leave that in the display while Celeste wore the real one.”

Shawna got up, took a gala invitation from the piano, and returned to the sofa. She looked at the printed necklace before laying the invitation on the table beside her.

“He said the blue mounting came off,” Shawna continued. “Marcellus marked it. But the real diamond had something inside it. A little irregularity. He told Dante you couldn't copy that.”

Aiden nodded toward her. “Keep going.”

At the private showing, both necklaces were taken. So were the loose jewels in the back room and the book containing Marcellus's customers' names.

T held Marcellus against his own workbench. The lamp swung when his shoulder struck it. Valentino stood behind Celeste and opened the clasp at her neck. He lifted the necklace clear without pulling a strand of her hair. She kept both hands flat against her dress.

Red stood in the doorway.

“Now,” Red said, “perhaps we can agree on a price.”

Marcellus lunged toward him. T struck him across the face and drove him back into the bench. The lamp fell. Its bulb broke against the floor, leaving one side of the room lit only by the doorway.

Celeste dropped beside her husband.

“Take it,” she told Red. “Go.”

Red looked down at her. “You should've said that in the beginning.”

He left an envelope on the bench. When Celeste opened it, a small stack of bills lay inside. She showed Marcellus. He looked from the money to the open display case and did not take it.

She brought a towel and held it against his face. Blood darkened the cloth beneath her fingers.

“Look at me,” Celeste said. “It's gone. Look at me.”

Marcellus's eyes remained on her bare throat. She moved her face into their line and said his name again.

Shawna stopped speaking. Aiden got up from the sofa with one hand against its arm. He went to the window and stood beside the piano, the cross lifted between his fingers.

“He went after it,” Aiden said.

Shawna nodded. “In the same suit.”

Aiden looked back at her. “He told Dante that?”

“He said Celeste tried to stop him,” Shawna answered.

“With words?” Aiden asked.

“With his car keys,” Shawna told him.

Aiden nodded once. “Smart woman.”

***

Marcellus left in a taxi. He sat in the back with a gun inside his coat and Red's envelope on his knee. Celeste stood in the doorway holding his car keys. She did not wave. The taxi turned at the end of the street, and the house disappeared behind it.

At Valentino's club, light shone upward through the stair treads beyond a pair of gold doors. A singer stopped halfway through a line when Marcellus entered. He passed the stage and went to the private booth where Valentino sat with two men.

Marcellus put the envelope on the table.

“Your money,” he told Valentino.

Valentino looked inside, smiled, and set it beside his drink.

“You came to buy something?” Valentino asked.

“I came for what's mine,” Marcellus said.

The man on Valentino's right stood. Marcellus struck him with the pistol before he had cleared the table. The man fell forward into a champagne bottle. It rolled beneath him and broke against the table's edge.

The other man reached under his jacket. Marcellus fired. The man struck the booth's padded back and slid down with both hands pressed against his shoulder.

“That man lived,” Shawna told Aiden. “Dante was clear about that. Marcellus kept saying it.”

By the time Marcellus turned toward Valentino's place, the seat was empty. A door beside the bar swung inward. Marcellus went through it, crossed a narrow corridor, and pushed into a brightly lit room.

Jewelry covered the tables. Rings and watches lay beside little boxes carrying makers' names. His tools stood in a row near the wall.

He picked one up and turned it over. His initials remained engraved near the handle. He put it down and opened the nearest box. Then the next. No blue necklace lay inside either one.

A door opened behind him. Two men entered from the corridor; another came through a door at the far end of the room. Marcellus raised his gun.

“Where is it?” he asked.

The nearest man struck him behind the ear. Marcellus hit the table with his shoulder. Rings scattered across the tiles. One man caught his gun wrist while another hit him in the stomach. His knees bent. They took the pistol as he fell, and his cheek struck the floor among the loose jewelry.

When the police arrived, Valentino stood in the doorway talking about a dangerous man and an attack on his guests. Marcellus tried to rise at the sound of his voice. The men holding him pushed him down until an officer came forward with handcuffs.

They took him out past the singer, the gold doors, and the waiting taxi. Its driver stood at the curb with the meter lifted toward the window. He pointed at the fare as the officers passed him with Marcellus between them.

Celeste came to the police station wearing blue slippers. One heel had folded under her foot. Marcellus looked down at them when she reached the other side of the barrier, then lifted his face.

“I didn't find it,” he told her.

Celeste looked at the blood along his jaw.

“I found you,” she said.

He put one hand against the barrier and said nothing.

At the lake house, Aiden remained by the window. Shawna rose and came beside him.

“He told Dante he'd spent the years since trying to get back to that moment with something better to say,” Shawna said.

Aiden lowered the cross.

“Nigga, Red got to die,” he said.

She squeezed his hand. “Keep listening.”

“I'm listening,” Aiden told her. “That's what the listening is doing.”

***

Marcellus went to prison. In the visiting room, he found a magazine announcing Red's new hotel, the Saint. The article described a blue diamond bought from a respected jeweler and the money raised against it. He folded the magazine to that page and brought it to the glass when Celeste visited.

She wore a bright dress and had brought candy. He pointed to the article. She moved the candy nearer his side of the counter and asked whether he had eaten.

At first she told him stories from the lounge. A pianist had arrived wearing two different shoes. A couple had argued through a whole song, then both requested it again. When Marcellus interrupted to ask about men who had admired the necklace, she tapped the candy bag with one finger.

Later, Celeste came less often. On one visit, Marcellus gripped the receiver close to his mouth and leaned toward the glass.

“You missed the last two,” he said.

“I've been sick, Marcellus,” Celeste told him.

He stopped leaning forward. “What doctor?”

She named one. He asked another question, then began talking about money. She put her hand against the glass until he stopped speaking.

During her last visit, she wore the earrings he had made before they knew Red. She sat carefully and kept her coat around her shoulders.

“I want you to do something,” Celeste told him.

“What?” Marcellus asked.

“Remember the first thing you ever gave me,” she said.

He looked at the earrings. “Those.”

Celeste shook her head. “No.”

Marcellus lowered his eyes to her wrist. “The little bracelet.”

“No, Marcellus,” Celeste said.

He looked back at her face. His hand tightened around the receiver.

“A ride,” Celeste told him. “I was standing in the rain. You drove me home. You didn't ask for my number until we got there.”

Marcellus nodded. “I remember.”

“Then remember that man too,” she said.

She put her hand against the glass. He placed his opposite it, their palms separated by the pane.

Shawna stopped the account there for a moment. Outside the lake house, water struck softly against the dock. She and Aiden stood side by side at the window, looking toward it.

“After she died, a letter came with her things,” Shawna said. “Marcellus kept it. Carried it till the folds were splitting. One night he read it to Dante.”

Aiden turned toward her. She rubbed her thumb along the back of his hand.

“Dante wrote down what he remembered when he got out,” she continued. “He read that to me on our wedding night. I can't give you every word the way it was on her page.”

“Give me what you got,” Aiden said.

Shawna took a breath.

“She knew about the stone,” she said. “All the hours he spent. And she had found the words he hid underneath the blue mounting. He'd put something there that he used to say when he came to her door.”

Aiden looked at the cross again. “What words?”

“I came for you,” Shawna told him.

He let the cross fall against his shirt.

“She said she found them after a missed supper,” Shawna continued. “He'd fallen asleep with his shoes on. She took the necklace off and almost dropped it. Then there were the words. She wore them against her skin after that.”

Shawna's voice grew quieter. Aiden stayed facing her.

“She wanted him to know she hadn't stopped visiting because she'd stopped loving him,” Shawna said. “She was sick. She wanted what strength she had left for things that didn't mean going through another locked door.”

She turned toward the water, then back to him.

“This part I remember,” Shawna said. “If you get your beautiful stone back, put it down before you touch somebody you love.”

Aiden's fingers opened beside hers.

“She told him to open the curtains when he got home,” Shawna continued. “Pay the piano man. There was chicken in the freezer because she never learned to cook for one.”

Her mouth moved into a small smile and closed again.

“And she would take the ride again,” Shawna said. “That's how she ended it.”

Aiden turned back toward the window. He wiped the corner of one eye with the heel of his hand and kept looking at the lake while Shawna stood beside him.

“What did Dante do?” Aiden asked after a while.

“Told Marcellus he'd get it back,” Shawna said.

Aiden looked at her. “For a man he met in prison?”

“For him,” Shawna answered. “For her. For whatever he thought that would make him.”

“And you?” Aiden asked.

Shawna looked down at her watch. “I thought I married the kind of man who'd do that.”

Aiden nodded once. She led him back to the sofa, and he lowered himself onto it without letting go of her hand.

“Dante took the three million first,” Shawna said. “He wanted a lawyer for Marcellus. And a way out for us when Red found out what else he wanted. He thought T would choose him. T was his brother.”

“But he didn't,” Aiden said.

Shawna shook her head. “No.”

She lifted Dante's watch. Its weight drew the bracelet toward her hand.

“The last night Dante came home, he asked if this still looked too big on me,” Shawna said. “I told him everything he owned was too big on me. He said he'd put something in it that fit.”

Aiden leaned closer. “What something?”

Shawna turned the watch over. “I thought he meant himself.”

She worked at the clasp with both thumbs. Aiden held his hand beneath hers as the bracelet opened between them.
