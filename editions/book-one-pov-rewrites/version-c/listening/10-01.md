## 10. Forty Million and One Woman

The morning after graduation, Aiden came out of a coffee shop with two cups in his hands. A van had parked across the Lamborghini's bumper while he was inside. He stopped on the pavement and looked at the narrow space between the vehicles.

Shawna sat in the passenger seat wearing sunglasses and his captain's jacket over her dress. She turned one wrist inside the sleeve, running her fingers along the gold braid.

Across the street, four men came out of a casino carrying bags. An alarm rang behind them. One kept his gun pointed backward toward the doors. Another wiped a bleeding lip with his sleeve while the man with the van keys hurried ahead of them.

Aiden looked at his coffee, the van, and Shawna.

“Baby, hold these,” he told her.

She took both cups through the open window.

“You getting involved?” Shawna asked.

Aiden pointed at the bumper. “Nigga involved me when he parked.”

The driver reached the van and opened its front door. He saw Aiden approaching and laughed.

“Keep walking, pretty boy,” the driver said.

Aiden opened the front of his uniform shirt enough to show the captain's badge clipped inside it. The driver's laugh stopped.

“Police?” the man asked.

“Captain,” Aiden told him.

The driver looked him over. “You don't look like police.”

“Nigga, police improving,” Aiden said. “Move the van.”

The robber with the bleeding lip came up behind the driver and raised his pistol. Aiden caught the open door and drove its edge into the man's arm. The gun fired into the street. Aiden struck him against the side panel, and the man dropped to his knees as his pistol fell beneath the van.

The driver scrambled into his seat. One of the other robbers dragged the injured gunman through the sliding side door and shoved him into the front passenger seat. The fourth man climbed into the rear with the bags. His companion joined him as the van began moving.

Its rear doors remained open. A bag slid toward the step. The man inside caught one handle and shouted at the driver while Aiden ran after the van and took hold of the rear door handle.

“Aiden!” Shawna called.

“Coffee!” he shouted back.

The van pulled him past the corner. His shoes hit the curb, slipped, and found the rear step. He drew himself upward while the man holding the bag tried to kick his hands off the door.

“Nigga, let go!” the robber shouted.

Aiden caught the man's shoe. “You let go!”

“It's my van!” the man shouted.

“Then your nigga should've moved it!” Aiden told him.

He came through the doors and drove the man backward into the pile of bags. The other rear passenger caught a seat bracket to keep himself upright. Up front, the driver turned to look. The van went halfway onto the sidewalk and struck a newspaper stand, breaking its frame against the windshield.

Papers flew over the hood and into the open doors. A page wrapped around Aiden's shoulder before he pulled it free. The man beneath him reached for a gun.

Aiden put his foot on the man's wrist as the pistol cleared his waistband. The gun dropped to the floor beside the money bags.

“Don't,” Aiden said.

The robber looked up at his face. “You're Aiden.”

Aiden held the wrist down. “Nigga, you would've had a easier day remembering that at the coffee shop.” He pushed the gun beneath the front seat with his shoe before taking his weight off the man's arm.

The driver turned into a parking structure. Concrete closed over the van. Its tires screamed on the first ramp, and Aiden caught the back of the front seat with one hand while the three passengers braced themselves against the floor and dashboard.

Behind them, a siren came through the entrance.

Shawna had set both coffees in the Lamborghini's holders, climbed into the driver's seat, and draped the captain's jacket over the empty seat beside her. The dark-blue car entered the garage with its lights flashing. She followed the van up the first ramp without spilling either cup.

The van reached the second floor and swung around the outer lane toward the next ramp. The driver glanced into his mirror.

“She's still behind us!” he shouted.

Aiden held the seatback. “She's thorough.”

“Nigga, who the fuck asked you?” the driver said.

Aiden leaned away from a swinging bag. “You brought me.”

At the end of the second floor, Shawna took the inner ramp while the van went around the longer outer loop. She emerged on the third floor ahead of it, turned the Lamborghini across the van's route, and stopped.

The van driver braked. Its rear end slid toward a pillar. Aiden caught the front seat with both hands, and the two men behind him hit the bags of money. The injured man in the passenger seat struck the dashboard with his good arm and held himself there.

Shawna got out with her silver pistol in her hand.

“Everybody out,” she ordered.

The driver stared through the cracked windshield.

“She's police too?” he asked Aiden.

Aiden leaned beside his seat. “Nigga, she's worse. She's disappointed.”

The driver raised his hands. Aiden opened the sliding door and made the three passengers get out before following them onto the concrete. Shawna kept her pistol level while he gathered them beside the van, clear of the Lamborghini's hood.

Ten minutes later, Knox's officers found all four men on the ground. The bags of stolen money remained in the van. Shawna took the cups from the Lamborghini's holders and set them on its roof while the officers searched the van. A sergeant with a gray mustache shook Aiden's hand until Aiden lifted his other hand toward the waiting coffee.

“Captain, we have been after that crew for months,” the sergeant said.

“Niggas parked wrong,” Aiden told him.

The sergeant looked at the van, then back at Aiden. “We should've tried that.”

Shawna put away her pistol and handed Aiden his cup. He took a drink. A faint curl of steam rose between them.

The sergeant saluted her too.

“Ma'am,” he said.

“You can call me Shawna,” she told him.

He lowered his hand. “Yes, ma'am.”

Before lunch, the casino owner came to the station with two gold watches and an envelope. Two employees accompanied him, one with a dressing at his hairline and the other holding an ice pack against his jaw.

“They knocked both these men down,” the owner told Aiden. “Could've been worse.”

Aiden took the watches, opened their boxes, and put one into each employee's hands. The man with the ice pack lowered it and looked at the gold face. His colleague closed the box, opened it again, and turned it toward him.

The envelope held cash. Aiden gave it to an officer near the desk and sent him to buy lunch for the shift. By the time the food arrived, officers were taking turns standing beside the Lamborghini while someone photographed them.

Knox watched from her office doorway. Aiden stood near the car with Shawna's empty cup in his hand.

“How's your first morning?” Knox asked.

Aiden showed her the cup. “Need a bigger cup holder.”

She laughed. “Anything else?”

“Nigga wants a raise,” Aiden told her.

Knox looked at the clock above the desk. “You've been here four hours.”

He waved the cup toward the arrested men being taken down the corridor.

“And look what the four hours did for you,” Aiden said.

By the next afternoon, officers were volunteering to wait outside the Saint for his evening at Red's gala. Aiden stood near the station entrance while two of them described where they could park.

“Stay close to Knox,” Aiden told them. He touched the badge inside his jacket. “She knows who's coming.”

Then he went to buy the suit.

***

The tailor brought black, then dark blue. Aiden stood on the fitting platform while Shawna sat behind him in a velvet chair, her phone in one hand and a champagne glass resting against the other knee.

“Handsome in both,” Shawna said without looking up.

Aiden looked at her reflection, then at the tailor.

“Nigga, white,” he said. “I want the whole room seeing who came.”

The tailor glanced toward the bandage beneath Aiden's open shirt.

“Your injury—” the tailor began.

“Nigga, is the suit going to heal me?” Aiden asked.

The tailor put the dark jacket away and brought white fabric. Beside the door, Tank reached toward a dish of peppermints. The black suit they had bought forty minutes earlier tore across one shoulder as his arm extended.

Aiden turned his head at the sound. Tank stopped with a wrapped mint between two fingers.

“Get the nigga another one too,” Aiden told the tailor.

The tailor looked at the split seam. “A larger size?”

“Get that nigga whatever size a cathedral is,” Aiden said.

Tank returned the mint to the dish.

“Nigga, eat,” Aiden told him. “We rich enough for the candy and whatever seam your ass takes with it.”

Shawna looked up and laughed. Aiden smiled toward the mirror while the tailor knelt beside his trouser leg and went on pinning.

On each of the two evenings since graduation, Shawna had called Red as part of the plan. Aiden had sat beside her for the calls, watching her hold the phone on speaker. On the first evening, she had called Red baby before ending the conversation.

“You don't have to say it to that nigga like that,” Aiden had told her afterward.

Shawna put the phone down. “Like what?”

“With your mouth,” Aiden said.

She stared at him. “Aiden.”

He pointed toward the phone. “Nigga got a name. Use the name.”

Shawna leaned over and kissed him. He took her face between his hands and drew her closer. The next evening, when she ended another call and saw him turn toward her, she set the phone aside before he spoke.

“Come here,” Shawna told him. “You can do that without starting the fight first.”

At the tailor's, another message appeared on her phone. Aiden's eyes went to it in the mirror.

“That nigga again?” he asked.

“Rosa,” Shawna said.

Aiden held out a hand. “Let me see.”

She turned the screen toward him. Rosa had sent a photograph of six gowns spread across a bed. Beneath it, she had written: *Your nigga paying for all this?*

“Tell her your nigga said yes,” Aiden told Shawna.

“You don't know how much it is,” Shawna said.

Aiden looked at the screen again. “Tell her yes in capital fucking letters.”

Shawna put the phone away. She rose, set down her champagne, and came around the kneeling tailor to straighten Aiden's collar.

“You know you're allowed to be scared tonight,” she told him.

Aiden looked at her face in the mirror. “I'm not.”

“Okay,” Shawna said.

He turned toward her. “You are?”

“Yes,” she answered.

Aiden caught her wrist lightly before she stepped away. Dante's watch slid against his fingers. He left his hand beneath it.

“Then your nigga scared too,” Aiden told her.

She kissed the uncut corner of his mouth.

“Good,” Shawna said. “Maybe you'll listen.”
