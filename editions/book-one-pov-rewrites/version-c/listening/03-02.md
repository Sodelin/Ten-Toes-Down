Rosa came that evening with three bags and a red velvet coat folded over one arm. Tank rose halfway out of his chair when she reached him. She went past before he could finish standing.

“Where the fuck is she?” Rosa called through the door.

Shawna turned from the window. “Rosa.”

They met halfway across the room. Rosa dropped the bags and caught Shawna in both arms. Copper hair fell over the broken-heart tattoo beneath Rosa's ear as she pressed her face against her friend's.

“You don't answer your fucking phone?” Rosa demanded.

“I broke it,” Shawna said against her shoulder.

Rosa held her tighter. “You borrow a phone, bitch. You send a pigeon. You don't let me hear you're dead from some nigga in a rhinestone hat.”

“I'm here,” Shawna told her.

“I see you,” Rosa said. “I'm mad anyway.”

She held Shawna at arm's length and turned her face toward the light. Her thumb touched the little scar above Shawna's eyebrow without pressing it. Then she looked toward the bed.

“This him?” Rosa asked.

Shawna nodded. “Aiden.”

Rosa came closer. “You shoot that nigga T?”

Aiden pushed himself a little higher against the pillow. “Yeah.”

“Good,” Rosa said.

She leaned over the bed and kissed his forehead, leaving another mark beside Shawna's. Aiden started to lift a hand toward it. Rosa caught the hand and put it back on the sheet.

“Stay alive,” Rosa told him. “She doesn't have another black dress in her.”

Shawna's face folded. Rosa turned and took her back into the crook of her arm. Aiden held out his hand, then left it open on the bed until Shawna came close enough to take it.

Later, Rosa unpacked clothes onto the visitor's chair and set covered food on the table. Steam rose when she lifted a lid. Tank leaned through the doorway. Rosa handed him a container without looking up, and he withdrew with it held level in both hands.

“Still singing weekends?” Shawna asked her.

“At the Saint,” Rosa said. “Till they tell me I can't. Then some nigga'll pay for a room and tell me his wife don't understand him.”

Shawna shook her head. Rosa set another container beside the first.

“Thirty-four,” Rosa said. “I charge extra when the problem got grandchildren.”

Aiden laughed once and held his side. Shawna turned toward him; he raised a hand before she could get up.

From the last bag, Rosa took a worn songbook. Its cover had bent around the corners, and loose pages stuck out at one side. She laid it in Shawna's lap.

Shawna opened the middle of the book. Her fingers stopped on a page. She closed it so quickly that the paper snapped beneath her palm.

“Later,” Shawna said.

“Whenever,” Rosa told her. “Kept it for you. It ain't going anywhere.”

Shawna ran her hand down the cover and set it on the windowsill.

That night, Rosa painted Shawna's nails beside the window. She held each finger toward the lamp before releasing it. Tank ate in the hallway, his chair turned far enough that he could see both approaches to the door.

A man came upstairs with another bag. Aiden heard the voices outside and called through the open doorway.

“Leave the money downstairs,” he told Tank. “I got enough furniture in here.”

Tank sent the man back. Rosa screwed the cap onto the polish and fanned Shawna's fingers with a folded magazine.

Long after the room lights had been lowered, Shawna lay against Aiden with her eyes closed. He rested one hand on her hair. The cross hung between them, its chain crossing the edge of his gown.

“Dante,” Shawna whispered in her sleep.

Aiden's hand stopped. He looked down at her face. Her breathing continued against his chest. He did not move her. When the first gray light reached the window, his eyes were still open above her head.

On the third afternoon, two men rolled a black coffin through the hospital's private entrance. Silver handles ran along both sides. Aiden's name had been engraved across the lacquered lid in large letters.

The nurse at the downstairs desk put down her pen. She watched the men bring the coffin toward the shallow fountain in the center of the atrium. Then she picked up the telephone and called Aiden's room.

“Mr. Aiden, there is an item here for you,” the nurse said.

Upstairs, Aiden held the receiver against his ear. “Flowers?”

“It is larger than flowers,” the nurse told him.

Aiden looked at the fruit basket Mercedes had brought. “Piano?”

The nurse kept her eyes on the men. “It is a coffin.”

Tank came out of his chair. His plate struck the floor, spilling food beneath the table.

“Don't open it,” Aiden told the nurse.

“I wasn't planning to try it on,” she said.

Shawna took the receiver from him and moved toward the window.

“Get people away from the entrance,” she told the nurse. “Please. Anybody sitting near those men, get them into the back.”

Aiden reached for his trousers. Tank caught the fabric before he could pull it across the bandage.

“Nigga, no,” Tank said.

“Get my shoes,” Aiden told him.

Tank pointed at his feet. “You got hospital socks.”

“Then get shoes worthy of the fucking socks,” Aiden said.

Mercedes stood beside the unopened champagne she and Porsche had carried upstairs. Against the wall, an enlarged photograph showed Aiden's Mercedes above the speedboat. Shawna's open mouth could be seen through its windshield; one of Aiden's hands remained on the wheel while the other braced beside it.

Mercedes looked through the window. It overlooked the atrium; beyond its glass front, the private drive remained visible in the afternoon light.

“That's the coffin they're sending you?” she said to Aiden. “Baby, even your enemies know you're expensive.”

Porsche stood beside her. Two white sedans waited across the drive. Four men had got out, two remaining beside the cars while two approached the building's side entrance.

“Four more by the cars,” Porsche told Tank. She pointed down toward the atrium. “Those two aren't delivering shit.”

Below, both men beside the coffin reached beneath their jackets. Tank caught Aiden's arm and pulled him away from the window as a shot broke the glass at the ground-floor desk.

The nurse dropped behind it with the receiver still in her hand. The line filled with a scream, then the crash of the entrance doors. An alarm began. The deliveryman nearest the fountain shoved the stand sideways. One of its small wheels hit the basin's rim, and the coffin slid off into the water. His companion shouted for everyone to get down.

Aiden took his pistol from the bedside cabinet. Shawna put the phone down and faced him.

“Shawna, with Rosa,” Aiden said. “Bathroom. Lock it.”

She reached for his wrist. “I'm not—”

“Nigga, be alive when I come back so you can cuss me out,” Aiden told her.

Shawna's hand stayed against his wrist for another second. Then she took Rosa's hand. Mercedes lifted the heavy glass vase off the table, leaving the flowers in a wet heap, and followed them into the bathroom. The lock clicked behind her.

Porsche had gone to the hall door. She pulled in the wheelchair parked beside it and pressed down its footrests.

“Wheelchair,” Porsche said.

Tank looked from the chair to Aiden.

“The man can't walk fast,” Porsche told him. “He can roll.”

Tank pulled the trousers over Aiden's feet and helped him stand long enough to fasten them below the dressing. Aiden took the shirt Porsche held out, worked his arms through it, and left it open. Tank got his shoes on before lowering him into the chair. Porsche took the handles. The first turn through the doorway pulled at his side, and he gripped the armrest until the chair ran straight.

Outside, the corridor led toward an upper gallery overlooking the entrance atrium. A stairwell opened at the far end, beyond the bend. Off the gallery itself stood a recessed service lift and a separate broad stair leading down to the lobby. Through the glass above the gallery doors, light flashed as another shot broke something below.

A nurse was trying to turn an older man's bed out of a room. One of its wheels had caught the doorframe. The patient held his gown shut with both hands; a cast enclosed one leg from below the knee to the foot.

“Go with them,” Aiden told Porsche.

She held the wheelchair still. “Who pushes you?”

Aiden laid the pistol across his lap and set his hands on the chair's rims. “Nigga, I got hands.”

Porsche caught the corner of the bed and pulled it free. Tank moved ahead to the bend in the corridor. Aiden followed with short pulls on the wheelchair rims, stopping between each one to breathe.

The far stairwell door opened. One of the men from the white sedans came through with a pistol. He stopped at the sight of Tank, then looked past him to the wheelchair. His mouth spread into a grin.

“That's the king?” the gunman asked.

Tank raised his own weapon. “That's the nigga.”

The man fired. Tank struck the wall beside Aiden as a bullet broke the corner above his shoulder. Aiden took the pistol from his lap, leaned away from the chair's back, and fired. The gunman's shoulder jerked; he fell backward through the stairwell door, dropping his pistol onto the polished floor.

Another man appeared behind him and pulled him out of the doorway. Tank fired into the upper doorframe. Both men dropped from view.

Porsche and the nurse reached the bend with the bed. The patient pressed his gown more tightly against himself as another bullet hit a pipe fitting above the corridor. Water burst down from the damaged sprinkler head and spread beneath the wheels.

“Don't look down,” Aiden told the patient.

The older man kept his eyes on the ceiling. “Wasn't planning to, son.”

“Good,” Aiden said. “Your nigga's got this.”

Tank caught the wheelchair's handles and ran. Porsche and the nurse pushed the bed after them. Water struck Aiden's hair and shirt. Each seam in the floor jarred his body against the chair.

Behind them, the two men came through the stairwell door again. The first held his injured shoulder with one hand. The second fired down the corridor, his shoes sliding on the wet floor when he tried to stop at the bend.

Aiden turned far enough to see them. “Tank!”

“I see them!” Tank shouted.

“Then quit sightseeing and push!” Aiden told him.

The wheelchair struck the gallery doors and drove them apart. Tank swung it behind the wall beside the opening. Aiden fired back through the gap. The uninjured man dragged his wounded companion behind the stairwell door as plaster fell from the corridor ceiling.

Tank braced against the doorframe and kept his pistol trained on the far end. From the stairwell below the men came a shout.

“Police! Drop it!” an officer called.

One shot followed, then a long scraping sound against the stairwell door. The two attackers did not reappear in the corridor. Through the narrow opening came another order, answered by a man's voice.

“It's down!” the man shouted. “Gun's down!”

Porsche and the nurse rolled the bed onto the gallery. She shut the corridor doors behind them and took a chair from beside the lift, holding it by its back while she stood at the seam.
