Downstairs, the valet stood beside the Aston Martin holding a towel. The vase had punched through the roof, leaving crystal and roses in the torn opening. He reached toward the damage, lowered the towel, and stepped back as Aiden came through the doors.

“Mr. Red is going to be upset,” the valet said.

“Nigga, he got a head start upstairs,” Aiden told him. “Where's mine?”

Tank went to the Mercedes. Its windows had been replaced, the back repaired and polished. Light ran along the straightened panels as he opened the passenger door for Aiden.

A man came from the next hotel's entrance with a helmet under one arm. He stopped to look at the Aston Martin, then turned toward Aiden. A scar ran down his chin. When he moved, a silver eagle spread across the back of his leather jacket.

“You Aiden?” the man asked.

“Depends who's hoping,” Aiden said.

The man grinned. “Nigga, you flew over a boat.”

“Word getting around,” Aiden told him.

The man pointed across the drive toward a black motorcycle.

“Name's Saint,” he said. “Everybody calls me Little Saint. I ride.”

“Good for you,” Aiden said.

Little Saint came a step closer. “I mean I ride. Thought maybe you should know that.”

Tank held the car door wider. “Everybody got something they want this nigga to know today.”

Little Saint's grin disappeared. He looked past Aiden toward the exit.

“Then know those two cars ain't valets,” Little Saint said.

A gray sports car had stopped across the drive. Behind it, a black SUV rolled forward with its windows descending. Its front passenger held a pistol against his chest. As the window cleared, he moved the weapon toward the opening.

“Inside,” Tank told Aiden.

The first bullet struck the Mercedes's door while Tank's hand was still on it. Aiden caught the valet by the collar and threw him behind the car. Glass burst from the hotel's revolving door. A woman dropped behind a stack of luggage, pulling her companion down beside her. Other guests crouched behind the planters.

Little Saint ran toward his motorcycle. He pulled on his helmet as he reached it and climbed aboard. Tank fired from beside Aiden's shoulder. Aiden got into the passenger seat while Tank ran around the hood.

The Mercedes lurched backward before Tank had pulled his door shut. He turned sharply, put the car into forward motion, and struck the gray car across its front corner. The gray car moved sideways. Its driver shouted through the window and tried to reverse into the opening.

Tank hit it again. The gap widened, and the Mercedes came through with metal scraping along Aiden's door.

“Nigga, somebody really hates breakfast,” Aiden said.

“Put your head down,” Tank told him.

Aiden looked into the mirror. “I need it where I can see.”

Tank turned onto the street. “Then see lower.”

The gray car followed. Its loosened front fender struck the wheel at each turn. The SUV remained behind it, its passenger firing through the open window. A shot broke the Mercedes's rear glass. Aiden bent toward the dashboard as pieces scattered over the back seat.

At the first corner, a taxi braked across their path. Tank turned around its front bumper with inches to spare and took the Mercedes into a narrow street lined with delivery trucks. The gray car made the turn behind them. The SUV hit a streetlight with its front corner and continued, dragging a piece of broken trim beneath it.

Little Saint appeared beside the Mercedes. His helmet visor was raised, and his mouth was open in a grin. He pointed down the street. A tour bus stood across the next intersection. Outside it, the driver argued with a man in a yellow vest; both turned when the approaching engines rose above their voices.

“Tank!” Aiden called.

“I see it!” Tank shouted.

Aiden braced a hand against the dashboard. “Nigga, I keep hearing that right before we hit something!”

Tank braked. The Mercedes slid. The gray car struck its rear quarter and turned it halfway across the street. Aiden's shoulder hit the door, and he caught the edge of the seat as the car stopped rotating.

The gray car now faced them through the broken rear window. Its driver lifted his head above the steering wheel. Aiden drew his pistol, turned in his seat, and fired. The windshield whitened across the driver's face. He ducked and turned blindly into the bus's open luggage compartment.

The gray car struck with its whole front end. The compartment door folded beneath the hood. The bus driver abandoned the argument and ran toward the pavement with the man in the yellow vest beside him.

Tank drove into the narrow gap along the bus's other side. The Mercedes scraped panel after panel. Behind a low window, a pink suitcase shook against a stack of bags. Metal squealed along Aiden's side of the car before they came out onto the open road beyond the bus.

Little Saint rode beside them. He raised a fist. Aiden raised his free hand back.

The SUV came around the end of the bus and caught the motorcycle's rear wheel with its bumper.

The bike snapped sideways. Little Saint flew across the hood of a parked car and rolled off onto the street. His helmet struck the pavement. The motorcycle slid ahead in a shower of sparks and stopped beneath the rear of a delivery van.

Tank swore and braked. Aiden slipped his pistol into his waistband and opened his door before the Mercedes stopped.

“Nigga, where the fuck—” Tank began.

“Get him!” Aiden shouted.

He ran across the road with one hand pressed against his side. Little Saint had raised himself onto an elbow. Blood ran from beneath the cracked edge of his helmet. He pushed one foot under himself and fell back onto his knee.

Aiden caught his jacket at the shoulder.

“Your leg?” Aiden asked.

“Works,” Little Saint said.

“Then work it with me,” Aiden told him.

He pulled Little Saint up. The SUV turned toward them. Its driver leaned close to the windshield, both hands high on the wheel. Aiden and Little Saint ran together toward the parked trucks, Little Saint's arm across Aiden's shoulders.

Tank drove the Mercedes across the SUV's path.

The collision drove both cars sideways. Aiden shoved Little Saint behind a truck, drew his pistol again, and turned toward the road. The SUV had stopped against the Mercedes, steam rising between their crushed front panels.

Its driver tried to open his door. Tank came out of the Mercedes and slammed it shut against him. Before the man could move away from the window, Tank caught his jacket, hauled his upper body through the opening, and dragged him onto the street.

Aiden kept his gun on the passenger. The man looked toward Tank, then lowered his own weapon onto the dashboard.

“Nigga, you can keep breathing,” Aiden told him. “That's all you getting from me today.”

The passenger raised both hands beside his head. Tank pulled the driver away from the SUV and put him face down beside the curb.

Little Saint came out from behind the truck. His jacket had torn across one shoulder. He took off the cracked helmet and looked from Tank to Aiden, then toward the motorcycle beneath the van.

“You came back,” Little Saint said.

Aiden kept the pistol pointed toward the SUV. “You told me you ride. I ain't seen enough yet.”

Little Saint's mouth opened into a grin again, more slowly this time. He touched the tear in his jacket, then let his hand fall.

“Any road,” Little Saint told him. “Any fucking road, Aiden. You call, I'm there.”

A crowd gathered at the corner. Someone shouted Aiden's name. A woman held up her phone and waved for him to turn toward her. He turned the uncut side of his face while keeping his body between the SUV and Little Saint.

Knox's officers arrived while steam still rose from the cars. They took the two men into custody and went back toward the bus, where another patrol had stopped beside the gray sports car. One officer looked from the SUV to Aiden.

“Chief said you were something,” the officer told him.

Aiden put his pistol away. “Chief been paying attention.”

Tank made a call. Another car arrived for Little Saint, who climbed into it with his broken helmet on his lap. The Mercedes started when Tank turned the key. Its passenger door would not latch. Tank tied it shut with a belt before returning to the driver's side.

They drove away slowly while the crowd watched. Before they reached the highway, Aiden called Shawna.

“Baby, you want lunch?” Aiden asked when she answered.

“Why are there sirens behind you?” Shawna asked.

Aiden looked back through the ruined rear window. “Niggas wanted breakfast. I'm thinking ahead.”

***

At the house, Shawna stood barefoot on a small fitting platform while a woman pinned silver fabric around her hips. Rosa sat beside a tray of sewing supplies, holding a strip of ribbon between two fingers. Shawna's watch hung above the hand keeping the unfinished dress in place.

Aiden stopped in the doorway. He looked at the dress, then at the watch. Rosa followed his face with her eyes.

“Why you got that face?” Rosa asked.

“What face?” Aiden said.

Rosa put the ribbon down. “The one that's going to make this dress cost more.”

Aiden took the blue invitations from his coat and held them up.

“Blue Mercy,” he said.

Shawna stopped moving. The dressmaker continued placing a pin until Rosa touched her shoulder.

“Let's get coffee,” Rosa told her.

The woman looked up from the hem. “I don't drink coffee.”

“Today's a good fucking day to start,” Rosa said.

She helped the woman gather the loose pins, took the tray, and led her out. Shawna stepped down from the platform. One pin fell from the dress and struck the floor between her bare feet and Aiden's shoes.

“What did he tell you?” Shawna asked.

Aiden laughed once. He laid one invitation on the piano and kept the other in his hand.

“Nigga, everybody keeps asking me that,” Aiden said. “What did she tell you? What did he tell you? What the fuck did the dead man tell the other dead man? How about you tell me something for once?”

Shawna drew back a step. Aiden's eyes followed her, but his voice continued at the same volume.

“You knew about forty million dollars?” he asked.

“I knew about the stone,” Shawna said.

Aiden shook the invitation. “And you didn't think that shit mattered?”

“I thought you being shot mattered,” Shawna told him. “I thought getting you through yesterday mattered.”

He pointed at the silver dress. “You got time to pick a fucking dress, Shawna.”

She looked down at the pinned fabric, then back at his face.

“I picked this dress because I wanted one night where you looked at me and didn't see somebody else bleeding,” Shawna said.

Aiden's hand lowered. He looked at the invitation without opening it again.

Shawna came forward and took it from him. Her thumb passed over Red's name.

“He told you about his piano?” she asked.

“Yeah, he told me about the piano,” Aiden said. “He tell everybody about the piano?”

“I smashed the keys with a bottle the night I left,” Shawna said. “Before you came upstairs. He bought another one so he could pretend I was coming back.”

Aiden looked toward the black piano beside the lake windows.

“He said you knew what Dante died for,” Aiden told her.

“I know what Dante told me,” Shawna said.

Aiden faced her again. “Then tell me.”

Shawna set the invitation beside the other one. “It starts before me.”

“Nigga, everything in this house starts before me,” Aiden said.

She looked at the cross beneath his open collar.

“You want me to take that back?” Shawna asked.

Aiden closed his hand around it. “No.”

Shawna pointed toward the sofa. “Then sit down, Aiden.”

He went to it and lowered himself onto the cushion. She picked up the fallen pin, put it on the piano, and sat beside him, keeping the unfinished hem clear of his shoes.

“Dante told me after we got married,” Shawna said. “We were in the room Red paid for. I thought we'd go downstairs and drink with everybody. He locked the door instead.”

Aiden's jaw tightened. He moved his hand from the cross to his knee.

“I'm listening,” Aiden said.

Shawna took that hand in hers.

“He said there was something I ought to know before I decided what kind of man I'd married,” she continued. “Then he told me about the old man.”
