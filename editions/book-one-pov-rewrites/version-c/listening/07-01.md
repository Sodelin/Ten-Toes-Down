## 7. The Man Who Cut the Heart Out of a Mountain

Shawna drew the unfinished silver hem clear of her feet and settled beside Aiden on the sofa. The two gala invitations lay on the piano. She kept one of his hands between hers while she began describing the room where she and Dante had spent their wedding night.

“Rose petals everywhere,” Shawna said. “On the bed. On the floor. Dante was picking them up and putting them in the ice bucket.”

Aiden looked at her hands. She opened one palm above his, turning it upward as she spoke.

In the room she described, Dante stood beside the bed with a fistful of petals. Shawna still wore her wedding dress. Below the window, the party continued without them, voices rising whenever someone opened the door to the hall.

“Are they bothering you?” she asked Dante.

He held out the petals. “They got dirt on them.”

“Dante,” Shawna said.

He dropped them into the ice bucket. “I don't want you sleeping in dirt.”

She laughed and kicked off her shoes. Dante watched each one land on the carpet before sitting beside the bucket. She came to the bed and gathered the dress around her knees.

He told her about prison. He had been twenty-eight when he went in and had served nine months before they met. On his first afternoon, he carried a tray to a table in the dining room and sat facing an older prisoner at the next one.

A man approached the older prisoner and tipped soup down the front of his shirt. The older man looked at the spreading stain, moved his bowl a little closer, and continued eating what remained in it.

“You hear me, old man?” the man with the empty bowl asked.

“I heard you,” the older prisoner said.

“Then look at me,” the other man told him.

The older prisoner put his spoon down and raised his face.

“You have my soup on your hand,” he said.

The man slapped him.

Dante rose with his tray still in his hand. A shaved-headed man with a towel over one shoulder stepped between the tables and spread his feet in Dante's path.

“Sit your ass down,” the man told him.

Dante kept walking. The man moved forward to meet him.

“Nigga, you deaf?” the man asked.

Dante stopped close enough that the tray almost touched his stomach.

“No,” Dante said. “You finished?”

The man swung. The blow caught Dante across the jaw. Dante turned with it and brought the tray back across the man's face. Food struck the wall. A cup rolled from the tray and cracked beneath somebody's shoe.

The shaved-headed man fell against the table. Other prisoners stood, pushing their seats away. Across the room, the man who had spilled the soup caught the older prisoner by his collar and pulled him halfway off the bench.

“You want the old nigga?” the man called to Dante. “Come get him.”

Dante planted both hands on the table between them and went over it. His knees struck the man holding the collar. They fell together. The older prisoner hit the bench with his hip, caught himself with one hand, and bent to retrieve his spoon before a passing foot came down on it.

On the sofa by the lake, Aiden lifted his face.

“He picked up the spoon?” Aiden asked Shawna.

She nodded. “He liked that spoon.”

Aiden looked at the cross against his shirt. “Nigga, I respect an attachment.”

Shawna moved his hand gently away from the chain and continued. She said the soup man had two friends. The third man in the fight came behind Dante carrying a chair.

The older prisoner put a foot against that man's ankle as he passed. The man's step shortened abruptly. His chair struck the table instead of Dante's head, and Dante came off the floor into him.

Dante caught the chair with both hands. The man pulled it back, dragging him a step, then lost his grip when Dante drove the wooden back into his chest. He fell over the bench. Dante struck him once more with the chair's padded seat and turned toward the shaved-headed man getting up beside the first table.

The man stayed on one knee. Blood dripped from his chin onto the towel at his shoulder. The soup man lay near the older prisoner's feet with one hand over his mouth.

Guards came through the dining-room doors. Dante stood between the tables with food and blood on his shirt. The older prisoner remained beside the bench holding his spoon.

“Another bowl,” Dante told the nearest guard.

The guard pointed toward the door. “Move.”

Dante looked at the older man. “Nigga, the old man ain't eaten.”

“Move now,” the guard said.

The older prisoner put a hand on Dante's shoulder.

“I can wait,” he said.

Dante looked at the men on the floor. Then he picked up the empty bowl and put it into the guard's hand.

“So can they,” Dante told him.

Shawna smiled as she repeated that line. Aiden watched her mouth before looking down at their joined hands.

“Three niggas,” Aiden said.

“Three,” Shawna confirmed.

He lifted his free hand. “I had twelve.”

She picked up a sofa cushion and touched it against his shoulder, clear of the wound.

“You asked for the story,” Shawna told him.

“I'm listening,” Aiden said. “Nigga can listen and hold the record.”

She laughed, set the cushion behind his back, and waited while he shifted against it.

“Dante said he didn't notice the broken finger till afterward,” she continued. “He could tell me everything he hit except whatever hit that.”

Aiden looked at his own knuckles. Shawna took his hand again before going on.

When Dante returned from his punishment, the older prisoner sat on his bunk with a little paper box. Inside lay a broken plastic comb, several buttons, and a gold-colored wrapper folded into a heart.

“Marcellus Vale,” he told Dante, holding out the box. “For your trouble.”

Dante lifted the heart with his uninjured hand. Its small folds lay straight against one another. He turned it toward the corridor light and then back toward Marcellus.

“Nigga, what am I supposed to do with this?” Dante asked.

“Give it to somebody,” Marcellus said.

Dante held it by one corner. “A woman?”

“If you like women,” Marcellus told him.

“I like women,” Dante said.

Marcellus moved his comb back into the box. “Then perhaps one will like a heart.”

Dante put the folded wrapper into his shirt pocket.

“You make jewelry?” he asked.

“I did,” Marcellus said.

Dante grinned. “Used to steal it myself.”

Marcellus looked up from the box. “Then we may have met professionally.”

Dante laughed. Marcellus moved over on the bunk and made room for him to sit.

For the next two months they ate together. Marcellus laid cards between them and taught Dante how to play. Dante pointed out men in the dining room and told Marcellus which ones to avoid. Marcellus listened, nodded, and sometimes greeted the same men by name when they passed.

At night, Marcellus held a photograph in the light that entered from the corridor. It showed Celeste on a small stage with a microphone in her hand. Her mouth was open in song. A blue stone lay at the base of her throat.

“Your wife?” Dante asked one night.

“Yes,” Marcellus said.

Dante leaned nearer the photograph. “She beautiful.”

“I know,” Marcellus told him.

“She still come see you?” Dante asked.

Marcellus turned the photograph over.

“No,” he said.

Dante sat back. He did not ask again that night.

Later, a letter arrived with a bank's name printed on the envelope. Marcellus left it unopened beside his bowl throughout dinner. Dante finished eating and looked at the envelope while Marcellus's food cooled.

“You want me to read it?” Dante asked.

Marcellus ran a finger along the sealed edge. “I know what it says.”

“Then why they send it?” Dante asked.

“Because some people think they can finish a love affair by closing a bank account,” Marcellus said.

Dante put down his spoon. Back in the cell, he sat on the opposite bunk while Marcellus held the envelope against his knee.

“She died three months ago,” Marcellus told him.

Neither man spoke for a while. Footsteps passed beyond the door. A voice farther along the corridor asked someone to lower a radio.

Dante looked at the photograph beside Marcellus's pillow.

“Tell me about that blue stone,” Dante said.

Shawna paused on the sofa. She turned one of the silver dress pins between two fingers before setting it safely on the table.

“This is what Marcellus told him,” Shawna said to Aiden. “Dante told it to me that night.”

***

The seller carried the rough stone in an empty cigarette packet. Marcellus counted money onto the table until nothing remained in the envelope he had brought. He took the packet and put it inside his coat.

Two men stopped him on his way out. One held out a hand. The other stood across the doorway.

“Part of that belongs to us,” the first man said.

Marcellus buttoned his coat over the packet. “Put a hand in here and you'll need help using it afterward.”

The man kept his hand out. Marcellus stepped closer until the hand touched the front of his coat. The man lowered it. As Marcellus went past, the other man punched him in the jaw and caught his sleeve. Marcellus struck the doorframe with one shoulder. He put a hand flat over the packet in his coat, drove his free elbow into the man's chest, and shoved him into his companion. Both men hit the wall beside the doorway. Marcellus pulled his sleeve free and left with blood at the corner of his mouth.

He brought the stone home inside the packet. His jaw had swollen around a bruise. Celeste stood at the stove frying chicken when he entered the kitchen. She looked at his face and turned the burner down.

“How much?” Celeste asked.

Marcellus put the packet on the table. “Wait until you see it.”

She wiped her hands. “How much, Marcellus?”

He gave her the figure. She stopped with the towel between her hands.

“What's left?” Celeste asked.

Marcellus looked down at the packet. “The stone.”

She opened it. The rough blue piece rested in the lining, cloudy along one edge and dark where dirt remained against its surface. She tilted the packet toward the kitchen light.

“We are not eating that,” Celeste told him.

He took the stone out and held it toward the window. Light passed through the blue surface and laid a small irregular shape across the white sleeve of her blouse. Celeste looked down at her arm. Her hand stopped holding the packet shut.

“Oh,” she said.

Marcellus watched her face instead of his raised hand.

“Yes,” he told her.

He worked in the room behind their house. Tools and lamps crowded the bench. Celeste carried meals to its door and returned for plates on which the food had gone cold. At the end of the first month, she put his pillow beside the work stool.

At the end of the second, she placed a photograph of herself beside the lamp. Marcellus looked up when its frame touched the bench.

“So you remember who the fuck lives out here,” Celeste told him.

He reached toward her. She put his dinner in that hand and went back into the house.

At the end of the third month, Marcellus came into the lounge wearing his good suit. Celeste was halfway through a song. The pianist saw Marcellus approaching with a small box and lost his place. His left hand continued alone for two beats before stopping.

Celeste turned toward the room. Marcellus stood below the stage with the box in both hands.

“Don't you propose to me again,” Celeste said into the microphone. “I fell for that shit once.”

People laughed. Marcellus moved closer to the steps.

“I made you something,” he told her.

She came down. He opened the box. The conversations at the nearest tables stopped first, then the ones behind them. The blue stone lay inside a necklace surrounded by smaller pale stones. As Marcellus turned the box, its color deepened in the shade of his hand.

Celeste put her fingers over her mouth.

“Marcellus,” she said.

He lifted the necklace from its lining.

“Turn around,” Marcellus told her.

She turned. He fastened it behind her neck, lifting her hair clear of the clasp. The diamond settled at the base of her throat. She touched its lower edge, then let her hand fall so it lay against her skin.

“What'd you call it?” Celeste asked.

“Blue Mercy,” Marcellus said.

She looked at him. “Mercy for what?”

“The last three months,” Marcellus told her.

Celeste put both arms around him and kissed him in front of the room. Behind them, the pianist found the opening chord and began the song again.

Near the back sat Valentino and Red. Red was twenty-eight then. He held his glass close to his mouth without drinking while Valentino leaned toward him. Both men kept their faces turned toward Celeste's necklace.

On the sofa, Shawna touched the bare place at her own throat.

“Dante held my hand under the lamp,” she told Aiden. “Trying to show me the color Marcellus described. He kept moving it in and out of the light.”

Aiden looked at her hand in his. She turned it once, letting the daylight pass over Dante's watch. Aiden's thumb stopped moving. He remained quiet while she continued.
