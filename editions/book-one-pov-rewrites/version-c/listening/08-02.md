Tank stood by the lake throwing pieces of bread toward the birds. He turned when Aiden came down the path. His eyes stopped at the blood on Aiden's mouth.

“What happened?” Tank asked.

“Don't,” Aiden said.

Tank put the rest of the roll on the low wall beside him. Aiden stopped at the wall and gripped its edge with both hands, facing the water.

“She hit you?” Tank asked.

Aiden turned his face. “Nigga, I said don't.”

Tank raised one hand. “All right.”

A bird walked between them, reached up onto the low wall, and took the remaining roll. Tank watched it carry the bread toward the water with its head tilted to keep the whole piece above the ground.

“That was mine,” Tank said.

Aiden looked at the bird. “Go tell him.”

Tank stayed beside the wall. Aiden's mouth opened into the beginning of a smile, then tightened as the cut pulled. He sat down and touched it with the back of his hand.

“Red ordered it,” Aiden told Tank. “Dante. We got his own fucking handwriting.”

Tank looked toward the water.

“You know?” Aiden asked.

“No,” Tank said.

“You heard anything?” Aiden asked.

Tank's jaw worked. “I heard Dante took money.”

“And that's all?” Aiden asked.

“That was all anybody was saying around me,” Tank told him.

He faced Aiden again. The empty bread bag moved against his leg in the breeze.

“I didn't know him like that,” Tank said. “T had me moving things. Driving people. I wasn't in every room.”

Aiden looked at Tank's face for several seconds before turning toward the lake.

“I'm taking Red's diamond,” Aiden said.

Tank lowered his chin. “And then?”

“And then I'm going to look at how poor the motherfucker got,” Aiden told him.

Tank folded the bread bag. “Forty million ain't all he has.”

“Then I'll stay busy,” Aiden said.

The patio door opened. Shawna came down the path with a wet cloth in one hand and Dante's watch in the other. Tank picked up the empty bag and went toward the house, passing her without speaking.

She stopped in front of Aiden.

“I shouldn't have done that,” Shawna said.

“No shit,” Aiden told her.

She lifted the cloth a little. “Can I see?”

He turned his chin toward her. She cleaned the blood from his mouth, stopping whenever he drew back. His eyes remained on the lake beyond her shoulder.

“I love him,” Shawna said.

Aiden looked down at the watch in her other hand. “I fucking know, Shawna.”

“I love you too,” she told him.

He raised his face. “Did you tell Dante you loved him before he left that night?”

Shawna shook her head.

“I was mad about dinner,” she said.

Aiden stayed looking at her. She put the cloth on the wall and touched the uncut side of his face.

“I love you,” Shawna said again.

He took that hand and pressed it against his chest. Her other hand rested beside it, holding the watch by its open bracelet.

“Don't hit me again,” Aiden told her.

“I won't,” Shawna said.

He nodded toward the watch. “And don't take that off because you think it'll shut me up. Put it back on. I want the whole thing when you tell me something.”

She looked down at it, then fastened it around her wrist. Aiden waited while she closed the clasp. When she finished, he kept her hand against his chest for another moment before standing beside her.

***

They ate the food Tank had brought. Marcellus sat at the dining table with a clear protective sleeve beside his plate. Inside lay a letter whose folds had worn pale across the writing. Shawna's watch was closed on her wrist. The separate note ordering Dante's death remained on the jeweler's cloth near the middle of the table.

Marcellus passed the sleeve to Aiden.

“Celeste's,” Marcellus said. “What Dante heard.”

Aiden set down his fork. He looked at the handwriting, then at Marcellus, who nodded once. Aiden began reading the letter aloud.

“My love,” Aiden read. “You think I never understood that stone. I did. I understood every hour you spent getting it right. I know the little words you hid beneath the blue mounting. I know you were waiting for me to find them, and I know you were disappointed when I liked the front so much I forgot to look behind it.”

He moved the sleeve closer to the lamp and continued reading.

“I found them the night you fell asleep with your shoes on,” Aiden read. “I was angry with you for missing supper. I took the necklace off and nearly dropped it, and there you were underneath, saying what you used to say at my door. I came for you.”

Marcellus put down his fork. Aiden's eyes stayed on the letter as he read the next paragraph.

“I wore those words against me every night afterward,” Aiden read. “You never asked whether I'd found them. You just watched me wear it and smiled.”

Shawna laid her hand beside Aiden's plate. He moved his free hand to hers before continuing.

“I am so tired now, Marcellus,” Aiden read. “I can't keep driving out there. Don't let anybody tell you I stopped because I stopped loving you. I am stopping because I am sick, and I want the strength I have left for things that don't require me to pass through a locked door.”

He paused at the next fold. Marcellus reached across, flattened the sleeve beneath his fingertips, and withdrew his hand. Aiden read on.

“If you get your beautiful stone back, put it down before you touch somebody you love,” Aiden read. “I know your hands. They are warmer without all that metal in them.”

Tank sat with both hands on his knees beneath the table. Aiden read the final lines more quietly.

“And if you are still angry when you come home, be angry,” Aiden read. “You always were. Just open the curtains. Pay the piano man what I owe him. There is chicken in the freezer because I never learned how to cook for one. I would take the ride again. Celeste.”

He lowered the sleeve onto the table. For a while nobody spoke. Then he pushed it back toward Marcellus, who placed it inside his coat with both hands.

Aiden looked across at him.

“You want your stone?” Aiden asked.

“Yes,” Marcellus said.

“You want Red dead?” Aiden asked.

Marcellus looked at Shawna, then back at Aiden.

“Yes,” Marcellus said.

Aiden nodded. “Nigga, I think we can work together.”

Marcellus did not smile. Shawna laid the gala invitations beside the empty place where the letter had rested.

“He's putting it on display in three nights,” Shawna said.

Marcellus picked up an invitation. A picture of the blue necklace covered its front. He tilted it beneath the lamp, then laid it flat again.

“He's putting something on display,” Marcellus said.

“There's two,” Aiden said.

“Yes,” Marcellus answered.

Aiden leaned forward. “You tell them apart?”

“Of course,” Marcellus said.

“Then you're going,” Aiden told him.

Marcellus touched the picture. “He knows my face.”

“He knows the face he put in prison eight years ago,” Aiden said. “We'll give you another suit and somebody else's name.”

Rosa came into the dining room as he spoke. She stopped beside Marcellus and looked him over from his coat to his shoes.

“I know a woman who can make you look like a man with money,” Rosa told him.

Marcellus looked around the dining room, then toward the horse statue visible beneath the stairs.

“Apparently it's easy to confuse people,” he said.

Tank covered his mouth with his napkin. Aiden looked at the statue and raised one finger toward Marcellus, then lowered it without answering.

Marcellus turned the invitation over and used its plain back to draw a small outline with the jeweler's pencil.

“There are different settings,” Marcellus said. “The blue outer mounting can be removed. A buyer might wear the stone another way. The original has a flaw inside it. Very small. I remember exactly where it is. The copy doesn't.”

Shawna pointed at the photograph on the other invitation. “But the necklace in the picture?”

“Could hold either,” Marcellus told her.

Aiden sat back. “Then we get you close enough to look.”

Shawna moved her chair nearer the table.

“I can get him to invite us closer,” she said.

“No,” Aiden told her.

She turned toward him. “Aiden.”

“No,” he repeated.

Shawna held out her hand. “You haven't heard what I'm saying.”

Aiden looked at the chair she had moved. “I heard it in the way you sat up.”

She took his hand and held it between them on the table.

“I tell him I made a mistake,” Shawna said. “That you scare me. That I'm bringing you to the gala because I want him to handle it quietly.”

Tank lowered the napkin. “And he believes that?”

“He wants to,” Shawna said.

Aiden's fingers tightened around hers. “What else?”

“I tell him I know where your money is,” Shawna said.

“I don't have his fucking money,” Aiden told her.

“He doesn't know that,” Shawna answered.

Tank lifted his glass, held it just short of his mouth, and set it down again without drinking.

“I tell him I'm tired of running,” Shawna continued. “I'll come back if he clears the debt and leaves you alive.”

Aiden turned his face toward hers. “Nigga, why he going to leave me alive?”

“Because I'm going to say you can give him the west side,” Shawna said.

Aiden let go of her hand. The table fell quiet. Shawna leaned toward him, keeping her hand open where his had been.

“I'm saying what he'll listen to,” she told him. “You know that.”

Marcellus folded the invitation along its crease.

“If he believes she can give you to him, he'll want you close enough to take,” Marcellus said.

Aiden put both hands on the table. “Then he can fucking try.”

Rosa looked from him to Shawna.

“You get near the jewel, the old man looks at it, we leave with what's his,” Rosa told her. “You do not decide to kill somebody in the middle because you get mad.”

Shawna looked down at the invitation. Rosa waited, then pointed toward Aiden.

“You either,” Rosa said.

Aiden looked around the table. “Nigga, why everybody looking at me?”

Rosa folded her arms. “Because you have the face.”

Tank nodded. Aiden looked at him, then took Shawna's hand again.

***

Shawna made the call upstairs, sitting on the edge of the bed with Aiden beside her. She put the phone on speaker and held it between them. Outside the window, the lake had gone black. The room's light reflected their faces in the glass.

Red answered on the second ring.

“There she is,” Red said.

Shawna closed her eyes. Aiden put a hand on her knee.

“Can you talk?” she asked.

“For you?” Red said.

Aiden's fingers curled against the fabric of her dress. She opened her eyes and looked at his hand before answering.

“I think I made a mistake,” Shawna told Red.

The line remained quiet for a moment. From somewhere near Red's phone came the clink of a glass.

“He hurt you?” Red asked.

Shawna looked at the reopened cut on Aiden's mouth.

“I don't know what he's going to do next,” she said.

“Come home,” Red told her.

Shawna kept her face toward Aiden. “I can't just leave.”

“Yes, you can,” Red said.

She drew a breath. “You want the money. You want his streets. I can bring him to you, Red. But you have to promise me something.”

“Anything,” Red said.

Aiden lifted his face toward the phone. Shawna's free hand moved over his.

“You let him live,” she told Red.

Red sighed into the receiver. “You're too good to these boys.”

Aiden took his hand off Shawna's knee. She caught it before he could rise, holding it between both of their legs.

“I know where he's keeping the money,” Shawna said. “I can get him to bring what you need. At the gala, I'll stay close to you. Everybody can see I'm back. After that, you talk to him.”

“And you stay?” Red asked.

Shawna looked directly at Aiden.

“Yes,” she said.

Red breathed out audibly. Aiden's hand closed inside hers.

“Wear the blue dress,” Red told her. “The one I liked.”

“I will,” Shawna said.

“And Shawna?” Red asked.

She held the phone still. “What?”

“I knew you'd remember who loved you,” Red said.

Shawna ended the call without answering. She laid the phone facedown on the bed and took Aiden's face in both hands.

“Look at me, Aiden,” she told him.

He turned toward her.

“I'm here,” Shawna said.

He pulled her close. His face tightened as her body pressed against his side, but his arms stayed around her. She moved onto his uninjured side and kept her forehead against his.

Downstairs, Marcellus sat beneath the dining-room lamp with the pencil in his hand. He had drawn the blue mounting and a small mark inside the outline of the stone. Celeste's letter remained inside his coat. Rosa stood beside the dressmaker at a table of blue fabric, lifting one piece into the light and putting another aside.

Tank stood by the side door with his phone against his ear. When footsteps approached from the kitchen, he turned toward the dark garden and lowered his voice.

Upstairs, Aiden rested his chin against Shawna's hair.

“Three nights,” he said.

She held his hand against her shoulder. Beyond the window, a light moved slowly along the far shore and disappeared behind the trees.
