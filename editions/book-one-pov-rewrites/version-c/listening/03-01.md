## 3. A King with a Hole in Him

The first doctor who approached the car asked Shawna who she was to Aiden. Two attendants had brought a wheeled stretcher beneath the entrance canopy. One held its sheet back while the other reached into the Mercedes.

“His wife,” Shawna said.

Aiden opened his eyes. His hand moved against the towel at his side and stopped.

“Name?” the doctor asked.

“Shawna,” she said.

The doctor bent over Aiden. “His name.”

“Aiden,” Shawna answered. “His name is Aiden.”

The attendants drew him out of the seat. Shawna followed with both hands caught in his open shirt. Her bare feet left small marks on the polished floor beyond the entrance. The front of her black dress had stiffened with dried blood; one diamond earring remained beneath her hair.

“Let go of him, Shawna,” the doctor said.

Shawna's hands stayed clenched in the cloth. The stretcher moved several feet before the attendant nearest her stopped it.

“Baby,” Aiden said.

She bent over him at once. His voice had barely carried past the pillow.

“Don't let them cut my chain,” he told her.

Shawna lifted his head enough to draw the chain clear. She kissed the cross, put it around her own neck, and tucked it inside her dress. Then she released his shirt. The attendants rolled the stretcher through a pair of doors, and Aiden's eyes closed before they swung shut behind him.

***

When he opened them again, daylight crossed the bottom of a white curtain. A woman in a white coat stood beside his bed with two fingers against his wrist. She watched the second hand of her own watch, then set his hand carefully on the sheet.

“Dr. Marisol Vega,” she told him. “You're alive.”

Aiden moved his head against the pillow. “Shawna?”

“Sleeping,” Vega said.

“Where?” Aiden asked.

The doctor pointed toward the window. Shawna lay curled in a chair with Aiden's bloody shirt clutched against her. Hospital socks covered her feet. The cross had slipped out of her dress. Dante's watch hung loose above the hand beneath her cheek.

“Don't wake her,” Aiden said.

“She'll wake herself,” Vega told him. “She has been doing it every eleven minutes.”

Aiden put an elbow against the mattress and tried to rise. His face tightened. He dropped back onto the pillow with his mouth open.

“Fuck,” he said.

Vega adjusted the sheet without moving the dressing beneath it. “Yes. That. You will be doing quite a lot of that if you continue.”

He lay still until his breathing slowed.

“Where's the bullet?” Aiden asked.

“Still inside you,” Vega said. “Removing it would add risks you do not need. We treated the damage. You need time.”

Aiden stared at the ceiling. He turned his face toward the dressing, then back toward her.

“Nigga shot me and moved in,” he said. “How much time?”

“More than you want,” Vega told him.

Aiden raised two fingers off the sheet. “Niggas waiting downstairs, Doc. A nigga can't run a whole west side horizontal.”

“They can keep waiting,” Vega said.

She brought a cup to his mouth and held it while he drank. A little water ran down his chin. He took the cup from her, tried to hold it upright, and lost it when his hand shook. Plastic struck the bed rail and fell onto the floor.

Shawna came out of the chair so quickly that the bloody shirt slid beneath it.

“Aiden?” she said.

“Right here,” he told her.

She touched his face, his shoulders, the hair above his ear. Her fingers stopped whenever they reached a cut, moved away, and returned somewhere else. Her eyes stayed above the sheet.

“You scared me,” Shawna said.

“I'm sorry, baby,” Aiden told her.

She kissed his forehead. “Don't be sorry. Don't do it.”

She slapped his uninjured arm, then bent and kissed him again. Tears fell onto his cheek. He held her wrist against the pillow while she pressed her mouth to the corner of his.

Vega picked up the cup. She put a fresh one on the bedside table and went out, closing the door quietly behind her.

Aiden looked at Shawna's face. “You told them you're my wife.”

Shawna wiped beneath both eyes with the back of her free hand. “They weren't going to let me in.”

“You could've said sister,” Aiden said.

“I wanted them to let me in,” Shawna told him. “I didn't want to make it weird when I kissed you.”

He laughed and caught his side. She put her hand above his, waiting while he breathed through his teeth.

“Say it again,” Aiden said.

Shawna sat carefully on the bed's edge. “What?”

“What you told the doctor,” Aiden said.

She touched his cheek. “Nigga, you trying to die married?”

“Trying to live that way,” Aiden told her.

Her hand stopped. He kept looking at her. After a moment she moved closer and laid her head beside his on the pillow.

“Let me get you home first,” Shawna said.

***

Tank arrived that afternoon in a fresh suit. Dirt remained around the edges of his shoes, and a bruise darkened one side of his jaw. He stopped in the doorway with a bunch of flowers held against his chest.

Shawna straightened beside the bed. Aiden turned his head on the pillow. Tank lifted the flowers an inch.

“These for her,” Tank said.

Shawna did not reach for them.

“Nigga, put the flowers down before we both start feeling strange,” Aiden told him.

Tank laid them on the visitor's table. A little water leaked from their wrapping onto a hospital brochure. He moved the brochure out of the wet patch, then stood with both hands empty.

“You alone?” Aiden asked.

“Niggas downstairs waiting,” Tank said.

“Whose niggas?” Aiden asked.

Tank's mouth worked once against the bruise. “Were T's.”

“And now?” Aiden asked.

Tank looked toward Shawna before answering. “That's what they wanna know.”

She stood up. “I'm not leaving.”

“Didn't ask you to,” Tank said.

Shawna faced him across the bed. “You looked.”

Tank spread his hands. “I got eyes.”

Aiden held out his hand. Shawna took it without turning away from Tank. The monitor beside the bed continued its small, steady sounds.

“Where's T?” Aiden asked.

“Funeral people got him,” Tank said.

“His other niggas?” Aiden asked.

Tank looked down at the foot of the bed. “One alive. One ain't.”

Shawna lowered her face. Aiden pressed his fingers around hers. Neither spoke until Tank shifted his weight on the wet edge of the brochure he had dropped.

“The money?” Shawna asked.

Tank kept his eyes on the bed. “Pool was empty when I got back.”

Shawna pulled her hand free of Aiden's. “Three million doesn't walk.”

“Plenty people got legs,” Tank said.

She came around the bed far enough that Tank lifted his face.

“Look at me when you say it,” Shawna told him.

Tank met her eyes. “I looked, Shawna. Niggas move fast when nobody watching. Money wasn't there.”

Aiden raised a hand between them.

“Leave it,” he said. “Nigga, she threw it away to save me. It saved me. That's money spent.”

Tank turned toward him. “You always this rich?”

Aiden's hand settled onto the sheet. “I woke up.”

Tank looked at the monitor, then at the tube running beside Aiden's arm.

“Yeah,” Tank said. “You did.”

He took Aiden's pistol from inside his jacket and held it with the grip toward him.

“Brought this from the car,” Tank said.

Aiden pointed at the bedside cabinet. “Put it there.”

Tank laid the pistol inside and closed the drawer. Shawna watched his hand leave the handle before stepping away from the cabinet.

He took a chair but sat only on its front edge. Shawna went to the window. Sunlight caught the face of Dante's watch as she folded her arms.

“Club men are downstairs,” Tank said. “Suppliers too. Everybody calling about what they're owed. Some of the niggas who owe us won't answer now.”

Aiden rubbed his thumb along the edge of the sheet. “Red?”

“Stopped answering the west side,” Tank said. “T's office is locked. Leave it that way till sundown, some nigga gonna decide we ain't got an office anymore.”

Aiden looked at him without speaking.

“T put you there,” Tank continued. “People heard him. But putting you there and keeping you there ain't the same shit.”

“Nigga, you keeping me there?” Aiden asked.

Tank worked his jaw. “I drove your car.”

Aiden did not look away. “That ain't what I asked.”

Tank's eyes went to the watch on Shawna's wrist. She unfolded her arms and let the hand hang at her side. The watch slid toward her knuckles.

“I ain't putting another man in the desert,” Tank said.

He rose, moved the flowers clear of the wet table, and went to get the men downstairs.

***

Vega returned to find nine men in Aiden's room and two more in the hall. The visitor's chair held a duffel bag. A man wearing three gold teeth had pressed himself against the window to make room for another man whose jacket hung open over a pistol.

“Absolutely not,” Vega said.

Everyone turned toward her. One man stepped farther into the room, blocking the door behind himself. Vega pointed past him at the hallway.

“Two visitors,” she said. “The rest of you leave.”

Nobody moved. Aiden lifted his head off the pillow.

“Niggas, she said move,” he told them. “You want the woman who kept me alive having to step over you?”

The man in the doorway backed out. Others followed, turning sideways between the bed and the visitor's chair. One caught his chain on a coat button and had to stop while another unhooked it. Vega held the door until the last of them had passed.

Tank remained. So did an older man from T's club, who laid a stack of papers across Aiden's knees. Shawna took her chair near the window and drew it beside the bed. Vega looked at her, looked at the two standing men, and held up two fingers once more.

“One conversation,” Vega told them. “Then she gets her chair back and you get out.”

Tank nodded. The older man straightened the papers with both palms.

“Money coming in Friday,” the older man said. “People need paying today.”

“What's in the bag?” Aiden asked him.

The man pointed to the duffel. “Yesterday.”

Tank opened it. Cash filled the bag almost to the zipper. Aiden looked into it, then at the date at the top of the first sheet.

“Niggas worked, niggas getting paid,” Aiden said.

The older man touched the bag's handle. “From this?”

Aiden looked at him. “Unless you got some fucking magic beans.”

The man nodded and made a mark beside the first name. He moved his pen to the next page without looking at the bandage beneath Aiden's open gown.

“Red wants a meeting,” he said.

“Nigga, Red can call,” Aiden told him.

The man lowered his pen. “He wants it face to face.”

“Nigga, look at mine,” Aiden said. “I'm busy.”

The older man's eyes rose to the cuts and swelling. Tank closed the duffel. Outside, one of the waiting men laughed at something, then stopped when another voice hissed at him.

Aiden pushed the papers toward the older man.

“Nobody touches Shawna,” Aiden said. “Nobody says her name to Red. Anybody got a problem with that can leave the keys on the bed.”

The man looked at Tank. Tank stood beside the door with one hand still on the bag.

“You heard him,” Tank said.

The older man gathered the papers. His keys stayed in his pocket. He nodded once to Shawna and left.

Aiden's head sank onto the pillow. A dark patch showed along the edge of the dressing beneath his gown. Shawna rang for the nurse, then laid her hand against his forehead.

Tank moved the duffel closer to the bed.

“You want me counting it?” Tank asked.

Aiden closed his eyes for a breath and opened them on Tank again. “Nigga, I want you watching that door like your brother in here.”

Tank looked toward the hall. “For who?”

“Every fucking nigga,” Aiden said.

Tank took a chair outside. When the nurse arrived, he stood and moved it back to give her room. After she entered, he sat down facing the corridor.
