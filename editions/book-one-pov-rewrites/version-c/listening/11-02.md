Red guided Shawna past the musicians, where the piano notes sounded clearly between the beats of the bass. He bent close enough that she turned her face slightly away from his mouth.

“You used to sing this upstairs,” Red said.

“I sang a lot of things,” Shawna answered.

Red's hand moved farther around her waist. “Before Dante.”

Shawna looked up at him. “I had a life before all of you.”

Red brought her through a turn. Her wristwatch touched his cuff. The blue stone on his hand passed beneath a white light, then disappeared as he lowered their joined arms.

“You could have that life now,” Red told her.

“Could I?” Shawna asked.

Red smiled. “Whatever you want.”

Shawna looked down at the ring. Red rotated his hand inward and drew her through another step.

“I want him left alone,” Shawna said.

Red's mouth stopped moving with the song. “Already thinking about that nigga?”

“His name is Aiden,” Shawna told him.

“I know what the nigga's name is,” Red said.

A couple passed close to them. Red smiled at the man over Shawna's shoulder. Shawna returned the woman's greeting. The couples separated, and Red's hand settled firmly at Shawna's back.

“You said you were tired of running,” Red said.

“I am,” Shawna answered.

Red drew her closer. “You said you wanted to come home.”

“I said I wanted somewhere I could sleep,” Shawna said.

Red lowered his voice. “My bed was never the problem.”

Shawna's shoulder pulled back. Red's arm remained around her, and the fabric at her waist tightened between his fingers.

“Don't do that tonight, Shawna,” Red said.

She looked directly at him. “Then don't talk to me like that.”

They stopped. Another couple went around them, the woman's skirt brushing Shawna's ankle. Red's jaw worked once before he spoke again.

“Do you know what it felt like?” Red asked. “Standing there while he put that ring on you? Paying for a room where you were going to spend the night with him?”

Shawna held her chin level. “Dante was your friend.”

“Nigga knew,” Red said.

“Knew what?” Shawna asked.

Red brought their hands up between them. “That his nigga saw you first.”

Shawna pulled her hand far enough back to look at the marks his fingers had left. “You all keep saying that like I was a fucking parking space.”

Red loosened his grip. Another smile went across his face as a man raised a glass toward them. Red nodded to the man and began dancing again.

“I could've given you everything,” Red told Shawna.

She waited until they had passed the next couple. “Then give me the diamond.”

Red's feet slowed. Shawna kept moving, drawing his arm with her until he followed.

“That's what tonight is for,” Shawna said. “You wanted me to know how serious you were. Show me.”

Red looked across the room. “And Aiden?”

“You let him walk out,” Shawna answered.

Red watched Aiden standing beside Tank. “Will he?”

Shawna placed her free hand against Red's cheek. “He will if I tell him to.”

At the side of the floor, Aiden lifted his glass. He emptied it without looking away from Shawna's hand.

Earlier that afternoon, before the cars left the house, Aiden and Shawna had stood in front of their bedroom mirror. Her blue dress lay across the bed. She held one earring against her ear while he adjusted his collar behind her.

“If he keeps you beside him, I'll come over,” Aiden had said. “Make him think I can't stand it. You tell me to leave you alone.”

Shawna lowered the earring. “I can do that without hitting you.”

Aiden met her eyes in the mirror. “If he's still watching us like he doesn't believe you, one slap.”

“No,” Shawna said.

Aiden turned her gently by the elbow until they faced each other. “One. I can take a slap if it gets you close enough to finish this.”

“You say that now,” Shawna told him. “Then you get in there and start being you.”

Aiden put both hands at his sides. “I listen. You tell me to go, I go.”

Shawna studied his face before raising her palm halfway between them. “And this doesn't turn into another excuse for you to hit something.”

“Agreed,” Aiden said.

She lowered her hand. “One slap. Only if we need it.”

Now, at the ballroom's edge, Aiden put the empty glass down and walked toward them. Tank called his name. Rosa stepped away from Marcellus and reached for Aiden's sleeve, but her fingertips brushed the white fabric without catching it.

Aiden stopped beside Red. “Your dance is over.”

Red continued the turn, drawing Shawna around with him. “You hear the band? The band's still playing.”

Aiden put a hand on Red's shoulder. Red stopped beneath it. Shawna looked first at that hand, then at Aiden's face.

Red spoke without raising his voice. “That hand comes off me.”

“Yours comes off her first,” Aiden replied.

Three men in dark jackets left the wall. Tank stepped onto the floor behind Aiden. The singer missed a word and stared at the men for half a measure before finding the next line.

Shawna stepped between Aiden and Red, forcing both men to release their holds. Her watch swung down against the heel of her hand.

“Aiden,” Shawna said, “go sit down.”

Aiden's mouth remained open for a moment. “What?”

“You're embarrassing me,” Shawna said.

Aiden glanced at the approaching men. He looked back at her. “You mean that about your nigga?”

Shawna's palm struck his cheek. The sound reached the tables nearest the floor. Aiden's head turned with the blow. Her hand stayed raised for a second, trembling visibly, before she lowered it against her dress.

“I told you,” Shawna said, “to listen to me.”

Aiden turned his face back. A red patch spread above his jaw. He stood still while Shawna looked at him, then stepped away. Red moved up beside her. The three men stopped a few feet behind him.

Shawna extended her hand to Red. “Let's finish our dance.”

Red took it. He led her away while looking past her shoulder at Aiden. Tank caught Aiden's sleeve and pulled once toward the tables.

“Come on,” Tank said.

Aiden jerked his arm but did not free it. “Don't.”

Tank kept his grip. “Come on.”

Aiden went with him. Rosa met them at the edge of the floor and stood close enough to keep her words beneath the music.

“She just kept you alive,” Rosa told Aiden.

He looked at the carpet. “I know.”

Rosa waited until he looked up. “Then act like you know.”

Aiden put his palm against the side where T's bullet remained. Across the room, Red kissed Shawna's hand. Aiden stayed beside Tank.

At nine, the bandleader lowered his baton and stepped back from the microphone. Red walked onto the low stage with Shawna beside him. Two attendants drew open a curtain behind them, exposing a glass display case beneath a narrow white lamp.

A necklace lay in the case. Its central blue stone rested against dark velvet, small enough to cover with a thumb. The lamp sent blue light across the edges and left a bright point burning at its center.

Aiden leaned toward Tank. “All that for something a nigga could lose in a couch.”

Tank did not answer. Marcellus had moved in front of them. His mouth hung slightly open; the small jeweler's case was pressed so tightly against his leg that one finger had gone white along its handle.

Red took the microphone. “Some things wait a long time to come home.”

He turned toward Shawna. Applause rose from the tables, and he waited for it to end before bringing the microphone close again.

“Tonight isn't an auction,” Red said. “Nobody in this room can afford what this means to me.”

A man in front laughed loudly. Red looked over his head.

“Tonight is a promise,” Red said.

He raised Shawna's hand. Dante's watch slid toward her elbow, exposing the pale skin beneath the band. Tank said something too low to carry beyond Aiden's shoulder.

Marcellus approached the steps. An attendant stopped him, spoke with him, then gave his name and profession to Red. Marcellus climbed onto the stage and inclined his head. The gray in his disguise caught the white light as he bent toward the case.

Red lowered the microphone. “You approve?”

Marcellus kept his eyes on the necklace. “I would like to see it closer.”

“Later,” Red said.

Marcellus raised his case a little. “A piece of this age deserves—”

“Later,” Red repeated.

The blue ring flashed as Red moved the microphone into his other hand. Marcellus's gaze followed it. He lowered his case and stepped back.

Shawna looked past the front tables until her eyes met Aiden's. She held his gaze. He nodded once, and she turned toward the applause when Red faced the room again.

After the presentation, an attendant found Aiden beside a column. Tank stood with him. Marcellus had returned from the stage and was closing the empty straps on his little case.

“Mr. Redmond would like you upstairs,” the attendant said.

Aiden kept his back against the column. “Nigga can come get me.”

Tank touched his arm. Across the room, Shawna spoke to Rosa beside the stage. Rosa's hand rested at her back. Both women looked toward the column when the attendant repeated the invitation. Shawna nodded.

Aiden pushed away from the stone. “All right. Show me.”

Tank followed. Marcellus picked up his case and came with them. At the foot of the stairs, the attendant turned and held a hand in front of the jeweler.

“The invitation was for—” the attendant began.

Aiden stopped two steps above him. “Your boss is giving away forty million dollars. He can let a jeweler look at it before everybody leaves.”

The attendant lowered his hand. Marcellus passed him without speaking.

The office stood above the ballroom. The bass reached them through the floor, a dull repeated pressure beneath the carpet. Dark wood covered the walls between the city-facing windows. A wide desk stood opposite the entrance; another door, set into the wall beyond it, opened as Aiden came in.

Shawna entered through that inner door carrying a small blue box. Red followed her and went behind the desk. Two men stood outside the main doorway while the attendant withdrew.

“I thought you might prefer privacy,” Red said.

Aiden stopped on the visitors' side of the desk. “For what?”

Red rested both hands on the polished wood. “The end.”

Shawna took a step toward Aiden. Red moved from behind the desk and put a hand on her shoulder.

“Tell him,” Red said.

Shawna held the box in both hands. “Aiden, take the box.”

Aiden extended his empty hand toward her. “Come here to your nigga.”

“Take it,” Shawna said.

The lid tapped against her thumb. Aiden stepped close enough to lift the box from her shaking fingers. He opened it. Inside, the blue stone and its necklace lay in a fitted velvet bed, exposed to the desk lamp.

Red watched him over Shawna's shoulder. “Forty million. Enough to start over anywhere.”

Aiden raised his head. “You think I'm selling her?”

“I think she just sold you,” Red said.

Marcellus came forward. Red raised one finger, and the two men entered from the main doorway. One stood behind Tank; the other moved beside Marcellus. The jeweler stopped before reaching the box.

Shawna looked at each man in turn. Her hands had closed against the seams of her dress. Aiden lowered the open box to the desk without releasing it.

Red returned to his chair and opened a drawer.

“And since your friends are so interested in history,” Red said.

He placed a flat leather folder on the desk. Across its white label, in heavy black letters, was one name: DANTE.

Shawna drew a sharp breath. Aiden reached across the desk and pulled the folder toward him. Red left his hands flat on either side of the empty space.
