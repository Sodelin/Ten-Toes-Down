## 9. Our Valedictorian Has a Gun

Aiden had two pistols on the kitchen table and a piece of toast in his hand when Tank brought in the cream envelope. A gold crest shone above his name. Tank held it away from the coffee while Aiden looked from one gun to the other.

“School,” Tank said.

Aiden put down the toast. “Whose?”

“Yours,” Tank told him.

Aiden looked up. “Nigga, I got a school?”

Shawna stopped pouring coffee. Rosa turned from the counter. Mercedes, still in slippers from the guest room, lowered her own toast.

“Las Vegas College,” Tank said.

Aiden nodded. “Oh. Those niggas.”

Shawna set the pot down. “You went to college?”

“I went to the building,” Aiden said.

She came around the table. “When, nigga?”

“Twenty-two,” Aiden told her. “Free lunch. Excellent chicken.”

Tank took a second page from the envelope. His finger stopped halfway down it.

“Nigga, it says valedictorian,” Tank said.

Aiden picked up his toast. “They paying attention.”

“Give me that,” Rosa told Tank.

Aiden raised a hand toward the paper. “Nigga, don't wrinkle my achievement.”

“I'm looking for the payment demand,” Rosa said. She spread the letter beside Mercedes's plate. “Mercedes, check if his picture's on a milk carton too. This nigga been somewhere for two years and forgot.”

Mercedes leaned closer to the page. “Highest marks in the college's history.”

Tank looked across at her. “Marks in what?”

“History, nigga,” Aiden said. “She just said.”

Shawna turned his face toward hers with two fingers beneath his chin.

“Baby,” she said, “what did you do to these people?”

Aiden set the toast down again.

“Friend told me about scholarship day,” he said. “Adults twenty-two and up. I was twenty-two. Nigga said lunch was free.”

He described the registration desk, the woman behind it, and the tables beyond her where plates were being filled. When she told him the meal was for applicants, he had picked up a pen before she finished the sentence.

“She gave me a booklet,” Aiden told Shawna. “Math. Reading. Music with notes in the wrong places. Some question about making two promises when you couldn't keep both.”

Mercedes turned the letter over while he spoke. Rosa leaned against the counter with her arms folded.

“I ate and wrote,” Aiden continued. “Finished the chicken. Fixed the music. Took two rolls. Apparently they kept the paper.”

Tank looked at the letter in Mercedes's hands.

“You ate a degree,” Tank said.

Aiden spread his hands. “Nigga, I thought while I chewed. You act like this forbidden technology.”

Mercedes tapped the lower part of the page. “They want him there at eleven.”

Shawna looked at the clock. It read nine twenty.

“We need clothes,” she said.

“Nigga, I got plans,” Aiden told her.

Porsche came through the doorway wearing sunglasses and carrying a garment bag.

“Your plans just got a hat,” Porsche said. “You're going.”

Aiden pointed toward the folded blue invitation near the window. “Red's gala—”

“Is in two nights,” Porsche told him. “This is in a hundred minutes. Even your nigga can count that far.”

Tank lifted a finger. “Actually he has the highest—”

“Don't help him,” Shawna said.

Rosa pointed her coffee spoon at Tank. “Don't.”

Mercedes lifted the letter out of his reach. “He can read about it in the car.”

Shawna bent and kissed Aiden. Her hand stayed against his cheek when she drew back.

“I want to see my man graduate,” she told him.

Aiden stood, took one pistol, and put the other into the kitchen cabinet before closing its door.

“Then get dressed,” Aiden said. “My niggas going to college.”

Tank looked down at his plate.

“Bring it,” Aiden told him. “Apparently that's the entrance exam.”

***

Tank drove the black Mercedes to the college. Porsche followed in Leon's newest Bentley, dark green this time. Mercedes sat beside her with a camera. At the first light, Porsche lowered the window and held up the card that had come with the car.

“White brings back too many memories,” Porsche read to Aiden through the open windows.

Aiden looked at the green hood. “Nigga learning colors.”

Behind them, Rosa sat beside Marcellus in another car. Her yellow suit filled the seat with color. He wore his clean shirt and a tie she had knotted for him. Shawna sat beside Aiden in white, smoothing the edge of his open-necked shirt so the cross lay straight.

He wore black trousers and a jacket that covered the dressing beneath his shirt. His pistol stayed inside the jacket at his waist. As the convoy reached the campus gates, he looked out at grown men and women in blue gowns carrying flowers, checking programs, and finding places to stand with their families.

Near the fountain, a man pointed toward the Mercedes.

“Nigga. That's him,” the man said to his friend.

The friend looked up from a program. “Who?”

“The Mercedes nigga,” the first man said.

His friend looked around the parking lot. “This a college, Marcus. There's seventeen Mercedes in the parking lot.”

Marcus leaned closer. “Nigga, the flying Mercedes.”

The friend looked properly this time. Then he looked down at the program and began hitting Marcus's shoulder with the folded edge.

“Our valedictorian?” he asked.

“That's what I'm saying!” Marcus told him.

“That nigga?” his friend asked.

Marcus spread both hands toward the car. “That nigga!”

People around the fountain turned as Aiden stepped out. A woman hurried down the main steps carrying a blue robe over both arms. Silver glasses sat beneath carefully pinned hair. She stopped in front of him and drew a breath before speaking.

“Aiden,” she said. “Professor Yvonne Halcyon. We met at your admission.”

Aiden looked at her face. “You had the chicken.”

She smiled. “You remember.”

“Nigga doesn't forget a good plate,” Aiden told her.

Halcyon put the robe into his hands. “We have been looking for you.”

“I been around,” Aiden said.

She looked toward the Mercedes. “We've seen.”

Principal Percival Mercer came down the steps behind her. Gold trim covered his blue academic robe. He brushed a hand over his gray beard, then held it out toward Aiden. Tears had begun running beneath his eyes before they shook hands.

“At last,” Mercer said.

Tank leaned toward Aiden. “Nigga, what the fuck was in that booklet?”

“Apparently me,” Aiden answered.

Mercer took Aiden's hand between both of his.

“Your work changed this college,” Mercer told him.

“Which part?” Aiden asked.

Mercer opened a folder. “The final page.”

Aiden looked at the copy inside. His handwriting ran beside a faint circular stain.

“The blank one?” he asked.

“You used it to correct the examination,” Mercer said.

Aiden pointed at the edge of the copied page. “There was a mistake.”

“There were six,” Mercer told him. “We had found two in twenty years.”

He held the folder while Halcyon turned to the others.

“The scholarship board accepted his independent work in place of the standard course sequence,” Halcyon said. “The essays circulated through four departments. His music analysis repaired the conservatory's examination. Professor Lorne has been teaching the final solution since last spring.”

Aiden looked past her. “Nigga, I hope Lorne got a nice voice.”

A man standing behind Halcyon lifted a hand.

“I do my best,” Lorne said.

Shawna turned toward Aiden. “You didn't tell me any of this.”

“Baby, I didn't know the niggas kept it,” Aiden told her.

Nearby graduates laughed. One took off his cap and looked down at it, shaking his head.

“I came every night for three years,” the graduate said.

Aiden turned toward him. “And you here, ain't you?”

The man straightened. “Yeah.”

“Then come on,” Aiden said. “Niggas graduating.”

The man put his cap back on and walked beside them toward the college steps. Halcyon helped Aiden into the robe he had been carrying and handed him his square blue cap. He turned it once in his hands before following Mercer.

***

Mercer led them through the science hall on the way to the auditorium. Over the entrance, gold letters read AIDEN HALL. Aiden stopped beneath them. Tank raised his phone while Shawna put her hand over her mouth.

“Nigga, I got a hall,” Aiden said.

“You got several,” Mercer told him.

Rosa turned toward the principal. “Don't tell him that. He'll start naming the doors.”

Inside, three professors stood around a long table covered in papers. Their conversation stopped in pieces as Aiden entered. One finished a sentence after the other two had turned toward the door.

Lorne approached the table and touched a drawing near its center.

“Perhaps you could settle something,” Lorne said to Aiden.

Halcyon raised a hand. “He has a ceremony.”

“One minute,” Lorne said.

Aiden came to the table. “Nigga, everybody thinks they need a minute until I get here. Show me.”

Lorne turned the drawing around. It showed the auditorium roof, lines crossing above numbers and a span circled three times in red.

“The model gives us two possible answers,” Lorne said.

Aiden looked at the drawing, then through the window toward the actual auditorium. He stepped nearer the glass and counted across its upper windows with one finger.

“You got the old roof in here,” Aiden told him.

Lorne bent over the paper. “The dimensions—”

“Changed when they moved the stage,” Aiden said. “Look at that window. Eight panels across. Your drawing got seven.”

The professors turned toward the window. One went to stand beside him. Aiden returned to the table, took the offered pencil, and moved a line on the page. He wrote beneath it and placed the pencil alongside the new figures.

“Now your load goes there,” Aiden said. “Those two numbers stop fighting.”

Lorne read what he had written. The professor beside him pulled another drawing from beneath the pile, compared the pages, and sat abruptly. His wheeled chair moved backward until it touched the wall.

“He's right,” the professor said.

Aiden looked at him. “Nigga, say it like you enjoyed finding out.”

The professor raised his face. “He's right!”

The room laughed. Mercer wiped his eyes again. Aiden turned toward Shawna.

“Your man got a roof,” Aiden said.

She hooked two fingers into his blue robe. “My man better get under it before they name another building.”

At the door, Lorne touched Aiden's arm.

“What do you do for a living?” Lorne asked.

Tank answered beside him. “Nigga improves transportation.”

***

The auditorium was full. A choir stood on risers above the stage. Blue banners hung from the balcony. The printed program listed the youngest graduate as twenty-two and the oldest as sixty-eight. Families carried flowers into the rows and lifted phones above one another's shoulders.

Shawna took a seat in the middle of the front row. Tank sat at the aisle, moved his knees, looked down at the chair arms, and shifted one leg into the open space beside him. Rosa sat with Marcellus on her other side. Mercedes checked her camera while Porsche watched the people entering from the rear doors.

Aiden passed his cap to Shawna before going behind the stage curtain.

“Hold that till they finish telling niggas what I did,” he told her.

She placed it in her lap. “Try to recognize some of it.”

Chief Knox stood beside the stage. Aiden stopped when he saw her.

“Nigga, you following me to school now?” he asked.

“I was invited,” Knox told him.

“You graduating?” Aiden asked.

Knox folded her arms. “I was hoping to recruit the valedictorian.”

Behind her, a dark blue cloth covered something low and wide. The fabric followed a sharply sloped hood before dropping toward the floor. Aiden moved sideways to see past her.

“That's a Lamborghini,” Aiden said.

“Finish your speech first,” Knox told him.

He looked at her. “Chief, that's cruel.”

“Motivation,” Knox said.

She took her place beside Mercer. The ceremony began with names and applause. Graduates crossed the stage in blue gowns while their families stood to photograph them. Aiden waited behind the curtain, his robe open around the cross.

Shawna looked back toward him. He lifted two fingers. She smiled and put his cap more securely between her knees.

The lights flickered.

Aiden looked upward. A man in a gray suit stood in the upper aisle, turning his face between the exit doors. Another man stood below the balcony with his hand inside his jacket. Aiden caught Knox's eye and raised his chin toward the upper aisle.

She followed his look.

The man under the balcony drew his gun as a line of flame ran up the curtain behind the choir.
