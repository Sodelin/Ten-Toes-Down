## 2. First Night in Vegas

On the night Aiden met Shawna, white powder dusted the front of his shirt, and blood had dried inside the bezel of his watch. He rubbed at the watch with his thumb while Big T held the nightclub's back door open. The second hand passed beneath a thin red smear and emerged on the other side.

“You coming?” T asked.

Aiden showed him the watch. “Nigga, somebody got inside it.”

T took Aiden by the shoulder and steered him through the doorway. The bass shook the metal door against its stop. Beyond the loading light, cars stood crookedly across the lot, blue cloth hanging from pockets and mirrors. Eleven men waited among them. One bent his knee and pulled his heel up behind him. Another removed his rings and laid them on the hood of a Cadillac.

Aiden lowered his wrist.

T shut the door. With him, there were twelve.

“Once a nigga in, that nigga in,” T said. “Understand?”

“Nigga, I understand,” Aiden told him.

T kept his hand on Aiden's shoulder. “Summer ends, a body drops, suddenly a nigga got somewhere else to be.”

“I got nowhere else to be,” Aiden said. “That's why I'm standing here instead of enjoying the fucking music.”

The man stretching his leg laughed. T looked at him. He put his foot down and rubbed his palms together.

“You think this funny?” T asked Aiden.

Aiden looked around the circle. “Nigga, I think twelve is a committee. Which one of you takes the minutes?”

T drove a fist into his face.

Aiden struck the Cadillac with one shoulder. The man who had left his rings on its hood snatched them away before Aiden's hand came down. A second fist split Aiden's lip against his teeth. He turned into another man's elbow, staggered sideways, and lost his footing when a shoe struck behind his knee.

His palms hit the concrete. Somebody kicked him under the arm. His breath broke into a dry cough against the ground.

“Stay down,” a man in a blue cap told him.

Aiden caught the man's trouser leg and pulled himself up far enough to hit him in the stomach.

“Nigga, y'all just told me to stay IN,” Aiden said. “Pick an instruction.”

The man bent over him. Aiden shoved him into the others and got to his feet with one eye running blood. He wiped it with the back of his hand. The next punch caught his raised forearm; his answer struck a jaw with an audible click.

Tank came through the gap. His shirt pulled tight across his shoulders, and its top button had already disappeared. He ducked beneath Aiden's arm and landed a short punch in his ribs. Aiden folded around it, hooked both hands into Tank's shirt, and kept going forward.

They hit another car. The alarm began screaming. Tank's elbow dented the door as he tried to turn Aiden against it.

“Not the paint!” the car's owner shouted.

Aiden drove a fist into Tank's face. “Nigga, you should've thought about the paint before you started throwing people at it!”

Tank caught one of his wrists. Aiden hit him with the other hand, short blows with no room to draw back. The cloth tore under his knuckles. Tank planted a foot against the tire and shoved, but Aiden's grip pulled him back against the dent.

“Get this nigga off me!” Tank called.

T stood several feet away, laughing. The others had stopped advancing. One wiped his mouth; another held a hand against his stomach.

“T!” Tank shouted.

“You said he was little,” T told him.

Tank ducked another blow. “Nigga, I have new information!”

T caught Aiden around the chest from behind and dragged him clear. Tank came off the car with both fists raised. T put an arm between them.

“Enough,” T said. “Twelve niggas. Look at him. Still shopping.”

Aiden stood with his fists beside his face. Blood dripped from his chin onto the powder on his shirt. His hands shook.

“Nigga, I'm done,” he said.

T looked at the fists. “You sure?”

“I'm waiting for my hands to hear it,” Aiden told him.

T laughed once, then reached into his pocket. He took out a gold chain and opened it across both hands. The little cross at its center swung beneath his fingers. He lowered the chain over Aiden's head, holding it clear of the cut above his eye, and let the cross settle against his chest.

The car alarm stopped. Nobody spoke while Aiden lifted the cross away from his bloody shirt.

“My brother wore that,” T said. “Real nigga.”

Aiden raised his face. “Where my nigga at, then? I should thank him.”

“Dead,” T said.

A car passed on the street beyond the lot. Its light traveled over the Cadillac and disappeared behind the club. Aiden closed his hand around the cross. T took the back of his neck, drew him forward, and kissed his forehead.

“Nigga, don't make me bury you too,” T said.

Aiden swallowed. “Not this nigga.”

T kept hold of his neck as they turned toward the door.

“Come get your money,” T said. He raised his voice to the men ahead of them. “Niggas, make room for my son.”

Tank opened the door. Aiden reached for it, and Tank moved his hand away.

“Nigga, walk through,” Tank told him. “I can still defeat a door.”

***

Blue light filled the back room. It struck the smoke above the tables and lay in bright bars along bottles, gun barrels, and the edges of bundled cash. Bricks of cocaine covered a glass tabletop beside an ashtray too full to contain its ashes. A woman in a diamond necklace leaned against a man with two pistols tucked into the same belt. When he shifted in his chair, both grips knocked against the glass.

The woman raised her drink toward Aiden.

“Damn, baby. You survive a war?” she asked.

Aiden wiped his mouth. “Yeah. And I won.”

Several people cheered. T pushed him toward a chair beside the money. A man handed him an open bottle; another held out a burning blunt. Aiden took the bottle first. The liquor touched his split lip, and he lowered it with one eye screwed shut before taking a second drink.

“This the nigga beat Tank?” the woman asked T.

“That's the nigga,” T said.

She folded a thousand dollars into Aiden's shirt pocket, pressing the bills down with two fingers so they sat clear of the blood. Aiden looked at the pocket and then at her.

“Keep looking like that,” she told him. “I'll need change.”

Tank came in holding a plastic bag of ice against his jaw. He took the bottle from Aiden, drank, and returned it before pulling out the next chair.

“Nigga, who taught you to fight dirty like that?” Tank asked.

“Same nigga taught you to fight unconscious,” Aiden said.

Tank stayed upright beside the chair. Laughter broke around the table. Aiden lifted the bottle a little but did not drink. Then Tank's mouth opened, showing blood on his teeth, and his laugh joined the others. He dropped into the chair hard enough to send an ash down from the edge of the tray.

“Twenty-eight years old,” Tank said, moving the ice to the other side of his jaw. “Nigga, I'm too old to learn shit like this.”

Aiden leaned toward him. “I'm twenty-four. You got four years to catch up.”

Tank reached for the bottle again. This time Aiden passed it to him before he asked.

T threw a set of keys onto the table. They slid between two stacks of money and stopped against Aiden's forearm.

“Black Mercedes outside,” T said. “That's yours.”

Aiden lifted the keys and pressed the emblem with his thumb.

“Nigga, these keys real?” he asked. “You serious?”

“Where's that car you had?” T asked him.

“Burned outside my ex-boyfriend's apartment,” Aiden said. “Nigga still thinks that means I want him back.”

T pointed at the keys. “Then drive this one somewhere else. West side need a nigga. West side got you.”

Aiden's thumb stopped on the emblem. Around him, cash continued passing from one pile to another. Tank knocked the neck of the bottle against his wrist. Aiden took it and drank without looking away from T.

T opened a photograph on his phone and put the screen in front of him.

A woman stood beside a swimming pool. A black dress followed the line of her body, and diamond earrings caught the pool lights beneath her dark hair. Behind her, the Strip rose in rows of light against the night. Aiden brought the screen closer. Tank lowered his ice bag and watched him.

“Shawna,” T said. “Twenty-six. Works for Red.”

“Nigga, working for Red doing what?” Aiden asked.

T took the phone back. “Whatever Red wants.”

Aiden set the bottle down.

“She's at the Saint tonight,” T continued. “Penthouse. She's bringing a case down at eleven. You take her and the money to my house in Henderson. That's all you gotta do.”

“Why she leaving him?” Aiden asked.

“Because she finally got smart,” T said.

Aiden held out his hand for another look at the picture. T showed it to him without releasing the phone.

“Does Red know about this little family pickup?” Aiden asked.

“If he knew, we wouldn't be having this conversation,” T said.

He bent over Aiden's chair and gripped the back of his neck. The people at the next table were shouting over a count of money. T brought his mouth close to Aiden's ear.

“Don't fall in love with her,” T said.

Aiden laughed. T's hand stayed where it was.

“I'm serious,” T told him. “Look at me.”

Aiden turned his face. “Nigga, look at me. Do I look like the nigga falls in love?”

T studied him, then released his neck. Aiden stood, pushed the keys into his pocket, and adjusted the pistol already tucked at the back of his waistband. Tank tipped the bottle toward him. Aiden touched its neck with two fingers on his way to the door.

***

Water fell through a gold fountain outside the Saint. The valet lane curved around it beneath a canopy lit so brightly that the blood on Aiden's collar showed as soon as he stepped out of the Mercedes. A valet approached holding a ticket and slowed beside the open door.

“You need somebody to call a doctor?” the valet asked.

“Nigga, my car needs you first,” Aiden told him. He placed a hundred-dollar bill against the ticket. “Nothing happens to it.”

The valet took the money. Aiden looked back once at the car before going through the revolving door.

Upstairs, the penthouse windows reflected a room full of people. Dice struck a marble table between bank-wrapped stacks of bills. Dresses brushed against holstered pistols. A man in a white fur coat bent over a trophy with a rolled note at his nose, then carried the trophy with him when he went to get a drink. Someone reached toward it. He turned his whole body away.

Near the bar, Mercedes saw Aiden and held her glass against her chest. She looked him over from the cut on his face to the new keys swinging in his hand.

“You finally got a Mercedes you can afford, baby?” she asked.

“Come see it,” Aiden said.

Mercedes lifted an eyebrow. “Nigga, you still owe me flowers.”

Aiden reached into the pocket the woman at T's club had filled. “You took my shoes.”

“You called me my sister's name,” Mercedes said. “In a hotel room. While I was in it.”

“I noticed when I had to leave barefoot,” Aiden told her. He counted five hundred dollars into her hand. “Buy the whole fucking garden.”

Mercedes folded the money and kissed his cheek. Her lipstick touched the edge of a dry streak of blood.

“You bleeding on the good side of your face,” she said.

“Both sides good, baby,” Aiden told her.

She laughed. Aiden smiled back, then looked past her toward the windows. His hand stopped turning the keys.

Shawna stood near the glass with a cigarette between her fingers and a silver case beside her ankle. A man in a dark suit spoke close to her shoulder. She had turned her face away from him. Her gaze followed Aiden as he crossed the room.

He stopped in front of her. At this distance the little scar above her eyebrow showed beneath the light. She lowered her cigarette. The man beside her looked at Aiden's cut mouth and stepped aside with his glass.

“T sent me,” Aiden said.

“I know,” Shawna told him.

“How?” Aiden asked.

Her cigarette rose toward her mouth and stopped. With her other hand, she touched the gold cross against his chest.

“Where'd you get that?” she asked.

“T,” Aiden said.

“Tonight?” Shawna asked.

Aiden nodded. “Yeah.”

She turned the cross over. Three small letters had been cut into its back: D. B. M. Her thumb moved across them. Aiden looked down at her hand and let the keys fall still beside his leg.

“You know what those mean?” Shawna asked him.

“No,” Aiden said.

She let the cross drop against his shirt. “Drive before you find out.”

She crushed the cigarette into a nearby ashtray and picked up her purse. Aiden took the case. Shawna caught his free hand, and they crossed the room together. A man by the door put down his drink and took out a phone. At the marble table, another man stopped counting.

The elevator stood beyond a short corridor. A guard stepped into their path with one hand against the front of his jacket.

“Nigga, where the fuck you taking her?” the guard asked Aiden.

Aiden lifted their joined hands. “With this nigga right here. You need my picture?”

“Nigga, I'm asking her,” the guard said.

Aiden released Shawna's hand and struck him in the throat. The guard hit the wall with both shoulders. His mouth opened without a word coming out. Aiden shoved him down as his fingers went under his jacket. The guard's heels slid on the polished floor, and the back of his head struck the paneling.

Shawna pressed the elevator button. The doors opened with a soft chime. Aiden stepped over the fallen man's shoes, carrying the case close against his leg.

Another man came around the corner. Shawna took a small silver pistol from her purse and aimed it at his chest.

“You get one chance,” she told him.

The man stopped. His hands remained at his sides. Shawna backed into the elevator beside Aiden and kept the muzzle level until the doors met between them and the corridor.

For several floors neither spoke. The numbers descended above the doors. Shawna held the pistol in both hands, low now, with its barrel pointed at the floor in front of her shoes.

“Niggas chasing you out of every party,” Aiden asked, “or I got the special one?”

Shawna looked at him. “Only the ones I survive.”

The elevator slowed. She put the pistol into her purse before the lobby doors opened. Aiden held out his hand. She took it again.

***

They had barely cleared the Saint's canopy when Shawna looked into her side mirror and tightened her grip on the case.

“Aiden,” she said. “Don't look.”

He looked into his mirror. A black Charger came up behind them with its lights off. The driver sat close to the wheel; a passenger leaned toward his window. Across the back seat, a third man brought a long gun up between the front headrests.

The Mercedes's rear glass burst inward.

Shawna dropped over the case. Aiden bent low and turned the wheel. The right front fender struck the fountain's edge as they cut across the end of the valet loop. Water rose over the hood and broke across three men standing beside a limousine. One kept his phone held high while the other two ran.

The Mercedes shot into the street with its horn sounding continuously. Shawna braced one hand across the center of the wheel as the car jolted straight.

“Your niggas?” Aiden shouted.

“You think I'd ride with you if those were my niggas?” Shawna shouted back.

“You met me tonight!” Aiden said.

Shawna lifted her face above the dashboard. “I'm trying to meet you tomorrow! Drive!”

A yellow Ferrari slid out of a side street and accelerated beside them. Its driver grinned through his lowered window. The passenger raised a pistol across the driver's chest. The muzzle followed the Mercedes as the two cars drew level.

Aiden glanced at Shawna's wrist.

“Baby, move your hand,” he said.

“What?” Shawna asked.

“The horn,” Aiden told her.

She pulled her hand away. The horn stopped. The Ferrari's engine filled the gap.

Aiden turned sharply toward it.

The yellow car swerved. The passenger's gun went upward as his elbow struck the window frame. The Ferrari's front wheel hit the curb, and its nose struck a street pole. Yellow panels folded. The rear end swung across a valet entrance, scattering sparks beneath a row of men who jumped backward onto the hotel steps.

Shawna twisted in her seat. “Nigga!”

Aiden straightened the wheel. “That's one.”

“That's a Ferrari!” Shawna said.

“That's one Ferrari,” Aiden told her. “Keep up.”

The Charger crossed behind the wreck and stayed with them. Its long gun shifted beneath the windshield. Aiden moved the Mercedes across a lane, then another. Behind them the Charger made the same moves.

A red convertible burst from a hotel drive into the space between the two cars. Mercedes stood in its passenger seat with her heels clutched in one hand. Wind flattened her dress against her legs.

“I told you about my fucking flowers, Aiden!” she shouted.

Porsche drove with both hands on the wheel. Her dark red hair streamed across one cheek. She shook it free without taking her eyes off the lane ahead.

“Sit down, Mercedes!” Porsche shouted.

Mercedes pointed her shoes at Aiden's car. “He owes me!”

“Nigga, he owes the whole street right now!” Porsche told her.

Mercedes dropped into the seat as Porsche swept across the Charger's nose. Its front end dipped under braking. The man holding the long gun struck his chin against a headrest, and the barrel disappeared below the dashboard.

Shawna looked from the convertible to Aiden.

“Your friends?” she asked.

“Good people,” Aiden said.

“Your good people have terrible timing,” Shawna told him.

Aiden nodded toward her mirror. “Look behind us.”

The Charger moved left. Porsche went left. It moved right, and the convertible slid in front of it again, close enough that the Charger's driver lifted a hand off the wheel to hit his own windshield. Mercedes turned in her seat and held up the shoes.

Shawna laughed once. Her hand loosened on the handle of the case.

Then the Charger struck the convertible's rear bumper.

The red car swung toward the roadside wall. Mercedes caught the windshield frame with both hands. One shoe flew over her shoulder and through the light above an outdoor restaurant. A diner ducked beneath his table as it struck a plate.

Aiden braked hard. Shawna caught his forearm.

“Aiden!” she shouted.

He pulled behind the convertible. “Not leaving them.”

The Charger came up in the mirror. Its driver had a white scar across his eyebrow. His face turned toward the red car, then toward the Mercedes now between them.

Aiden watched the mirror. “Nigga, choose me.”

The impact drove both occupants against their seats. Metal folded behind them. The Mercedes's bumper broke loose at one end and scraped the street in a shower of sparks. Aiden's head struck the headrest, bounced forward, and came up with his eyes on the road.

Ahead, Porsche turned into the skid. The convertible's rear wheel passed within inches of the wall before the car straightened. She lifted one finger above its windshield and accelerated away from the curb.

At the next intersection, a transport truck crossed the boulevard with a silver speedboat high on its load. An empty rear trailer followed it. Beside the intersection, a closed hotel's delivery ramp climbed steeply from the street toward an elevated service entrance.

Aiden lined the Mercedes up with the ramp.

Shawna turned her face toward him. “No.”

“You don't know what I'm thinking,” Aiden said.

“Nigga, I know exactly what you're thinking,” Shawna told him.

Aiden pressed the accelerator. “Then hold the case.”

She wrapped both arms around it. The Mercedes hit the ramp, its suspension flattening before the nose lifted. The loose bumper struck the concrete and tore away. The wheels left the ramp's edge.

For a moment the truck passed beneath the windshield. The speedboat's flag bent under the car. Shawna screamed Aiden's name. His arms stayed straight against the wheel, his mouth open, the cross lifted clear of his shirt by the car's flight.

They cleared the boat. The Mercedes struck the road beyond it with a crash that slammed the case against Shawna's chest. A tire burst. The car leaned toward the ruined wheel, scraped, and rolled upright again under Aiden's hands.

Behind them, the Charger took the turn too late. Its back end broke loose beneath the truck's empty trailer. The collision flung a black wheel into the intersection. It rolled upright between two stopped taxis and fell against the curb.

The Mercedes continued on three full tires and the shuddering remains of the fourth. Its engine rattled. Aiden eased off the accelerator, and Shawna turned far enough to look through the hole where the rear window had been.

Nothing followed them out of the intersection.

At the next light, Porsche brought the convertible alongside. Mercedes sat low with one silver heel held upright in her lap. Glass glittered in Aiden's hair.

“You jumped a fucking boat!” Porsche shouted across the lane.

Aiden brushed glass from his collar. “Had to get over it somehow.”

Porsche leaned toward him. “Aiden, nigga, you jumped the boat with a car!”

A man waiting beside the taxi stand began clapping. Two people near him joined in. Suitcases stood abandoned around their owners' feet as the applause spread along the curb.

Aiden lifted a hand. Shawna caught it and placed it back on the wheel.

“They can admire you moving,” she told him.

Mercedes pointed her remaining shoe at Shawna. “Keep him. He's expensive, but nigga, he comes with a show.”

The light changed. Porsche blew Aiden a kiss and turned onto another street. Aiden took the Mercedes toward the edge of town at a slower speed. Each turn of the damaged tire brought a slap of loose rubber against the wheel arch.

Shawna touched the uncut side of his face.

“You could've kept going when they hit those girls,” she said.

Aiden kept his eyes on the road. “Wouldn't have been me.”

She left her hand against his cheek for another moment, then lowered it to his leg.

***

The last casino signs fell behind them. Beyond the windshield, the road ran between dark lots and low buildings with their windows shuttered for the night.

“Pull over,” Shawna said.

“T's house is farther out,” Aiden told her.

“I know where his fucking house is,” Shawna said. “Pull over.”

He turned behind a wedding chapel whose sign had lost half its light. The damaged wheel crunched over gravel. He stopped beside the rear wall and left the engine running while Shawna opened the case on her lap.

Bundles of cash filled the lining from edge to edge. Some had shifted during the landing. She pushed one aside with two fingers.

“Three million,” Shawna said.

Aiden leaned toward the open case. His hand hovered above it, then returned to the steering wheel.

“T said—” he began.

“T says a lot,” Shawna told him.

From beneath the money she took an envelope. Inside lay a photograph bent across the middle. She unfolded it and held it where the dashboard light fell across the faces.

In the picture, a younger Shawna wore a white dress in front of the chapel. A man stood with his arms around her waist. The gold cross on his chest had the same small shape as the one resting against Aiden's shirt.

“Dante,” Shawna said. “His brother. My husband.”

Aiden looked from the photograph to the chapel wall. He lifted the cross and held it beside the image without touching the paper.

Shawna took a cigarette from behind her ear. The lighter clicked three times without catching. Aiden put the cross down, took the lighter, and cupped the flame for her. She leaned toward it, drew smoke, and sat back with the photograph against her knee.

“I was twenty-three when I came out here,” she said. “Thought I was going to sing. Suitcase, eight hundred dollars, a number some girl gave me in Phoenix.”

Aiden switched off the engine. The damaged car shook once and fell still.

“The number was Valentino's,” Shawna said.

Aiden's hand closed around the key. “White suits? Diamond tooth?”

Shawna nodded. “He bought me clothes. Got me a room. Told me he had people in music. Every favor came with another thing I owed him. By the time I knew what the fuck was happening, everything I had belonged to him.”

She blew smoke toward the open crack in her window. A truck passed on the main road, its headlights moving across the chapel wall.

“Dante came into the club on my birthday,” Shawna continued. “Everybody else wanted something. He asked what kind of cake I liked.”

The cigarette stopped above the ashtray. Aiden looked at her mouth. She pressed her lips together, then drew another breath.

“Came back the next night with carrot cake,” Shawna said. “Whole fucking cake. Put it between the bottles on the table. Valentino laughed at him.”

Aiden waited. Shawna turned the photograph a little, bringing Dante's face into the dashboard light.

“Dante knocked out that diamond tooth,” she said.

“My nigga,” Aiden said.

Shawna looked up. “That's what I said. That's exactly what I fucking said.”

She wiped beneath one eye with her thumb. The cigarette trembled between the fingers of her other hand.

“We got married here six weeks later,” Shawna said. “T stood beside him. Red paid for the room. They all smiled in the pictures.”

Aiden lowered the cross. “What happened?”

“Dante took money out of a deal,” Shawna said. “Three million. Told me we were going to leave. Said he had a brother, so he didn't have to be scared.”

She turned the photograph over. Words ran across its back. Aiden bent close enough to read them aloud.

“Damn, boy. Magnificent,” he read.

Shawna touched the little cross. “That's what the letters mean. He looked in the chapel mirror after we got married and said it like God had just finished him. I laughed so hard I nearly dropped my flowers. Next morning I had it put on the cross.”

Aiden looked at her. “Nigga was right about you.”

“He was talking about himself,” Shawna said.

Aiden held the cross flat against his own chest. “Then the nigga finally got it on the right neck.”

Shawna's laugh came out before she covered her mouth. The photograph shook against her fingers. Aiden's smile widened, pulling the cut on his lip. He touched it; she laughed again, more softly, then held the picture against her chest.

For a while the only sound inside the car was the click of its cooling engine.

“They left him in the desert,” Shawna said. “Valentino brought me his watch. Told me Dante died begging for somebody to take care of me.”

Aiden's jaw tightened. He put the lighter on the dashboard with both hands.

“What did you do?” he asked.

“Waited,” Shawna said.

She tapped ash through the opening in the window.

“Eight months,” she continued. “Valentino thought I came back because I had nowhere else to go. He thought I loved him by the end.”

She turned toward Aiden, keeping the photograph against her dress.

“Last Tuesday, I shot him in his car,” Shawna said. “He was talking when I did it. He kept talking after the first shot, so I shot him again. Then I took Dante's watch out of his glove compartment.”

She raised her wrist. The watch slid toward her hand, too large for it. Aiden looked at its face without reaching for it.

“Red took me in,” Shawna said. “Said he'd protect me. Tonight I took the three million back.”

“And T?” Aiden asked.

“T says he's going to get me out of Vegas,” Shawna told him.

Aiden turned the keys over on his knee. “You believe him?”

Shawna looked at Dante's photograph. “I believed his brother.”

She put the picture inside her dress and closed the case. The catches snapped one after the other. Then she leaned across the space between them and kissed Aiden, her hand flat against the uncut side of his face.

He let the keys fall onto the seat. His hand came up behind her head. When she drew away, neither moved back into a seat for several breaths.

“Don't take me to that house,” Shawna said.

Aiden picked the keys up. “Then this nigga ain't taking you. Give me somewhere else.”

***

The motel stood beyond town, two rows of rooms above an empty pool. Trucks passed on the highway behind it. A strip of blue neon hummed over the office window, and a moth struck the glass while the clerk counted Aiden's cash.

“All six rooms?” the clerk asked.

“All six,” Aiden said. “Nobody comes up.”

The clerk looked past him at the Mercedes. Its rear window was gone, its bumper absent, and a ribbon of tire lay curled beneath the wheel. Shawna waited beside the passenger door with the case. When she turned toward the office, the clerk lowered his eyes to the money and pulled six keys from their hooks.

Aiden took her upstairs. They entered the room at the far end of the balcony, where two beds stood beneath a yellow wall lamp. Shawna placed the case on one bed, her purse and silver pistol on the nightstand. She set Dante's photograph faceup beside the lamp. Aiden locked the door and laid his own pistol within reach on the other side of the bed.

An argument rose from the parking lot below, then faded behind the sound of a passing truck. Shawna stood in front of Aiden and began opening his shirt. He held still as she drew the cloth away from his ribs.

“Jesus, Aiden,” she said.

“I'm good,” he told her.

Her fingers stopped above a swelling bruise. “You're bleeding.”

Aiden glanced down. “I've been bleeding all night.”

She went into the bathroom and returned with a wet towel. He sat on the edge of the bed. She cleaned the blood from his eyebrow, working toward the cut instead of dragging the cloth across it. When he flinched, she stopped and waited for his shoulders to lower.

“Sorry,” Shawna said as the towel touched his lip.

Aiden caught her wrist lightly. “Don't be.”

She turned the towel to a clean fold and tried again. He kept his mouth closed until she had finished. Then he reached behind his neck, lifted the chain over his head, and placed the cross in her hand.

Shawna's fingers closed around it. The towel fell against her knee. Her first breath broke before she could take the next one, and she bent over her closed hand with her face against Aiden's chest.

He put his arms around her. She cried without lifting her head. The lamp cast their shadows across the other bed and the silver case. He moved one hand slowly between her shoulders and stayed seated when she pressed closer.

When her breathing grew quieter, she drew away enough to look at him.

“Stay,” Shawna said.

“I'm here, baby,” Aiden told her.

She held his shirt. “Tonight. I mean tonight.”

“I'm right fucking here,” Aiden said.

She laid the cross beside the photograph, took his face in both hands, and kissed him. He slipped out of the torn shirt. She reached for the lamp and switched it off herself.

Later, blue light from the motel sign crossed the ceiling. Their clothes lay beside the bed. Shawna rested against Aiden's chest, one hand above the bruise on his ribs. The case remained closed on the second bed; the two pistols stayed on either side of the first.

“What do you want?” Shawna asked.

“You,” Aiden said.

She lifted her head. “You don't know me.”

“Then tell me more,” Aiden told her.

She lay back and talked about Phoenix. About songs she had written on scraps of paper and a mother who still asked whether the restaurant kept her working late. Aiden moved his hand along her arm as she spoke. When she mentioned a house near the water, he turned his face toward hers.

“Dante wanted one,” Shawna said. “A place where we could open the windows and hear it.”

“Then we'll buy it,” Aiden told her.

She touched his chin. “Neither of us could swim.”

Aiden nodded against her hand. “We can learn.”

“You can't either?” Shawna asked.

“Nigga, tonight I went over it,” Aiden said. “I ain't claim I could go in it.”

She laughed against his chest. He held her until the laugh stopped shaking the bruise.

“Anywhere you want,” Aiden told her. “Give me somewhere. We'll go.”

His phone lit up on the nightstand. T's name filled the screen. Aiden watched it ring without reaching for it. Shawna raised herself on one elbow.

The ringing stopped. A message appeared with a photograph beneath it.

The black Mercedes stood in the motel parking lot, its damaged wheel turned toward the office. The picture had been taken from close enough to show the broken glass along the rear window's edge.

Under it, T had written: *Come down, son.*

Aiden sat up. Shawna reached for the lamp. He pulled his clothes on and took his pistol from beside the bed, sliding it into his waistband before he opened the door.

***

T stood beside the empty pool with Tank and two other men. The cracked blue tiles showed beneath them through the fence. A little of Aiden's blood remained on T's shirt cuff.

Aiden came down the metal stairs with his shirt open and his hands raised clear of his body. Each footstep rang against the treads. T waited until he reached the bottom before speaking.

“Where's the money?” T asked.

“Upstairs,” Aiden said.

“The girl?” T asked.

Aiden stopped outside the pool gate. “Upstairs.”

T nodded. “Nigga, one thing. I gave you one fucking thing to do.”

“She told me about Dante,” Aiden said.

Tank turned his head toward the dark office window. T continued looking at Aiden.

“Dante stole from his family,” T said.

Aiden took a step closer. “Nigga, he was your brother.”

“Then the nigga should've known better,” T said.

Aiden's raised hands lowered several inches. T came through the pool gate and stopped in front of him. The two men behind Tank spread apart along the fence.

“That woman put him in the ground,” T told Aiden. “Now you standing here ready to follow him.”

Aiden looked directly at him. “You put him in the ground.”

T seized the open edge of Aiden's shirt. “Nigga, who made you? Who put you in that car?”

He pointed across the lot at the Mercedes, then struck Aiden's chest with the same hand.

“I gave you money. A car. A fucking name in this city,” T said. “You think she would've looked at you without all that?”

Aiden did not answer. His hand closed at his side and opened again.

“She's upstairs with my three million,” T continued. “That's what she's in love with.”

Behind T, Tank shifted one foot along the concrete. Aiden looked toward the movement. T caught his chin and turned his face back.

“Last chance,” T said. “Go get her.”

A door opened above them.

Shawna stood on the balcony in her black dress. She had the silver case in one hand and her pistol in the other, held down beside her leg.

“You want your money, T?” she called.

T released Aiden's face and looked up.

Shawna swung the case over the railing. It fell into the empty pool and struck a corner on the tiles. The hinge broke. Bundles of cash burst from it and slid down the sloping bottom, some coming apart as they hit the drain.

“There,” Shawna said. “Now tell him what I love.”

T drew his pistol.

Aiden struck his forearm upward as the gun fired. A window shattered beside the balcony. Shawna ducked behind the rail. Tank caught Aiden from behind, pinning one arm against his body, while a man from the fence came forward swinging.

Aiden drove his forehead into that man's face. Their heads struck with a dull crack. The man fell backward holding his nose. A fresh line of blood ran from Aiden's hairline.

Aiden twisted beneath Tank's arm and shoved him into the fence. The wire bowed over the pool. Tank's grip tore at the back of Aiden's shirt before coming loose.

Shawna rose above the railing and fired. The second man beside T fell to the concrete. His gun struck the pool gate and stayed beneath it.

T came against Aiden's side. His pistol pressed into the open shirt below the ribs.

The shot snapped across the lot. Aiden's knees bent. He caught T's wrist with both hands, the muzzle trapped against his body. Blood spread beneath his shirt and ran over T's knuckles.

“Should've listened,” T said.

Aiden tried to draw a breath. His mouth opened, but only a short rasp came out. T pulled at the pistol. Aiden held the wrist down.

Tank struck Aiden from the side. Aiden staggered without releasing T. He drove his knee upward into T's body, tore one hand loose, and reached behind his own back.

His pistol cleared the waistband.

He fired once. T struck the fence with his shoulders. Aiden fired again. T slid down the wire and fell on his side, one hand still extended toward Aiden's open shirt.

Tank stopped moving.

Aiden stood above T with his gun raised and blood running past his belt. His free hand pressed against the hole in his side. Tank looked at T, then lifted his face toward the pistol.

“Aiden—” Tank began.

Aiden backed away from him. “Nigga, back—Tank, back the fuck up!”

Tank took two steps backward and held his hands clear.

Shawna ran down the stairs barefoot, her pistol still in her hand. At the bottom she saw Aiden's shirt and broke into a run across the concrete.

“Baby,” Shawna said. “Oh, fuck. Baby, look at me.”

Aiden's mouth moved. She put her pistol down beside her foot and pressed both hands against his side. He folded over her shoulder. She spread her feet under his weight and turned toward Tank.

“Get the fucking car!” she shouted.

Tank looked once more at T. Then he ran across the lot toward the Mercedes.

***

Cash lay across the bottom of the pool. The broken case rested on its side, one lining flap moving whenever the highway wind came through the fence. Aiden climbed into the passenger seat with Shawna under his arm. He lowered his pistol into the space beside the seat. She pushed a folded motel towel against his side and closed his hand over it.

Tank stepped away from the door. Shawna retrieved her pistol from beside the fence, took the cross, photograph, and her purse from the room, and came down the stairs at a run. She put the photograph and pistol into the purse, threw it behind the driver's seat, laid the chain beside her, and started the car.

The damaged tire slapped the pavement as they pulled onto the road. Light had begun spreading above the desert. Behind the Mercedes, the towers of Vegas still shone against the fading dark.

Shawna drove with one hand. With the other she pressed Aiden's hand and the towel against his wound. His head had fallen back against the seat. Blood marked his lower lip where he had bitten the old cut open again.

“Stay with me,” Shawna said.

Aiden turned his face toward her. “I'm here.”

“Keep talking,” she told him.

He drew a shallow breath. “House.”

Shawna looked at him, then back to the road. “Tell me.”

“Near the water,” Aiden said. “Room for your music. Whole room. Nobody knocking on the wall.”

Shawna pressed the towel harder. Tears ran down her cheeks, and she wiped one side of her face against her shoulder without taking either hand away.

“What about the windows?” she asked.

“Sunrise,” Aiden said. He paused, breathing through his mouth. “Face the sunrise.”

“And swimming?” Shawna asked.

Aiden lifted his head a little. “Nigga, don't make that the first lesson.”

A sound came out of her between a laugh and a sob. She turned the car around the next bend, following the first signs back toward the city.

At the edge of town she pulled onto the shoulder. Aiden's hand slipped from the towel when she let go of it. She put it back, held it there, and took the gold chain from beside her.

“Don't,” Aiden said as she leaned over him. “That's his.”

“I know,” Shawna told him.

She lowered the cross around his neck and closed his fingers around it for a moment. Then she kissed his face, returned his hand to the towel, and put the Mercedes back on the road.

His phone rang from the seat between them. One caller's name appeared and vanished; another replaced it. On the third call, Aiden picked it up. He missed the button once before answering.

“Aiden,” he said.

For a moment only the road could be heard. Then a man's voice came from the phone, thin enough that Shawna leaned nearer without looking away from the windshield.

“What you want us to do?” the caller asked.

Aiden looked at Shawna's hands. Dante's watch had slid down against the hand pressing his wound. The cross lay against his open shirt, its lower edge dark with blood.

“Some nigga better find me a doctor,” Aiden said. “And tell everybody the girl is with me.”

He lowered the phone onto his leg. Shawna kept the car moving toward the city. When the first sunlight reached the dashboard, Aiden's hand still held the towel beneath hers.
