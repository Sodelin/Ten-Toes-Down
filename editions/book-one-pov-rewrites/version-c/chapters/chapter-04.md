## 4. The Palace of the Woman I Just Met

At Lake Las Vegas, the real estate agent lost one heel to the lawn before she reached the front path. Her foot came up without the shoe. She stood on one leg with her folder held out for balance while Tank bent, pulled the heel from the grass, and handed it back.

She looked at his hands. Then at the black cars behind him, the men beside them, and Aiden standing with Shawna's hand beneath his elbow. His shirt remained open at the neck; farther down, a bandage showed through a gap between the buttons.

“Will anybody else be joining the viewing?” the agent asked.

Tank straightened. “Nigga, you want MORE of us?”

“We're fine,” Aiden told her. “Show my woman the water.”

The agent fitted the shoe back onto her foot and stepped onto the stone path. She opened her folder as she walked, holding it against the breeze coming off the lake.

“Five bedrooms,” she said. “Private dock. Heated pool. The piano can be negotiated separately.”

Aiden stopped beside the door. “Why? The nigga in a band?”

“The owner may wish to retain it,” the agent said.

“Tell that nigga to wish for a smaller instrument,” Aiden told her.

Shawna squeezed his hand. “Baby. Let her do her job.”

The agent unlocked the door. Beyond it, a curved staircase rose beneath a chandelier. White curtains hung from windows high enough that their upper folds disappeared behind the second-floor rail. Across the living room, glass doors opened toward a patio, a stretch of lawn, and the dock. Lake water shifted in the light beyond the last boards.

A black piano stood beside the doors. Its polished side reflected a wavering stripe of blue from the water.

Shawna let go of Aiden's arm and crossed the room.

“Aiden,” she said.

He followed more slowly. “Yeah.”

She stopped beside the keyboard. “Baby, you can't just—”

“You like it?” Aiden asked.

Shawna touched two keys without pressing them. Her fingers stayed there while she looked through the glass.

“Yeah,” she said.

Aiden turned toward the agent. “Nigga, we have a yes. Do whatever paper follows yes.”

The woman opened her folder farther. “We haven't discussed the price.”

“Don't say it louder than she said yes,” Aiden told her.

Tank went through the glass doors and across the lawn. He stopped at the first board of the dock and put one foot on it. A duck floated a few feet away. The board creaked. Tank took his foot back. The duck turned its head toward him.

Inside, Shawna sat on the piano bench. Aiden lowered himself beside her, stopping halfway with both hands braced on the seat. Shawna caught his arm before he went the rest of the way down.

“Slowly, you ridiculous nigga,” she said.

Aiden settled beside her. “That IS slowly. At my normal speed we'd own next door already.”

She laughed and looked out toward the water. Tank put his foot onto the dock again, this time nearer the center of the board.

“We're doing this backward,” Shawna said.

Aiden spread a hand toward the bench. “Nigga, then turn around.”

She swung one leg across it until she faced him. “We barely know each other.”

“I know you want water,” Aiden said.

Shawna's fingers rested on the edge of the seat. “Dante wanted water.”

Aiden's smile remained for a moment. Then his mouth closed. Through the glass doors, the duck moved several inches away from Tank's shoe.

“You said it to me,” Aiden told her.

Shawna put her hand on his knee. “I know.”

He looked down at it. She left it there while the agent made a call near the front door.

When the agent returned, she held a written figure between two fingers. Aiden read it and took out his phone. He called the older man who had brought the club accounts to his hospital bed.

“House at the lake,” Aiden told him. “I'm putting her on. Get it paid.”

The man's voice came faintly from the phone. Aiden listened, his hand resting over Shawna's on his knee.

“T's place is promised to somebody else,” he said. “Leave it promised. The club money still coming in?”

The man answered. Aiden held the phone out to the agent.

“Then handle this one,” Aiden said. “Put the house in Shawna's name.”

The agent took the phone but paused before raising it. “And yours?”

“My nigga lives where I live,” Shawna told her.

Aiden turned toward Shawna. She tilted her face toward his.

“That the arrangement?” she asked.

“Nigga, that's the whole constitution,” Aiden said.

Shawna smiled. “Then stop negotiating over my piano.”

Aiden pointed toward the keys. “Piano stays,” he told the agent. “My wife said—”

Shawna raised an eyebrow. He stopped with his hand still extended.

“My future,” Aiden corrected. “My future said.”

She kissed him hard enough that he caught the bench's edge with both hands. The agent turned toward the front hall with the phone against her ear.

Outside, Tank finally stepped onto the dock with both feet. The duck paddled away. He watched it go, then walked another three boards toward the lake.

***

By evening, Shawna held the keys. Rosa arrived carrying her red coat and stopped beneath the chandelier without taking off her sunglasses. She leaned back to look up at it, then lowered her face toward the staircase.

“Nigga, this bitch gets shot at one time and comes home with a staircase,” Rosa said.

Shawna laughed so loudly that Tank looked up from the living room. She caught Rosa by the wrist and pulled her toward the first step.

“Come see upstairs,” Shawna said.

Rosa pointed above them. “You got upstairs too?”

“Nigga, you looking at the stairs,” Tank called.

Rosa turned and pointed at him instead. “And you got furniture that talks.”

Tank looked toward Aiden. Aiden sat back on the sofa with an ice pack wrapped in a towel against his side. He covered his mouth with the back of his free hand while Shawna and Rosa went upstairs.

Within an hour, Rosa had carried one of her bags into a bedroom, examined the light in every bathroom, and taken a painting down from the upstairs hall. She brought it to the railing with its back toward her.

“Where we putting this dead nigga's painting?” Rosa called.

Shawna appeared behind her. “He's not dead. He moved to Arizona.”

Rosa turned the painting around and looked at the painted man's long face.

“Nigga didn't tell the artist,” she said.

She carried it into an empty room. A moment later, Shawna's laughter came down the staircase again.

Tank sat opposite Aiden and leaned forward with his elbows on his knees.

“You sure about all this?” Tank asked.

Aiden shifted the ice pack. “House?”

“Woman,” Tank said.

Aiden's hand stopped. “Watch it.”

Tank looked toward the stairs. “Nigga, I been watching.”

Aiden waited until Tank faced him again.

“Say it,” Aiden told him.

“She loved Dante,” Tank said.

“He's dead,” Aiden answered.

Tank leaned farther forward. “Nigga, that's what I'm telling you.”

Aiden threw the ice pack at him. Tank caught it against his chest without leaving the chair. A damp mark spread across the front of his shirt.

“Nigga, I got a bullet in me,” Aiden said. “You want me jumping up to fight your big ass?”

“No,” Tank said.

“Then stop saying shit I can't sit down for,” Aiden told him.

Tank stood, crossed the room, and put the ice pack back against Aiden's side. He kept his hand beneath it until Aiden took the weight.

“Some nigga gotta tell you something,” Tank said.

Upstairs, Rosa called for Shawna to come look at a bathtub. Shawna answered with another laugh. Both men turned toward the sound.

Aiden pressed the ice pack against himself.

“Tell me where T's funeral is,” he said.

***

The first housewarming guests arrived while Shawna was still looking for towels. Mercedes came through the front door with twelve women, two men, and a box of champagne balanced against her hip.

“Gift,” Mercedes announced when Aiden looked at the box.

He took a bottle from the top. “Who died?”

“Nigga, leave a little mystery in the evening,” Mercedes told him.

Porsche came up the drive behind them in a white Bentley with its roof down. A man sat in the back with a bottle across his lap and wet tracks beneath his eyes. Porsche parked beside the patio drive and took the keys out before anybody opened a door.

Tank looked into the back seat. “Who that?”

“His car,” Porsche said. “My evening.”

The man raised a hand. “We're friends.”

Aiden leaned against the doorframe. “Nigga, blink if she bought you too.”

The man laughed. He wiped his cheeks with both hands and leaned forward as Mercedes came to take the bottle from his lap.

“Leon,” he said to Aiden. “I own the steakhouse. We had dinner. I may have got ahead of myself.”

Porsche rested an elbow on the open driver's door. “Proposed over appetizers.”

Leon lifted one hand. “I'm thirty-seven. I know what I like.”

“She took the ring?” Aiden asked him.

“Declined the ring,” Porsche said. She raised the keys. “Accepted permission to drive.”

“That's growth,” Mercedes told Leon.

He laughed again and climbed out after she had moved the bottle clear. Porsche closed the doors and led him toward the patio by one sleeve.

Within twenty minutes, women were in the heated pool, their diamonds moving under the surface whenever they lifted their arms. Champagne stood in bowls packed with ice from a delivery truck. Another truck, refrigerated and loaded with steaks, had backed into the drive. Its driver was explaining to Tank how much grill space they would need.

Beside the kitchen door, a dealer held his wrist up for Rosa. She took it lightly, tilted the watch toward her ear, and listened while he smiled.

“Can't hear the personality inside it,” Rosa told him.

Tank laughed from the doorway.

The dealer turned toward him. “You got something to say?”

“She said it,” Tank answered. “I'm enjoying it.”

The man looked up at Tank's shoulders. His mouth opened into a smile. He laughed too and took his wrist back from Rosa.

Aiden lay in a patio chair with a clean shirt open over his bandage. His pistol rested beneath a folded napkin on the side table. Shawna sat beside him. Someone had left a statue of a horse on the table near the glass doors. It stood between two flower arrangements with one leg raised, its polished flank reflecting a bowl of melting ice.

“Baby, is this all yours?” Shawna asked.

Aiden moved his hand across the patio, the lawn, and the lake beyond it.

“Whatever you can point at,” he told her.

She pointed at the horse. “Who the fuck brought that?”

Aiden looked toward the nearest guests. None of them reached for it.

“I'm finding out,” he said.

An engine rose above the music. A red speedboat came around the point and headed directly toward the dock. The driver stood at the wheel with his white shirt open to the waist. A heavy chain struck his chest each time the bow rose. Two men sat behind him with rifles across their knees.

Tank put down his plate.

“Everybody inside,” he called.

The music continued. Several people kept talking. Then the boat hit the dock, lifting a board with a crack, and heads turned across the patio.

“Where's the new owner?” the driver shouted.

Aiden raised his glass from the chair. “Home.”

The driver pointed at the house. “You bought from my cousin.”

“He looked old enough to make that decision,” Aiden told him.

“Brock had no right selling my house,” the man said.

Shawna stood. “Your name on anything?”

The man stepped toward the side of the boat. “Nigga, I'm talking to the man.”

Aiden sat forward. His glass touched the table without a sound.

“Then look at me while you regret that,” Aiden said.

The man's eyes came to his. One of the riflemen lifted the weapon off his knees. On the patio, the song ended. The next had not begun. Women in the pool held the edge and stopped moving.

“Name's Roman,” the driver said. “Roman Pike. You can ask around.”

“You can go around,” Aiden told him. “My dock's occupied.”

Roman put one foot on the dock. “I got a claim.”

“Nigga, so does my insurance company,” Aiden said. “You just hit the fucking dock.”

Roman climbed out. Behind him the boat rose against the broken board and settled again. His two men stood with the rifles held low.

“I come for twenty thousand a month,” Roman said. “My cousin paid. You pay. We don't have trouble.”

Aiden looked up at Shawna. “Baby, you hear that?”

“I heard him,” she said.

Aiden pointed toward the house. “Nigga sold his house and left a subscription.”

Shawna laughed into her hand. Roman reached inside his open shirt and drew a pistol. Rosa pulled the napkin off Aiden's gun and snatched it from the side table.

Tank moved behind the stone barbecue. One of Roman's men fired from the dock. The glass doors behind Aiden broke across the patio, and he pulled Shawna down from her chair. They hit the stone together. His mouth opened against her shoulder. He stayed over her while another shot tore through the chair he had just left.

Mercedes dragged two women up the pool steps and shoved them toward the kitchen. One slipped on the wet stone; Mercedes caught her dress at the shoulder and kept her moving. Other guests followed, crouching low as they passed the shattered doors.

Porsche caught Leon by the collar and pulled him behind the Bentley's rear wheel.

“My car!” Leon shouted.

“Your head first!” Porsche shouted back.

Rosa rolled behind a fallen patio chair. She slid Aiden's gun across the stone toward him, keeping her body below the chair's back.

“Housewarming gift,” Rosa called.

Aiden caught the pistol. “You shouldn't have.”

“Nigga, I didn't,” Rosa told him. “It's yours.”

Roman ran toward the boat. Tank fired from behind the barbecue, striking splinters from the dock around the riflemen's feet. A bullet hit a champagne bottle above him. Glass and wine washed over his shoulders and down the back of his collar.

Tank ducked, then rose enough to fire again.

“Now I'm fucking mad!” he shouted.

Aiden pushed himself away from Shawna and looked toward the Bentley. It stood at the end of the short patio drive. Between it and the dock lay a strip of lawn, two garden chairs, and a low stone border curving down toward the last section of boards.

Shawna followed the direction of his face.

“Again?” she asked.

Aiden tucked the pistol into his waistband. “Different boat.”

She caught his open shirt. “Aiden.”

He put his hand over hers. “Baby, get inside.”

She kissed him, released the cloth, and rose into a crouch beside Rosa.

“Then come back in my house,” Shawna told him.

Rosa took her toward the kitchen. Aiden ran bent at the waist toward the Bentley. Porsche opened the driver's door before he reached it. Leon raised both hands from behind the rear wheel.

“It's new!” Leon called.

“Nigga, so's my house!” Aiden shouted back.

He got behind the wheel. Porsche put the keys into his hand, ran around the front of the car, and threw herself into the passenger seat. Leon crawled clear of the rear bumper toward the patio wall.

“I know this car,” Porsche said.

Aiden started it. “Then introduce us fast.”

Roman's boat pulled away from the dock. Its red hull swung broadside, propeller churning white beneath the stern. The riflemen were still on the damaged boards, crouched behind a mooring post as Tank fired from the barbecue.

The Bentley came off the drive and crossed the lawn. Grass tore beneath its tires. The two garden chairs disappeared under the front bumper, one leg spinning up over the hood. Porsche caught the door handle with one hand and braced the other against the dashboard.

“Beautiful, baby, beautiful!” Porsche shouted.

The front wheels hit the stone border. The car lifted, crossed the dock's broken end, and came down across the boat's rear deck.

Fiberglass split beneath it. The Bentley's hood folded upward. The boat's stern plunged beneath the car's weight, kicking its bow into the air. Roman struck the steering wheel and went over the side. Water rose over the windshield and dropped across the two open seats.

The car stopped with its rear wheels hanging above the water and its front end wedged into the boat. Aiden's hands remained against the wheel. Porsche lifted her face from the arm she had thrown across it and spat lake water over the door.

From the house came a roar of voices. People crowded the kitchen window. Mercedes pushed the door open, stepped onto the patio, and raised both arms.

Porsche turned toward Aiden. “You owe Leon a car.”

Aiden looked at the broken boards beneath them. “Nigga owes me a dock.”

Roman surfaced beside the boat with his hair across his eyes. His pistol was gone. He caught the red hull with both hands and coughed against it. One rifleman had jumped into the shallows. The other remained on the dock with his rifle on the boards and both hands raised. Tank stood over him with his gun pointed down.

Aiden leaned out over the Bentley's door.

“Roman,” he called.

Roman pulled his face clear of his hair and looked up.

“House still yours?” Aiden asked.

Roman shook his head. Water ran from his nose.

“Nigga, speak,” Aiden told him. “I bought acoustics.”

“Your house!” Roman shouted.

“And whose car?” Aiden asked.

Roman stared at the Bentley hanging over him.

“Wrong answer,” Aiden said. He raised his voice toward the patio. “Leon!”

Leon stood with both hands on his head. One of the women from the pool held his elbow, but he had not turned toward her.

“Come get your speedboat!” Aiden called.

The cheering started again. Mercedes climbed onto a sound patio chair with a bottle in each hand. Rosa came out of the kitchen with her arms around Shawna. Tank laughed, then pointed his gun back at the man seated on the dock.

“Stop fucking looking at me,” Tank told the prisoner.

The man turned his face toward the lake. Tank laughed harder and had to put one hand on the mooring post.

Shawna reached the edge of the damaged dock and held out both hands. Porsche climbed over the side of the Bentley onto the remaining boards. Aiden followed more slowly, stopping when his shirt caught on the door latch.

Shawna freed the cloth and took his hands.

“You are the most ridiculous man I've ever met,” she told him.

Aiden stepped onto the dock. “Best-looking too.”

“Nigga, I married a dream tonight and I haven't even married you,” Shawna said.

She pulled him close. Behind him, the boat settled lower beneath the car, sending a small wave against the dock supports. Leon came down the lawn and stopped at the water's edge.

“Never loved an automobile more in my life,” Leon said. He looked at its folded hood. “Never want to see that motherfucker again.”

Tank kept the riflemen on the dock and in the shallows until men from the house relieved him. Roman came out of the lake dripping onto the lawn. His open shirt clung to his chest beneath the heavy chain. When Knox's officers arrived, they took him and his men through the gate past the refrigerated steak truck.

Later, Aiden sat on the sofa with Shawna curled against his good side. Their friends still moved through the house, carrying food from the kitchen and stepping around the horse statue, which somebody had placed beneath the staircase.

His phone rang. He answered and held it away from the music.

“Two boats in five days?” Chief Knox asked.

“First one I cleared,” Aiden said.

“I'm beginning to understand the distinction,” Knox told him.

Aiden looked through the glassless patio doors toward the damaged dock. “You got any more questions?”

“One,” Knox said. “Can you do that in uniform?”

Shawna lifted her face from Aiden's shoulder. He smiled at her while she drew the blanket farther across their legs.

“Depends how nice you make the uniform,” Aiden told Knox.

***

The next night, blue lights ran along the sidewalk outside the club. Above the entrance, newly mounted letters spelled AIDEN. He stopped beneath them in a white suit and looked up until a car behind the one he had stepped out of sounded its horn.

Shawna stood beside him in blue silk. Her back was bare above the low line of the dress. Earrings long enough to brush her shoulders swung when she turned toward him. Dante's watch remained on her wrist.

She slipped her arm through Aiden's.

“They waiting on you,” Shawna said.

Aiden kept his face raised toward his name. “Let them.”

Inside, silver tubs held sweating champagne bottles beneath the blue light. Velvet jackets brushed against bare arms and jeweled dresses. At the back, men carried drinks into private rooms where mirrors lay on the tables beside rolled notes and lines of cocaine.

A dancer came down from a platform when she saw Aiden. She kissed his cheek and held his shoulders away from her to look at him.

“Look at my nigga!” she said. “Baby, you made it.”

“We made it,” Aiden told her.

He put a stack of cash into her hand. She pressed it against her chest, laughed, and climbed back onto the platform. Shawna moved closer to him as they continued through the room.

At a table near the back, a man in a silver shirt stood and opened his arms.

“Look at you,” the man said to Aiden.

Aiden smiled. “You too.”

The man kissed the corner of his mouth. Shawna's hand tightened around Aiden's arm.

“Congratulations,” the man said. “Last year you on my balcony swearing you'd own the view. Whole weekend you wouldn't shut up about it.”

Aiden glanced toward Shawna. The man still held him by one shoulder.

“Always knew you had something,” the man told Aiden.

Shawna stepped nearer. “Nigga, he still has it.”

The man looked at her and smiled. “Keep it, girl. Cost me a weekend's sleep.”

Aiden touched the man's shoulder once and moved on with Shawna. She waited until they reached their table before letting go of his arm.

“How many niggas in here have you fucked?” Shawna asked him.

Aiden looked across the room.

Shawna caught his chin between two fingers and brought his face back. “Don't look around. Answer me.”

“You want me counting niggas I only kissed?” Aiden asked.

She struck his good shoulder with her purse. “Nigga, I want you sitting your ass down.”

He sat. She lowered herself sideways onto his lap, then rose again when his face tightened. Moving into the chair beside him, she left her hand on his thigh. When someone came to greet him, she drew him closer by the collar before releasing it.

The music stopped between songs. Onstage, Rosa stood in her red velvet coat, gold fabric showing beneath it from her throat to the floor. She held the microphone at her waist until the conversations nearest the stage quieted.

“Somebody owes me a song,” Rosa said.

Shawna shook her head at the table. “No.”

Rosa looked directly toward her. “I ain't said your name yet, bitch.”

“Rosa,” Shawna said.

People began clapping. Rosa held out her free hand. Shawna turned toward Aiden.

“You set this up?” she asked.

“No, baby,” Aiden told her.

He moved his hand off her chair. She stood, smoothed the front of her dress, and started toward the stage. One hand stayed against her stomach. Rosa came to the edge, caught her fingers, and helped her up the last step.

Rosa kissed her cheek and gave her the microphone.

“You know the one,” Rosa told her.

Shawna closed her eyes. The pianist played an opening phrase, paused, and repeated its last two notes more softly.

She began singing. Her first line wavered. She lowered the microphone, drew a breath, and brought it up again as the piano carried her into the next. The song followed a porch light left on, a car passing a house, a woman dressed and waiting for someone who did not come home.

At the second verse, a man stopped pouring champagne with the bottle still tilted above a full glass. Wine ran over his fingers before he set it down. A woman beside the bar covered her face. Rosa stood just off the stage with her coat open and both hands pressed against its lining.

Dante's watch slid toward Shawna's hand as she lifted the microphone. At the table behind Aiden, a man leaned toward his companion.

“Dante's song,” the man whispered.

Aiden turned sharply. His hand went to his side. The man remained facing the stage, his mouth closed now. Aiden looked back at Shawna, picked up the drink in front of him, and swallowed it. He stared at the empty glass for a moment before pushing it away.

On the last verse, Shawna opened her eyes and looked toward his table. He sat with one hand flat against the tablecloth and the other curled over his bandage.

The final piano notes rang into the room. Shawna lowered the microphone. People rose from their tables. Applause struck across the room in waves, joined by fists on tabletops and voices calling her name.

Aiden stood more slowly than the people around him. He clapped with his eyes on her. Near the stage, Rosa wiped her face with both hands before going up to take the microphone.

Shawna came down smiling. She reached Aiden's table and took both his hands.

“Did you like it?” she asked.

He drew her toward him and kissed her. The kiss continued after the applause began to subside. She brought one hand up to his chest. When he let her go, she stayed close, looking at his face.

“Beautiful,” Aiden said.

Shawna touched his cheek. “Why you looking at me like that?”

“Like what?” he asked.

Her smile faded. She lowered her hand.

“Never mind,” Shawna said.

***

Later, Rosa passed the cash tables on her way back from the stage. A man caught the sleeve of her red coat. She stopped, looked at his hand, and pulled her arm toward herself.

“I'm done working,” Rosa told him.

The man tightened his grip. “I paid.”

“You paid for last time,” Rosa said.

She pulled free. He caught the coat again, higher on the arm, and drew her back toward his chair.

Aiden stood at his table. Tank moved from beside the wall toward him, but Aiden had already passed the next chair.

“Aiden,” Shawna called.

The man looked over his shoulder without letting go of Rosa. Aiden struck him in the face. The man's chair tipped backward, and he fell onto the carpet with his legs beneath the table. Rosa's coat came free. Aiden pulled back his hand to strike again, but his other hand closed against his side.

Tank caught his raised elbow.

“Nigga, whose hand you think that is?” Aiden shouted at the man on the floor. “Take your fucking hands off her.”

The man held both hands against his own face. Blood ran between his fingers.

“You hear me?” Aiden demanded.

The man nodded from the carpet. Rosa pulled her coat straight, then pushed both shaking hands into its pockets.

“Get him out,” Aiden told Tank.

Tank kept hold of his elbow. “You sit down.”

“Get that nigga out first,” Aiden said.

Two men came from the doorway. Tank pointed at the man beneath the table. They lifted him by his arms and took him toward the exit. Conversations had stopped around them. A red spot showed through Aiden's white shirt beneath his open jacket.

Tank guided him into the back office. Shawna followed with Rosa beside her. Aiden sat in the chair behind the desk, lowering himself with his jaw clenched.

“You dumb motherfucker,” Shawna said.

She opened his jacket and looked at the spreading mark. He lifted a hand toward her shoulder.

“I'm okay,” Aiden told her.

Shawna looked up at his face. “Nigga, you don't know another fucking sentence?”

Rosa stood beside the door with her hands still inside her coat. Tank stepped past her to ask someone in the hall for clean cloths.

“I didn't need—” Rosa began.

Aiden turned toward her. “I know. I wanted.”

Her lips pressed together. Then she crossed the room, bent over him, and kissed his forehead.

“Stupid nigga,” Rosa said softly.

She stayed beside the chair while Shawna took the clean cloths from Tank. Shawna pressed one over the bandage, waited for the fresh blood to stop spreading, then sent Tank to bring another shirt.

***

At home, the lake lay black beyond the bedroom windows. Shawna stood in the bathroom removing her earrings. Each one clicked against the counter when she put it down. Aiden leaned against the doorframe in the clean shirt from the club.

She opened the clasp of Dante's watch. Its weight fell into her palm.

“Let me buy you one that fits,” Aiden said.

Shawna stopped with the watch in her hand. “A watch?”

“Yeah,” Aiden said.

She looked at it. “I have one.”

Aiden pointed toward her wrist. “It hangs off you.”

“I like it,” Shawna told him.

“I can see that,” Aiden said.

She set the watch beside the earrings and turned toward him. His fingers pressed against the doorframe. Neither spoke while water dripped from the end of the tap into the basin.

“Say it, Aiden,” Shawna told him.

He shifted his weight from one leg to the other. “That song about him?”

“Yes,” Shawna said.

Aiden looked down at the floor, then up at her again.

“You look at me when you sing to him?” he asked.

Shawna stayed beside the counter. “I looked at you because you were there.”

“I'm always there,” Aiden said. He touched his open collar. “You wearing his watch, my earrings, got his chain on my fucking neck—”

Shawna stepped toward him. “You want it off?”

She reached for the cross. Aiden caught her wrist.

Her hand stopped between them. She looked at his fingers, then at his face. He released her. She drew the hand back and rubbed the place his thumb had covered.

“Don't,” Aiden said.

Shawna lowered her hand. “Nigga, what do you want from me?”

He stared at the marble between their feet. His shoulders lifted with a breath, and his hand returned to the doorway.

“I want one thing that's only mine,” Aiden said.

Shawna came close again. This time she placed her palm flat against his chest, above the wound.

“This is you,” she told him. “I know who this is.”

Aiden looked at her hand. “Then why'd you say his name in the hospital?”

Her eyes closed. She kept her palm against him until his breathing slowed beneath it.

“I dreamed he was there,” Shawna said. “And I was trying to tell him you were hurt.”

Aiden's mouth opened. He closed it without speaking. Shawna took his hand, turned back for the watch, and led him out of the bathroom.

She put Dante's watch on the nightstand beside her. Aiden sat on the bed, the cross still around his neck. She sat beside him and kissed his mouth. He drew her hand between both of his and held it against his knee.

When they lay down, she rested her head on his good side. The lake moved faintly beyond the windows, dark ripples carrying the lights from the opposite shore.

Before dawn, Aiden opened his eyes. He turned his face toward Shawna. Her head remained against him, her hand open beside his on the sheet. He moved his fingers beneath hers and lay still until light began to touch the curtains.
