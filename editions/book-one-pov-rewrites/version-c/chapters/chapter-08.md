## 8. Let Him Think You Love Him

The inner clasp shifted beneath Shawna's thumbnail. Aiden leaned forward on the sofa and caught the edge of her hand before she could fold the bracelet shut.

“Wait,” he said. “There's something there.”

She held the watch beneath the lamp beside them.

“Where?” Shawna asked.

Aiden pointed without touching it. “There. Nigga, that bit moved.”

She turned the clasp toward herself, then toward him. A thin seam ran beneath the polished surface.

“You know how this opens?” Shawna asked.

“No,” Aiden said.

She drew the watch out of his reach. “Then give me back my watch before you become the next thing I hit with it.”

Aiden lowered his hand. “A nigga makes a discovery, suddenly the museum throws him out.”

Shawna called Rosa and put the phone on the cushion between them.

“I need a jeweler I can trust,” Shawna told her.

“Trusted to do what, bitch?” Rosa asked through the speaker. “Those are different men.”

Shawna laid the watch across her palm. “Open a watch without breaking it.”

“Oh,” Rosa said. “I got that nigga. He owes me money.”

Aiden leaned toward the phone. “That makes him trustworthy?”

“It makes him findable,” Rosa told him. “Do you want reliable or do you want found?”

Aiden sat back. “Send the findable nigga.”

An hour later, the jeweler sat at their dining table with the clasp beneath his loupe. He moved the watch on a square of soft cloth, turning it a little at a time. When Aiden reached across the table, Shawna moved the cloth farther toward the jeweler without looking at Aiden.

“Who built this?” the jeweler asked.

“Marcellus Vale,” Shawna said. “Dante told me.”

The jeweler lowered the loupe. “You know where he is?”

“Prison,” Shawna answered.

The man shook his head. “Got out nine days ago. Came to my shop asking about his old bench.”

Shawna's hand closed around Aiden's arm. He covered it with his own and leaned across the table.

“Nigga, call him,” Aiden told the jeweler.

The man reached for his phone. “I'm not—”

“Then call him whatever you are,” Aiden said. “Just call the fucking man.”

The jeweler held the phone still. “I was going to say I'm not certain he answers this number.”

Shawna turned toward Aiden. He spread his free hand above the table.

“Your nigga is excited,” Aiden said.

“Your nigga is going to let the man dial,” Shawna told him.

Aiden leaned back. The jeweler called, waited, and gave his name when a voice answered. After a few short sentences he passed the phone to Shawna.

She held it against her ear and said her name. The answer carried far enough that Aiden looked up from the watch.

“Dante's wife?” Marcellus asked.

Shawna covered her mouth and nodded. Aiden touched her wrist. She drew the hand away from her face and answered into the phone.

“Yes,” Shawna said.

Aiden held her other hand while she gave Marcellus the address. The jeweler replaced the loupe in its case and left the watch open on the cloth.

***

Marcellus came before sunset. He wore a brown coat over his narrow shoulders despite the warmth. Close gray hair lay flat against his head. A paper bag swung from one hand as he approached the gate.

Behind him, a car stopped. Three men got out. Marcellus turned at the sound of their doors, and the tallest caught the front of his coat. The paper bag fell between them. A clean shirt slid out onto the drive.

Aiden stood on the front steps. He took one step down, saw the man's hand tighten in Marcellus's coat, and turned toward the side of the house.

“Tank!” Aiden called.

Tank came through a side door with bread in his mouth. He looked at the gate and took the bread out.

“Those your jeweler's friends?” Tank asked.

“They holding him like enemies,” Aiden said.

Shawna came through the front door behind them. She stopped on the top step when she saw the older man against the gate.

“Marcellus,” she said.

Aiden looked back at her, then went down the drive with Tank beside him.

The tall man wore a purple suit and rings on almost every finger. A pale scar crossed one cheek. He pulled Marcellus closer to the bars as Aiden approached.

“Go inside,” the man told Aiden.

Aiden stopped on the house side of the partly open gate. “Nigga, I came outside for you. Don't make the trip disappointing.”

The man glanced at Tank. “Private business.”

Aiden put a hand against the gate's edge. “My private gate. See how a nigga keeps turning up in your problem?”

One of the other men opened his jacket. A pistol showed at his belt. Aiden looked at it and raised his face again.

“That's nice,” Aiden told him. “You buy a second one for when that one leaves you?”

The man drew. Tank struck him against the car before the pistol came level. The back window broke under his shoulders. His gun hit the drive, and Tank kicked it beneath the car.

The third man swung at Aiden. Aiden caught his shoulder, turned with him, and drove his face into the stone pillar beside the gate. Blood marked the pale stone. The man dropped onto his knees.

The tall man pulled Marcellus in front of him. A pistol appeared against the brown coat. Aiden stopped with one hand still on the gate. Tank held the first man against the broken window.

“Don't,” Shawna called from the drive behind Aiden.

Marcellus looked down at the clean shirt in the dust.

“Pick it up,” Marcellus told the man holding him.

The man blinked. “What?”

“My shirt,” Marcellus said.

The pistol shifted against his coat. “Nigga, you out your fucking mind?”

“No,” Marcellus told him. “I've been out nine days. That is the only shirt I have bought.”

The man looked across at Aiden. Aiden held his empty hand out, palm upward.

“Let him get the shirt,” Aiden said.

“You think I'm stupid?” the man asked.

Aiden kept the hand extended. “Nigga, I'm giving you a chance to surprise me.”

The man dragged Marcellus back another step. Marcellus's shoe caught the fallen bag. He stumbled, pulling the pistol away from his chest for a moment.

Aiden hit the gate with both hands.

It swung into them. The metal edge struck the gunman's forearm. His pistol fired into the drive and knocked a white chip from the stone. Marcellus dropped below the weapon, and Aiden caught the man's wrist against the bars.

The man tried to pull free. His rings struck the iron. Aiden pushed the wrist against the gap between two bars until the pistol fell, then drove the man into the gate again. The man slid down with his empty hand held against his chest.

Tank brought the other two onto the ground beside the car. The man from the pillar kept one hand against his face. His companion swore into the gravel until Tank pressed a knee between his shoulders.

Shawna ran past Aiden and knelt beside Marcellus. She put her hands beneath his arms.

“You hurt?” Shawna asked.

Marcellus looked down at a tear in his coat. “My coat.”

“Fuck the coat,” Shawna told him. “Are you hurt?”

He shook his head. She helped him stand.

Mercedes came down the drive, picked up the clean shirt, and shook the dust from it. She brushed both sleeves with her palm and held it toward the light.

“Little wash,” Mercedes told Marcellus. “You're handsome again.”

He looked at the shirt, then at her face. His mouth opened without a word coming out. He took it in both hands.

Aiden stood over the man at the gate.

“Who are you?” Aiden asked.

“Felix,” the man said.

“Felix what?” Aiden asked.

The man swallowed. “Barrow.”

Aiden pointed toward Marcellus. “What you want with him?”

Felix looked at his empty hand. “He owes people.”

“Which people?” Aiden asked.

“People inside,” Felix said. “He made things. Things got promised.”

Marcellus turned toward them, holding the shirt against his chest.

“I promised him nothing,” Marcellus said.

Aiden opened a hand toward Felix. “There you go. Nigga ain't got your promise. You ain't got your gun. Everybody learned something.”

Felix looked past him at the house and the cars. Shawna stood beside Marcellus while Mercedes folded his clean shirt. Tank rose from the other men and pointed at the ground when one began to move. The man lay flat again.

“You're that Aiden,” Felix said.

“Which one you hoping for?” Aiden asked.

Felix looked up at him. “The boat. The hearse.”

“Nigga, you're behind,” Aiden said. “There's two boats.”

Mercedes laughed. Tank joined her. Marcellus looked toward Aiden and raised his eyebrows.

Felix leaned his head back against the bars.

“I should've asked where he was going,” Felix said.

“Nigga, you should've stayed home,” Aiden told him.

Tank called Knox's officers. They arrived and took the three men into custody. Aiden pushed the fallen pistol away from the gate with his shoe and pointed it out to an officer before going inside with Marcellus and Shawna. Mercedes followed, carrying the clean shirt and its torn bag.

At the front door, Marcellus stopped beside Aiden.

“Dante would've liked you,” Marcellus said.

Aiden held the door open. “Nigga, everybody gets there. Some men just take the long way.”

Marcellus smiled. “Apparently I came to the right house.”

Aiden held out his hand. “Aiden.”

“I know,” Marcellus said, taking it.

“People talking?” Aiden asked.

“Dante's widow said your name six times on the telephone,” Marcellus told him.

Aiden looked toward Shawna. She lowered her face with a smile before Marcellus noticed the cross at Aiden's neck. He touched its edge with one fingertip.

“I did those letters,” Marcellus said. “Dante sent the cross in with the watch. Said his wife thought he was magnificent, and the nigga wasn't arguing.”

Shawna looked up. “He said that?”

Marcellus nodded. Aiden closed his hand around the cross and grinned at her.

“Then come inside,” Aiden told him.

In the living room, Mercedes placed the shirt and bag on a chair. Shawna crossed toward Marcellus and stopped just short of him. He opened his arms. She went into them and cried against the brown coat.

Marcellus rested one hand between her shoulders. His eyes closed. Aiden remained beside the doorway with his hand on the cross until Tank came in from the gate carrying a bag of food.

Tank stopped at the sight of them. The bag lowered beside his knee.

“Who this?” Tank asked Aiden quietly.

“Marcellus,” Aiden answered.

Tank looked toward the door. Marcellus opened his eyes and studied Tank's face. Shawna stepped back enough that he could see past her.

“Your friend?” Marcellus asked Aiden.

“Yeah,” Aiden said.

Marcellus kept his eyes on Tank another moment. “I see.”

Tank put the food on the table and went back outside.

***

Marcellus sat at the dining table with Dante's watch on the jeweler's cloth. The jeweler moved his tools within reach and sat back while Marcellus adjusted the light.

“Dante asked me where a man could keep a letter,” Marcellus said. “Back when we were inside. After he got out, an old client arranged a legal repair commission. The watch came in with the cross. I made the clasp and sent them back.”

He held the watch between two fingers and touched a narrow point against the seam. The inner plate moved. A thin folded piece of paper lay in the space beneath it.

Marcellus placed the paper on the cloth. He put both hands flat on the table beside it and left it there for Shawna.

She unfolded it. One edge had been torn from a longer note. Red ink crowded the narrow page. Beneath it, four darker words had been added in a different hand.

Aiden leaned close enough to read beside her. Shawna's finger passed beneath the first line and stopped.

“T,” she read aloud. “He has the withdrawal and Vale's story. If he gets the stone, he can name every one of us. Finish Dante tonight. No more brother shit. Valentino takes the watch. Keep her alive. I'll deal with her. R.”

She looked down at the darker writing.

“Shawna,” she read. “If I'm wrong.”

Her eyes went back to the beginning. She read it silently again, then started a third time and stopped before her finger reached the middle.

“No,” Shawna said.

Aiden reached for her hand. She drew it away and stood, knocking her chair over behind her.

“No,” she repeated. “He knew?”

Marcellus looked at the note. “He suspected.”

“He fucking knew,” Shawna said.

Aiden picked up the paper. He held it still in both hands. The edges did not move until his thumb pressed down over the final initial.

“Nigga,” Aiden said quietly.

Marcellus turned toward him. Aiden looked from the note to the blue invitation on the table, then back at the red ink.

“His handwriting,” Aiden said. “I held that nigga's other note all the way to breakfast. Same hand.”

Shawna stood at the window with both hands on her head.

“He went to talk to his brother,” Shawna said. “He told me there was a mistake he had to straighten out. I was mad because he wouldn't stay for dinner.”

Marcellus closed his eyes. Aiden put the note down on the cloth before getting out of his chair.

“I told him I'd throw his plate away,” Shawna said.

Aiden came toward her. “Shawna.”

She turned. “Don't say it.”

“I wasn't—” Aiden began.

“Don't fucking say there was nothing I could do,” Shawna told him. “I could've locked the door. I could've gone with him. I could've—”

“You could've died too,” Aiden said.

Shawna slapped him across the mouth.

His head turned with the blow. Blood appeared along the healing cut on his lip. Her hand remained raised between them as he brought his face back toward hers.

“Aiden,” Shawna said.

He drew a breath through his nose, took her wrist, and lowered her hand. Then he released it.

“I'm going outside,” Aiden told her.

He went through the patio door. Marcellus remained at the table with the open watch and note, his hands resting on either side of the cloth.

***

Tank stood by the lake throwing pieces of bread toward the birds. He turned when Aiden came down the path. His eyes stopped at the blood on Aiden's mouth.

“What happened?” Tank asked.

“Don't,” Aiden said.

Tank put the rest of the roll on the low wall beside him. Aiden stopped at the wall and gripped its edge with both hands, facing the water.

“She hit you?” Tank asked.

Aiden turned his face. “Nigga, I said don't.”

Tank raised one hand. “All right.”

A bird walked between them, reached up onto the low wall, and took the remaining roll. Tank watched it carry the bread toward the water with its head tilted to keep the whole piece above the ground.

“That was mine,” Tank said.

Aiden looked at the bird. “Go tell him.”

Tank stayed beside the wall. Aiden's mouth opened into the beginning of a smile, then tightened as the cut pulled. He sat down and touched it with the back of his hand.

“Red ordered it,” Aiden told Tank. “Dante. We got his own fucking handwriting.”

Tank looked toward the water.

“You know?” Aiden asked.

“No,” Tank said.

“You heard anything?” Aiden asked.

Tank's jaw worked. “I heard Dante took money.”

“And that's all?” Aiden asked.

“That was all anybody was saying around me,” Tank told him.

He faced Aiden again. The empty bread bag moved against his leg in the breeze.

“I didn't know him like that,” Tank said. “T had me moving things. Driving people. I wasn't in every room.”

Aiden looked at Tank's face for several seconds before turning toward the lake.

“I'm taking Red's diamond,” Aiden said.

Tank lowered his chin. “And then?”

“And then I'm going to look at how poor the motherfucker got,” Aiden told him.

Tank folded the bread bag. “Forty million ain't all he has.”

“Then I'll stay busy,” Aiden said.

The patio door opened. Shawna came down the path with a wet cloth in one hand and Dante's watch in the other. Tank picked up the empty bag and went toward the house, passing her without speaking.

She stopped in front of Aiden.

“I shouldn't have done that,” Shawna said.

“No shit,” Aiden told her.

She lifted the cloth a little. “Can I see?”

He turned his chin toward her. She cleaned the blood from his mouth, stopping whenever he drew back. His eyes remained on the lake beyond her shoulder.

“I love him,” Shawna said.

Aiden looked down at the watch in her other hand. “I fucking know, Shawna.”

“I love you too,” she told him.

He raised his face. “Did you tell Dante you loved him before he left that night?”

Shawna shook her head.

“I was mad about dinner,” she said.

Aiden stayed looking at her. She put the cloth on the wall and touched the uncut side of his face.

“I love you,” Shawna said again.

He took that hand and pressed it against his chest. Her other hand rested beside it, holding the watch by its open bracelet.

“Don't hit me again,” Aiden told her.

“I won't,” Shawna said.

He nodded toward the watch. “And don't take that off because you think it'll shut me up. Put it back on. I want the whole thing when you tell me something.”

She looked down at it, then fastened it around her wrist. Aiden waited while she closed the clasp. When she finished, he kept her hand against his chest for another moment before standing beside her.

***

They ate the food Tank had brought. Marcellus sat at the dining table with a clear protective sleeve beside his plate. Inside lay a letter whose folds had worn pale across the writing. Shawna's watch was closed on her wrist. The separate note ordering Dante's death remained on the jeweler's cloth near the middle of the table.

Marcellus passed the sleeve to Aiden.

“Celeste's,” Marcellus said. “What Dante heard.”

Aiden set down his fork. He looked at the handwriting, then at Marcellus, who nodded once. Aiden began reading the letter aloud.

“My love,” Aiden read. “You think I never understood that stone. I did. I understood every hour you spent getting it right. I know the little words you hid beneath the blue mounting. I know you were waiting for me to find them, and I know you were disappointed when I liked the front so much I forgot to look behind it.”

He moved the sleeve closer to the lamp and continued reading.

“I found them the night you fell asleep with your shoes on,” Aiden read. “I was angry with you for missing supper. I took the necklace off and nearly dropped it, and there you were underneath, saying what you used to say at my door. I came for you.”

Marcellus put down his fork. Aiden's eyes stayed on the letter as he read the next paragraph.

“I wore those words against me every night afterward,” Aiden read. “You never asked whether I'd found them. You just watched me wear it and smiled.”

Shawna laid her hand beside Aiden's plate. He moved his free hand to hers before continuing.

“I am so tired now, Marcellus,” Aiden read. “I can't keep driving out there. Don't let anybody tell you I stopped because I stopped loving you. I am stopping because I am sick, and I want the strength I have left for things that don't require me to pass through a locked door.”

He paused at the next fold. Marcellus reached across, flattened the sleeve beneath his fingertips, and withdrew his hand. Aiden read on.

“If you get your beautiful stone back, put it down before you touch somebody you love,” Aiden read. “I know your hands. They are warmer without all that metal in them.”

Tank sat with both hands on his knees beneath the table. Aiden read the final lines more quietly.

“And if you are still angry when you come home, be angry,” Aiden read. “You always were. Just open the curtains. Pay the piano man what I owe him. There is chicken in the freezer because I never learned how to cook for one. I would take the ride again. Celeste.”

He lowered the sleeve onto the table. For a while nobody spoke. Then he pushed it back toward Marcellus, who placed it inside his coat with both hands.

Aiden looked across at him.

“You want your stone?” Aiden asked.

“Yes,” Marcellus said.

“You want Red dead?” Aiden asked.

Marcellus looked at Shawna, then back at Aiden.

“Yes,” Marcellus said.

Aiden nodded. “Nigga, I think we can work together.”

Marcellus did not smile. Shawna laid the gala invitations beside the empty place where the letter had rested.

“He's putting it on display in three nights,” Shawna said.

Marcellus picked up an invitation. A picture of the blue necklace covered its front. He tilted it beneath the lamp, then laid it flat again.

“He's putting something on display,” Marcellus said.

“There's two,” Aiden said.

“Yes,” Marcellus answered.

Aiden leaned forward. “You tell them apart?”

“Of course,” Marcellus said.

“Then you're going,” Aiden told him.

Marcellus touched the picture. “He knows my face.”

“He knows the face he put in prison eight years ago,” Aiden said. “We'll give you another suit and somebody else's name.”

Rosa came into the dining room as he spoke. She stopped beside Marcellus and looked him over from his coat to his shoes.

“I know a woman who can make you look like a man with money,” Rosa told him.

Marcellus looked around the dining room, then toward the horse statue visible beneath the stairs.

“Apparently it's easy to confuse people,” he said.

Tank covered his mouth with his napkin. Aiden looked at the statue and raised one finger toward Marcellus, then lowered it without answering.

Marcellus turned the invitation over and used its plain back to draw a small outline with the jeweler's pencil.

“There are different settings,” Marcellus said. “The blue outer mounting can be removed. A buyer might wear the stone another way. The original has a flaw inside it. Very small. I remember exactly where it is. The copy doesn't.”

Shawna pointed at the photograph on the other invitation. “But the necklace in the picture?”

“Could hold either,” Marcellus told her.

Aiden sat back. “Then we get you close enough to look.”

Shawna moved her chair nearer the table.

“I can get him to invite us closer,” she said.

“No,” Aiden told her.

She turned toward him. “Aiden.”

“No,” he repeated.

Shawna held out her hand. “You haven't heard what I'm saying.”

Aiden looked at the chair she had moved. “I heard it in the way you sat up.”

She took his hand and held it between them on the table.

“I tell him I made a mistake,” Shawna said. “That you scare me. That I'm bringing you to the gala because I want him to handle it quietly.”

Tank lowered the napkin. “And he believes that?”

“He wants to,” Shawna said.

Aiden's fingers tightened around hers. “What else?”

“I tell him I know where your money is,” Shawna said.

“I don't have his fucking money,” Aiden told her.

“He doesn't know that,” Shawna answered.

Tank lifted his glass, held it just short of his mouth, and set it down again without drinking.

“I tell him I'm tired of running,” Shawna continued. “I'll come back if he clears the debt and leaves you alive.”

Aiden turned his face toward hers. “Nigga, why he going to leave me alive?”

“Because I'm going to say you can give him the west side,” Shawna said.

Aiden let go of her hand. The table fell quiet. Shawna leaned toward him, keeping her hand open where his had been.

“I'm saying what he'll listen to,” she told him. “You know that.”

Marcellus folded the invitation along its crease.

“If he believes she can give you to him, he'll want you close enough to take,” Marcellus said.

Aiden put both hands on the table. “Then he can fucking try.”

Rosa looked from him to Shawna.

“You get near the jewel, the old man looks at it, we leave with what's his,” Rosa told her. “You do not decide to kill somebody in the middle because you get mad.”

Shawna looked down at the invitation. Rosa waited, then pointed toward Aiden.

“You either,” Rosa said.

Aiden looked around the table. “Nigga, why everybody looking at me?”

Rosa folded her arms. “Because you have the face.”

Tank nodded. Aiden looked at him, then took Shawna's hand again.

***

Shawna made the call upstairs, sitting on the edge of the bed with Aiden beside her. She put the phone on speaker and held it between them. Outside the window, the lake had gone black. The room's light reflected their faces in the glass.

Red answered on the second ring.

“There she is,” Red said.

Shawna closed her eyes. Aiden put a hand on her knee.

“Can you talk?” she asked.

“For you?” Red said.

Aiden's fingers curled against the fabric of her dress. She opened her eyes and looked at his hand before answering.

“I think I made a mistake,” Shawna told Red.

The line remained quiet for a moment. From somewhere near Red's phone came the clink of a glass.

“He hurt you?” Red asked.

Shawna looked at the reopened cut on Aiden's mouth.

“I don't know what he's going to do next,” she said.

“Come home,” Red told her.

Shawna kept her face toward Aiden. “I can't just leave.”

“Yes, you can,” Red said.

She drew a breath. “You want the money. You want his streets. I can bring him to you, Red. But you have to promise me something.”

“Anything,” Red said.

Aiden lifted his face toward the phone. Shawna's free hand moved over his.

“You let him live,” she told Red.

Red sighed into the receiver. “You're too good to these boys.”

Aiden took his hand off Shawna's knee. She caught it before he could rise, holding it between both of their legs.

“I know where he's keeping the money,” Shawna said. “I can get him to bring what you need. At the gala, I'll stay close to you. Everybody can see I'm back. After that, you talk to him.”

“And you stay?” Red asked.

Shawna looked directly at Aiden.

“Yes,” she said.

Red breathed out audibly. Aiden's hand closed inside hers.

“Wear the blue dress,” Red told her. “The one I liked.”

“I will,” Shawna said.

“And Shawna?” Red asked.

She held the phone still. “What?”

“I knew you'd remember who loved you,” Red said.

Shawna ended the call without answering. She laid the phone facedown on the bed and took Aiden's face in both hands.

“Look at me, Aiden,” she told him.

He turned toward her.

“I'm here,” Shawna said.

He pulled her close. His face tightened as her body pressed against his side, but his arms stayed around her. She moved onto his uninjured side and kept her forehead against his.

Downstairs, Marcellus sat beneath the dining-room lamp with the pencil in his hand. He had drawn the blue mounting and a small mark inside the outline of the stone. Celeste's letter remained inside his coat. Rosa stood beside the dressmaker at a table of blue fabric, lifting one piece into the light and putting another aside.

Tank stood by the side door with his phone against his ear. When footsteps approached from the kitchen, he turned toward the dark garden and lowered his voice.

Upstairs, Aiden rested his chin against Shawna's hair.

“Three nights,” he said.

She held his hand against her shoulder. Beyond the window, a light moved slowly along the far shore and disappeared behind the trees.
