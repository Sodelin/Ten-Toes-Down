## 11. Blue Mercy

The singer took a breath over the first piano chord, and Shawna lifted her head. Beside the dance floor, Aiden stopped with his glass halfway to his mouth. Red leaned down to Shawna's ear. Whatever he said disappeared beneath the first line of the song; she looked at their joined hands, then let him guide her around a couple turning beneath the chandelier.

Tank arrived at Aiden's shoulder with another drink. Aiden still had half of the first one. Tank took that glass away and put the fresh one in his hand.

“This one's for drinking,” Tank told him. “Don't throw it.”

“Then why bring it where I got hands?” Aiden asked.

Tank set the unfinished drink on a passing tray. “A nigga might surprise me once. I keep hoping.”

Aiden drank until the ice touched his upper lip. His free hand pressed against his side beneath the white jacket. Across the room, Marcellus spoke with an older woman whose necklace covered most of her bare chest. Every time she nodded, its lower edge struck a button on her dress. The jeweler inclined his head toward it, listened to her answer, and glanced past her at Red's hand.

On the dance floor, Shawna's skirt passed across Red's trousers in a blue sweep. Red bent toward her again. Aiden lowered his glass so hard that liquid jumped over its rim.

“Nigga,” Aiden said.

Tank followed his gaze. “I see.”

Aiden pointed at the dance floor with the bottom of his glass. “Nigga.”

“I know,” Tank said.

“No, you don't,” Aiden told him.

Tank retrieved the unfinished drink from another waiter's tray and swallowed the rest of it.

The man who approached them wore six championship rings and a gold mouthguard on a chain. His jacket stretched tightly across his shoulders. Behind him came three men with open collars, two women carrying their shoes, and a waiter who had been trying to deliver the same plate of scallops through the group for several minutes.

“Captain Aiden,” the man said.

Aiden looked from the mouthguard to his face. “Depends who's bleeding.”

The man held out his right hand, showing three of the rings. “Rex Crown.”

Tank leaned around Aiden to inspect the other three. “You bring the whole display?”

Rex kept his hand extended until Aiden shook it. “Heard you think you can fight.”

“Heard you get paid to,” Aiden replied. “Congratulations. Have a drink.”

Rex did not take the glass Aiden offered. “I'm asking you.”

Mercedes appeared between two of Rex's companions, with Porsche close behind her. Mercedes looked at Rex's rings and then at Tank. Tank put a hand over his mouth.

One of the men behind Rex stepped forward. “Champ asked you something.”

Mercedes held up a finger between them. “My nigga answered. You want him to sing it? He plays piano too.”

“Ruined a breakfast,” Tank said from behind his hand.

Rex stepped around Mercedes. “Five thousand says I put you down before your woman finishes dancing.”

Aiden turned his head. Across the floor, Red raised Shawna's arm and passed her beneath it. Her skirt brushed his green jacket. Aiden watched until Tank touched his elbow.

Aiden set the drink on the nearest table. “Five thousand? Rex, you famous. Don't come over here with the introductory offer.”

“Twenty,” Rex said.

“Mercedes,” Aiden asked, “what did a front-row ticket cost when this nigga used to win?”

Mercedes rested her elbow on Porsche's shoulder. “More than he can afford to lose, apparently.”

Rex's jaw moved above the gold chain. “A hundred thousand.”

One of his friends caught his sleeve. Rex shook the hand away so sharply that the man nearly spilled his champagne down a woman's back. Porsche put her own drink on the table and straightened.

“Somebody heard that besides me?” Porsche asked.

Rex looked around the growing circle. “Everybody heard.”

Porsche turned to Mercedes. “Find a pen.”

Aiden unbuttoned his jacket. Tank reached for it, then stopped when Aiden's hand went briefly to his ribs.

“Your side,” Tank said.

“Hold it on the good side,” Aiden told him.

“Your jacket hasn't been shot,” Tank said. “You have.”

Aiden slipped his arms free. “Then it's had an easier week. Let it help.”

Tank folded the jacket across his arm. The badge clipped inside flashed under the chandelier, and Rex's gaze dropped to it.

“You a cop?” Rex asked.

Tank spread the lining wider. “You walked over calling him Captain. What you think he captains? Breakfast?”

Rex's friends began cheering. One of the barefoot women shouted his name and slapped the sole of a high heel against her palm. The waiter finally pushed the scallops through the gap and escaped.

Rex pulled off his jacket. “I said a hundred.”

Mercedes uncapped a pen. “We heard you. Keep some breath for whatever happens after counting.”

They made space beside the tall windows. A waiter wheeled the champagne table back several feet, leaving a chair against the wall. The band continued playing, although the pianist looked away from his keys long enough to miss a chord. Red turned toward the gathering. Shawna looked over his shoulder and shook her head once at Aiden.

Aiden lifted one finger.

Shawna closed her eyes, then opened them and watched.

Rex removed the mouthguard from its chain and fitted it between his teeth. He rolled his shoulders, shifted his weight, and brought his fists up. His rings had gone into his jacket pocket. The gold in his mouth remained.

Tank leaned toward Mercedes. “Nigga brought jewelry he can chew.”

Mercedes bent over with both hands on her knees.

Rex's first punch crossed the space between the men before Mercedes straightened. His shoulder drove behind it, his other fist tucked close to his face. Aiden stepped inward and to the side. Rex's knuckles passed his cheek. Aiden's left foot planted beside Rex's forward shoe; his fist drove up beneath the champion's ribs.

There was a short, heavy sound through the shirt. Rex's mouth opened around the gold guard. His knees bent and his upper body folded over Aiden's arm.

Aiden caught the back of Rex's collar before the champion's forehead reached the champagne table. He held him there while Rex dragged air through his nose. A glass at the table's edge trembled, settled, and remained standing.

Porsche pressed her lips together. “Nigga.”

“Nigga,” Mercedes answered.

Tank looked down at the bent champion. “That's about the whole sentence.”

Aiden backed Rex toward the chair and lowered him into it. Rex braced his palms on his knees. One friend crouched beside him; another looked from the chair to his watch and back again.

“You said before the dance,” Aiden told Rex. “You still got two verses. Enjoy them.”

The circle broke into noise. A woman grabbed her husband's sleeve and dragged him nearer. Phones rose. A man with a cigar behind his ear tried to ask Aiden three questions while taking a photograph and received a picture of Tank's elbow. The woman carrying her shoes watched Rex's chest move, slowly, then with a deeper breath.

“I've seen every one of your title fights,” she told Rex. “I've never seen you sit down so respectfully.”

Rex held up a hand until he could speak. “Again.”

“Breathe first,” Aiden said.

Rex lifted his head. “Again.”

Aiden crouched far enough to meet his eyes. “You want a rematch or a hospital bracelet?”

Rex looked at him for several seconds. His hands loosened on his knees. He drew another breath and let it out through his open mouth.

“What the fuck are you?” Rex asked.

Aiden stood and took his jacket from Tank. “Her man.”

He looked toward Shawna while pulling the jacket over his shoulders. The people nearest him turned too. Shawna had stopped beside Red at the edge of the floor. Her mouth widened into a smile. Red looked down at her, then back at Aiden. His own smile disappeared.

Porsche approached Rex with Mercedes's pen. Rex took a checkbook from his jacket, rested it on his thigh, and wrote with the paper jumping slightly under his hand. He tore out the check and held it toward Aiden.

“I don't welch,” Rex said.

Aiden read the amount, then gave the check to Mercedes. “For you and Porsche. You got my car out of that first night.”

Mercedes held the paper by both edges. “That's a hundred thousand dollars.”

“Want it smaller?” Aiden asked.

Mercedes pulled it against her chest. “Don't you fucking dare.”

She kissed one of Aiden's cheeks. Porsche kissed the other, leaving a red mark beneath his eye. Rex sat between his friends and watched Porsche point at the amount again. She and Mercedes were still bent over the check when a thin man in a silver jacket reached beneath his lapel.

“Give it back,” the man said.

The barrel of his pistol pointed at Mercedes's chest. She moved behind Aiden, taking the check with her. The circle of guests opened abruptly. A woman stumbled over the shoes she had put on the floor.

Aiden kept his hands low. He looked at Rex. “Your nigga?”

Rex pushed himself out of the chair with both arms. “Put it away, Silas.”

Silas kept the pistol level. “He made you look—”

“He beat me,” Rex said. “Put it away.”

Silas turned his face toward Rex without lowering the weapon. Aiden caught his gun wrist and drove it upward. Tank hit Silas's shoulder from the side. The shot went into the ceiling; a small chandelier spat crystal across the tablecloth and floor.

Silas landed on his stomach with Tank's knee between his shoulder blades. Aiden twisted the gun free. It fell beside the table and slid across the polished floor. Porsche kicked it beneath the empty table, followed it in with one foot, and pinned it under her heel.

Mercedes put the check inside her purse. Then she struck Silas across the back of the head with the purse.

“You made me bend down,” Mercedes told him.

The band had stopped. A cymbal continued quivering beside the drummer's raised stick. Rex stood over Silas with both hands spread at his sides.

“You pull a gun because I lost?” Rex demanded.

Silas turned his face far enough to speak against the floor. “I was helping.”

“You was ruining my fucking reputation twice,” Rex said.

Two uniformed officers came through the lobby doors. Aiden pulled his jacket open to show his badge while keeping Silas's wrist bent behind him. Tank raised his free hand. Porsche called the officers toward the table and lifted her heel only when one of them crouched to collect the pistol.

The other officer drew out handcuffs. “Captain?”

Aiden stepped back as the cuffs closed. “This nigga's evening is over.”

The officers brought Silas upright and led him between the parted guests. His silver jacket had collected dark marks from the floor. A waiter looked at the broken crystal, picked up the champagne bucket, and moved it to another table without spilling a drop.

The drummer lowered his stick. The pianist found the opening notes. Music spread back across the room, first thinly, then under the chatter of people describing the gunshot to people who had also heard it.

Rex offered Aiden his hand. “You ever need somebody in your corner.”

Aiden shook it. “Stay out of mine first.”

Rex laughed, caught his ribs, and laughed more carefully. He gave Aiden a short bow of the head before a friend helped him toward the bar.

Aiden wiped Porsche's lipstick from his cheek with his thumb. He turned toward the dance floor. Red and Shawna had begun moving again.

Red guided Shawna past the musicians, where the piano notes sounded clearly between the beats of the bass. He bent close enough that she turned her face slightly away from his mouth.

“You used to sing this upstairs,” Red said.

“I sang a lot of things,” Shawna answered.

Red's hand moved farther around her waist. “Before Dante.”

Shawna looked up at him. “I had a life before all of you.”

Red brought her through a turn. Her wristwatch touched his cuff. The blue stone on his hand passed beneath a white light, then disappeared as he lowered their joined arms.

“You could have that life now,” Red told her.

“Could I?” Shawna asked.

Red smiled. “Whatever you want.”

Shawna looked down at the ring. Red rotated his hand inward and drew her through another step.

“I want him left alone,” Shawna said.

Red's mouth stopped moving with the song. “Already thinking about that nigga?”

“His name is Aiden,” Shawna told him.

“I know what the nigga's name is,” Red said.

A couple passed close to them. Red smiled at the man over Shawna's shoulder. Shawna returned the woman's greeting. The couples separated, and Red's hand settled firmly at Shawna's back.

“You said you were tired of running,” Red said.

“I am,” Shawna answered.

Red drew her closer. “You said you wanted to come home.”

“I said I wanted somewhere I could sleep,” Shawna said.

Red lowered his voice. “My bed was never the problem.”

Shawna's shoulder pulled back. Red's arm remained around her, and the fabric at her waist tightened between his fingers.

“Don't do that tonight, Shawna,” Red said.

She looked directly at him. “Then don't talk to me like that.”

They stopped. Another couple went around them, the woman's skirt brushing Shawna's ankle. Red's jaw worked once before he spoke again.

“Do you know what it felt like?” Red asked. “Standing there while he put that ring on you? Paying for a room where you were going to spend the night with him?”

Shawna held her chin level. “Dante was your friend.”

“Nigga knew,” Red said.

“Knew what?” Shawna asked.

Red brought their hands up between them. “That his nigga saw you first.”

Shawna pulled her hand far enough back to look at the marks his fingers had left. “You all keep saying that like I was a fucking parking space.”

Red loosened his grip. Another smile went across his face as a man raised a glass toward them. Red nodded to the man and began dancing again.

“I could've given you everything,” Red told Shawna.

She waited until they had passed the next couple. “Then give me the diamond.”

Red's feet slowed. Shawna kept moving, drawing his arm with her until he followed.

“That's what tonight is for,” Shawna said. “You wanted me to know how serious you were. Show me.”

Red looked across the room. “And Aiden?”

“You let him walk out,” Shawna answered.

Red watched Aiden standing beside Tank. “Will he?”

Shawna placed her free hand against Red's cheek. “He will if I tell him to.”

At the side of the floor, Aiden lifted his glass. He emptied it without looking away from Shawna's hand.

Earlier that afternoon, before the cars left the house, Aiden and Shawna had stood in front of their bedroom mirror. Her blue dress lay across the bed. She held one earring against her ear while he adjusted his collar behind her.

“If he keeps you beside him, I'll come over,” Aiden had said. “Make him think I can't stand it. You tell me to leave you alone.”

Shawna lowered the earring. “I can do that without hitting you.”

Aiden met her eyes in the mirror. “If he's still watching us like he doesn't believe you, one slap.”

“No,” Shawna said.

Aiden turned her gently by the elbow until they faced each other. “One. I can take a slap if it gets you close enough to finish this.”

“You say that now,” Shawna told him. “Then you get in there and start being you.”

Aiden put both hands at his sides. “I listen. You tell me to go, I go.”

Shawna studied his face before raising her palm halfway between them. “And this doesn't turn into another excuse for you to hit something.”

“Agreed,” Aiden said.

She lowered her hand. “One slap. Only if we need it.”

Now, at the ballroom's edge, Aiden put the empty glass down and walked toward them. Tank called his name. Rosa stepped away from Marcellus and reached for Aiden's sleeve, but her fingertips brushed the white fabric without catching it.

Aiden stopped beside Red. “Your dance is over.”

Red continued the turn, drawing Shawna around with him. “You hear the band? The band's still playing.”

Aiden put a hand on Red's shoulder. Red stopped beneath it. Shawna looked first at that hand, then at Aiden's face.

Red spoke without raising his voice. “That hand comes off me.”

“Yours comes off her first,” Aiden replied.

Three men in dark jackets left the wall. Tank stepped onto the floor behind Aiden. The singer missed a word and stared at the men for half a measure before finding the next line.

Shawna stepped between Aiden and Red, forcing both men to release their holds. Her watch swung down against the heel of her hand.

“Aiden,” Shawna said, “go sit down.”

Aiden's mouth remained open for a moment. “What?”

“You're embarrassing me,” Shawna said.

Aiden glanced at the approaching men. He looked back at her. “You mean that about your nigga?”

Shawna's palm struck his cheek. The sound reached the tables nearest the floor. Aiden's head turned with the blow. Her hand stayed raised for a second, trembling visibly, before she lowered it against her dress.

“I told you,” Shawna said, “to listen to me.”

Aiden turned his face back. A red patch spread above his jaw. He stood still while Shawna looked at him, then stepped away. Red moved up beside her. The three men stopped a few feet behind him.

Shawna extended her hand to Red. “Let's finish our dance.”

Red took it. He led her away while looking past her shoulder at Aiden. Tank caught Aiden's sleeve and pulled once toward the tables.

“Come on,” Tank said.

Aiden jerked his arm but did not free it. “Don't.”

Tank kept his grip. “Come on.”

Aiden went with him. Rosa met them at the edge of the floor and stood close enough to keep her words beneath the music.

“She just kept you alive,” Rosa told Aiden.

He looked at the carpet. “I know.”

Rosa waited until he looked up. “Then act like you know.”

Aiden put his palm against the side where T's bullet remained. Across the room, Red kissed Shawna's hand. Aiden stayed beside Tank.

At nine, the bandleader lowered his baton and stepped back from the microphone. Red walked onto the low stage with Shawna beside him. Two attendants drew open a curtain behind them, exposing a glass display case beneath a narrow white lamp.

A necklace lay in the case. Its central blue stone rested against dark velvet, small enough to cover with a thumb. The lamp sent blue light across the edges and left a bright point burning at its center.

Aiden leaned toward Tank. “All that for something a nigga could lose in a couch.”

Tank did not answer. Marcellus had moved in front of them. His mouth hung slightly open; the small jeweler's case was pressed so tightly against his leg that one finger had gone white along its handle.

Red took the microphone. “Some things wait a long time to come home.”

He turned toward Shawna. Applause rose from the tables, and he waited for it to end before bringing the microphone close again.

“Tonight isn't an auction,” Red said. “Nobody in this room can afford what this means to me.”

A man in front laughed loudly. Red looked over his head.

“Tonight is a promise,” Red said.

He raised Shawna's hand. Dante's watch slid toward her elbow, exposing the pale skin beneath the band. Tank said something too low to carry beyond Aiden's shoulder.

Marcellus approached the steps. An attendant stopped him, spoke with him, then gave his name and profession to Red. Marcellus climbed onto the stage and inclined his head. The gray in his disguise caught the white light as he bent toward the case.

Red lowered the microphone. “You approve?”

Marcellus kept his eyes on the necklace. “I would like to see it closer.”

“Later,” Red said.

Marcellus raised his case a little. “A piece of this age deserves—”

“Later,” Red repeated.

The blue ring flashed as Red moved the microphone into his other hand. Marcellus's gaze followed it. He lowered his case and stepped back.

Shawna looked past the front tables until her eyes met Aiden's. She held his gaze. He nodded once, and she turned toward the applause when Red faced the room again.

After the presentation, an attendant found Aiden beside a column. Tank stood with him. Marcellus had returned from the stage and was closing the empty straps on his little case.

“Mr. Redmond would like you upstairs,” the attendant said.

Aiden kept his back against the column. “Nigga can come get me.”

Tank touched his arm. Across the room, Shawna spoke to Rosa beside the stage. Rosa's hand rested at her back. Both women looked toward the column when the attendant repeated the invitation. Shawna nodded.

Aiden pushed away from the stone. “All right. Show me.”

Tank followed. Marcellus picked up his case and came with them. At the foot of the stairs, the attendant turned and held a hand in front of the jeweler.

“The invitation was for—” the attendant began.

Aiden stopped two steps above him. “Your boss is giving away forty million dollars. He can let a jeweler look at it before everybody leaves.”

The attendant lowered his hand. Marcellus passed him without speaking.

The office stood above the ballroom. The bass reached them through the floor, a dull repeated pressure beneath the carpet. Dark wood covered the walls between the city-facing windows. A wide desk stood opposite the entrance; another door, set into the wall beyond it, opened as Aiden came in.

Shawna entered through that inner door carrying a small blue box. Red followed her and went behind the desk. Two men stood outside the main doorway while the attendant withdrew.

“I thought you might prefer privacy,” Red said.

Aiden stopped on the visitors' side of the desk. “For what?”

Red rested both hands on the polished wood. “The end.”

Shawna took a step toward Aiden. Red moved from behind the desk and put a hand on her shoulder.

“Tell him,” Red said.

Shawna held the box in both hands. “Aiden, take the box.”

Aiden extended his empty hand toward her. “Come here to your nigga.”

“Take it,” Shawna said.

The lid tapped against her thumb. Aiden stepped close enough to lift the box from her shaking fingers. He opened it. Inside, the blue stone and its necklace lay in a fitted velvet bed, exposed to the desk lamp.

Red watched him over Shawna's shoulder. “Forty million. Enough to start over anywhere.”

Aiden raised his head. “You think I'm selling her?”

“I think she just sold you,” Red said.

Marcellus came forward. Red raised one finger, and the two men entered from the main doorway. One stood behind Tank; the other moved beside Marcellus. The jeweler stopped before reaching the box.

Shawna looked at each man in turn. Her hands had closed against the seams of her dress. Aiden lowered the open box to the desk without releasing it.

Red returned to his chair and opened a drawer.

“And since your friends are so interested in history,” Red said.

He placed a flat leather folder on the desk. Across its white label, in heavy black letters, was one name: DANTE.

Shawna drew a sharp breath. Aiden reached across the desk and pulled the folder toward him. Red left his hands flat on either side of the empty space.
