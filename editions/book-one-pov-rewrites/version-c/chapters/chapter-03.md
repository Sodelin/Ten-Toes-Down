## 3. A King with a Hole in Him

The first doctor who approached the car asked Shawna who she was to Aiden. Two attendants had brought a wheeled stretcher beneath the entrance canopy. One held its sheet back while the other reached into the Mercedes.

“His wife,” Shawna said.

Aiden opened his eyes. His hand moved against the towel at his side and stopped.

“Name?” the doctor asked.

“Shawna,” she said.

The doctor bent over Aiden. “His name.”

“Aiden,” Shawna answered. “His name is Aiden.”

The attendants drew him out of the seat. Shawna followed with both hands caught in his open shirt. Her bare feet left small marks on the polished floor beyond the entrance. The front of her black dress had stiffened with dried blood; one diamond earring remained beneath her hair.

“Let go of him, Shawna,” the doctor said.

Shawna's hands stayed clenched in the cloth. The stretcher moved several feet before the attendant nearest her stopped it.

“Baby,” Aiden said.

She bent over him at once. His voice had barely carried past the pillow.

“Don't let them cut my chain,” he told her.

Shawna lifted his head enough to draw the chain clear. She kissed the cross, put it around her own neck, and tucked it inside her dress. Then she released his shirt. The attendants rolled the stretcher through a pair of doors, and Aiden's eyes closed before they swung shut behind him.

***

When he opened them again, daylight crossed the bottom of a white curtain. A woman in a white coat stood beside his bed with two fingers against his wrist. She watched the second hand of her own watch, then set his hand carefully on the sheet.

“Dr. Marisol Vega,” she told him. “You're alive.”

Aiden moved his head against the pillow. “Shawna?”

“Sleeping,” Vega said.

“Where?” Aiden asked.

The doctor pointed toward the window. Shawna lay curled in a chair with Aiden's bloody shirt clutched against her. Hospital socks covered her feet. The cross had slipped out of her dress. Dante's watch hung loose above the hand beneath her cheek.

“Don't wake her,” Aiden said.

“She'll wake herself,” Vega told him. “She has been doing it every eleven minutes.”

Aiden put an elbow against the mattress and tried to rise. His face tightened. He dropped back onto the pillow with his mouth open.

“Fuck,” he said.

Vega adjusted the sheet without moving the dressing beneath it. “Yes. That. You will be doing quite a lot of that if you continue.”

He lay still until his breathing slowed.

“Where's the bullet?” Aiden asked.

“Still inside you,” Vega said. “Removing it would add risks you do not need. We treated the damage. You need time.”

Aiden stared at the ceiling. He turned his face toward the dressing, then back toward her.

“Nigga shot me and moved in,” he said. “How much time?”

“More than you want,” Vega told him.

Aiden raised two fingers off the sheet. “Niggas waiting downstairs, Doc. A nigga can't run a whole west side horizontal.”

“They can keep waiting,” Vega said.

She brought a cup to his mouth and held it while he drank. A little water ran down his chin. He took the cup from her, tried to hold it upright, and lost it when his hand shook. Plastic struck the bed rail and fell onto the floor.

Shawna came out of the chair so quickly that the bloody shirt slid beneath it.

“Aiden?” she said.

“Right here,” he told her.

She touched his face, his shoulders, the hair above his ear. Her fingers stopped whenever they reached a cut, moved away, and returned somewhere else. Her eyes stayed above the sheet.

“You scared me,” Shawna said.

“I'm sorry, baby,” Aiden told her.

She kissed his forehead. “Don't be sorry. Don't do it.”

She slapped his uninjured arm, then bent and kissed him again. Tears fell onto his cheek. He held her wrist against the pillow while she pressed her mouth to the corner of his.

Vega picked up the cup. She put a fresh one on the bedside table and went out, closing the door quietly behind her.

Aiden looked at Shawna's face. “You told them you're my wife.”

Shawna wiped beneath both eyes with the back of her free hand. “They weren't going to let me in.”

“You could've said sister,” Aiden said.

“I wanted them to let me in,” Shawna told him. “I didn't want to make it weird when I kissed you.”

He laughed and caught his side. She put her hand above his, waiting while he breathed through his teeth.

“Say it again,” Aiden said.

Shawna sat carefully on the bed's edge. “What?”

“What you told the doctor,” Aiden said.

She touched his cheek. “Nigga, you trying to die married?”

“Trying to live that way,” Aiden told her.

Her hand stopped. He kept looking at her. After a moment she moved closer and laid her head beside his on the pillow.

“Let me get you home first,” Shawna said.

***

Tank arrived that afternoon in a fresh suit. Dirt remained around the edges of his shoes, and a bruise darkened one side of his jaw. He stopped in the doorway with a bunch of flowers held against his chest.

Shawna straightened beside the bed. Aiden turned his head on the pillow. Tank lifted the flowers an inch.

“These for her,” Tank said.

Shawna did not reach for them.

“Nigga, put the flowers down before we both start feeling strange,” Aiden told him.

Tank laid them on the visitor's table. A little water leaked from their wrapping onto a hospital brochure. He moved the brochure out of the wet patch, then stood with both hands empty.

“You alone?” Aiden asked.

“Niggas downstairs waiting,” Tank said.

“Whose niggas?” Aiden asked.

Tank's mouth worked once against the bruise. “Were T's.”

“And now?” Aiden asked.

Tank looked toward Shawna before answering. “That's what they wanna know.”

She stood up. “I'm not leaving.”

“Didn't ask you to,” Tank said.

Shawna faced him across the bed. “You looked.”

Tank spread his hands. “I got eyes.”

Aiden held out his hand. Shawna took it without turning away from Tank. The monitor beside the bed continued its small, steady sounds.

“Where's T?” Aiden asked.

“Funeral people got him,” Tank said.

“His other niggas?” Aiden asked.

Tank looked down at the foot of the bed. “One alive. One ain't.”

Shawna lowered her face. Aiden pressed his fingers around hers. Neither spoke until Tank shifted his weight on the wet edge of the brochure he had dropped.

“The money?” Shawna asked.

Tank kept his eyes on the bed. “Pool was empty when I got back.”

Shawna pulled her hand free of Aiden's. “Three million doesn't walk.”

“Plenty people got legs,” Tank said.

She came around the bed far enough that Tank lifted his face.

“Look at me when you say it,” Shawna told him.

Tank met her eyes. “I looked, Shawna. Niggas move fast when nobody watching. Money wasn't there.”

Aiden raised a hand between them.

“Leave it,” he said. “Nigga, she threw it away to save me. It saved me. That's money spent.”

Tank turned toward him. “You always this rich?”

Aiden's hand settled onto the sheet. “I woke up.”

Tank looked at the monitor, then at the tube running beside Aiden's arm.

“Yeah,” Tank said. “You did.”

He took Aiden's pistol from inside his jacket and held it with the grip toward him.

“Brought this from the car,” Tank said.

Aiden pointed at the bedside cabinet. “Put it there.”

Tank laid the pistol inside and closed the drawer. Shawna watched his hand leave the handle before stepping away from the cabinet.

He took a chair but sat only on its front edge. Shawna went to the window. Sunlight caught the face of Dante's watch as she folded her arms.

“Club men are downstairs,” Tank said. “Suppliers too. Everybody calling about what they're owed. Some of the niggas who owe us won't answer now.”

Aiden rubbed his thumb along the edge of the sheet. “Red?”

“Stopped answering the west side,” Tank said. “T's office is locked. Leave it that way till sundown, some nigga gonna decide we ain't got an office anymore.”

Aiden looked at him without speaking.

“T put you there,” Tank continued. “People heard him. But putting you there and keeping you there ain't the same shit.”

“Nigga, you keeping me there?” Aiden asked.

Tank worked his jaw. “I drove your car.”

Aiden did not look away. “That ain't what I asked.”

Tank's eyes went to the watch on Shawna's wrist. She unfolded her arms and let the hand hang at her side. The watch slid toward her knuckles.

“I ain't putting another man in the desert,” Tank said.

He rose, moved the flowers clear of the wet table, and went to get the men downstairs.

***

Vega returned to find nine men in Aiden's room and two more in the hall. The visitor's chair held a duffel bag. A man wearing three gold teeth had pressed himself against the window to make room for another man whose jacket hung open over a pistol.

“Absolutely not,” Vega said.

Everyone turned toward her. One man stepped farther into the room, blocking the door behind himself. Vega pointed past him at the hallway.

“Two visitors,” she said. “The rest of you leave.”

Nobody moved. Aiden lifted his head off the pillow.

“Niggas, she said move,” he told them. “You want the woman who kept me alive having to step over you?”

The man in the doorway backed out. Others followed, turning sideways between the bed and the visitor's chair. One caught his chain on a coat button and had to stop while another unhooked it. Vega held the door until the last of them had passed.

Tank remained. So did an older man from T's club, who laid a stack of papers across Aiden's knees. Shawna took her chair near the window and drew it beside the bed. Vega looked at her, looked at the two standing men, and held up two fingers once more.

“One conversation,” Vega told them. “Then she gets her chair back and you get out.”

Tank nodded. The older man straightened the papers with both palms.

“Money coming in Friday,” the older man said. “People need paying today.”

“What's in the bag?” Aiden asked him.

The man pointed to the duffel. “Yesterday.”

Tank opened it. Cash filled the bag almost to the zipper. Aiden looked into it, then at the date at the top of the first sheet.

“Niggas worked, niggas getting paid,” Aiden said.

The older man touched the bag's handle. “From this?”

Aiden looked at him. “Unless you got some fucking magic beans.”

The man nodded and made a mark beside the first name. He moved his pen to the next page without looking at the bandage beneath Aiden's open gown.

“Red wants a meeting,” he said.

“Nigga, Red can call,” Aiden told him.

The man lowered his pen. “He wants it face to face.”

“Nigga, look at mine,” Aiden said. “I'm busy.”

The older man's eyes rose to the cuts and swelling. Tank closed the duffel. Outside, one of the waiting men laughed at something, then stopped when another voice hissed at him.

Aiden pushed the papers toward the older man.

“Nobody touches Shawna,” Aiden said. “Nobody says her name to Red. Anybody got a problem with that can leave the keys on the bed.”

The man looked at Tank. Tank stood beside the door with one hand still on the bag.

“You heard him,” Tank said.

The older man gathered the papers. His keys stayed in his pocket. He nodded once to Shawna and left.

Aiden's head sank onto the pillow. A dark patch showed along the edge of the dressing beneath his gown. Shawna rang for the nurse, then laid her hand against his forehead.

Tank moved the duffel closer to the bed.

“You want me counting it?” Tank asked.

Aiden closed his eyes for a breath and opened them on Tank again. “Nigga, I want you watching that door like your brother in here.”

Tank looked toward the hall. “For who?”

“Every fucking nigga,” Aiden said.

Tank took a chair outside. When the nurse arrived, he stood and moved it back to give her room. After she entered, he sat down facing the corridor.

***

Rosa came that evening with three bags and a red velvet coat folded over one arm. Tank rose halfway out of his chair when she reached him. She went past before he could finish standing.

“Where the fuck is she?” Rosa called through the door.

Shawna turned from the window. “Rosa.”

They met halfway across the room. Rosa dropped the bags and caught Shawna in both arms. Copper hair fell over the broken-heart tattoo beneath Rosa's ear as she pressed her face against her friend's.

“You don't answer your fucking phone?” Rosa demanded.

“I broke it,” Shawna said against her shoulder.

Rosa held her tighter. “You borrow a phone, bitch. You send a pigeon. You don't let me hear you're dead from some nigga in a rhinestone hat.”

“I'm here,” Shawna told her.

“I see you,” Rosa said. “I'm mad anyway.”

She held Shawna at arm's length and turned her face toward the light. Her thumb touched the little scar above Shawna's eyebrow without pressing it. Then she looked toward the bed.

“This him?” Rosa asked.

Shawna nodded. “Aiden.”

Rosa came closer. “You shoot that nigga T?”

Aiden pushed himself a little higher against the pillow. “Yeah.”

“Good,” Rosa said.

She leaned over the bed and kissed his forehead, leaving another mark beside Shawna's. Aiden started to lift a hand toward it. Rosa caught the hand and put it back on the sheet.

“Stay alive,” Rosa told him. “She doesn't have another black dress in her.”

Shawna's face folded. Rosa turned and took her back into the crook of her arm. Aiden held out his hand, then left it open on the bed until Shawna came close enough to take it.

Later, Rosa unpacked clothes onto the visitor's chair and set covered food on the table. Steam rose when she lifted a lid. Tank leaned through the doorway. Rosa handed him a container without looking up, and he withdrew with it held level in both hands.

“Still singing weekends?” Shawna asked her.

“At the Saint,” Rosa said. “Till they tell me I can't. Then some nigga'll pay for a room and tell me his wife don't understand him.”

Shawna shook her head. Rosa set another container beside the first.

“Thirty-four,” Rosa said. “I charge extra when the problem got grandchildren.”

Aiden laughed once and held his side. Shawna turned toward him; he raised a hand before she could get up.

From the last bag, Rosa took a worn songbook. Its cover had bent around the corners, and loose pages stuck out at one side. She laid it in Shawna's lap.

Shawna opened the middle of the book. Her fingers stopped on a page. She closed it so quickly that the paper snapped beneath her palm.

“Later,” Shawna said.

“Whenever,” Rosa told her. “Kept it for you. It ain't going anywhere.”

Shawna ran her hand down the cover and set it on the windowsill.

That night, Rosa painted Shawna's nails beside the window. She held each finger toward the lamp before releasing it. Tank ate in the hallway, his chair turned far enough that he could see both approaches to the door.

A man came upstairs with another bag. Aiden heard the voices outside and called through the open doorway.

“Leave the money downstairs,” he told Tank. “I got enough furniture in here.”

Tank sent the man back. Rosa screwed the cap onto the polish and fanned Shawna's fingers with a folded magazine.

Long after the room lights had been lowered, Shawna lay against Aiden with her eyes closed. He rested one hand on her hair. The cross hung between them, its chain crossing the edge of his gown.

“Dante,” Shawna whispered in her sleep.

Aiden's hand stopped. He looked down at her face. Her breathing continued against his chest. He did not move her. When the first gray light reached the window, his eyes were still open above her head.

***

On the third afternoon, two men rolled a black coffin through the hospital's private entrance. Silver handles ran along both sides. Aiden's name had been engraved across the lacquered lid in large letters.

The nurse at the downstairs desk put down her pen. She watched the men bring the coffin toward the shallow fountain in the center of the atrium. Then she picked up the telephone and called Aiden's room.

“Mr. Aiden, there is an item here for you,” the nurse said.

Upstairs, Aiden held the receiver against his ear. “Flowers?”

“It is larger than flowers,” the nurse told him.

Aiden looked at the fruit basket Mercedes had brought. “Piano?”

The nurse kept her eyes on the men. “It is a coffin.”

Tank came out of his chair. His plate struck the floor, spilling food beneath the table.

“Don't open it,” Aiden told the nurse.

“I wasn't planning to try it on,” she said.

Shawna took the receiver from him and moved toward the window.

“Get people away from the entrance,” she told the nurse. “Please. Anybody sitting near those men, get them into the back.”

Aiden reached for his trousers. Tank caught the fabric before he could pull it across the bandage.

“Nigga, no,” Tank said.

“Get my shoes,” Aiden told him.

Tank pointed at his feet. “You got hospital socks.”

“Then get shoes worthy of the fucking socks,” Aiden said.

Mercedes stood beside the unopened champagne she and Porsche had carried upstairs. Against the wall, an enlarged photograph showed Aiden's Mercedes above the speedboat. Shawna's open mouth could be seen through its windshield; one of Aiden's hands remained on the wheel while the other braced beside it.

Mercedes looked through the window. It overlooked the atrium; beyond its glass front, the private drive remained visible in the afternoon light.

“That's the coffin they're sending you?” she said to Aiden. “Baby, even your enemies know you're expensive.”

Porsche stood beside her. Two white sedans waited across the drive. Four men had got out, two remaining beside the cars while two approached the building's side entrance.

“Four more by the cars,” Porsche told Tank. She pointed down toward the atrium. “Those two aren't delivering shit.”

Below, both men beside the coffin reached beneath their jackets. Tank caught Aiden's arm and pulled him away from the window as a shot broke the glass at the ground-floor desk.

The nurse dropped behind it with the receiver still in her hand. The line filled with a scream, then the crash of the entrance doors. An alarm began. The deliveryman nearest the fountain shoved the stand sideways. One of its small wheels hit the basin's rim, and the coffin slid off into the water. His companion shouted for everyone to get down.

Aiden took his pistol from the bedside cabinet. Shawna put the phone down and faced him.

“Shawna, with Rosa,” Aiden said. “Bathroom. Lock it.”

She reached for his wrist. “I'm not—”

“Nigga, be alive when I come back so you can cuss me out,” Aiden told her.

Shawna's hand stayed against his wrist for another second. Then she took Rosa's hand. Mercedes lifted the heavy glass vase off the table, leaving the flowers in a wet heap, and followed them into the bathroom. The lock clicked behind her.

Porsche had gone to the hall door. She pulled in the wheelchair parked beside it and pressed down its footrests.

“Wheelchair,” Porsche said.

Tank looked from the chair to Aiden.

“The man can't walk fast,” Porsche told him. “He can roll.”

Tank pulled the trousers over Aiden's feet and helped him stand long enough to fasten them below the dressing. Aiden took the shirt Porsche held out, worked his arms through it, and left it open. Tank got his shoes on before lowering him into the chair. Porsche took the handles. The first turn through the doorway pulled at his side, and he gripped the armrest until the chair ran straight.

Outside, the corridor led toward an upper gallery overlooking the entrance atrium. A stairwell opened at the far end, beyond the bend. Off the gallery itself stood a recessed service lift and a separate broad stair leading down to the lobby. Through the glass above the gallery doors, light flashed as another shot broke something below.

A nurse was trying to turn an older man's bed out of a room. One of its wheels had caught the doorframe. The patient held his gown shut with both hands; a cast enclosed one leg from below the knee to the foot.

“Go with them,” Aiden told Porsche.

She held the wheelchair still. “Who pushes you?”

Aiden laid the pistol across his lap and set his hands on the chair's rims. “Nigga, I got hands.”

Porsche caught the corner of the bed and pulled it free. Tank moved ahead to the bend in the corridor. Aiden followed with short pulls on the wheelchair rims, stopping between each one to breathe.

The far stairwell door opened. One of the men from the white sedans came through with a pistol. He stopped at the sight of Tank, then looked past him to the wheelchair. His mouth spread into a grin.

“That's the king?” the gunman asked.

Tank raised his own weapon. “That's the nigga.”

The man fired. Tank struck the wall beside Aiden as a bullet broke the corner above his shoulder. Aiden took the pistol from his lap, leaned away from the chair's back, and fired. The gunman's shoulder jerked; he fell backward through the stairwell door, dropping his pistol onto the polished floor.

Another man appeared behind him and pulled him out of the doorway. Tank fired into the upper doorframe. Both men dropped from view.

Porsche and the nurse reached the bend with the bed. The patient pressed his gown more tightly against himself as another bullet hit a pipe fitting above the corridor. Water burst down from the damaged sprinkler head and spread beneath the wheels.

“Don't look down,” Aiden told the patient.

The older man kept his eyes on the ceiling. “Wasn't planning to, son.”

“Good,” Aiden said. “Your nigga's got this.”

Tank caught the wheelchair's handles and ran. Porsche and the nurse pushed the bed after them. Water struck Aiden's hair and shirt. Each seam in the floor jarred his body against the chair.

Behind them, the two men came through the stairwell door again. The first held his injured shoulder with one hand. The second fired down the corridor, his shoes sliding on the wet floor when he tried to stop at the bend.

Aiden turned far enough to see them. “Tank!”

“I see them!” Tank shouted.

“Then quit sightseeing and push!” Aiden told him.

The wheelchair struck the gallery doors and drove them apart. Tank swung it behind the wall beside the opening. Aiden fired back through the gap. The uninjured man dragged his wounded companion behind the stairwell door as plaster fell from the corridor ceiling.

Tank braced against the doorframe and kept his pistol trained on the far end. From the stairwell below the men came a shout.

“Police! Drop it!” an officer called.

One shot followed, then a long scraping sound against the stairwell door. The two attackers did not reappear in the corridor. Through the narrow opening came another order, answered by a man's voice.

“It's down!” the man shouted. “Gun's down!”

Porsche and the nurse rolled the bed onto the gallery. She shut the corridor doors behind them and took a chair from beside the lift, holding it by its back while she stood at the seam.

Below the gallery, the coffin lay partly inside the shallow fountain. Its wheeled stand had tipped against the basin's edge. One deliveryman crouched behind the black lid. The other stood near the reception desk with his gun raised toward the upper floor.

Aiden fired from the chair. The standing man's arm jerked back. He dropped behind the desk, and his pistol struck the floor beside it. The man at the fountain fired upward. Glass burst from a panel beneath the gallery's handrail, scattering across the tiles.

Tank pulled the wheelchair away from the rail. One wheel caught the broken panel's bottom bracket. The chair tipped. Aiden struck the floor on his side, rolled with a hoarse cry, and got one knee beneath him.

Porsche put down the chair and crouched beside him. Behind her, the nurse held the bed against the wall in the lift recess. The old man's fingers had whitened against his gown.

Across the atrium below, the broad stair ended beside the reception desk. The service lift opened on the same stretch of floor. The gunman behind the coffin turned his head between that landing and the broken gallery panel, his pistol following each movement.

Aiden looked through the opening beneath the handrail.

“Nigga, who sent it?” he called down.

The gunman raised his face above the coffin lid. “Who you think?”

“I think your taste is fucking awful,” Aiden told him.

He pushed himself upright against the wall. Tank reached toward him. Aiden caught the overturned wheelchair with his foot and shoved it toward the broken panel. Its first wheel passed through the opening, then the rest tipped outward beneath the rail.

The chair struck the fountain in a crash of metal and water. It hit the coffin and drove it across the shallow basin. The gunman jumped clear as the black lid swung toward his knees. Tank fired, and the man ducked behind a marble column.

Aiden pulled himself to the rail.

Tank turned toward him. “Don't you dare.”

“Catch up,” Aiden said.

He went over.

His shoes struck the middle of the coffin lid. Black wood split beneath him, and both knees bent into the opening. Water leaped around the box as it struck the bottom of the fountain. Aiden caught a silver handle with his free hand and hauled one foot clear.

For a moment he stood above his own engraved name, bent at the waist, his pistol held low beside him. People crouched along the walls of the atrium. A woman behind an overturned chair had both hands over her mouth.

The gunman came from behind the column. His pistol clicked without firing. He threw it at Aiden and lunged into the fountain.

Aiden tore a broken section of lid free and swung it. The lacquered edge struck the man's shoulder. They fell together into the water, scattering floating flowers from a nearby arrangement. The man caught Aiden at the throat and drove him against the coffin's jagged edge.

Aiden's mouth opened against the water. He planted a foot against the basin, rolled the man beneath him, and raised his pistol beside the man's face. The hands at his throat opened.

“You finished?” Aiden asked.

The man nodded.

Aiden pressed the muzzle closer without firing. “Nigga, use your words.”

“Finished,” the gunman said.

“Then stay fucking finished,” Aiden told him.

Tank came down the broad stair with his pistol trained across the lobby. He kicked the other deliveryman's dropped gun beneath the reception desk and went to the fountain. The man behind the desk remained on the floor with one hand pressed against his bleeding arm.

At the upper gallery, Porsche and the nurse pushed the bed into the service lift. The nurse held the control while Porsche steadied the patient's cast. When the ground-floor doors opened, Tank stood between them and the fountain with his gun pointed at the captive.

Porsche brought the bed out. The patient raised a fist from his gown toward Aiden.

“That's my boy!” the older man called.

Aiden tried to climb over the fountain's edge. His knee slid in the water, and Tank caught him beneath one arm. The bed passed them toward the rear treatment corridor while Porsche held its front corner clear of the broken entrance glass.

Aiden put his pistol back in his waistband while Tank held him upright.

Outside, the two drivers had got back into the white sedans. The first accelerated toward the gate. A police cruiser struck its side and pushed it across the exit. The second braked behind it with no room to pass.

A woman stepped from the cruiser carrying a long gun. Her dark glasses stayed in place as she lifted the weapon toward the first sedan.

“Hands where I can fucking see them!” she shouted.

Hands rose behind both windshields. Officers ran in from another car and opened the sedan doors.

Two minutes later, Chief Gloria Knox entered the atrium through the broken entrance. Silver showed at her temples above the arms of her glasses. She looked at the coffin, then up at the shattered gallery panel, and finally at Aiden leaning against Tank beside the fountain.

“You jumped from there?” Knox asked.

Aiden pointed toward the man still sitting in the water. “Nigga was in my way.”

Knox tilted her head. “And that solved it?”

The captive groaned. A broken strip of lacquer floated against his chest.

Knox looked down at him. “Apparently.”

Vega came from the treatment corridor with another nurse. She stopped at the sight of Aiden's wet shirt. Blood had begun spreading through it below the ribs.

“Aiden,” Vega said.

He opened his free hand toward the patient, the nurse, and Porsche. “Everybody's alive.”

Vega's eyes stayed on his side. “You were in bed.”

“Didn't have wheels,” Aiden said.

Porsche raised a hand. “I tried a chair.”

Vega shut her eyes, opened them again, and held out an arm for the fresh stretcher the nurse brought beside them.

“You saved my nurse,” Vega told Aiden.

“She saved my ass first,” Aiden said.

The nurse who had moved the old man's bed covered her face with one hand. The patient insisted on being rolled close enough to shake Aiden's. Aiden gave him his unhurt hand, and the older man gripped it with both of his.

Knox took a card from her pocket and held it in front of Aiden. He released the patient's hand to accept it.

“When you're tired of people sending coffins, come see me,” Knox said. “I'd rather give you a badge.”

Tank looked at her. “This nigga?”

“Especially this nigga,” Knox told him.

Upstairs, an officer knocked on the locked bathroom door and announced himself. Shawna opened it with Mercedes beside her, still holding the vase. Rosa came out behind them. The officer pointed toward the gallery, and the three women went down the broad stair together.

Shawna reached Aiden as Vega began lowering him onto the stretcher. She got both arms around his shoulders, kissed his face, and drew away to look at the fresh blood on his shirt.

“Impossible,” Shawna said. She kissed him again. “You are fucking impossible.”

Aiden's hand rested against her back. He kept the other pressed to his side while Vega waited beside them with a clean dressing.

“You can continue on the stretcher,” Vega told Shawna.

Shawna moved with him when the attendants laid him down. This time her hands stayed clear of the wound. Mercedes set the vase on the reception desk. It stood upright beside the hole a bullet had made in the wood.

That evening, workers carried the broken coffin outside. Knox stood beside a truck while they loaded it. Two of the captured attackers sat handcuffed near its rear wheel, including the man Aiden had fought in the fountain. He looked up toward the hospital windows as officers brought him to his feet.

Aiden raised two fingers from the edge of his bed. The man looked away.

Vega took the hand and placed it back on the sheet. Shawna drew the curtain before the doctor began checking the new dressing.

***

By Aiden's fifth day in the hospital, he could cross the room slowly with somebody beside him. He stopped halfway to the window and put a hand on the visitor's chair. Shawna stopped too. When he started again, she moved with him without taking her hand off his arm.

Vega stood by the door with the discharge papers.

“Leaving doesn't mean healed,” she told him. “Walk a little. Rest. Sit down when you're tired. No heroics.”

Tank waited behind the wheelchair with two duffel bags at his feet and Rosa's red coat over one arm. He looked at the papers, then at the fresh bandage beneath Aiden's shirt.

“He won't,” Shawna told Vega.

The doctor turned toward her. “You either.”

Shawna nodded. Aiden looked from one woman to the other and lowered himself into the wheelchair.

Before Tank pushed it toward the door, Shawna lifted the cross from around her neck. She held it in front of Aiden. He opened his collar, and she lowered the chain over his head, drawing it clear of the healing cut at his hairline.

The cross settled against his chest. Her hand stayed over it for a moment. Dante's watch slid toward her knuckles when she withdrew it.

Downstairs, six black cars waited beneath the hospital canopy. Men stood beside them as Tank brought the wheelchair out. One straightened his jacket. Another opened the rear door of a Mercedes and stepped back, holding it wide.

“Welcome back,” the man told Aiden.

Aiden set both feet on the pavement. Shawna took his arm while he pushed himself up from the chair. His shoulders lifted with the effort, then lowered when he stood straight.

She kept her hand beneath his elbow.

“Where we going?” Shawna asked him.

Aiden looked at the open car door, then down at her.

“Home, baby,” he said. “We just gotta get one.”
